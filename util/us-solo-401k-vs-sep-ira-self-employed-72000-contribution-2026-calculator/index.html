How the estimate works

For Schedule C, the model subtracts half of estimated regular self-employment tax from net profit, then applies the 20% employer rate. It uses 92.35% of positive profit for the tax calculation, at 12.4% up to the Social Security wage base and 2.9% for Medicare. It assumes no W-2 wages have used the Social Security wage base. For corporations it uses 25% of eligible owner W-2 wages.

Regular Solo 401(k) deferrals and employer contributions use the $72,000 annual additions cap. Eligible catch-up is added separately, with total contributions limited by compensation. Estimates assume an owner-only business, a plan that permits catch-up, and an unrelated day-job employer. Actual plan rules may lower your contribution.

The tax estimate compares potential pre-tax deductions at the marginal rate you enter. It applies the Publication 560 self-employed deduction worksheet cap where relevant and assumes any catch-up shown is pre-tax. The estimate excludes payroll taxes, changes to the qualified business income deduction, and the effect of crossing tax brackets. IRS Publication 560 and 2026 federal income tax brackets.

Frequently asked questions

How much can I contribute to a Solo 401(k) or SEP IRA in 2026?

With enough eligible compensation, the 2026 annual additions cap is $72,000. A Solo 401(k) that permits catch-up contributions can reach $80,000 at ages 50–59 and 64 or older, or $83,250 at ages 60–63. Use your age at year-end. A standard SEP IRA has employer contributions only and no catch-up.

What if I already contribute to a day-job 401(k)?

Traditional and Roth employee deferrals share the $24,500 regular limit across your 401(k) plans. Deferrals above that limit use available catch-up room. Employer contributions at an unrelated day job generally do not use your own business’s annual additions limit.

How is the employer contribution calculated?

The model uses 20% of Schedule C profit after half of estimated self-employment tax, or 25% of eligible owner W-2 wages. The annual additions and compensation limits still apply. Publication 560 can further limit the deductible employer amount when a sole proprietor defers most of their adjusted earnings.

When are 2026 catch-up contributions Roth?

If your 2025 FICA wages from the employer sponsoring the plan exceeded $150,000, the 2026 Roth catch-up requirement generally applies. Roth catch-up contributions count toward your contribution total and provide no current income tax deduction.

How does a traditional SEP IRA affect a backdoor Roth conversion?

Form 8606 generally aggregates traditional, traditional SEP and traditional SIMPLE IRA balances. A traditional SEP balance at year-end can increase the taxable share of a backdoor Roth conversion. Solo 401(k) balances are outside the IRA calculation. Other IRA balances still count.
