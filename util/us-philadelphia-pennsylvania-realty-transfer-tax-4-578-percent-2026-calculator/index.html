Frequently asked questions

What is Philadelphia’s realty transfer tax in 2026?

Philadelphia charges 3.578% and Pennsylvania charges 1%, for 4.578% combined. A taxable $500,000 sale produces $22,890 in transfer tax. A 50/50 split allocates 2.289% of the taxable value, or $11,445, to each party.

Can a builder make the buyer pay the entire transfer tax?

A purchase agreement can allocate 100% of the tax to the buyer. On a $500,000 taxable Philadelphia purchase, that adds $11,445 to buyer cash due compared with a 50/50 split. State law holds both parties liable for the full tax regardless of the contract. Certain contracts for improvements with an affiliated builder can also increase the taxable base.

When does the Common Level Ratio affect transfer tax?

For an ordinary arm’s-length sale, use actual consideration, including applicable assumed debt. For taxable transfers that require computed value, multiply the county assessment by the published CLR valuation factor for the applicable deed date. The higher-of option is a planning scenario.

Which family transfers can qualify for a zero-tax estimate?

Qualifying direct transfers between spouses, parents and children, grandparents and grandchildren, or siblings can be exempt from both Philadelphia and Pennsylvania transfer tax. Confirm eligibility and documentation for the deed with the recorder before relying on a zero-tax estimate.

PA Department of Revenue exemption and filing guidance

How much more is Philadelphia transfer tax than a 2% suburban rate?

The rate difference is 2.578 percentage points. On the same taxable value of $500,000, the total difference is $12,890. With the same 50/50 split in both locations, the buyer pays $6,445 more in Philadelphia.

Estimates cover realty transfer tax only. Each tax portion is rounded to cents, and a split that produces a half-cent rounds the buyer’s share up. Recording fees and other closing costs are separate.
