Key takeaways

  • Most people start with £60,000. The taper applies only when threshold income exceeds £200,000 and adjusted income exceeds £260,000.
  • The minimum is £10,000. You reach it at £360,000 of adjusted income if threshold income is above £200,000.
  • Carry forward can cover the excess. Use your 2026/27 allowance first, then use eligible balances from the previous three years, oldest first.

Check before you add to your pension

A bonus or an employer contribution can reduce your allowance and leave you with a tax charge. Check the 2023/24 balance first. Any unused amount from that year drops out of the carry-forward window after 5 April 2027.

The £200,000 threshold income strategy

An eligible gross relief-at-source contribution can bring threshold income to £200,000 or below and restore the full £60,000 allowance. For example, if threshold income is £210,000, a £10,000 gross contribution normally costs £8,000 before any further tax relief. The entire £10,000 also counts toward your pension input.

Check your current-year earnings and relief eligibility before relying on this scenario. HMRC can disregard qualifying arrangements that shift income between years to reduce the taper.

How this estimate works

Threshold income is net income minus gross relief-at-source contributions plus relevant salary sacrifice. Adjusted income is net income plus employer pension contributions and employee net pay contributions. For cases involving defined benefit accrual, certain lump sum death benefits or additional pension relief claims, use HMRC’s full calculation.

The estimate applies the selected top rate to pension input above the combined allowance. Your charge for Self Assessment may span tax bands. If you have triggered the MPAA, use HMRC’s separate rules for money purchase inputs.

Frequently asked questions

When does the 2026/27 pension taper apply?

Both tests must be met. Threshold income must exceed £200,000 and adjusted income must exceed £260,000. The £60,000 allowance falls by £1 for each full £2 above the adjusted income limit, reaching £10,000 at £360,000 of adjusted income.

How does three-year carry forward work?

Use your 2026/27 allowance first, then eligible unused allowance from 2023/24, 2024/25 and 2025/26 in that order. You must have been a member of a registered pension scheme in the year you carry forward from. Use the allowance that actually applied that year and subtract any balance already used.

Can a relief-at-source payment bring threshold income down to £200,000?

An eligible gross relief-at-source contribution reduces threshold income by its gross value. At £200,000 or below, the taper no longer applies. The new contribution also uses annual allowance, and tax relief depends on your relevant UK earnings and eligibility. HMRC can disregard qualifying arrangements that shift income between years to reduce the taper.

Do relevant UK earnings limit how much I can contribute?

Relevant UK earnings generally limit the tax relief on personal contributions. Eligible people with low or no earnings can usually receive relief at source on up to £3,600 gross. Carry forward increases annual allowance headroom and does not increase the personal tax relief limit. Employer contributions use annual allowance but sit outside the personal earnings limit.

How is the tax charge estimated and when should I check Scheme Pays?

This calculator applies a single top rate to the uncovered excess: 45% for England, Wales and Northern Ireland or 48% for Scotland. Your actual charge depends on the tax bands the excess falls into. A charge over £2,000 prompts a Scheme Pays check. Mandatory Scheme Pays also requires pension input above £60,000 in the individual scheme and a timely election.
