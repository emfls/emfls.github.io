Answer-First: Key Borrowing Capacity Rules

  • APRA 3.0% Buffer: Australian authorized deposit-taking institutions (ADIs) assess loan serviceability at your contract rate plus 3.00% (e.g. 5.95% + 3.00% = 8.95%), substantially compressing maximum loan limits.
  • 3.8% Credit Card Rule: Banks calculate 3.8% of your total credit card limit every month as an ongoing commitment, regardless of whether you pay the balance in full each month. A $15,000 card limit permanently deducts $570/month from debt-service capacity.
  • Marginal HECS Schedule: Under the Universities Accord marginal system, student debt is repaid on a progressive marginal basis above $69,528, eliminating the previous "cliff" effect while still treating repayments as a recurring income deduction.
  • Debt-to-Income (DTI) Cap: APRA monitors total residential mortgage lending where DTI exceeds 6.0x gross income. Borrowers approaching 6.0x face tighter credit standards and underwriting scrutiny.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the APRA 3% mortgage serviceability buffer?

The Australian Prudential Regulation Authority (APRA) mandates that banks assess all new mortgage applications at an interest rate at least 3.00% higher than the contractual interest rate to ensure borrowers can withstand future interest rate hikes.

How does HECS-HELP debt reduce borrowing capacity in Australia?

Banks treat compulsory annual HECS-HELP repayments as a recurring liability that directly reduces your net monthly disposable income. Because borrowing capacity is calculated at the buffered rate, a $400/month HECS deduction can reduce maximum borrowing capacity by $55,000 to $65,000.

Why do unused credit card limits reduce borrowing capacity by 3.8% per month?

APRA and Australian credit guidelines require lenders to assess credit cards at their full available credit limit rather than the actual balance, assuming a 3.8% monthly repayment commitment. A $15,000 credit card limit deducts $570/month from your borrowing capacity.

What is considered a high Debt-to-Income (DTI) ratio by APRA?

A total debt-to-income (DTI) ratio of 6.0x or greater is considered high risk under APRA macroprudential guidelines. Banks are restricted in the proportion of new residential lending they can write at or above 6.0x DTI.

Should I pay off my HECS-HELP debt before applying for a home loan?

If clearing a small remaining HECS balance frees up significant net disposable monthly cash flow, it can unlock substantial borrowing capacity. However, if using cash to pay HECS leaves you with less than a 20% deposit, you may incur Lenders Mortgage Insurance (LMI).
