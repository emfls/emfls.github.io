Key takeaways

£100,000 of adjusted net income (ANI) passes the income test. If either parent goes over, the household can lose England’s working-parent funded hours and Tax-Free Childcare. An agreed sacrifice of future pay equal to the excess brings ANI back to the cap.

Between £100,000 and £125,140, the Personal Allowance taper can take 60p of every extra £1 of employment income in Income Tax, plus 2p in standard employee National Insurance.

How to read the estimate

Funded hours assume a full year, with nursery costs displaced at the entered hourly value. Tax-Free Childcare is shown at the full cap. Use actual eligible bills after the funded hours to judge how much top-up you can use. Start dates, grace periods and term changes can affect the year’s figures.

Net cash includes salary, bonus, dividends and interest after the displayed tax, annualised NI and pension deductions. Benefits in kind affect tax and ANI. The estimate excludes other deductions or reliefs such as student loans, Gift Aid and pension annual-allowance charges. Actual NI is generally calculated by pay period. Scottish employment tax rates and devolved funded-hours schemes are outside this model.

Check future pay availability, the minimum-wage floor for salary sacrifice and your pension annual allowance with your employer. See HMRC’s ANI calculation if you have other income or reliefs.

Frequently asked questions

Does exactly £100,000 make me ineligible?

The income test is failed only above £100,000 of expected adjusted net income. The limit applies to each parent separately. Both must also meet the relevant eligibility rules.

What funded childcare can I lose in England?

In a full entitlement year, the working-parent offer is 30 hours a week for 38 weeks. Most children aged 9 months to 2 lose all 1,140 hours when the offer ends. Eligible two-year-olds can retain 15 hours through a separate scheme. Three- and four-year-olds generally retain the universal 15 hours, losing 570 hours annually.

Can I use Tax-Free Childcare with funded hours?

Yes. It helps pay eligible bills left after funded hours. The government pays £2 for every £8 you deposit, up to £500 per child per three months, or £1,000 for a disabled child. The annual maximum assumes enough eligible spending in every period.

Why can the marginal tax rate reach 62%?

Over the £100,000 to £125,140 taper, £2 of additional higher-rate employment income can cost £1.20 of Income Tax. Standard employee National Insurance can add 2%. Mixed income, different payroll circumstances and Scottish tax rates can change the result.

How much salary sacrifice is needed and when can I arrange it?

The gross amount is your expected adjusted net income above £100,000. An optional £1 buffer targets £99,999. Your employer must agree to sacrifice future pay. Check minimum wage, pension allowances and whether an alternative contribution method is available.
