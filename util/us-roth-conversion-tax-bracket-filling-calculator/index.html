How the estimate works

Use the additional-tax estimate to compare conversion amounts. The future comparison starts with the same pretax slice: the Roth case subtracts the conversion tax before modeling growth and assumes a qualified Roth withdrawal, while the traditional case applies your assumed rate at withdrawal. The projection assumes equal constant growth and excludes fees and reinvestment of any tax difference. The rate-schedule estimate may differ from the tax table used on your return.

  • IRS Revenue Procedure 2025-32, sections 4.01 and 4.14

Frequently asked questions

What is the 2026 basic standard deduction?

It is $16,100 for single filers, $32,200 for married couples filing jointly and $24,150 for heads of household. The before-deduction mode applies only the basic amount. Use taxable-income mode if your deductions are already included.

Does the entire Roth conversion get taxed at the target rate?

The tool applies each ordinary marginal rate only to the part of the conversion in that bracket. It subtracts the tax before conversion from the tax after conversion using the published rate schedules.

Does direct Roth IRA contribution eligibility limit conversions?

Income phase-outs apply to direct Roth IRA contributions. This estimate assumes your conversion amount is entirely pretax. If your IRAs contain after-tax basis, the taxable portion can depend on Form 8606.
