How tax drag is calculated

Australian income. The credit equals the cash distribution × franked share × 30 ÷ 70. The model taxes cash plus credit, subtracts the credit and reinvests cash after tax or with the eligible refund. For example, A$4,000 cash with 80% franked generates A$1,371 in credits. At a 15% rate, the modeled refund is A$566; at 47%, the modeled top-up tax is A$1,153. Actual eligibility depends on franking integrity rules. ATO franking refund guidance

International income. The model taxes the gross foreign distribution at the selected Australian rate. It credits eligible withholding up to that year’s modeled Australian tax on the foreign distribution. Withholding above the usable offset reduces reinvestment. This is a portfolio-only approximation of the non-refundable FITO. The actual limit depends on your tax position and the fund’s reported amounts. ATO foreign income tax offset guidance

Growth and sale. Each year, yield and price growth apply to the opening balance. Net distributions buy units at year end and add to the cost base. On sale, positive gains above that cost base are taxed at the effective sale rate for the profile. The final year’s reinvestment occurs at sale value and has zero price gain. The model assigns zero tax benefit to a capital loss.

Projection limits. Rates stay fixed at the selected marginal rate, including in the sale year. The estimate excludes fees, inflation, FX changes, Medicare threshold adjustments and surcharge, other tax offsets, fund-distributed gains, AMIT cost-base adjustments, super pension withdrawals and future rate or CGT changes. Actual results depend on fund performance and tax circumstances.

Loss aversion can distort the comparison

A refund or a smaller CGT bill can feel like a win even when the portfolio finishes with less money. Both portfolios can lose capital. Try lower and negative price growth to see how the final values change.

Frequently asked questions

When can a franked Australian ETF come out ahead?

At low tax rates, eligible unused franking credits can be refunded and reinvested. At higher rates, it takes more tax to top up the distribution and a lower-yield portfolio may keep more of its return compounding before sale. The result also depends on the assumed price growth, yield and holding period.

How much CGT does the model charge at the end?

The model applies the effective tax rate to a positive gain above the original investment plus reinvested distributions. Under the frozen 2026–27 settings, the effective rate is 23.5% at the 47% personal rate, 10% in accumulation super and 0% on fully exempt pension assets. Enacted CGT changes affect eligible personal gains accruing from 1 July 2027.

Does W-8BEN reduce withholding on VGS distributions?

VGS is Australian-domiciled. For Australian-domiciled international ETFs, the annual tax statement provides the foreign income and tax amounts attributed to you. W-8BEN is relevant to direct holdings of US-domiciled securities for eligible individuals. US withholding on dividends generally falls from 30% to the 15% treaty rate with valid documentation for eligible Australian residents.

Can a foreign income tax offset turn into a cash refund?

The Australian foreign income tax offset is non-refundable. The model caps it at the Australian tax on the foreign distribution and the amount of eligible foreign tax paid. This differs from eligible franking credits, which can generate a refund.

What changes if I reinvest distributions or sell at a loss?

Reinvested cash buys additional units and increases the cost base, which avoids taxing the same cash as a capital gain at sale. If sale proceeds fall below the cost base, the model charges zero CGT and assigns zero value to any potential capital-loss carryforward.
