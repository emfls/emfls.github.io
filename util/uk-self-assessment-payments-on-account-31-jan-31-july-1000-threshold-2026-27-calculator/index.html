Frequently asked questions

Do I owe payments on account if my eligible bill is exactly £1,000?

Yes, unless more than 80% of your assessed Income Tax and Class 4 NIC was covered at source. Exactly 80% does not qualify for that exemption. The £1,000 test uses the eligible bill after deductions at source and before subtracting earlier advances.

Why can my first January payment be 150%?

With no earlier advances, £6,000 in eligible tax produces a £6,000 balancing payment and a £3,000 first advance. That makes £9,000 due in January and £3,000 in July. Any unpaid Capital Gains Tax and student or postgraduate loan repayments add to the balancing bill.

When are the 2026/27 payments on account due?

The first is due 31 January 2027, alongside the 2025/26 balancing payment. The second is due 31 July 2027. Any 2026/27 balancing payment is due 31 January 2028.

What if I paid too much on account last year?

Earlier advances reduce the balancing payment. A credit can offset the first new advance in the January calculation. If there is still a credit, check your HMRC statement for its allocation or request a repayment if available.

Can I reduce payments on account if profits fall?

You can claim a reduction through HMRC online or with form SA303 if you reasonably expect a lower eligible tax bill. Estimate net Income Tax and Class 4 NIC for the full year. HMRC can charge late-payment interest from the original due dates if you reduce the advances too far.
