Key takeaways

  • File T1161 above $25,000 CAD in worldwide reportable property. Canadian real estate generally counts, even when the departure tax is $0.
  • Include 50% of the net deemed gain in taxable income. The calculator stacks that amount on your taxable income and applies the 2026 federal and provincial brackets.
  • Elect by April 30, 2027 to defer eligible tax for a 2026 departure. Federal security may be needed above $16,500, or $13,777.50 for Quebec.

Plan for the tax before you move

You can owe tax without selling an asset or receiving cash. Preserve records of your departure-date values and Canadian cost bases, and check the filing and deferral deadlines before deciding whether you need to sell or arrange collateral.

After departure: An actual sale of Canadian real estate can trigger tax and section 116 notice and clearance certificate procedures. Non-resident RRSP and RRIF withdrawals generally face 25% Part XIII withholding. Qualifying periodic pension or RRIF payments may qualify for a 15% treaty cap. Non-resident TFSA contributions generally incur a 1% monthly Canadian tax, and U.S. tax residents may owe U.S. tax on TFSA earnings.

How the estimate works

T1161 adds reportable FMV without subtracting cost basis or debt. T1243 uses FMV minus Canadian tax ACB for assets subject to deemed disposition. Ordinary capital losses in those assets offset gains in the estimate, with a floor of zero. For a personal-use item, the estimate applies the $1,000 minimum deemed cost and proceeds and gives no credit for personal-use losses.

This is a bracket estimate for individuals who cease Canadian tax residency in 2026. It excludes credits and deductions beyond those already reflected in your starting taxable income, Ontario surtax and health premium, minimum tax, loss carryovers, listed personal-property loss rules, foreign-currency cash gains, depreciation recapture, and treaty or foreign-tax adjustments. Check Canadian ACB rules, including the immigration-date FMV basis where applicable, before filing. If you hold foreign-currency cash, verify its separate reporting and gain treatment.

Frequently asked questions

When do I need to file Form T1161?

File when total worldwide reportable fair market value exceeds $25,000 CAD. Canadian real estate generally counts. Exclude Canadian-dollar cash and deposits, eligible registered plans including TFSAs, and personal-use items worth less than $10,000 each. File by your filing due date even if you otherwise have no return to file.

What assets go on Form T1243?

Report property subject to deemed disposition, which can include non-registered stocks and ETFs, private-company shares, crypto held as capital property, and foreign real estate. A net capital gain is generally included at 50%. A personal item below the T1161 reporting cutoff can still produce a capital gain.

How does the 60-month rule work?

An individual who was resident for 60 months or less in the 120 months before departure can generally exclude property owned when they last became resident or inherited afterward from deemed disposition. Such property can still count for T1161 if it is taxable Canadian property.

Can I defer departure tax and when is security needed?

File Form T1244 by April 30, 2027 for a 2026 departure. The election can defer eligible tax without interest until disposition. Federal security may be needed when federal departure tax exceeds $16,500, or $13,777.50 for former Quebec residents. Confirm any provincial security separately.

What happens to my RRSP, RRIF or TFSA after I leave?

These accounts are excluded from departure tax. The usual Part XIII withholding on non-resident RRSP or RRIF payments is 25%. An applicable treaty may cap qualifying periodic pension or RRIF payments at 15%. Non-resident TFSA contributions generally incur a 1% monthly Canadian tax while they remain in the account. U.S. tax residents may owe U.S. tax on TFSA earnings.
