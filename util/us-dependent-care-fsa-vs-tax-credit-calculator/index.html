How the estimate works

The 2026 dependent care exclusion is up to $7,500, generally $3,750 for a separate married return. Use the smaller amount allowed by your plan, qualified expenses and applicable earned-income limits, including your wages remaining after the election. This estimate uses fixed marginal federal and state rates and employee FICA of 6.2% Social Security up to $184,500 in wages plus 1.45% Medicare. Social Security savings apply only to the wages actually moved below that cap.

For 2026 the credit starts at 50% through $15,000 AGI. It falls one percentage point per $2,000 or part thereof to 35% immediately above $43,000. The 35% band ends above $75,000 for single or head of household and above $150,000 for joint returns. The next steps are $2,000 and $4,000 respectively, reaching 20% immediately above $103,000 or $206,000.

The excluded FSA amount reduces both qualified expenses and the $3,000 or $6,000 credit expense cap dollar for dollar. The model recalculates AGI and applies earned-income and available-tax limits before comparing elections in whole cents. For a $120,000 joint AGI, two qualifying people, $10,000 of care, wages fully below the Social Security cap and rates of 22% federal and 5% state, the full $7,500 FSA saves $2,598.75 versus a $2,100 credit alone, assuming sufficient earned income and tax liability.

This is a planning comparison for qualifying work-related care under the ordinary filing rules. Confirm plan election increments, provider eligibility and your actual Form 2441 limits. Special rules for married people living apart, deemed student or disabled spouse earnings, differing wage bases, Additional Medicare Tax, state credits and changes to other tax provisions are outside the model.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use the FSA and the credit in the same year?

Yes, when otherwise eligible. Excluded FSA benefits reduce the credit expense cap and available expenses dollar for dollar. For two qualifying people and enough expenses, a $4,000 exclusion can leave up to $2,000 for the credit.

Can I claim the credit on a separate married return?

Ordinarily a joint return is required for married people. A statutory living-apart exception can apply. The separate-return option models the ordinary rule and a $3,750 FSA ceiling.

Why can a split beat the full FSA election?

A partial exclusion may leave some expenses for the credit and move adjusted gross income into a higher credit-rate band. The calculator also shows the results of each endpoint.
