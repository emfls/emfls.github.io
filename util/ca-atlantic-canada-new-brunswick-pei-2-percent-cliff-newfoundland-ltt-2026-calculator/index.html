Atlantic Canada Land Transfer Tax Calculator 2026

Compare closing taxes and registration fees in New Brunswick, Prince Edward Island, Newfoundland and Labrador, and Halifax, Nova Scotia.

Key takeaways

  • New Brunswick and PEI generally charge 1% of the higher of the purchase price and assessed value. PEI waives the tax on transfers with a base of $30,000 or less and for eligible first-time buyers.
  • PEI’s proposed $1 million cliff would apply 2% to the full taxable value and remove the first-time buyer exemption at that threshold. Select the budget scenario to see its effect.
  • Newfoundland and Labrador charges deed and mortgage registration fees. The mortgage fee tops out at $5,000. Halifax charges a 1.5% municipal deed transfer tax on the sale price.

PEI $1 million cliff under the budget proposal

A move from $999,999 to $1,000,000 in taxable value would increase the tax by about $10,000 for a repeat buyer and $20,000 for an eligible first-time buyer. The proposed 2% applies to the entire taxable value.

The $999,500 negotiation target keeps the proposal’s taxable base below $1 million only when the assessed value is also below $1 million.

Frequently asked questions

Does PEI charge 2% at $1 million in 2026?

PEI's 2026 published rules specify a 1% rate. The 2025 budget proposed 2% of the full taxable value starting at $1 million. The calculator provides that proposal as a what-if scenario.

Who can use the PEI first-time buyer exemption?

All purchasers must qualify. PEI considers citizenship or permanent residence, prior homeownership and exemption history, and residence or tax filing history. A declaration and principal residence occupancy rules apply. Some buyers may qualify for a refund after 183 days of occupancy.

How does New Brunswick calculate transfer tax?

New Brunswick applies 1% to the higher of the price and assessed value. A standard single-parcel registry estimate adds $85 per instrument, and there is no general first-time buyer rebate.

Which Newfoundland and Labrador registration fee is capped?

The $5,000 maximum applies to the mortgage registration fee. The deed registration fee starts at $100 and adds $0.40 for each $100 or part above $500 of property value.

Is the Halifax 1.5% benchmark province-wide?

The 1.5% benchmark applies to Halifax Regional Municipality and uses the sale price. Other municipalities set their own rates. Nova Scotia also has a separate provincial non-resident deed transfer tax for eligible transactions.
