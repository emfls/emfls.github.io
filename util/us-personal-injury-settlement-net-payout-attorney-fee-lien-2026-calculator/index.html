Understanding Personal Injury Settlements & Tax Rules

Resolving a personal injury lawsuit involves navigating complex legal disbursement waterfalls and IRS statutory exclusions:

1. The IRS 26 U.S. Code § 104(a)(2) Tax Exclusion

  • Tax-Free Physical Injury Damages: Under federal law, gross income does NOT include amounts received on account of personal physical injuries or physical sickness. This applies to economic losses (past and future medical bills, lost wages) as well as non-economic general damages (pain, suffering, loss of consortium).
  • Taxable Exceptions: Any punitive damages awarded to punish the defendant, as well as pre-judgment or post-judgment interest, are 100% taxable as ordinary income.

2. The Disbursement Waterfall

When insurance settlement proceeds arrive in an attorney's trust (IOLTA) account, disbursements follow strict legal priority: attorney contingency fees (33.3% to 40%) and case expenses are satisfied first, followed by valid medical liens (health insurers, Medicare, Medicaid, and hospital liens), with the remaining balance wired directly to the client.
