Estimate the £90,000 VAT cliff, find the sales needed to recover lost profit, and compare annual cash under both VAT schemes.

Key takeaways

  • UK-established businesses generally must register when taxable turnover exceeds £90,000 over the past 12 months or is expected to exceed £90,000 in the next 30 days alone.
  • If you keep a standard-rated consumer price unchanged, one sixth of it becomes VAT. Eligible input VAT reduces your standard-scheme bill.
  • A limited cost business pays 16.5% of VAT-inclusive turnover under the Flat Rate Scheme, or 15.5% during the eligible first year of VAT registration.

Frequently asked questions

Does exactly £90,000 trigger compulsory VAT registration?

The turnover tests are triggered when taxable turnover exceeds £90,000. Check the past 12 months and the next 30 days alone separately. The two tests have different notification deadlines and effective registration dates.

Why can VAT registration reduce my profit?

When you hold a standard-rated consumer price at £120, £20 is output VAT after registration. Reclaimable input VAT can offset part of that cost. If you add VAT to a £120 business fee, the customer instead pays £144.

How is the recovery turnover calculated?

The calculator finds the sales at current prices where standard-scheme cash equals the unregistered £89,999 benchmark, using the same costs and input VAT. The planning target rounds up to a penny and starts at £90,000.01 if the benchmark is already met at the cliff.

When does the limited cost rate apply?

The 16.5% rate applies when relevant goods cost less than 2% of flat rate turnover including VAT or less than the period's share of £1,000 a year. Check the test for each return. The rate is 15.5% while the first-year registration discount applies.

Can I join the Flat Rate Scheme and reclaim input VAT?

The entry turnover limit is £150,000 excluding VAT and other eligibility conditions apply. Ordinary purchase VAT generally stays in your costs. Certain purchases of capital expenditure goods can qualify for separate VAT recovery.
