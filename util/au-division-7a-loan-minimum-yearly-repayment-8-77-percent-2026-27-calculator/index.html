Division 7A repayment FAQs

What is the Division 7A benchmark interest rate for 2026–27?

The ATO benchmark is 8.77% p.a. for the standard income year from 1 July 2026 to 30 June 2027. It is based on the RBA indicator lending rate published on 5 June 2026. Use the benchmark for each later income year when recalculating.

How is the minimum yearly repayment calculated, and when is it due?

Under section 109E(6), multiply the amount outstanding at the end of the previous income year by the current benchmark rate, then divide by one minus (one plus that rate) raised to the negative remaining term. Use whole years, rounding the statutory remaining term up where required. Minimum repayments begin in the income year after the loan was made and must be made by 30 June 2027 for the standard 2026–27 income year.

When can a Division 7A loan use the 25-year term?

A registered mortgage over real property must secure the whole loan. When the loan is made, the property’s market value less debts secured ahead of the Division 7A loan must be at least 110% of the loan amount. The maximum is seven years for other loans. A complying written agreement must be in place before the company’s lodgment day for the year the loan was made.

How does missing the 30 June repayment affect my tax?

A repayment shortfall can be treated as an assessable, generally unfranked dividend, subject to the company’s distributable surplus. The calculator models a single potential Division 7A dividend. If several deemed dividends share the cap, the surplus may need to be allocated across them. The estimated personal tax is up to 47% of the deemed amount at the listed rates. The loan debt still needs to be accounted for.

Can cash or a fully franked dividend cover the shortfall?

A qualifying cash repayment from existing funds creates no additional dividend tax. A lawful dividend may cover the shortfall if the set-off is properly documented and completed by year end. At a 25% imputation rate, the franking credit is the cash dividend multiplied by 25/75. Eligible shareholders include the grossed-up dividend in assessable income and apply the credit as a tax offset. Check that the company can pay and fully frank the dividend and that the shareholder qualifies for the credit.
