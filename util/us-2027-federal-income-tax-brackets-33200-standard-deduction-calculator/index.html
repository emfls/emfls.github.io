How to read your estimate

Start with gross ordinary income, subtract the pre-tax amounts and your selected deduction, then apply the rates to each slice of taxable income. The marginal rate is the rate on the next dollar of taxable income. The effective rate divides tax after credits by gross income.

This planning estimate uses the rate schedules. Filing software may use IRS tax tables and whole-dollar rounding, which can produce a different result. State tax, payroll and self-employment taxes, AMT, preferential capital gains rates, refundable credits and special deductions such as the enhanced senior deduction are outside this estimate. Enter only amounts you qualify to claim. Dependent standard deduction rules require a separate calculation.

2027 federal tax FAQ

What is the 2027 standard deduction?

The 2027 base standard deduction is $33,200 for married filing jointly, $24,925 for head of household, and $16,600 for single or married filing separately. For 2026, those amounts are $32,200, $24,150, and $16,100.

How much does age 65 or blindness add to the 2027 standard deduction?

Each qualifying age or blindness condition adds $2,100 for single or head-of-household filers and $1,700 for married filers. Two qualifying spouses age 65 or older add $3,400 on a joint return. The separate, temporary enhanced senior deduction has its own eligibility and phaseout rules.

Does moving into the 22% bracket raise the rate on all my income?

Only the part of taxable income above the 12% bracket's ceiling enters the 22% bracket. For a single filer in 2027, the 22% rate starts above $52,025 in taxable income. The lower portions still use the 10% and 12% rates.

Why can the 2026 and 2027 estimates use different deduction methods?

The larger-deduction option compares your eligible itemized amount with each year's standard deduction independently. A single filer with $16,300 in itemized deductions and no age or blindness adjustments would use itemized deductions in 2026 and the standard deduction in 2027.

How do tax credits affect the comparison?

The calculator subtracts the eligible nonrefundable credit amount you enter after computing regular income tax. It caps the credit used at each year's tax before credits. Both years use the same entered amount to isolate changes in the brackets and standard deduction.
