Key takeaways

  • With the standard deduction, qualifying direct cash gifts can add up to $1,000 to your deductions, or $2,000 on a joint return.
  • With Schedule A, only eligible gifts above 0.5% of AGI are deductible for the current year. At $180,000 of AGI, the floor is $900.
  • For income in the 37% bracket, Section 68 can limit the value of itemized deductions to 35 cents per dollar.

Frequently asked questions

Can I deduct donations if I take the standard deduction in 2026?

Yes. Eligible cash gifts can add up to $1,000 to the standard deduction, or $2,000 on a joint return. The deduction reduces taxable income. Gifts to donor-advised funds and supporting organizations, noncash gifts, and gifts to most private foundations do not qualify.

How does the 0.5% Schedule A floor work?

At $180,000 of AGI, the floor is $900. If $5,000 is otherwise eligible after the percentage limits, $4,100 remains as a Schedule A charitable deduction before the overall Section 68 limit. The floor applies once to combined eligible giving.

Can donations disallowed by the floor carry forward?

Generally, a floor amount is lost if there is no applicable carryforward. When the contribution category also generates an excess that carries forward under its percentage limit, the floor amount attributable to that category can join the carryforward.

How does the 35% benefit limit work for top-bracket filers?

Section 68 reduces total itemized deductions by 2/37 of the smaller of total itemized deductions or income above the start of the 37% bracket, measured before itemized deductions. A deduction dollar fully affected within the 37% bracket can save 35 cents in federal income tax.

When does three-year donation bunching save tax?

Combining three years of gifts can reduce how often the Schedule A floor applies. Compare your best deduction path in each year because bunching can also give up non-itemizer deductions in the two years without gifts or put more gifts above the annual percentage limits.
