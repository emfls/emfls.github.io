Low salary and recurring fees can erase savings

The IRS can reclassify distributions as wages when an owner’s salary is too low, which may lead to back employment taxes, penalties, and interest. Check comparable pay and keep records of the owner’s duties and hours before relying on a savings figure. The ratio measures pay as a share of profit. The IRS evaluates reasonable compensation using the facts of each business.

Frequently asked questions

When does an S corporation save money over a single-member LLC?

An S corporation saves money when lower employment taxes and any income-tax benefit exceed payroll and tax-preparation costs. The break-even result shows the first whole-dollar profit that covers those costs at the salary and other inputs you entered. Savings can change again as deductions and tax brackets change.

How much W-2 salary is reasonable for an S-corp owner?

Start with market pay for comparable duties, hours, experience, and location. Document compensation using those facts and the source of business receipts. The salary-to-profit ratio is a review prompt. The IRS bases reasonable compensation on the facts and circumstances of the work.

How do another job and the $184,500 Social Security wage base affect the comparison?

Your own wages reduce the Social Security wage base available for self-employment tax and the final employee share of S-corp Social Security tax. The S corporation still owes its employer share on wages it pays up to its own wage base. An employee with multiple employers generally claims excess employee withholding on the federal return.

How does an S-corp salary affect the Section 199A deduction?

Owner wages are excluded from QBI and may count toward the S corporation’s W-2 wage limit. Below the income threshold, paying wages can reduce the deduction by shrinking business QBI. Above the phase-in range, a non-SSTB can use eligible wages or qualified property, while an SSTB is excluded. Eligible active business income can qualify for the $400 minimum deduction.

What counts toward the 0.9% Additional Medicare Tax?

Medicare wages and taxable self-employment earnings count toward the filing-status threshold. Joint filers include both spouses’ Medicare wages. The comparison shows only the additional tax generated by this business. Schedule SE’s half-tax deduction covers regular self-employment tax.
