Charitable Remainder Trust Calculator 2026

Compare a charitable remainder unitrust (CRUT) with a charitable remainder annuity trust (CRAT) using the IRS §7520 rate.

Key takeaways

  • A fixed-term CRT must pay 5%–50% annually over 1–20 years and leave charity at least 10% of the contribution’s initial value in present-value terms.
  • A CRUT pays a percentage of assets revalued annually. A CRAT pays a fixed dollar amount based on the initial contribution.
  • A qualifying trust's sale can defer immediate federal capital gains tax and NIIT. The deduction covers only the charitable remainder, subject to §170 limits.

What you give up when you fund a CRT

The transfer is irrevocable. You give up direct access to the donated principal and charity receives the remaining assets. A weak market can shrink CRUT payouts or leave a CRAT unable to make its scheduled annuity payments. Beneficiary distributions can carry taxable income, capital gains and NIIT.

How the estimates work

Present value. With payout rate p, §7520 rate r and term n, the CRUT uses (1 − p / (1 + r))n for the assumed annual timing. The CRAT uses max(0, 1 − p × (1 − (1 + r)−n) / r), with an annuity factor of n at a hypothetical zero rate. Results use unrounded factors. Verify the actual timing and applicable tables before filing. CRUT valuation regulation · IRS actuarial tables

Deduction. Assumes an individual already itemizes, has no other gifts or carryovers, and the remainder goes outright to an eligible public charity. The upfront value is the remainder’s present value. The year-one deduction applies the 30% cap and then the 0.5% floor. Eligible excess may be carried forward for five years, subject to future limits. The tax savings estimate multiplies the year-one deduction by the entered federal tax benefit rate. Other gifts, deduction ordering, overall itemized deduction limits or different property and beneficiary arrangements can change the amount. Deduction limits by recipient

Sale and payouts. The sale estimate multiplies positive appreciation by the combined rates entered. It assumes the trust makes an independent sale after funding and qualifies for exemption. Confirm assignment-of-income and state treatment before funding. The estimate excludes debt, depreciation recapture, transaction costs, trust expenses and unrelated business taxable income. Frequently asked questions

What does the 10% remainder test measure?

It compares the present value of charity’s remainder at funding with the net value contributed. It uses the applicable §7520 assumptions. The final gift depends on actual investment performance and payouts.

Which §7520 months can I compare?

For an eligible charitable transfer, compare the transfer month and the two preceding calendar months. A higher rate raises the calculated remainder for both trusts under this annual timing. A prior-month election requires a statement with the tax return.

Can a CRT sale permanently eliminate capital gains tax?

A qualifying CRT can generally sell appreciated assets without immediate federal capital gains tax or NIIT at the trust level. Its carryover basis preserves the gain, and beneficiaries can owe capital gains tax and NIIT when distributions carry that income out.

How much of the deduction can I use in 2026?

For the modeled long-term gain property going outright to an eligible public charity, the starting limit is 30% of the contribution base. The 2026 itemized deduction floor is 0.5% of that base. Eligible excess can carry forward for up to five succeeding tax years.

Why does a fixed CRAT payout create exhaustion risk?

A fixed payment can deplete principal when returns are weak. For a fixed term, review the limited-fund rule in Treasury Regulation §1.7520-3. Rev. Rul. 77-374 addresses the separate probability that a life beneficiary will outlive a CRAT’s assets.
