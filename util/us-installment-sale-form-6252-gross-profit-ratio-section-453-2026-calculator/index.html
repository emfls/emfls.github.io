How the estimate works

Gain before recapture is selling price minus adjusted basis and selling expenses. Form 6252 adds ordinary recapture to adjusted basis and selling expenses for line 13. Contract price is selling price less the smaller of the assumed mortgage and that line 13 amount. Mortgage above line 13 is a deemed principal payment in 2026. The seller note is selling price less the full assumed mortgage and cash down payment.

The note uses equal annual principal payments with any cent remainder in the last payment, plus interest on the opening note balance. The first note payment occurs in 2027. Future years reuse 2026 federal rates, filing status and other income. Interest on the note is assumed to be net investment income. The gain setting applies to both ordinary recapture and capital gain.

Use for a qualifying long-term sale with a single gross profit ratio and adequate stated interest. The estimate uses statutory rate schedules and excludes state tax, AMT, credits, capital losses, other preferential income, §1231 lookback, §121 exclusions, imputed interest, §453A charges and changes to deductions. For a sale involving several assets, verify the allocation and any separate Form 6252 calculations.

Installment sale FAQ

What is the Form 6252 gross profit ratio?

Divide installment gross profit by contract price. When the price is $600,000, gain after ordinary recapture is $270,000, and contract price is $500,000, the ratio is 54%. A $50,000 principal payment then recognizes $27,000 of gain.

Is §1245 recapture taxable when I receive no cash?

Yes. Under IRC §453(i), the full ordinary depreciation recapture amount is reportable in the year of sale. Form 6252 includes the recapture in line 13 so that the same gain is not deferred and taxed again.

What happens when the buyer assumes a mortgage?

The assumed balance up to adjusted basis plus selling expenses and ordinary recapture reduces contract price. The excess is treated as a principal payment in the sale year, even though you receive no cash for it.

How does unrecaptured §1250 gain apply to installments?

Each installment’s recognized capital gain is treated as unrecaptured §1250 gain until that portion is exhausted. The federal rate is capped at 25%. After that portion is used, remaining eligible gain takes the regular long-term capital gains rates.

Can paying in installments increase total federal tax?

Yes. Spreading gain can preserve lower capital gains brackets or reduce the 3.8% net investment income tax. Note interest produces ordinary income and may also be subject to NIIT, so total federal tax can still rise.
