Canada GST/HST Quick Method vs Regular ITC Calculator

Compare 2026 remittances and after-tax profit, find your break-even expense ratio, and check the CRA $30,000 registration threshold.

Key takeaways

  • Quick Method leaves more after-tax cash when the GST/HST you retain exceeds the operating input tax credits (ITCs) you give up. Eligible capital ITCs apply under both methods.
  • For most businesses, taxable revenue above $30,000 triggers mandatory registration. The date you start charging GST/HST depends on how you crossed the threshold.
  • Quick Method has a separate $400,000 eligibility test based on tax-included worldwide sales. An eligible election can provide a credit of up to $300 per fiscal year.

How the after-tax estimate works

Regular remittance equals tax collected less operating and capital ITCs. Quick remittance applies the remittance rate to tax-included eligible sales, then subtracts the applicable 1% credit and capital ITCs.

Quick Method adds retained GST/HST and the credit to business income. Eligible unrecovered GST/HST on deductible operating costs or cost of goods sold reduces that income. The model assumes those costs are deductible this year and applies one marginal income tax rate to the net difference. Capital ITCs cancel in the comparison. Absolute profit figures apply the same flat rate to both scenarios and assume any loss is usable at that rate.

The break-even ratio divides Quick Method’s retained GST/HST, including the credit, by the effective ITC rate and then by pre-tax sales. The effective ITC rate is operating ITCs divided by the pre-tax costs tied to them. If there are no such costs, the calculator uses the selected sales tax rate for the projection. Above break-even, Regular Method wins if the expense mix and ITC rate stay the same.

Frequently asked questions

When do I have to register for GST/HST after crossing $30,000?

If one calendar quarter goes over $30,000, charge GST/HST on the transaction that crosses the limit and register within 29 days of your effective registration date. If only the rolling total goes over, small-supplier status ends at the end of the following month. Your effective date is no later than your first taxable supply after that, and you have 29 days from the effective date to register.

Who can elect to use the Quick Method?

The ordinary Quick Method is for eligible GST/HST registrants with a permanent establishment in Canada. The worldwide sales test, including GST/HST and associates’ taxable sales, is $400,000 or less in at least one of two overlapping four-fiscal-quarter windows. Eligible new registrants can use a reasonable first-full-year forecast. Excluded activities and other CRA election conditions still apply.

Which Quick Method rate applies to my business?

Your rate depends on the place of supply, the location of your permanent establishment and the rate category. The lower resale rates generally apply when qualifying tax-included resale purchases reach at least 40% of eligible tax-included revenue for the applicable prior period. Cross-region sales may need a different rate from this calculator’s same-rate examples.

How do the 1% credit and capital ITCs work?

The annual 1% credit applies to the first $30,000 of eligible tax-included sales, up to $300. The election must be in effect at the start of the fiscal year or on the registration date for a new registrant. Eligible capital property ITCs can still be claimed under either method.

How do income tax and operating expenses affect the winner?

Retained GST/HST and the 1% credit increase business income under Quick Method. Unrecovered tax on deductible operating costs can reduce it. In this model, Regular Method comes out ahead when the operating ITCs you give up exceed the retained GST/HST and credit. The marginal income tax rate changes the size of the after-tax difference.
