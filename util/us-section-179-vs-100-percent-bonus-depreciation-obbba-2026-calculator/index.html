2026 Section 179 vs. 100% Bonus Depreciation Under OBBBA and IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-32

Business owners purchasing commercial machinery, medical equipment, computer servers, software, qualified improvement property (QIP), or heavy business vehicles in 2026 operate under the most generous federal capital expensing framework in modern US tax history. Under the enacted One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA) and IRS Revenue Procedure 2025-32, taxpayers now combine two powerhouse accelerated write-off tools: dramatically expanded Section 179 expensing and permanently restored 100% Bonus Depreciation under IRC Section 168(k).

For tax years beginning in 2026, the IRS indexed the Section 179 maximum deduction limit to $2,560,000 and lifted the investment phase-out threshold to $4,090,000. Once a business places more than $4,090,000 of qualifying Section 179 property in service during 2026, the $2,560,000 deduction ceiling decreases dollar-for-dollar, reaching zero at exactly $6,650,000. Simultaneously, OBBBA permanently reinstated 100% Bonus Depreciation for qualifying property with a MACRS recovery period of 20 years or less—eliminating the TCJA scheduled phase-down to 40% and 20% and imposing zero annual dollar cap or purchase phase-out threshold.

Critical Differences Between Section 179 and 100% Bonus Depreciation

When both provisions permit a 100% Year-1 write-off, why do CPAs carefully choose between Section 179, 100% Bonus Depreciation, or a layered combination of the two? Five technical tax rules govern the optimal election:

  • Business Taxable Income Limitation & NOL Creation: Section 179 cannot reduce active business taxable income below zero (any disallowed excess carries forward indefinitely to future profitable years, though W-2 wages of a sole proprietor or S-Corp shareholder count toward the taxable income limit). By sharp contrast, 100% Bonus Depreciation has no taxable income limit—it can drive business taxable income negative, generating a Net Operating Loss (NOL) that offsets other ordinary income (subject to IRC §461(l) excess business loss caps) or carries forward to offset up to 80% of future taxable income.
  • Asset-by-Asset Flexibility vs. Class-Wide Election: Section 179 can be cherry-picked on an individual asset basis—and even applied to a partial dollar slice of a single machine—allowing a business owner to dial taxable income precisely down to the top of the 22% or 24% federal bracket without wasting deductions against lower 12% brackets. Bonus depreciation applies automatically to an entire MACRS asset class (for example, all 5-year property) unless the taxpayer files an affirmative election out for that entire class.
  • Qualified Real Property Advantage for Section 179: Section 179 covers four major non-residential building improvements that do not qualify for 100% Bonus Depreciation: roofs, HVAC heating/ventilation/air-conditioning systems, fire protection/alarm systems, and security systems placed in service on existing commercial real estate.
  • State Income Tax Conformity: Many high-tax states (including California, New York, New Jersey, Illinois, and Pennsylvania) disallow federal 100% Bonus Depreciation completely—forcing businesses to add back the entire bonus deduction on state returns—while partially or fully conforming to Section 179 (California caps Section 179 at $25,000, whereas several Midwest and Southern states match the federal Section 179 ceiling).
  • New and Used Property Eligibility: Both Section 179 and OBBBA 100% Bonus Depreciation apply to both brand-new and used equipment, provided the used asset is "new to you" (acquired in an arm's-length purchase from an unrelated party).

The 2026 Heavy SUV Rule ($32,000 Section 179 Cap + 100% Bonus Remainder)

Passenger automobiles weighing 6,000 pounds Gross Vehicle Weight Rating (GVWR) or less remain strictly capped under the IRC §280F luxury auto limits. However, heavy sport utility vehicles (SUVs) with a GVWR strictly above 6,000 pounds and up to 14,000 pounds (such as a Ford Expedition, Chevrolet Tahoe/Suburban, GMC Yukon, BMW X5/X7, Mercedes-Benz GLE/GLS, or Rivian R1S) follow a two-step statutory rule in 2026:

  1. Step 1 — Section 179 Cap ($32,000): Under IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-32, the maximum Section 179 deduction on a qualifying heavy SUV is capped at $32,000 for 2026 (up from $31,300 in 2025), provided qualified business use strictly exceeds 50%.
  2. Step 2 — 100% Bonus Depreciation on the Remaining Basis: Under IRS ordering rules, Section 179 is applied first, and OBBBA 100% Bonus Depreciation applies to 100% of the remaining depreciable business basis! On a $95,000 heavy SUV driven 100% for business, taking $32,000 via Section 179 plus $63,000 via 100% Bonus Depreciation unlocks the entire $95,000 write-off in Year 1—saving $33,250 in federal tax at a 35% marginal rate.

Keep in mind that pickup trucks with an open cargo bed of at least 6 feet in interior length, dedicated cargo vans with no seating behind the driver, and commercial vehicles exceeding 14,000 lbs GVWR are exempt from the $32,000 Heavy SUV cap and qualify for full Section 179 expensing directly.

Recapture Trap Warning: Maintaining >50% Business Use

Taking an immediate 100% deduction carries a strict multi-year compliance obligation: you must maintain contemporaneous mileage logs proving qualified business use stays strictly above 50% throughout the vehicle's entire 5-year MACRS recovery period. If business use drops to 50% or below in Year 2, Year 3, or Year 4, IRC §179(d)(10) and §280F(b)(2) trigger mandatory depreciation recapture—forcing you to add back the excess accelerated deduction over straight-line ADS depreciation directly into ordinary taxable income (and self-employment income for Schedule C owners) in the year business use falls below threshold. Always verify both your 2026 placed-in-service deadline (December 31, 2026—meaning delivered and ready for business use, not merely ordered or paid via deposit) and your state's bonus conformity before locking in year-end capital expenditures.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the 2026 Section 179 deduction limit and phase-out threshold?

Under IRS Revenue Procedure 2025-32 for tax year 2026, the maximum Section 179 deduction is $2,560,000, and the dollar-for-dollar investment phase-out begins when total qualifying property placed in service exceeds $4,090,000 (phasing out completely at $6,650,000).

Can I write off 100% of a Heavy SUV over 6,000 lbs GVWR in 2026?

Yes, if business use strictly exceeds 50%. Although Section 179 alone is capped at $32,000 for heavy SUVs in 2026, permanently restored OBBBA 100% Bonus Depreciation applies to the remaining business-use cost after the $32,000 Section 179 cap, enabling a full 100% Year-1 federal write-off.

When is 100% Bonus Depreciation better than Section 179?

100% Bonus Depreciation outperforms Section 179 when your business purchases exceed the $4,090,000 phase-out threshold, when you purchase a heavy SUV above $32,000, or when your deduction exceeds active business taxable income and you want to generate a Net Operating Loss (NOL) immediately rather than carrying Section 179 forward.

Does equipment have to be paid in cash before December 31 to qualify for 2026?

No. Financed or leased-to-own (capital lease) equipment qualifies in full even if you make zero down payment, provided the asset is physically delivered, installed, and 'placed in service' (ready and available for its assigned business function) on or before December 31, 2026.
