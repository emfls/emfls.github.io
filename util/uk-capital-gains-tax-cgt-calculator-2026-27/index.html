Frequently asked questions

Do shares, crypto and residential property use the same CGT rates?

Yes. For individuals in 2026/27, ordinary gains on all three use 18% up to the remaining basic-rate band and 24% above it. Use the chargeable property gain after any Private Residence Relief, and the allowable matched or pooled cost for shares or crypto.

When can I use brought-forward capital losses?

Deduct allowable current-year losses first, even when they push gains below the annual exemption. Use claimed losses from earlier years only to reduce what remains to the available exemption. Any unused losses can generally be carried forward.

How does BADR affect the rate on my other gains?

In 2026/27 eligible gains within the remaining £1 million BADR lifetime limit are taxed at 18%. The taxable relief gains use your remaining basic-rate band before ordinary gains, so some ordinary gains can still be taxed at 24%. Investors’ Relief has its own £1 million lifetime limit and the same rate and band ordering.

Can transferring an asset to my spouse or civil partner reduce CGT?

A qualifying no-gain/no-loss transfer before disposal can let each owner use their own annual exemption and unused basic-rate band. The recipient takes the tax basis carried over on the transfer, and their existing gains and income can affect household savings.

Does a SIPP contribution reduce the taxable capital gain?

An eligible relief-at-source contribution extends the basic-rate band by its gross amount. That can move some ordinary gains from 24% to 18% while leaving the gain itself unchanged. Enter the gross amount, including provider-added basic-rate relief, and check the pension tax relief rules.
