UK PENP, PILON & £30,000 Termination Tax Calculator

2026/27 tax year · 6 April 2026 to 5 April 2027

Key takeaways

Post-employment notice pay (PENP) and separately taxable payment in lieu of notice (PILON) are earnings subject to Income Tax and Class 1 National Insurance (NI). The first £30,000 of combined qualifying compensation, including statutory redundancy, can be tax-free. Any qualifying excess attracts Income Tax and employer Class 1A NI, with zero employee NI.

Check the split before you sign

If payroll treats £5,000 of an apparent £30,000 severance award as PENP, that £5,000 is taxable earnings. Ask for the employer’s BP, D, P and T inputs and the itemised tax and NI figures before budgeting around the offer.

Frequently asked questions

How are PENP and PILON different?

PENP is the statutory amount of a relevant termination award treated as earnings for unworked notice. A separately taxable PILON reduces the PENP calculation through T and remains taxable earnings in its own right.

When do I use months or 30.42 days for PENP?

For 12 equal monthly instalments and a monthly last pay period, use P = 1 and D in months when the whole-month conditions apply. In other cases with equal monthly pay and a monthly last pay period, use calendar days for D and 30.42 for P.

Does statutory redundancy use the £30,000 exemption?

Yes. Statutory redundancy is excluded from the PENP calculation and shares the £30,000 exemption with the qualifying severance left after PENP, as well as other qualifying awards that use the same exemption.

Who pays National Insurance on severance above £30,000?

The employer pays 15% Class 1A National Insurance on the taxable excess of the qualifying award. Employees pay Income Tax on that excess and zero employee National Insurance on it. PENP and taxable PILON are subject to ordinary Class 1 rules.

Why might payroll deposit a different amount?

This estimate uses the change in annual Income Tax after the settlement, including any Personal Allowance taper, plus estimated employee National Insurance. Payroll withholding uses your tax code and payment circumstances, so the bank deposit can differ.
