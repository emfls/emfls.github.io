2026 Canada Lifetime Capital Gains Exemption ($1,275,000) for QSBC Share Sales

For Canadian founders and owners of Canadian-Controlled Private Corporations (CCPCs), the Lifetime Capital Gains Exemption (LCGE) under section 110.6 of the Income Tax Act is the single largest personal tax shelter in Canada. Following the mid-2024 jump to $1,250,000, the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) resumed annual inflation indexing in 2026, raising the 2026 LCGE limit to C$1,275,000 per individual shareholder. At Canada's 50% capital gains inclusion rate and Ontario's 53.53% top marginal bracket, a single shareholder claiming the full C$1,275,000 exemption shields C$341,254 in regular personal income tax—and when multiplied across a properly structured family trust with a spouse and adult children, families can shelter millions of dollars on a qualifying business exit.

The Three Non-Negotiable QSBC Qualification Tests (Purification Before Closing)

The LCGE applies only to shares of a Qualified Small Business Corporation (QSBC)—never to an asset sale of equipment, goodwill, or client lists out of the corporation. Failing any single one of the three statutory tests below reduces your exemption to zero:

  1. 90% Active Business Asset Test (At the Moment of Sale): On the exact closing date, at least 90% of the fair market value (FMV) of all corporate assets must be used principally in an active business carried on primarily in Canada. Excess surplus cash, GICs, marketable securities, or passive rental real estate sitting inside the operating company routinely blow up the 90% test! Tax lawyers solve this pre-closing through a corporate "purification" (paying down debt, distributing tax-free Capital Dividend Account balances, or transferring passive investments to a sister holding company via a safe-income butterfly/reorganization).
  2. 50% Active Business Asset Test (24-Month Lookback): Throughout the entire 24-month period immediately preceding the sale, more than 50% of the corporation's FMV must have been attributable to active Canadian business assets. Unlike the 90% closing-day test, you cannot "purify" past historical breaches—if your company held 60% passive ETFs 14 months before closing, you must wait until a clean 24-month window exists.
  3. 24-Month Holding Period Test: During the 24 months immediately before closing, the shares must not have been owned by anyone other than you or a person/partnership related to you (with specific rollover exceptions when incorporating a sole proprietorship or issuing shares for active business assets).

The 2026 Alternative Minimum Tax (AMT) Cash-Flow Shock on LCGE Claims

Many Canadian entrepreneurs claiming the $1,275,000 LCGE are stunned when their accountant tells them they still owe tens of thousands of dollars to the CRA on April 30 under the overhauled Alternative Minimum Tax (AMT) rules. Under the modern AMT framework enacted in 2024 and indexed for 2026:

  • Higher Federal AMT Rate (20.5%) & 2026 Basic Exemption (C$181,440): The flat federal AMT rate sits at 20.5% (up from 15% historically), while the 2026 federal AMT basic exemption matches the fourth federal bracket threshold at C$181,440 (indexed up from C$177,882 in 2025).
  • Effective 30% AMT Inclusion of LCGE-Sheltered Gains: Under ITA paragraph 127.52(1)(h), regular capital gains enter AMT Adjusted Taxable Income at 100%, while the capital gains deduction is scaled by 7/5—leaving an effective 30% inclusion rate on LCGE-exempt gains inside the AMT calculation! On a C$1,275,000 LCGE claim, 30% equals C$382,500 added straight to your AMT base, which easily clears the C$181,440 exemption even if you earn zero other income.
  • 7-Year Carryforward Recovery Rule: Critically, AMT is a prepayment of tax, not always a permanent tax—provided you earn sufficient regular taxable income over the next 7 taxation years to recover the federal and provincial AMT carryforward credits. However, if you retire immediately after selling your business and draw minimal taxable income for seven years, unused AMT expires permanently! Spreading the capital gain over up to 5 years using a Capital Gains Reserve (Form T2017) when receiving vendor take-back (VTB) promissory notes can often reduce or eliminate AMT altogether.

Share Sale vs. Asset Sale: Bridging the Buyer–Seller Tax Gap

Sellers strongly prefer a share sale to capture the C$1,275,000 LCGE and 50% capital gains taxation, whereas buyers prefer an asset sale so they can step up the depreciable Capital Cost Allowance (CCA) tax basis of equipment and goodwill while leaving historical corporate legal liabilities behind. In an asset sale, the corporation first pays corporate tax on recaptured depreciation and capital gains (often 26.5%–50.17% on investment portions, partially refunded via RDTOH and Capital Dividend Account credits), and then the shareholder pays high non-eligible dividend tax (42%–49% top rates) to extract the cash personally. Quantifying the exact after-tax cash gap between a Share Sale and Asset Sale arms you with concrete math when negotiating a price gross-up if a buyer insists on purchasing assets instead of shares.

Worked 2026 Ontario Exit Example: C$1.5 Million QSBC Share Sale

Assume an Ontario founder sells 100% of their qualifying CCPC shares for C$1,500,000 with an Adjusted Cost Base of C$100,000 and C$25,000 in legal/advisory fees, realizing a net capital gain of C$1,375,000 alongside C$120,000 of regular salary in the same year. Without the LCGE, 50% of the gain (C$687,500) would be taxed at Ontario's 53.53% top rate—creating a C$368,019 regular tax bill. Claiming the indexed 2026 LCGE of C$1,275,000 reduces the taxable gain to just C$50,000 (50% of the C$100,000 excess), slashing regular capital gains tax to C$26,765—a massive C$341,254 regular tax saving!

However, the 2026 AMT rules immediately step in: adding 30% of the C$1,275,000 exempt gain (C$382,500) plus 100% of the C$100,000 non-exempt gain plus C$120,000 salary pushes AMT Adjusted Taxable Income to C$602,500. Above the C$181,440 basic AMT exemption, estimated federal and Ontario AMT totals roughly C$56,298 due next April—recoverable over the next 7 years if you maintain regular taxable income. Even after factoring in upfront AMT, the QSBC share sale delivers C$1,291,937 in net cash compared with roughly C$718,575 from a comparable corporate asset sale and dividend extraction—a staggering +C$573,362 after-tax exit advantage.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the Canada Lifetime Capital Gains Exemption (LCGE) limit in 2026?

For 2026, the CRA indexed Lifetime Capital Gains Exemption limit for Qualified Small Business Corporation (QSBC) shares and qualified farm/fishing property is C$1,275,000 per individual (up from C$1,250,000 in 2025).

Why do I still owe Alternative Minimum Tax (AMT) when claiming the LCGE in 2026?

Under the overhauled Canadian AMT rules, 30% of LCGE-exempt capital gains are included in AMT Adjusted Taxable Income, and balances above the 2026 federal AMT basic exemption of C$181,440 are taxed at 20.5% federal plus provincial AMT surtax. AMT paid can generally be recovered against regular tax over the following 7 years.

Can I claim the LCGE if my buyer insists on an asset sale instead of buying shares?

No. The Lifetime Capital Gains Exemption applies strictly to the sale of qualifying shares by an individual (or allocated from a family trust). If the corporation sells its underlying business assets, the LCGE is unavailable and you must negotiate a higher purchase price to offset corporate and personal dividend taxes.

What happens if my company holds too much cash or investments right before closing?

If passive assets (excess cash, GICs, ETFs, rental properties) exceed 10% of total company fair market value on the closing date, your shares fail the 90% Active Business Asset Test and lose the entire LCGE. Work with a Canadian tax lawyer months ahead of closing to purify passive assets out of the operating company.
