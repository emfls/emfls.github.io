UK Pension PCLS vs UFPLS Calculator 2026/27

Compare upfront 25% tax-free cash, phased flexi-access drawdown and UFPLS. See each strategy’s cash, tax, remaining pension and £10,000 MPAA trigger.

Key takeaways

  • Pension Commencement Lump Sum (PCLS) can provide up to 25% tax-free cash, subject to your remaining £268,275 Lump Sum Allowance (LSA).
  • PCLS alone leaves the standard £60,000 annual allowance available if it otherwise applies. A first UFPLS or taxable flexi-access drawdown payment triggers the £10,000 Money Purchase Annual Allowance (MPAA).
  • Phased drawdown can use annual basic-rate headroom while leaving part of the pension uncrystallised. The £50,270 threshold includes taxable income. The standard tax-free Personal Allowance is £12,570.

First UFPLS payment and emergency tax

Budget for the amount you may actually receive on payment day. A Month 1 tax code uses only one month’s allowances and bands. Taking extra cash to cover the withholding can deplete the pension sooner and may create additional Income Tax.

If you plan to keep contributing, a first UFPLS reduces the standard allowance available for future money purchase savings from £60,000 to £10,000 where both limits apply. Employer contributions count, and unused allowance cannot restore the £50,000 difference.

The estimate assumes one UFPLS payment in the first tax year under the standard 1257L Month 1 calculation. Your pension provider’s tax code and your full-year income determine the actual deduction and any refund. Check your pension tax refund route with HMRC.

How the projection works

  • Upfront PCLS. The whole pot moves into drawdown after the maximum available PCLS is paid. The model takes zero taxable drawdown throughout. If the LSA is exhausted, this scenario releases no cash.
  • Phased drawdown. The model starts with up to 25% of the annual target as PCLS. If the taxable portion would exceed basic-rate headroom, it can crystallise a larger slice to pay more PCLS and leave the unused taxable balance invested. The cash target can be missed when headroom, LSA or funds are limited.
  • UFPLS. Take one gross slice per tax year, limited by the remaining pot. Once the LSA is exhausted, the whole slice is taxable.

Withdrawals occur at the start of each year. Remaining money in both pension balances grows at the entered rate. Tax rules and outside income stay at entered 2026/27 levels throughout. Figures are nominal and exclude future contributions, inflation and returns on cash outside the pension.

This planning estimate assumes a standard, accessible defined contribution pension with sufficient lump sum and death benefit allowance. It excludes Scottish Income Tax, protected allowances, other reliefs, annual allowance tapering, small-pot rules and contribution tax charges.

Pension withdrawal FAQs

How much PCLS can I take tax-free in 2026/27?

With the standard allowance, use the lower of 25% of the amount you crystallise and the £268,275 Lump Sum Allowance left after earlier use. Your pension provider can confirm prior use, protection and any transitional adjustment.

Does taking 25% tax-free cash trigger the £10,000 MPAA?

A PCLS with no taxable income taken from flexi-access drawdown leaves the MPAA untriggered. A first taxable flexi-access drawdown payment or a first UFPLS triggers it. Once triggered, the £10,000 limit applies to qualifying money purchase inputs and unused annual allowance cannot be carried forward to increase it.

How does phased drawdown use the £50,270 threshold?

When the full Personal Allowance applies, subtract other annual income from £50,270 to estimate the remaining room for pension income before the higher rate. PCLS can supplement that income and the part of the pot still uncrystallised can support future PCLS, subject to the remaining allowance.

What happens to UFPLS when I use up my Lump Sum Allowance?

Up to 25% of each UFPLS is tax-free while there is sufficient allowance. If the remaining allowance is smaller, only that remaining amount is tax-free. After exhaustion, the entire UFPLS is included in taxable income.

Which form applies to emergency tax on a UFPLS payment?

P55 may apply to a partial withdrawal if you are eligible for an in-year claim. If you emptied the pot, P53Z generally applies when you have other employment or private pension income and P50Z may apply if you have stopped working and have no other income apart from possible State Pension. HMRC’s checker confirms the route.
