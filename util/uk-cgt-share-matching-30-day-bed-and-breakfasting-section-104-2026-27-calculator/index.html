Key takeaways

HMRC matches same-day purchases first, then qualifying purchases in the next 30 calendar days, then the Section 104 average-cost pool. This statutory order overrides broker FIFO and LIFO.

For 2026/27, individuals generally have a £3,000 annual exemption. Chargeable gains use the remaining basic-rate band at 18%, then 24%. An eligible ISA repurchase leaves the original taxable sale subject to CGT and stays outside its share matching.

Frequently asked questions

Which shares does HMRC match first?

Match taxable purchases of the same share class in the same capacity on the sale date first, then qualifying purchases on days 1 to 30, then the Section 104 pool. If shares are still unmatched, later acquisitions may be needed under the statutory fallback.

Does day 30 count if it falls in the next tax year?

Yes. Day 30 is included, even across 5 April. The disposal still belongs to the tax year of the original sale. The 30-day rule generally requires UK tax residence at the time of acquisition.

How does the Section 104 pool change after a sale?

Remove the shares matched to the pool and their proportional share of the pooled cost. Add any unmatched taxable purchases entered for the period at their acquisition cost. Shares already matched to the sale do not enter the pool.

Does Bed and ISA eliminate CGT on the original sale?

The sale outside the ISA still realises a gain or loss. A qualifying purchase inside the ISA is outside the seller’s taxable matching calculation. The available annual exemption may shelter part or all of the gain, and the repurchase needs ISA eligibility and subscription room.

How does Bed and Spouse differ from a direct transfer?

For a market sale followed by an independent market purchase by a spouse or civil partner as beneficial owner, the spouse’s purchase is separate from the seller’s matching. A qualifying direct transfer generally takes place on a no-gain/no-loss basis and passes the existing tax basis to the recipient.
