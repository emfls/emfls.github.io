Key takeaways

  • A valid capital dividend within the available CDA generally carries 0% personal income tax for Canadian-resident shareholders.
  • An excessive election can cost the corporation $600 for every $1,000 over the CDA balance, plus interest.
  • Use the CDA immediately before the dividend becomes payable. Capital losses realized before that time can reduce the room available for the election.

Form T2054 filing checklist

  1. Reconcile the balance. Prepare the CDA computation immediately before the dividend becomes payable. Use Schedule 89 (T2SCH89) for the calculation or to request verification. For certified-software filing, submit Schedule 89 first if you have never filed either form or disagree with the CRA’s stated balance.
  2. Document the election. Have the directors authorize the election and document the full dividend and payable date. File a certified copy of the resolution or equivalent authorization and the CDA computation with the election.
  3. File on time. Submit Form T2054 by the earlier of the day the dividend becomes payable and the first day any part is paid. Elect on the entire dividend. CRA filing instructions

Frequently asked questions

What is a capital dividend account and who can use it?

The CDA tracks certain tax-free amounts of a private corporation, including a CCPC. A valid subsection 83(2) election generally lets Canadian-resident shareholders receive a capital dividend without personal income tax. Non-resident shareholders are generally subject to withholding, which a treaty may reduce.

Can a capital loss reduce my T2054 election room?

Yes. A qualifying loss realized before the dividend becomes payable reduces the cumulative capital gains component. The component bottoms out at zero, so a deficit does not offset separate insurance or dividend credits. Model the new loss separately and confirm the legal payable date before filing.

How does corporate-owned life insurance add to the CDA?

When a corporation receives qualifying proceeds because of an insured person’s death, the usual credit is the positive difference between the proceeds and the policyholder’s adjusted cost basis immediately before death. Confirm that basis with the insurer and check for any special policy adjustments.

What happens if the T2054 election exceeds the CDA balance?

The corporation generally owes Part III tax equal to 60% of the excess, plus interest. If eligible, it can use Form T2184 under subsection 184(3) to treat the excess as a separate taxable dividend. The statutory deadline is generally 90 days after CRA sends the Part III assessment. Agreement from all affected shareholders is generally required.

How does the calculator estimate personal tax savings?

It applies the applicable eligible and non-eligible marginal dividend rates to the same cash amount covered by the CDA, using either the planned election up to the safe maximum or the full safe maximum. Use separate custom rates if your shareholder’s marginal rates differ from the provincial top rates.
