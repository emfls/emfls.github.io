FAQs

How much employer NI do I pay on a £45,000 salary in 2026/27?

Standard employer NI is £6,000 before reliefs and Employment Allowance: 15% of the £40,000 above the £5,000 annual Secondary Threshold. In 2024/25 it was £4,954.20 on the same salary, using 13.8% above £9,100.

When does the 0% employer NI band apply?

Eligible under-21 employees, apprentices under 25 and qualifying veterans in their first 12 months of civilian employment have a £50,270 annual upper secondary threshold. Employer NI starts at 15% above that threshold. Use the relief only for the qualifying period.

Can a single-director company claim Employment Allowance?

A single-director company cannot claim when the director is its only employee liable for secondary Class 1 NI. Eligible employers can offset up to £10,500 against their total eligible employer NI. The previous £100,000 liability cap was removed from April 2025.

When is the minimum workplace pension due?

Automatic enrolment generally applies from age 22 to below State Pension age when earnings exceed £10,000 a year. On a qualifying-earnings basis, the employer minimum is 3% of earnings between £6,240 and £50,270. Some staff outside the automatic enrolment criteria can opt in and receive employer contributions.

How do salary sacrifice and the Apprenticeship Levy affect hiring costs?

Pension salary sacrifice can reduce NI on pay above the applicable threshold, at 15p per £1 of pay removed from that band before Employment Allowance. It can also reduce the levy pay bill. With the full £15,000 levy allowance, the employer pays 0.5% on pay above £3 million. Connected employers share the allowance.
