Nova Scotia Deed Transfer Tax Calculator 2026

Estimate Halifax’s 1.5% municipal tax and the 10% provincial tax on non-resident ownership. All amounts are in Canadian dollars.

Key takeaways

In Halifax, a $600,000 purchase carries $9,000 in municipal deed transfer tax. If the assessed value is $650,000 and a non-resident buys a 50% share, provincial PDTT adds $32,500 before exemptions. Budget $41,500 for both taxes if paying upfront. If that buyer qualifies for a relocation refund, the $32,500 provincial portion could come back.

Use the sale price for municipal tax. For provincial PDTT, multiply the higher of the purchase price or assessed value by 10% and by the taxable non-resident share.

Keep the PDTT in your closing budget

If you pay at closing, allow for the full upfront amount while your refund application is pending. If you claim the exemption at transfer, each qualifying buyer must file an affidavit of intent and prove residency on time. Missing the proof deadline can trigger tax, interest and a possible penalty.

For transfers on or after August 6, 2026, the proof deadline for an exemption claimed at transfer is one year. Earlier transfers normally had six months for proof. For the current paid-tax refund route, establish residency within one year and submit the refund application with proof within two years of acquisition.

Frequently asked questions

How do Halifax deed transfer tax and provincial PDTT use different values?

Halifax charges 1.5% of the sale price. The provincial tax uses whichever is higher: the purchase price or the assessed value. Where HST applies, enter the purchase price before HST.

Which properties are subject to the 10% Nova Scotia PDTT?

PDTT can apply to qualifying residential property with one to three units, including residential vacant land. This estimate assumes the whole parcel has the selected property classification. Mixed-use parcels can require an allocation.

What happens if a Nova Scotia resident and a non-resident buy together?

PDTT applies to the interest acquired by the non-resident buyer. On a $650,000 provincial tax base, a 50% non-resident share produces $32,500 of PDTT before exemptions.

How do I claim the Nova Scotia relocation exemption or refund?

For transfers on or after August 6, 2026, an individual claiming the exemption at transfer must file an affidavit of intent and provide proof of residency within one year. If the individual paid PDTT and became resident within one year, they can apply with proof within two years of acquisition. Only the qualifying buyer’s provincial tax is potentially refundable.

When did PDTT rise from 5% to 10%?

The 10% rate applies to purchase agreements signed on or after April 1, 2025, and to transfers without an agreement on or after that date. The calculator’s 5% comparison holds the taxable share and base constant. Earlier transactions can have different ownership rules.
