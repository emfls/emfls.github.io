Key takeaways

Alberta has no provincial land transfer tax. On a $750,000 purchase with a $600,000 registered mortgage, Alberta charges $800 for the title and $650 for the mortgage, for a total of $1,450. Before buyer relief, that is $11,550 less than BC’s transfer tax, $10,025 less than Ontario’s tax outside Toronto, and $21,500 less than the combined tax in Toronto.

Frequently asked questions

Does Alberta charge land transfer tax in 2026?

Alberta has no provincial land transfer tax. It charges a Land Titles fee to register the transfer and a separate fee to register a mortgage. A cash buyer pays the transfer registration fee only.

How does Alberta round the $5,000 increments?

Round each amount up separately to the next $5,000 increment. A $500,000 land value costs $550 to register. A $500,000.01 land value costs $555. The same rule applies to the principal secured by a new mortgage.

Does a first-time buyer pay zero BC tax on an $835,000 home?

The exemption covers the tax on the first $500,000, up to $8,000. An eligible buyer of an $835,000 home still pays $6,700 in standard BC tax. The exemption then phases out and reaches zero at $860,000.

Why does the Toronto registration period matter?

Toronto adds its municipal tax to the Ontario provincial tax. On April 1, 2026, the city raised its marginal rates above $3 million. An eligible first-time buyer can offset up to $4,475 of the city tax and up to $4,000 of the provincial tax.

Can Alberta registration fees exceed another market’s net tax?

Yes. For example, an eligible BC first-time buyer can owe zero transfer tax on a $400,000 home. On the same price, an Alberta cash buyer pays $450 to register the transfer, so Alberta costs $450 more for the charges compared here.
