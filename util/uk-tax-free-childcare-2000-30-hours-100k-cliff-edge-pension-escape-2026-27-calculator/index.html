Key takeaways

Each parent can have up to £100,000 in adjusted net income (ANI). If either parent exceeds it, the household fails the income test for Tax-Free Childcare and England’s working-parent hours.

At £7.50 per hour, 1,140 funded hours can offset up to £8,550 per child. Tax-Free Childcare can then cover 20% of eligible remaining bills, subject to the per-child cap.

A pay rise can leave you with less cash

Two under-3 places billed at £16,000 each before funding can mean around £20,080 a year in lost support above the threshold. In the Personal Allowance taper, the next £1 of employment income can also cost 60p in Income Tax and 2p in employee National Insurance. Actual losses depend on your bills, term dates and any grace period.

Frequently asked questions

Does £100,000 exactly pass the income test?

Yes. Each parent must have expected adjusted net income of £100,000 or less. Adjusted net income can include taxable income beyond salary. £99,999 is the calculator’s planning target.

When do the 30 funded hours start and what happens if income rises?

In England they can start from the term after the child turns nine months and continue until school starts. An existing funded place can enter a grace period after eligibility ends. Eligible 3- and 4-year-olds can still use the universal 15 hours.

Can I use Tax-Free Childcare with funded hours?

Yes. Use the account for eligible charges still due after funded hours. The government pays £2 for each £8 you deposit, up to £500 per child every three months or £1,000 for an eligible disabled child.

How do salary sacrifice, a SIPP and Gift Aid differ?

Pension salary sacrifice reduces contractual cash pay and can reduce employee National Insurance. A relief-at-source SIPP and qualifying Gift Aid reduce adjusted net income by the grossed-up payment and leave National Insurance unchanged. Gift Aid goes to charity. Higher or additional rate pension relief may need to be claimed from HMRC.

Why can income tax reach 60% on the next pound?

Between £100,000 and £125,140 of adjusted net income, every £2 above £100,000 removes £1 of Personal Allowance. On English employment income in the higher-rate band, that can add 20 percentage points to the 40% tax rate. Employee National Insurance and lost childcare support are separate effects.
