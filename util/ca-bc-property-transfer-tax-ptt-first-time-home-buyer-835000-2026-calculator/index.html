BC Property Transfer Tax Calculator 2026

Estimate the tax due at closing on a British Columbia home, including buyer exemptions, joint ownership and additional foreign buyer tax.

Key takeaways

  • $6,700 due at $835,000 for a fully eligible first-time buyer. The exemption saves up to $8,000 and covers all standard PTT on eligible homes worth $500,000 or less.
  • $0 due at $1.1 million for a fully eligible newly built home purchase. The maximum saving is $20,000 before the exemption phases out.
  • 20% additional tax can apply to the taxable foreign ownership share of residential property in designated B.C. areas.

Check the thresholds before raising your offer

For a fully eligible first-time buyer, going from $835,000 to $860,000 raises estimated PTT from $6,700 to $15,200. For a fully eligible new-build purchase, going from $1.1 million to $1.125 million raises it from $0 to $10,250. Both exemptions taper across their phase-out ranges.

The estimate assumes a transfer of the entire property and wholly residential use. Exemption estimates assume a qualifying principal residence on up to 0.5 hectares. Larger lots, mixed-use property and special exemptions or refunds can require a different calculation. This estimate covers provincial PTT and excludes GST and other closing costs.

Frequently asked questions

How much tax does an eligible first-time buyer pay at $835,000?

At $835,000, the standard tax is $14,700. A fully eligible purchase saves $8,000 and owes $6,700. The exemption covers the tax on the first $500,000 of value and phases out above $835,000, reaching zero at $860,000.

How much does the newly built home exemption save?

A fully eligible $1.1 million purchase saves $20,000 and owes $0 in standard PTT. At $1,125,000 the exemption covers half of the $20,500 standard tax. It reaches zero at $1,150,000.

What if only one buyer qualifies?

Apply the exemption to the qualifying ownership share. If one buyer qualifies and owns 50% of an $835,000 home, the first-time buyer exemption saves $4,000, leaving $10,700 in standard PTT after the exemption.

How is the additional 20% foreign buyer tax calculated?

When the tax applies, multiply the residential fair market value by the taxable foreign ownership share and then by 20%. A taxable 25% share of a $1 million home adds $50,000 to the closing tax.

What eligibility conditions should buyers check before closing?

Both programs generally require Canadian citizenship or permanent residency and use as a principal residence. The first-time program also has B.C. residence or tax-filing requirements and restrictions on prior principal-residence ownership and prior program use. The new-home program requires a qualifying newly built home. Plan to move in within 92 days and meet the first-year occupancy requirements.
