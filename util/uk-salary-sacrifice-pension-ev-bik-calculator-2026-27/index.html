Frequently asked questions

How is the annual tax on a 4% EV benefit calculated?

Multiply the car's P11D list price by 4% to get the taxable benefit. A £45,000 car adds £1,800 to taxable income in 2026/27. The tax is the increase in Income Tax after adding that benefit to post-sacrifice cash pay, including any Personal Allowance lost.

Can pension and EV sacrifice take adjusted net income below £100,000?

Yes, if the salary given up is large enough to offset income above £100,000 and the taxable car benefit. For example, £110,000 of salary less £13,800 of combined sacrifice plus £1,800 of EV benefit gives £98,000 of adjusted net income when salary and the EV are your only taxable income.

How do National Insurance and student loans affect EV cost?

Employee Class 1 NI and payroll student loan deductions are calculated on reduced cash pay in this model. The EV benefit increases Income Tax. Reduced student loan deductions improve current take-home pay and can leave more of the loan to repay later.

How should I compare a salary sacrifice EV with private contract hire?

Use the drop in take-home pay between pension-only and pension-plus-EV payroll. Compare that amount with the effective monthly private contract hire cost after spreading the initial rental and fees over the agreement. Match the mileage and included services.

What happens if sacrifice reduces my cash pay below minimum wage?

Your employer must cap salary sacrifice to preserve minimum wage. Check the hourly band that applies to you and give payroll the hours and deductions for each pay reference period. The company car and pension contributions sit outside the cash-pay floor in this estimate.

Calculation assumptions

The model uses full-year employment and EV availability, standard NI, and annualised pay and student loan thresholds before payroll-period rounding. It assumes employment income plus this EV benefit only, and a registered workplace pension. Other income, tax-code adjustments and pension annual allowance charges can change your result.

The 2027/28 projection changes the BiK rate alone and holds pay, lease and 2026/27 payroll assumptions constant. Employer NI sharing is modelled as an extra pension contribution based on gross employer Class 1 savings. Class 1A on the car benefit is shown separately.
