Australia Business Vehicle Finance: Chattel Mortgage vs Finance Lease (2026–27 ATO Guide)

Choosing between a Chattel Mortgage and a Finance Lease is one of the most critical tax and working capital decisions for Australian small businesses, sole traders, and corporate fleets. While both facilities allow you to acquire essential commercial vehicles, machinery, and equipment, their statutory treatments under Australian Goods and Services Tax (GST) and income tax laws differ drastically.

1. Ownership Structure and Statutory Day-One Title

Under a Chattel Mortgage, the legal title of the asset transfers to your business immediately upon settlement. The financier registers a specific security interest over the equipment on the Personal Property Securities Register (PPSR). Because you hold legal ownership, your business can claim the entire upfront GST credit on your next Business Activity Statement (BAS).

Conversely, in a Finance Lease, the financier purchases the vehicle and retains legal ownership throughout the agreement term. Your business leases the asset from the financier in exchange for regular monthly rental payments. At the conclusion of the lease term, you may pay the residual payout figure to purchase the asset, refinance the residual, or return the vehicle.

2. The 2026–27 ATO Car Cost Limit ($69,883 Threshold)

The Australian Taxation Office (ATO) updates the luxury car tax and depreciation limits annually. For the 2026–27 financial year, the relevant statutory thresholds apply as follows:

  • Car Cost Limit: Capped at $69,883 for passenger vehicles designed primarily to carry fewer than 9 passengers and with a payload under 1,000 kg.
  • Maximum Upfront GST Claimable: $6,353 (calculated as 1/11th of $69,883). Even if a passenger SUV costs $120,000, your business cannot claim more than $6,353 in input tax credits on your BAS.
  • Commercial Vehicle Exemption: Commercial utes with a payload capacity exceeding 1,000 kg (such as Ford Ranger, Toyota Hilux dual-cabs rated over 1 tonne), delivery vans, and heavy earthmoving plant equipment are 100% exempt from the car cost limit, allowing full uncapped GST and depreciation deductions.

3. Cashflow Timing: Upfront GST Lump Sum vs Progressive Rental Deductions

The decisive economic factor often hinges on short-term working capital needs:

  • Chattel Mortgage: Maximizes immediate cash injection. If purchasing a $110,000 commercial truck, your business receives a $10,000 GST refund from the ATO on the next quarterly BAS return, which can be reinvested into working capital or paid toward the loan balance.
  • Finance Lease: Eliminates upfront GST financing. You do not pay GST on the purchase price upfront; instead, GST is applied to each monthly repayment and recovered progressively on each BAS cycle.
