How the room is calculated

Start with the lower of $75,000 and 100% of eligible compensation. Subtract your regular deferral, employer match and other annual additions. The calculator also limits the result to your plan’s after-tax cap and gross pay left after regular and catch-up deferrals. Actual take-home pay may limit what you can contribute.

The annual match estimate applies the match rate to the smaller of your regular deferral and the matchable percentage of capped pay. If your employer matches catch-up contributions, enter its confirmed annual match. Include annual additions from plans of the same employer and related employers that share a Section 415(c) limit.

Mega Backdoor Roth 401(k) FAQ

How much after-tax room is left in 2027 after maxing my regular 401(k) deferral?

If your eligible compensation is at least $75,000, a $25,500 regular deferral leaves $49,500 before employer additions and plan limits. A $6,000 employer match reduces that to $43,500. Below $75,000 of eligible compensation, the 100%-of-compensation limit also applies.

Does my age-based catch-up reduce my Section 415(c) room?

Eligible catch-up contributions are excluded from annual additions. For 2027 the dollar ceiling including catch-up is $83,500 at ages 50–59 and 64 or older, or $86,750 at ages 60–63. Use your age at year-end and confirm that your plan treats the amount as catch-up.

How does the $375,000 compensation limit affect my employer match?

In the annual formula, eligible pay for the 2027 match is capped at $375,000. A 50% match on the first 6% of that pay is at most $11,250, assuming you defer enough. For tiered formulas or a payroll match without a year-end true-up, enter the annual match confirmed by your plan.

What features does my plan need for a Mega Backdoor Roth?

Your plan must accept voluntary after-tax employee contributions and offer a way to move those dollars to Roth, such as an in-plan Roth conversion or an eligible in-service distribution to a Roth IRA. Ask about contribution caps, nondiscrimination testing and conversion frequency before setting your payroll rate.

What happens to earnings before I convert my after-tax contributions?

After-tax contributions already have tax basis. Earnings that build up before conversion are generally pre-tax and taxable if moved to Roth. On an eligible rollover, your plan may allow the pre-tax portion to go to a traditional IRA or other eligible pre-tax plan while the after-tax portion goes to Roth.
