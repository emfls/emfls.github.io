Plan the gap before State Pension, compare two ways to take tax-free cash, and see what an early market loss does to your SIPP. Uses 2026/27 tax rules.

Key takeaways

  • A full new State Pension adds £12,547.60 a year per eligible person and can reduce the amount you need from your SIPP.
  • A temporary 5.5%–7.0% bridge withdrawal can be compatible with 30+ years of spending in some projections. The default example draws about 6% in its first year and meets its spending target for more than 30 years in the baseline.
  • Phased UFPLS spreads tax-free cash over time. Upfront pension commencement lump sum (PCLS) provides a cash reserve for early spending. Compare both the tax and the assets left.

Sequence risk and loss aversion

Selling investments after an early market fall leaves fewer assets to participate in a recovery. The stress test replaces the first year with a 25% loss and adjusts years two to five to match the baseline's five-year compound return before withdrawals.

A sharp drop can make selling everything feel safer. Compare the first spending shortfall under both paths and consider how much planned spending could flex. A 4% benchmark, a successful baseline, and a single crash scenario each leave substantial uncertainty.

Frequently asked questions

Can I withdraw more than 4% while bridging to State Pension?

A temporary withdrawal above 4% may work when future State Pension income substantially reduces the amount needed from investments. Check whether planned spending remains funded after a poor first year and over your full planning horizon.

How much of my phased UFPLS can be tax-free?

Up to 25% of each withdrawal can be tax-free while the applicable allowances last. With no other taxable income and the full Personal Allowance, £16,760 of UFPLS generates £12,570 of taxable income before State Pension starts.

How does State Pension affect tax on SIPP withdrawals?

State Pension counts as taxable income. For someone with a full £12,547.60 State Pension and no other income, £22.40 of the standard Personal Allowance remains before taxable SIPP income is added.

How does upfront tax-free cash change the results?

The calculator takes up to 25% of the initial pot, capped at £268,275, and spends it before taking taxable SIPP income. Taking cash upfront changes both the timing of income tax and how much stays invested.

Can I start SIPP drawdown at 55 or 56?

Most savers can access private pensions from age 55 before 6 April 2028. From that date the normal minimum pension age rises to 57, subject to protections or exceptions. Confirm access with your scheme.
