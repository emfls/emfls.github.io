Australia Debt Recycling Calculator 2026–27

Compare recycling your owner-occupied mortgage into investment debt, keeping cash in a 100% offset, and investing cash directly in ETFs.

Key takeaways

At 6.00% and a 47% marginal rate, A$100,000 of fully deductible investment debt can reduce annual tax by A$2,820, bringing its effective interest cost to 3.18%. A 100% offset at the same rate saves 6.00% on the cash offsetting your debt, equivalent to an 11.32% taxable yield at that marginal rate before fees.

Redraw is a new borrowing. The ATO looks at how the borrowed funds are used. This calculator assumes a clean investment split with a traceable transfer directly to income-producing ETFs. Private use of borrowed funds, including commingling them with household cash in an offset, can complicate tracing and require apportionment.

An ETF loss can outweigh the tax saving

A 20% drop on A$100,000 invested is a A$20,000 paper loss. In the example above, the estimated tax saving is A$2,820 and the loan principal is still owed. Try negative growth and a higher mortgage rate to see how the comparison changes. If your home secures the loan, falling behind on repayments could put it at risk. Moneysmart on borrowing to invest.

How the three strategies are compared

  1. Debt recycling. Use the lump sum and each year's new savings to repay private principal and redraw the same amount into an investment split. Deductible interest is estimated on that year's opening investment balance after contributions. At year-end, use after-tax distributions and estimated interest tax savings to recycle more private debt. Invest any excess cash directly once the private split is cleared.
  2. 100% offset. Deposit the same lump sum and yearly savings. Interest is charged only on the loan balance above the cash held in the offset. Keep the same scheduled payment so lower interest sends more of that payment to principal. Offset cash remains available and earns no modeled interest once it covers the debt.
  3. Direct ETF investing. Invest the same lump sum and yearly savings with the same ETF yield, franking and price growth. Reinvest after-tax distributions and continue paying the private mortgage without an investment-interest deduction.

Each strategy uses the same starting home loan, fixed rate and scheduled annual principal-and-interest budget. Scheduled principal pays down the private balance first, then any investment balance. Any payment budget left after both loans are repaid accumulates as cash. Yearly contributions earn a full year of modeled returns, while year-end recycling affects interest and returns from the next year. Payoff timing is measured in annual steps against the direct investing mortgage schedule.

The model assumes you can redraw as planned, the investment split stays eligible, and you can use the full deduction and franking credit at the selected rate in the same year. It uses annual rather than daily interest and excludes fees, changes in tax brackets, the Medicare levy surcharge, capital gains tax, foreign tax offsets, ETF cost-base adjustments and inflation. Results are scenario estimates. A registered tax agent can assess your borrowing and ETF statements.

Frequently asked questions

When might interest on a redrawn home loan be deductible?

Under ATO TR 2000/2, redraw is a new borrowing. Deductibility depends on how the borrowed money is used. A separate split used exclusively for income-producing investments makes the fund trail easier to show. A mixed-purpose loan generally requires interest apportionment.

How much can an eligible interest deduction save?

At a constant 47% marginal rate, A$100,000 in eligible debt at 6.00% incurs A$6,000 annual interest and may reduce tax by A$2,820 if the full deduction is usable at that rate. The effective annual interest cost is 3.18%. Actual savings can change if the deduction crosses a tax bracket.

How does a 100% offset compare with debt recycling?

An offset reduces interest on the linked home loan without buying ETFs. A 6.00% interest saving on the amount eligible for offset is equivalent to earning 11.32% before tax at a 47% marginal rate, assuming equal fees and a constant rate. Offset funds above the loan balance save no extra interest.

How do franking credits affect ETF cash available to reinvest?

The model multiplies the franked cash distribution by 30/70 to calculate its credit. It adds the credit to taxable income, calculates tax at the selected marginal rate, then subtracts the credit from tax. Actual ETF statements may include foreign income, capital gains and other components.

Does clearing the private home-loan split mean the mortgage is gone?

A recycled borrowing can still be outstanding after the private split reaches zero. You still owe the investment balance, even if the ETFs fall in value. The table reports both loan balances and deducts both from net wealth.
