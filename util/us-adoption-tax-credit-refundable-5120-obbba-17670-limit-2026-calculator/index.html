Frequently asked questions

Can I get the adoption credit with zero federal income tax?

Yes. The refundable portion is the smaller of $5,120 or the allowed credit for each eligible child after the MAGI reduction. Report it on Form 1040 line 30 and complete Schedule 3-A. Your actual refund depends on Schedule 3-A eligibility and the rest of your return.

How does the 2026 MAGI phase-out work?

The phase-out begins above $265,080 and fully eliminates the new credit or exclusion at $305,080. Within this range, the allowed credit falls in proportion to the income above $265,080. The $5,120 per-child refund cap applies after this reduction. Form 8839 uses separate MAGI calculations for the credit and the exclusion.

Can a special-needs adoption qualify with no expenses?

Yes. If an eligible U.S. child with a state or Indian tribal special-needs determination was adopted and the adoption became final in 2026, the $17,670 credit basis can apply with zero expenses. Previously used credit basis and the MAGI phase-out can reduce the amount.

Can I claim the credit and exclude employer assistance for the same child?

Yes. Each benefit has its own $17,670 per-child limit. Take employer-covered expenses out of the ordinary credit basis, including amounts the employer paid directly. For a finalized U.S. special-needs adoption, a qualified employer plan can allow the special-needs exclusion even without a payment. The exclusion reduces gross income.

How long can unused nonrefundable credit carry forward?

A new unused 2026 nonrefundable balance can offset available federal income tax from 2027 through 2031. Any balance left after 2031 expires. An older eligible carryforward uses available 2026 liability before the new 2026 credit.
