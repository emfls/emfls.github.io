UK Overseas Workday Relief Calculator 2026/27

Compare the SA109 election with standard UK Income Tax and Capital Gains Tax under the four-year foreign income and gains (FIG) regime.

Key takeaways

  • OWR deducts qualifying overseas earnings up to the lower of 30% of qualifying employment income and £300,000 a year.
  • An SA109 election can raise your tax if the deduction saves less than the loss of your Income Tax and CGT allowances.
  • Under the standard post-April 2025 rules, qualifying earnings can be paid into a UK bank account or transferred from overseas while retaining relief.

Frequently asked questions

Who can elect for Overseas Workday Relief?

The standard post-April 2025 regime is available in UK-resident years within the four-tax-year window that starts when you become UK resident after at least 10 consecutive non-UK-resident tax years. Non-resident years within the window still count towards its end. Check the Statutory Residence Test, disqualifications and any transitional rules.

How do the 30% and £300,000 limits interact?

The annual cap is the lower of 30% of qualifying employment income and £300,000. Both limits equal £300,000 at £1 million of qualifying income. Above £1 million the cash ceiling is lower. Relief also cannot exceed the qualifying earnings attributable to overseas duties.

Do qualifying earnings need to stay in an offshore bank account?

Post-April 2025 qualifying earnings can be paid into a UK account or transferred from overseas without losing OWR. Check separate rules for pre-6 April 2025 earnings and amounts covered by the former remittance basis.

Can an SA109 election increase my UK tax bill?

Yes. In the election year you lose your Income Tax Personal Allowance and CGT Annual Exempt Amount. A small deduction may save less than those allowances would. The standard Personal Allowance is already zero at adjusted net income of £125,140 or more. Run the comparison again if your income, gains or workdays change.

Does the four-year figure include NIC or other FIG claims?

The figure repeats the 2026/27 Income Tax and CGT comparison across four identical eligible years. OWR provides no UK National Insurance relief. Separate FIG claims on foreign investment income or gains can alter the overall decision and are outside this calculation.
