How the comparison works

For an investment of P before tax, the TFSA starts with P × (1 − working rate). With the refund reinvested, the RRSP starts with the full P. Both grow by the same factor (1 + annual return)years. The RRSP then keeps 1 − effective retirement rate of its gross value. Dividing out the common growth factor gives the break-even effective rate: your working marginal rate.

For example, $10,000 before tax at a 40% working rate gives $6,000 in the TFSA. With no growth and a 30% retirement tax rate, the grossed-up RRSP leaves $7,000. Add 15 points of OAS recovery and it leaves $5,500. Spending the refund means investing $6,000 in the RRSP and receiving $2,400 in tax savings for current spending. At a 30% effective retirement rate, that RRSP leaves $4,200.

Frequently asked questions

Who reaches $109,000 in cumulative TFSA room in 2026?

The annual limits total $109,000 if you were born in 1991 or earlier and qualified in every year from 2009 through 2026. Your remaining room also depends on contributions and withdrawals. Withdrawals restore room the following calendar year.

When does the RRSP beat the TFSA?

With the deduction fully reinvested and the same investment return, the RRSP wins when your working marginal tax rate exceeds the effective retirement rate, including any modeled benefit reduction. At equal rates the results tie.

What changes if I spend my RRSP refund?

Both accounts start with the same after-tax contribution. Spending the RRSP tax savings gives you cash today, but leaves less for retirement than the TFSA whenever the effective withdrawal rate is positive.

Can an RRSP withdrawal trigger OAS or GIS reductions?

Taxable RRSP or RRIF withdrawals can increase the income used to assess OAS recovery tax or GIS eligibility. The OAS recovery threshold is $95,323 for 2026 income. GIS depends on household circumstances. TFSA withdrawals leave both income tests unaffected.

Can I contribute the full $33,810 to an RRSP in 2026?

The $33,810 ceiling applies to the new room calculation, which starts with 18% of prior-year earned income. Unused room and pension adjustments affect your personal limit. Check your CRA notice of assessment before contributing.
