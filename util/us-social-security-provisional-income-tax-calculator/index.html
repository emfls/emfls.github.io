Key takeaways

  • Up to 85% of benefits can count as taxable income. The first combined-income threshold is $25,000 for single filers and $32,000 for joint filers.
  • Eligible seniors can deduct up to $6,000 each. A joint return can claim up to $12,000 when both spouses qualify. The deduction reduces taxable income after Social Security taxability is calculated.
  • A $1,000 fully taxable IRA withdrawal can add up to $850 in taxable benefits. It can also reduce the senior deduction. A qualified Roth IRA withdrawal leaves both calculations unchanged.

What this estimate covers

Provisional income uses other AGI, tax-exempt interest and half of net benefits. Schedule 1-A MAGI here uses other AGI plus taxable benefits and assumes no excluded foreign or territory income. The estimate uses regular federal ordinary-income brackets before credits and holds other deductions constant across the IRA scenarios.

Special Social Security worksheets for prior-year lump sums, excluded income and certain deductible IRA contributions can change the result. This estimate excludes standard-deduction adjustments for blindness, dependents and nonfiling spouses. It also excludes changes to itemized deductions, preferential investment income rates, AMT, IRA penalties, Medicare surcharges, withholding and state taxes. The traditional IRA comparison assumes the entire $1,000 is taxable.

Frequently asked questions

Did OBBBA repeal federal income tax on Social Security benefits?

Social Security remains subject to the combined-income rules in 2026. The enhanced senior deduction lowers federal taxable income after the taxable portion of benefits is determined. You can qualify for the deduction even if you collect no Social Security.

How do I calculate provisional income for Social Security?

Add your adjusted gross income before Social Security, your tax-exempt interest and half of your annual net Social Security benefits. Compare the total with the thresholds for your filing status. IRS Publication 915 explains the additional worksheet adjustments for special cases.

Who can claim the $6,000 or $12,000 senior deduction?

Each person who meets the age-65 rule and has a valid Social Security number can qualify for up to $6,000 in tax years 2025 through 2028. Married taxpayers must file jointly. Eligible filers may take the deduction with standard or itemized deductions.

When does the senior deduction phase out?

The deduction falls by 6 cents per eligible senior for each dollar of MAGI above $75,000 for eligible non-joint filers or $150,000 for joint filers. It reaches $0 at $175,000 or $250,000, respectively. Two eligible spouses lose 12 cents total per dollar above the joint threshold.

How does a qualified Roth IRA withdrawal affect the tax torpedo?

A qualified Roth IRA withdrawal stays out of gross income, so it leaves provisional income and Schedule 1-A MAGI unchanged. A fully taxable traditional IRA withdrawal can raise both, making more benefits taxable and reducing the senior deduction.
