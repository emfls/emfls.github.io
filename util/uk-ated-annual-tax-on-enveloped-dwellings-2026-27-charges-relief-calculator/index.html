Frequently asked questions

Who is within scope of ATED?

ATED generally covers UK dwellings worth more than £500,000 held wholly or partly by a company, a partnership with a corporate member or a collective investment scheme. Exemptions and reliefs can apply.

Which valuation date applies in 2026/27?

For an existing dwelling, use its open-market value at 1 April 2022 if held on that date, or at acquisition if bought later. Certain transactions or changes to a dwelling can trigger a new valuation.

How is part-year ATED calculated?

Multiply the annual charge by the number of chargeable days divided by 365 and round the total down to a whole pound for the return. Count acquisition day, exclude disposal day and subtract any qualifying relief days.

Does £0 ATED still require a return?

An in-scope dwelling fully relieved from ATED generally requires a Relief Declaration Return. The usual deadline is 30 April for dwellings held on 1 April, or the 30th day counting acquisition day as day one. A qualifying acquisition can be covered by a same-type relief return already filed for that period.

Can a late nil return cost £1,600?

Yes. The standard scenario combines £100 initially, up to £900 of daily penalties if HMRC gives notice, and £300 at each of six and twelve months. For a taxable return, the standard further penalties can each be 5% of liability if that exceeds £300.
