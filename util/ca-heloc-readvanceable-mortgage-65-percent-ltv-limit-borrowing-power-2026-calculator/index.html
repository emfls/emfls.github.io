Answer-First: OSFI B-20 HELOC Rules

  • 65% Standalone Revolving HELOC Cap: OSFI Guideline B-20 dictates that revolving lines of credit secured by residential property cannot exceed 65% of the property’s appraised market value.
  • 80% Combined LTV Maximum: Canadian lenders can approve combined mortgage and HELOC financing up to 80% LTV, but any lending portion between 65% and 80% must be an amortizing fixed or variable term loan.
  • Canadian Statutory Compounding: Under Section 6 of the Canadian Interest Act, mortgage interest is calculated semi-annually, not in advance. Monthly payments use $r_m = (1 + r/2)^0.16666666666666666 - 1$.
  • Automatic Readvanceable Growth: In a readvanceable mortgage (e.g. RBC Homeline, TD FlexLine, BMO Homeowner ReadiLine), each dollar of principal paid on the term mortgage automatically increases available HELOC borrowing capacity until the 65% cap is met.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the OSFI 65% HELOC limit rule in Canada?

Under OSFI Guideline B-20, federally regulated financial institutions in Canada cannot grant a revolving Home Equity Line of Credit (HELOC) exceeding 65% of the property’s appraised market value.

How does a readvanceable mortgage work up to 80% LTV?

In a readvanceable mortgage (or combined collateral mortgage plan), the total combined borrowing limit can reach up to 80% LTV, but any lending between 65% and 80% LTV must be held in an amortizing term mortgage.

How does statutory Canadian semi-annual compounding affect mortgage payments?

Section 6 of the Canadian Interest Act mandates that mortgage interest cannot be compounded more frequently than semi-annually. This results in an effective monthly interest rate of (1 + r/2)^(1/6) - 1, slightly lower than US monthly compounding.

What is the OSFI mortgage stress test qualifying rate?

Canadian borrowers must qualify at the greater of 5.25% or the contractual mortgage interest rate plus 2.00%, ensuring serviceability under potential interest rate increases.

When does automatic readvanceable credit expand?

With every regular monthly mortgage payment, the principal portion paid down is automatically converted into available credit on the HELOC portion, dollar-for-dollar, until the total HELOC balance reaches the 65% LTV cap.

Calculation assumptions and methodology

Calculations use statutory Canadian semi-annual compounding for amortizing mortgages and simple monthly interest for the revolving HELOC portion. Appraisals and property values are assumed constant over the 5-year projection period.
