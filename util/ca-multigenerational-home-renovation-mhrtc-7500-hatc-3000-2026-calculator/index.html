Frequently asked questions

What qualifies a secondary suite for the MHRTC?

The renovation must create a self-contained unit with a private entrance, kitchen, bathroom and sleeping area for a senior aged 65 or older or a DTC-eligible adult aged 18 or older to live with a qualifying relative. The lifetime limit is one qualifying renovation per qualifying individual. Claim in the year the renovation is completed using Schedule 12 and line 45355.

Which accessibility renovations may qualify for the HATC?

Permanent work that improves access, mobility or safety in an eligible dwelling may qualify for a senior or a DTC-eligible person. Examples include a permanent ramp, stairlift or widened doorways. The $20,000 annual expense cap is shared when eligible claimants claim costs for the same individual or dwelling.

Can I claim the same expense under HATC and METC in 2026?

Bill C-15 ended same-expense HATC and METC claims beginning January 1, 2026. Use different eligible expenses for each credit. Keep any amount claimed under the MHRTC separate from both claims.

How is the federal medical expense threshold calculated?

For line 33099, subtract the lesser of 3% of the claimant’s net income or $2,890 from eligible unreimbursed medical expenses in the claim period. The 14% base credit applies to the positive remainder and is limited by available federal tax. Medical eligibility for home modifications has its own conditions.

What happens if I owe no federal income tax?

The refundable MHRTC can still generate a refund. The HATC and METC reduce federal tax payable only, so they provide no federal savings when the available federal tax is zero.
