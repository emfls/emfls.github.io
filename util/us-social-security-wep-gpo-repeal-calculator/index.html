How the estimate works

Enter the full gross monthly worker PIA or full spouse or survivor entitlement. The WEP formula estimates a PIA reduction on the 2024 eligibility-year scale before age-related benefit adjustments. Use the exact prior dollar reduction if you have it and pair it with the matching gross full benefit.

For WEP, the first $1,174 of AIME used 40% with up to 20 substantial-earnings years instead of the regular 90%. The WEP factor rose 5 percentage points per year until it reached 90% at 30 years. The estimate caps restoration at half the non-covered pension and at the full benefit. For GPO, it restores two-thirds of the pension, capped at the spouse or survivor entitlement.

Scope and assumptions

This gross benefit estimate holds the restored monthly amount flat. It excludes 2025 and 2026 COLAs, Medicare Part B and IRMAA, filing and entitlement rules, and federal income tax. Depending on filing status and combined income, up to 85% of benefits may be included in federally taxable income. Compare the estimate with your SSA award or adjustment notice.

Frequently asked questions

Which months qualify for the repeal?

January 2024 was the first benefit month covered. December 2023 was the final month under the old provisions. Count eligible months for which the increase has yet to be paid, starting no earlier than January 2024.

What was the maximum under the 2024 WEP formula?

A worker with at most 20 years of substantial covered earnings could have a maximum $587 monthly reduction under the 2024 eligibility-year scale, subject to the pension guarantee. With 30 or more years, the formula reduction was zero.

How is GPO different from WEP?

GPO affected spouse and survivor benefits. The old offset was two-thirds of the non-covered government pension, capped at the full affected entitlement. WEP reduced the worker’s own benefit.
