Understanding Canada Spousal Support Advisory Guidelines (SSAG)

Drafted by Professors Carol Rogerson and Rollie Thompson for the Federal Department of Justice, the Spousal Support Advisory Guidelines (SSAG) are accepted by courts across all Canadian provinces and territories as the benchmark for determining quantum (amount) and duration of spousal support under the Divorce Act.

1. The "Without Child Support" Quantum Formula

When there are no dependent children of the marriage, the formula calculates spousal support based on the gross income disparity:

  • Multiplier: 1.5% to 2.0% of the gross income difference for each year of cohabitation.
  • Maximum Cap: Capped at a maximum of 37.5% (low) to 50% (high) of the gross income difference, which is reached after 25 years of marriage.
  • Tax Deductibility: Periodic spousal support payments are taxable income to the recipient and fully tax-deductible to the payor on Line 22000 of Canadian income tax returns.

2. Duration Rules & Indefinite Support Exceptions

  • Standard Duration: Between 0.5 and 1.0 year of support for every year of cohabitation.
  • The 20-Year Rule: Any marriage lasting 20 years or longer results in an indefinite duration (no fixed termination date, subject to material change in circumstances upon retirement).
  • The Rule of 65: If the marriage lasted at least 5 years AND the recipient's age at separation plus the years of marriage equals 65 or greater, support is indefinite. This protects older spouses who separated after moderate-length marriages and face significant barriers to financial self-sufficiency.
