Key takeaways

  • The 2026 IRA contribution limit is $7,500, or $8,600 if you’re 50 or older by year-end, including the $1,100 catch-up.
  • Pre-tax money in any of your traditional, rollover, traditional SEP or traditional SIMPLE IRAs can make part of your conversion taxable.
  • If a 401(k) accepts all eligible pre-tax funds by year-end, a conversion fully covered by your IRA basis can have $0 in estimated conversion tax.

Frequently asked questions

Which accounts count toward the December 31 IRA balance?

Include your own traditional, rollover, traditional SEP and traditional SIMPLE IRAs across all custodians. Exclude Roth IRAs, separately held inherited IRAs, your spouse’s IRAs and employer 401(k), 403(b) and 457(b) plans. An IRA a surviving spouse treats as their own belongs in their own pool.

Can a 401(k) reverse rollover make the conversion tax-free?

Yes, if the employer plan accepts the eligible pre-tax IRA funds, the transfer is completed by December 31 and the remaining conversion is fully covered by IRA basis. After-tax IRA basis cannot go into the plan. Earnings converted above your basis remain taxable. SIMPLE IRAs generally have a two-year rollover restriction.

Do spouses combine IRA balances on a joint return?

Each spouse calculates the IRA pro-rata share using their own accounts and basis. If both spouses need Form 8606, each files a separate form with the joint return.

Where does unused IRA basis go after a partial conversion?

Form 8606 line 14 generally carries unused basis forward. Keep the form for your next applicable filing so that you can account for the remaining after-tax dollars when calculating future distributions or conversions.

What if I contribute in 2027 for 2026 or take another IRA distribution?

Form 8606 line 4 removes 2026 contributions made in 2027 from basis available for 2026 distributions. Any other applicable IRA distribution goes on line 7 and shares the same pro-rata allocation. This calculator assumes both line 4 and line 7 are zero.
