Key takeaways

Statutory Maternity Pay (SMP) pays 90% of average weekly earnings for the first 6 weeks, then £194.32 a week or 90% of earnings if lower for the next 33 weeks. At the cap, those 33 weeks total £6,412.56. SMP ends after week 39. Holiday continues to accrue through unpaid maternity leave.

Month-by-month cash flow

Each planning month is 1/12 of 52 weeks. Monthly payroll prorates maternity pay across these periods and books KIT pay at the start of the work week. Weekly payroll groups whole pay packets by estimated payday. “Net change” compares with the previous month, using normal monthly take-home as the starting point. How take-home pay is estimated

PAYE uses the England, Wales and Northern Ireland bands with a full standard allowance and estimates each pay period independently. Actual payroll often uses cumulative earnings and your tax code. Scotland, allowance tapering above £100,000, tax-year changes, student loans and other deductions are outside the estimate.

Employee pension is a percentage of actual pay, deducted before Income Tax under a net pay arrangement. NI uses gross pay. During paid leave, employer contributions are generally based on normal pre-leave pensionable pay. Frequently asked questions

How do I know if I qualify for Statutory Maternity Pay?

For a qualifying week in 2026/27, you generally need at least £129 in average weekly earnings and 26 continuous weeks with your employer up to a day in the 15th week before your expected week of childbirth. Payroll uses qualifying earnings actually paid in the relevant period, normally at least eight weeks. Notice and proof of pregnancy also apply.

How does enhanced maternity pay interact with SMP?

When your employer's full- or half-pay rate includes SMP, their top-up is the difference between the policy amount and SMP, floored at zero. If SMP is higher than the policy amount, you still receive the full SMP. Check your policy for eligibility and any top-up repayment conditions.

Can I work Keeping in Touch days while receiving SMP?

You and your employer can agree up to 10 KIT days without ending maternity leave or losing SMP. Agree timing and pay in advance. Part of a day counts as a day and KIT work is barred during compulsory leave after birth, normally two weeks or four for factory workers.

Do tax, National Insurance and pension come out of maternity pay?

SMP and enhanced pay are processed through payroll, with Income Tax and employee National Insurance due where applicable. Your pension deduction depends on the scheme. This estimate uses a net pay pension arrangement with employee contributions calculated from actual pay.

Do I still earn annual leave in weeks 40 to 52?

Yes. Holiday entitlement continues to accrue throughout statutory maternity leave, including unpaid weeks 40 to 52. The calculator estimates the accrued days pro rata for your selected leave and values them at your normal daily salary.
