Ontario Severance & Common Law Notice Legal Principles

Under Canadian employment law, dismissed employees are entitled to compensation governed by two distinct frameworks: the statutory floor set by the Employment Standards Act, 2000 (ESA), and common law reasonable notice established in landmark judicial decisions.

1. Ontario ESA Statutory Minimums

  • Termination Pay (In Lieu of Notice): 1 week of regular pay per year of service, starting after 3 months of service, capped at 8 weeks maximum.
  • Statutory Severance Pay: Granted if the employee has 5 or more years of service AND the employer has an annual Ontario payroll of $2.5 million or more. Calculated as 1 week per year of service (including partial years prorated), capped at 26 weeks.
  • Total ESA Ceiling: Maximum possible statutory entitlement is 34 weeks (8 weeks termination + 26 weeks severance).

2. Common Law Reasonable Notice (Bardal Factors)

Unless ousted by an enforceable employment contract with a valid termination clause, dismissed employees are entitled to common law reasonable notice determined under Bardal v. Globe & Mail Ltd. (1960). Courts assess:

  1. Age: Older workers (especially 50+) generally receive enhanced notice periods due to greater re-employment hurdles.
  2. Character of Employment: Senior executives and specialized technical professionals command higher notice periods.
  3. Length of Service: Tenured employees frequently receive approximately 3 to 4 weeks (or ~1 month) of pay per year of service.
  4. Availability of Similar Employment: Industry labor market conditions at the time of termination.

3. CRA Retiring Allowance RRSP Rollover (ITA s. 60(j.1))

A retiring allowance (severance pay) qualifies for a unique tax shelter under paragraph 60(j.1) of the federal Income Tax Act. Employees who worked prior to 1996 can roll over $2,000 for each year of service before 1996, plus $1,500 for each year before 1989 without pension vesting, directly into their RRSP without using any existing RRSP contribution room. This defers thousands in immediate CRA withholding tax.
