How the Medicaid look-back and penalty work

When a person applies for Medicaid payment for nursing-facility care, the agency can review what the applicant or their spouse did with assets before the application. A gift, a sale below fair market value, or a waiver of an enforceable right to property may leave an uncompensated amount. Under the federal rule, an uncompensated transfer on or after the look-back date can trigger a period in which Medicaid withholds payment for specified long-term care services. The agency makes separate findings about the amount transferred, whether an exception applies and when the penalty can begin. See 42 U.S.C. § 1396p(c).

1. Establish the dates and document the transfer

For an institutionalized applicant, the federal reference date is the first date on which the person is both institutionalized and has applied for Medicaid. If admission and application occur on different days, the later event generally determines when both conditions are met. This calculator subtracts 60 calendar months and uses the final valid day of the target month when needed. A transfer before the cutoff produces a zero federal-model penalty. A transfer on the cutoff remains in the federal model. Keep the bank record, deed, settlement statement or other evidence of when ownership or access actually changed. Ask the agency how it records the dates if the transaction sat near a month boundary or involved several settlement steps.

2. Find the uncompensated value and evaluate exceptions

Start with the fair market value of what left the household and subtract the documented compensation received. For example, a property interest worth $80,000 sold for $55,000 gives a starting uncompensated value of $25,000. The calculator subtracts the qualifying exempt portion entered and floors the result at zero. When the agency reviews several gifts, it can consider cumulative uncompensated amounts. This form uses one date, so avoid assigning one date to a total that mixes transactions from inside and outside the review period. Prepare a dated transfer log and confirm which amounts belong in the aggregate before modeling them.

Federal exceptions include certain transfers to or for the sole benefit of a spouse and transfers to or for the sole benefit of a blind or disabled child, subject to the statutory conditions. A home transfer to a qualifying caregiver son or daughter generally requires that the child lived in the home for at least two years immediately before institutionalization and provided care that allowed the parent to remain there. Other home exceptions have their own requirements, including an equity and one-year residency rule for a qualifying sibling. Preserve occupancy records, professional statements and evidence of what care was provided. Ask how the state reviews returned assets, evidence of another purpose and an undue-hardship claim. The relevant exceptions appear in 42 U.S.C. § 1396p(c)(2).

3. Separate the divisor, the billed rate and the start date

The federal formula divides cumulative uncompensated value by a private-pay nursing-facility average for the state or an authorized local community at application. The divisor determines the modeled duration. The home’s actual private-pay rate determines the budget estimate. This page divides in cents, reports full months, and expresses the remaining dollar ratio as an exact fraction of 30 days. Dollar formatting and the displayed decimal days are rounded for readability, while the payment estimate uses the underlying unrounded duration. The remainder is the portion of the transfer used to compute a partial month. Federal law bars disregarding a fractional period of ineligibility. Handling of fractional days and daily billing is specific to the agency.

For transfers made on or after February 8, 2006, federal law generally begins the penalty at the later of the statutory transfer-month starting point and the date the applicant could otherwise receive covered institutional care based on an approved application, with restrictions on overlapping penalty periods. Money may already have gone to the recipient when a family learns that coverage is delayed. The gross gap shown here multiplies the model duration by the entered facility rate. Ask the facility for a written private rate, the room type, additional services and partial-month billing terms. Available income and insurance can change how much cash the family must supply. The estimate focuses on the penalty window and excludes earlier costs while the applicant meets other eligibility criteria.

California’s asset-test transition

California restored asset limits for affected Medi-Cal groups in January 2026 after a two-year gap. The county’s institutional long-term care review examines up to 30 months before the application or, for a current member entering care, before admission. It excludes January 2024 through December 2025. In the first half of 2026 the earliest reviewed months can include transfers before January 2024. From July 2026 the review adds months from January 2026 until the 30-month window fills. The calculator shows this prior-month screen separately. Ask the county about same-month transactions, the asset balance at transfer, applicable private-pay rate and hardship criteria. The federal-model gap remains illustrative when California is selected. See DHCS transfer guidance and review calendar.

How the community spouse allowance fits

The community spouse is the spouse who remains in the community. The agency can evaluate the couple’s combined countable resources using an assessment tied to the start of a qualifying period of institutionalization. Under the federal spousal impoverishment framework the agency determines how much the community spouse may keep, considering the state’s method and any applicable order or hearing decision. The 2026 minimum and maximum resource standards give context for that calculation. An income maintenance allowance is a separate calculation. A qualifying transfer to the spouse can avoid a transfer penalty while the underlying assets still affect eligibility under the resource rules. Use the asset field for the scenario budget and ask for an actual resource assessment. See 42 U.S.C. § 1396r-5.

Worked examples and the gift-and-annuity comparison

Partial month and private-pay gap

Assume a $62,500 uncompensated transfer in October 2024, of which $10,000 qualifies for an exception. With a November 24, 2026 federal reference date, the October 2024 transfer falls inside the window. The $52,500 result divided by a $10,500 illustrative divisor yields five full months and a zero remainder. At $12,000 a month, the gross care bill for that period is $60,000. If the exempt portion were $5,000, the numerator would rise to $57,500. Five full months would account for $52,500, leaving $5,000. That remainder represents exactly 100/7 days on a 30-day model, or about 14.285714 days. The gross cost becomes $65,714.29 using the unrounded duration.

A transfer on the cutoff

Suppose the person was already institutionalized when they applied on November 24, 2026. The cutoff is November 24, 2021. A transfer on November 23, 2021 falls outside the federal screen and adds zero to this model. A gift made on November 24, 2021 remains within review and may create a penalty if it was uncompensated and no exception applies. If several gifts crossed the boundary, verify individual dates with the agency instead of applying one date to the total. Separately, the final assessment may depend on an earlier application or additional facts about the institutional stay.

What the half-a-loaf model compares

The comparison starts with the entered countable-asset balance and assumes a new, non-exempt hypothetical gift equal to half of it, rounded down to the nearest cent. It computes a separate theoretical penalty horizon from that gift and uses the same horizon in both scenarios. The private-pay case budgets from the full starting balance. The gift-and-annuity case treats the retained half as though a contract could make the principal available for care across that period, with no fees or interest. The model reports the gross bill, the shortfall and what remains on each side. Its estate-value row adds the gift held by recipients and deducts any uncovered care cost, with a floor of zero. Recipient-held value is at risk if care funding falls short or the recipient cannot help.

For a $120,000 balance, $10,500 divisor and $12,000 care bill, a $60,000 hypothetical gift creates about 5.714286 months of modeled penalty. The same-period care bill is $68,571.43 either way. Private pay leaves $51,428.57 in the household. The hypothetical payout funds $60,000 of care and leaves $8,571.43 unfunded. Gift recipients hold $60,000, so the combined illustrative value after accounting for the uncovered bill is also $51,428.57. This equal-period comparison makes the funding risk visible. The household loses direct access to the gift and needs a separate source of cash to meet the bill.

Actual annuities can carry fees, contract restrictions, and income or resource consequences. Depending on the contract, federal law addresses disclosure, state remainder-beneficiary priority, actuarial soundness, equal payments and restrictions on assignment, deferral and balloon payments. A modeled payout over a fixed horizon does not establish that a contract is available or would satisfy those tests. Timing, other resources, income, post-eligibility contributions, taxes and estate recovery can change the outcome. Ask an elder law attorney to review the proposal and the state’s requirements under 42 U.S.C. § 1396p(c) and (e) before making a transfer or signing a contract.

Frequently asked questions

Does a transfer exactly five years before applying count?

Under the federal rule, a transfer on the cutoff date is within review because the statute covers transfers on or after that date. The federal reference date is when the applicant is both institutionalized and has applied. Confirm how your agency records the reference and transfer dates, especially for month-end dates and California cases.

Can I use the federal gift-tax exclusion as a Medicaid exemption?

Medicaid evaluates what you received in exchange and whether a Medicaid-specific exception applies. A gift can be within a federal tax exclusion and still count as an uncompensated transfer for Medicaid. Keep proof of payment, fair market value and any facts supporting a statutory exception.

When does the penalty start, and who pays during it?

For modern transfers under the federal rule, the penalty generally starts when the applicant would otherwise qualify for covered institutional care, or at the statutory transfer-month start if later, subject to existing penalties. During the gap, determine what the facility bills and what income, insurance or other funds are available. The calculator shows a gross private-pay estimate at the entered rate.

Why can my state's notice show a different number of days?

The model expresses fractions using a 30-day month and preserves the exact ratio in the breakdown. An agency may use a published daily divisor, a facility-region rate, or its own treatment of partial days. Check the divisor, effective date, included transfers and rounding shown on the notice before comparing it with this estimate.

Can a community spouse keep the maximum CSRA or buy an annuity automatically?

The state calculates the allowance under spousal impoverishment rules using the household's facts and applicable standards. An annuity has separate federal and state requirements, including disclosure and potentially a state remainder-beneficiary designation. Ask an elder law attorney to review both the resource assessment and any proposed contract before committing funds.
