Key takeaways

A fully deductible £40,000 employer contribution puts £40,000 into a registered SIPP. With 25% tax-free cash and 20% tax on the rest, the estimated retirement value is £34,000, or 85% of the contribution.

From 6 April 2026, the basic and higher dividend rates rise by 2 percentage points to 10.75% and 35.75%. The additional rate stays at 39.35%. In the example below, £40,000 of profit produces £23,314.50 of spendable dividend cash after Corporation Tax and personal tax.

Keep enough cash outside your pension

The pension figure is money for retirement. If the company needs cash for tax or trading, or you need money before pension access age, moving the whole slice into a SIPP can leave a cash shortfall even when the pension has the highest modelled value. Check the scheme’s access rules and the company’s distributable reserves before acting.

How the estimate works

Corporation Tax on the slice is the difference between the tax on company profit before and after deducting the slice. The pension and bonus routes assume the full cost qualifies as a deduction. The bonus uses part of that budget for employer NI, then pays incremental Income Tax and employee NI on the gross bonus. The dividend pays Corporation Tax first and uses the change in total personal tax to account for existing dividends, band usage and the Personal Allowance taper.

This estimate assumes a UK trading company eligible for Marginal Relief, a full 12-month accounting period and a full-year category A director using England, Wales or Northern Ireland Income Tax rates. It assumes no Employment Allowance or special NI relief, savings interest, Gift Aid, student loan deductions or other adjustments to net income. Pension allowance inputs must reflect any taper, eligible carry forward and prior inputs. Complex post-flexible-access cases can require a separate HMRC calculation.

Net dividend cash can fund an ISA within your available subscription limit, or a general investment account. The estimate assumes a lawful dividend can be paid from distributable reserves. Check that the SIPP accepts employer payments and that the proposed payment is deductible in the relevant period.

Frequently asked questions

Can my limited company pay directly into my SIPP?

Yes, if the pension provider accepts employer payments. A payment to a registered scheme can be deductible when made wholly and exclusively for the trade. The contribution itself generally attracts no employer NI or dividend tax.

When does a pension contribution save 26.5% Corporation Tax?

When both profit figures are in the adjusted Marginal Relief band and taxable and augmented profits are equal. If the deduction crosses a threshold, the saving reflects the change in tax across both bands. Relevant exempt distributions can also change the result.

How are 2026/27 dividends taxed?

Dividends use the remaining Personal Allowance, where available. The next £500 of taxable dividends is charged at 0% and still uses band space. The rates above the allowance are 10.75%, 35.75% and 39.35% depending on the band.

Can I contribute more than £60,000 to a SIPP?

A contribution above the standard annual allowance can trigger a tax charge. Employer and personal inputs across pensions count. Eligible unused allowance from the prior three years can help, but cannot raise the £10,000 money purchase annual allowance. High incomes can reduce the standard allowance.

How much of my SIPP can I keep after retirement tax?

If you take 25% tax-free and pay 20% on the remainder, you keep 85% before growth and fees. The tax-free amount uses your remaining lifetime lump sum allowance, normally £268,275 before earlier use. Your withdrawal tax depends on your other income and when you take it.
