Frequently asked questions

What is the pension annual allowance in 2026/27?

The standard allowance is £60,000 for the tax year ending 5 April 2027. Gross personal and employer contributions count towards it across all defined contribution schemes. If you have a defined benefit pension, growth in its value can count towards the same allowance.

When do the high-income taper rules apply?

Both threshold income over £200,000 and adjusted income over £260,000 are required. The allowance falls by £1 for each full £2 above the adjusted income gate. For example, £300,000 adjusted income gives a £40,000 allowance if the threshold income gate is also met.

Which years can I carry forward and in what order?

Use your 2026/27 allowance first, then use available balances from 2023/24, 2024/25 and 2025/26 in that order. You must have belonged to a registered pension scheme in each year you use. Account for tapering and any allowance already used in later years.

Can carry forward increase the £10,000 MPAA?

Carry forward cannot offset an excess above the £10,000 MPAA. If you triggered MPAA in a prior tax year, the limit applies to eligible DC contributions throughout 2026/27. If you first trigger it during 2026/27, the MPAA test covers contributions from the following day. Other pension savings can be subject to separate rules.

How do the personal earnings cap and tax charge differ?

The earnings cap generally limits tax relief on personal payments to 100% of relevant UK earnings, subject to a separate £3,600 gross basic amount where eligible. Employer payments do not use that cap, but they do use annual allowance. An excess above the available annual allowance can be subject to a charge at one or more income tax rates.
