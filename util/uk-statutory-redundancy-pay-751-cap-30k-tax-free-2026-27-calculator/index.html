Key takeaways

  • Up to £22,530 in statutory redundancy pay in England, Scotland and Wales from 6 April 2026. The weekly cap is £751 and only the latest 20 complete years count.
  • One £30,000 exemption covers statutory pay and qualifying enhanced severance combined. Any qualifying excess incurs Income Tax and zero employee National Insurance.
  • PILON and accrued holiday pay are taxable earnings. Employee National Insurance applies under the normal pay-period rules.

Budget from take-home cash before accepting an offer

A package under £30,000 can still leave a shortfall if it includes notice or holiday pay. Ask payroll for the written split and the deductions on each component before relying on the headline offer to cover bills.

Frequently asked questions

What is the statutory redundancy pay cap from 6 April 2026?

In Great Britain the weekly cap rises from £719 to £751 on 6 April 2026. The corresponding maximum rises from £21,570 to £22,530. Northern Ireland has separate limits.

How are birthdays and years of service counted?

You normally need two complete years of continuous employment. Count back up to 20 complete years from the relevant date. A year in which you turn 22 or 41 partway through uses the lower band for that whole year.

Does the £30,000 exemption include both statutory and enhanced redundancy pay?

Yes. Statutory redundancy pay and qualifying enhanced severance share one £30,000 exemption. Qualifying cash above it is subject to Income Tax and employer Class 1A National Insurance at 15%. Employee National Insurance on that excess is zero.

How do PILON, holiday pay and post-employment notice pay affect take-home cash?

PILON and accrued holiday pay are earnings subject to Income Tax and employee Class 1 National Insurance rules. If payroll identifies additional post-employment notice pay (PENP) within enhanced severance, that part is also treated as earnings and comes out of the exemption pool.

Why might my payslip show a different net amount?

This estimate applies one selected marginal tax rate and standard category A employee National Insurance. Your tax code, tax bands, Scottish Income Tax rates, the Personal Allowance taper above £100,000, and other payroll deductions may change your take-home amount.
