Check whether your foreign accounts or assets cross the filing thresholds. Compare missed-filing exposure with the IRS streamlined offshore penalties.

Key takeaways

  • FBAR: A U.S. person with reportable foreign accounts generally files when the sum of account maximums exceeds $10,000. The April 15 deadline automatically extends to October 15.
  • Form 8938: The filing test uses separate year-end and any-day thresholds. They start at $50,000 and $75,000 for single or separately filing U.S. residents.
  • Late filing: Non-willful FBAR penalties can reach $16,536 per annual report. Form 8938 penalties can reach $60,000 per missed year after IRS notice.

Frequently asked questions

How do I apply the $10,000 FBAR rule across accounts?

Add the highest balance of each reportable foreign account during the calendar year, even when the accounts peak on different days. File if the sum exceeds $10,000. A total of exactly $10,000 leaves the threshold unmet. The April 15 deadline automatically extends to October 15.

Can I be required to file both FBAR and Form 8938?

Yes. FBAR goes to FinCEN and covers reportable foreign financial accounts. Form 8938 goes with the required income tax return and can cover other specified foreign financial assets. For Form 8938, either the tax year-end value or the any-day value can cross its applicable threshold.

How does Bittner affect FBAR penalties?

Bittner applies the non-willful penalty per annual report. For willful account-reporting violations, the statute accounts for the balance of the relevant account at the time of violation. This calculator uses an aggregate-balance illustration using the same input for each missed year.

When do Form 8938 continuation penalties begin?

A missed filing can incur $10,000 initially. Once 90 days have elapsed after the IRS mails notice, each additional 30-day period or part of one can add $10,000. The additional penalty is capped at $50,000 per missed filing.

How do streamlined domestic and foreign penalties differ?

Eligible domestic filers generally pay 5% of the highest aggregate year-end value of covered penalty-base assets. Eligible foreign filers who comply with the program face a 0% miscellaneous offshore penalty. Both routes require non-willful conduct, and tax and interest may still be due.
