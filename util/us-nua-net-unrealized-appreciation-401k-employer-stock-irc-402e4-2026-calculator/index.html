2026 NUA Calculator: 401(k) Company Stock vs. IRA Rollover

Compare a taxable transfer of employer shares under IRC § 402(e)(4) with a direct traditional IRA rollover. See how your share basis, age and sale timing change the outcome.

Key takeaways

Low-basis shares can favor NUA. You generally owe ordinary income tax on the taxable basis when shares leave the plan, then long-term capital gains tax on realized NUA when you sell. That realized NUA is exempt from the 3.8% NIIT. An eligible direct rollover defers tax until IRA withdrawal.

Loss-aversion check

A drop in the stock price can erase some or all of the NUA benefit while your upfront tax bill stays due. Try a negative growth rate to see how holding concentrated employer stock could affect your after-tax wealth.

How the comparison works

The model assumes pretax 401(k) assets and a direct rollover with $0 upfront tax on rolled amounts. Under the NUA route, the upfront taxable amount is the selected shares’ value less their positive NUA. Year 0 uses the current ordinary rate for an immediate IRA withdrawal. Later IRA withdrawals use the later ordinary rate on the entire balance. The model includes the standard 10% early tax where applicable and evaluates the IRA age at each withdrawal.

Both routes use the same price growth and dividend yield. IRA dividends compound before tax. Brokerage dividends are modeled as qualified, taxed annually and held in cash at 0%. Upfront tax is paid from outside cash and deducted from wealth at face value. Year 1 is the exact first anniversary, so any gain above distribution value is short-term at Year 1. Break-even checks use annual checkpoints.

The estimate uses flat rates and assumes the full 3.8% marginal NIIT when selected. It excludes tax bracket stacking, capital loss benefits, required minimum distributions, fees, and estate or beneficiary-specific cash flows. The plan’s in-kind distribution and reported basis determine the actual tax treatment.

Frequently asked questions

Is NUA taxed as long-term gain if I sell on the distribution date?

Yes. Realized gain up to the NUA measured at distribution receives long-term treatment even on a same-day sale. If the stock falls, only the NUA actually realized on sale receives that treatment. Subsequent gains have a separate holding period of more than one year for long-term treatment.

When does the Rule of 55 waive the 10% additional tax?

The standard employer-plan exception applies after separation in or after the calendar year you turn 55. Leaving earlier and waiting until 55 to withdraw does not qualify. The exception does not follow money into an IRA. For a qualifying NUA transfer, the additional tax applies only to the taxable part of the distribution.

Does the 3.8% NIIT apply to NUA or inherited employer stock?

Realized NUA is excluded from net investment income. Taxable dividends and appreciation after distribution may be subject to NIIT. Original NUA is income in respect of a decedent and receives no basis step-up. Post-distribution appreciation may qualify for a basis adjustment at death.

Can I distribute low-basis lots and roll the rest into an IRA?

Ask whether the plan administrator can identify specific lots and transfer those shares in kind to a taxable brokerage. The remaining eligible shares and other assets can generally go by direct rollover. To use the lump-sum rule, the required balance across the employer’s plans of the same type must leave the plans within one tax year.

Why might the full IRA rollover catch up over time?

A direct rollover of eligible pretax money avoids current tax and reinvests gross dividends inside the IRA. A lower future ordinary income rate can improve the IRA result. The NUA route pays ordinary tax on the taxable basis upfront and incurs annual taxes on taxable dividends.
