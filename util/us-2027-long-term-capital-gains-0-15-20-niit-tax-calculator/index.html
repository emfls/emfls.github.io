How the estimate works

Net losses, then stack gains. Net gains and losses across the two holding periods. Positive short-term gains left after netting are taxed at ordinary rates. The deduction offsets ordinary income first and can also absorb some gains or qualified dividends. The remaining preferential dollars stack on top of taxable ordinary income at 0%, 15%, and 20%.

Check NIIT separately. The model treats adjusted gross income as NIIT MAGI and includes the entered taxable investment gains and dividends in net investment income. Wages and taxable distributions from qualified retirement plans can increase MAGI. The standard or itemized deduction does not lower MAGI, and the model assumes zero additional investment expenses deductible for NIIT.

Read the tax change against a no-investment baseline. The calculator compares the regular tax on the full scenario with regular tax on the same wages and retirement income alone, then adds NIIT. This includes ordinary tax on net short-term gains and can show savings when a capital loss reduces taxable income. Enter gross proceeds to include the cash recovered from your investment basis.

Review the harvesting amount. The 0% bracket room measures remaining taxable-income headroom. Additional gain before the 0% cutoff may be larger when unused deductions or capital losses absorb gains. Filling that room can also use up a capital loss deduction, restore short-term gains taxed at ordinary rates, or affect other tax benefits.

The estimate uses the basic standard deduction or your allowable custom total and applies the marginal rate schedules. It excludes other investment income, foreign-income MAGI adjustments, credits, AMT, state taxes, future loss carryovers, and special rates for collectibles or unrecaptured real-estate depreciation. If you file separately and your spouse itemizes, use your allowable itemized deduction.

Frequently asked questions

What are the 2027 federal 0% capital gains limits?

The top of the 0% bracket is $51,050 for single and married filing separately, $102,100 for joint filers and qualifying surviving spouses, and $68,350 for heads of household. The limit applies to total taxable income, including net long-term gains and qualified dividends.

Do qualified dividends use up my 0% harvesting room?

Yes. Qualified dividends share the 0%, 15%, and 20% brackets with eligible net long-term gains. For a single filer with $43,400 of taxable ordinary income and $1,000 of qualified dividends, $6,650 of the 2027 bracket remains for net long-term gains.

How do short-term losses affect long-term gain?

A net short-term loss first offsets net long-term gain. A $3,000 short-term loss and $10,000 long-term gain leave $7,000 eligible for preferential rates before deductions. If total capital losses exceed gains, up to $3,000 can reduce this year's other income, or $1,500 for married filing separately.

When do the combined 18.8% and 23.8% rates apply?

Those rates apply where the same gain dollars face both the 15% or 20% regular rate and the full 3.8% NIIT. NIIT applies to the smaller of net investment income and modified adjusted gross income above $200,000 for single or head of household, $250,000 for joint or qualifying surviving spouse, or $125,000 for married filing separately.

How do I estimate cash after selling an investment?

Enter net gains or losses for the tax calculation and enter gross sale proceeds for the cash calculation. The cash figure equals entered sale proceeds plus qualified dividends minus the change in modeled federal tax, including NIIT. With sale proceeds blank, the estimate uses net gains and losses plus dividends.
