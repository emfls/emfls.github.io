Key takeaways

An eligible FHSA pairs a deduction today with a tax-free qualifying home withdrawal. An RRSP can build more after-tax wealth when your deduction saves more tax than you expect to pay on withdrawal and you reinvest the refund. A TFSA offers tax-free withdrawals with no OAS clawback impact.

How the estimates work

The model applies federal and provincial brackets to 2026 income, includes basic personal amounts and their federal and Manitoba phase-outs, and applies the Quebec federal abatement. It excludes other tax credits, surtaxes, health premiums and benefit clawbacks. Taxable income stands in for net income in the phase-outs.

Refunds are estimated by subtracting tax after the deduction from tax before it. Reinvested refunds fund a larger same-year deposit while room lasts. This assumes you can bridge the timing of the refund. Unused cash earns no return in the comparison. The projection uses a constant nominal return and a single estimated rate for the entire RRSP withdrawal. Actual withdrawals may span brackets and years.

The allocation fills eligible FHSA room first, then compares the remaining RRSP and TFSA opportunities across tax brackets and room limits. Use the result alongside your need for accessible savings and your home-purchase timeline.

Frequently asked questions

Should I contribute to my FHSA, RRSP or TFSA first?

For a qualifying first-home purchase, an eligible FHSA can combine a deduction today with a tax-free withdrawal. After that, compare the tax saved on each RRSP dollar with the expected withdrawal rate. Use the TFSA for savings you may need before retirement or when the RRSP tax saving is lower.

Why does reinvesting an RRSP refund matter?

An RRSP deduction reduces the cash cost of contributing. Reinvesting the savings lets the same after-tax budget fund a larger deposit when room is available. Spending the refund leaves less invested while the RRSP withdrawal remains taxable.

How much can I contribute in 2026?

The RRSP annual dollar limit is $33,810 before carry-forward room and adjustments. The TFSA adds $7,000 of new room for eligible individuals. The FHSA generally provides $8,000 a year, up to $8,000 of carry-forward and a $40,000 lifetime limit. Use your actual available room after contributions and transfers.

Will withdrawals affect Old Age Security?

Taxable RRSP withdrawals generally increase net income and can trigger the OAS recovery tax. The 2026 income threshold is $95,323, with recovery generally at 15% above that point up to the benefit received. TFSA withdrawals do not increase the OAS recovery tax.

What if I do not buy a qualifying first home?

A non-qualifying FHSA withdrawal is generally taxable. You can generally transfer FHSA funds directly to your RRSP or RRIF without immediate tax or using RRSP room if you have no excess FHSA amount. Plan around the earliest applicable FHSA closure deadline.
