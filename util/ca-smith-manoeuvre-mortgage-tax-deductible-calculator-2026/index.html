See when your mortgage reaches 65% loan-to-value and whether borrowing to invest improves your after-tax wealth.

Key takeaways

  • Above 65% loan-to-value (LTV), principal paid on the excess reduces your debt instead of opening revolving credit. A combined uninsured plan can reach 80% at origination.
  • HELOC interest may be deductible when you can trace the borrowed funds to a qualifying income-producing investment. The mortgage on your home remains personal debt.
  • A larger tax refund can coincide with a lower net worth. The stress tests show how a higher borrowing rate or a weaker portfolio changes the result.

How the comparison works

Both mortgages start with the same balance and scheduled payment. The fixed mortgage rate converts to a monthly rate using (1 + annual rate / 2)^(1/6) − 1. HELOC interest uses the opening balance and annual rate divided by 12. The simulation starts with zero HELOC debt and draws only against principal released below 65%, subject to total room. With interest funding on, available room pays interest before investing.

Eligible interest on directly invested principal generates estimated tax savings at the selected 2026 marginal rate. The model holds that rate constant, including across future years, and omits most credits and bracket effects. It excludes interest on financed interest from the deduction. Each year’s savings are applied immediately at year-end, or at the end of a partial final year. The estimate assumes sufficient mortgage prepayment privileges and lender-approved readvancing. Tax return timing and actual refunds depend on your circumstances.

The model reinvests distributions after taxing them at the ordinary marginal rate. Eligible Canadian dividends may have a different effective rate because of gross-ups and credits. Liquidation taxes half of any positive gain above adjusted cost basis at the estimated marginal rate. It gives no tax value to losses. Net wealth compares the sale proceeds, remaining debts and cumulative difference in cash paid, with no earnings on retained cash. Home value, rates and returns stay constant in each run. Lender restrictions, renewal changes, fees and inflation are outside the projection.

Frequently asked questions

When can I start readvancing under OSFI’s rule?

Payments on the portion of a combined loan above 65% LTV reduce the authorized limit. In the month the mortgage crosses the threshold, only the principal paid below 65% can open revolving room. Your lender may set a lower limit or apply additional credit conditions.

Is Smith Manoeuvre HELOC interest tax-deductible?

Interest can qualify under paragraph 20(1)(c) when you have a legal obligation to pay it and can trace reasonable interest to money borrowed to earn income from business or property. Keep separate borrowing and investment records. Personal expenditures and contributions to registered accounts do not qualify in this model.

Can I borrow from the HELOC to pay its interest?

The model funds interest from available room and charges any shortfall to household cash. Interest on interest has separate tax rules. The model excludes that compound portion from estimated tax savings.

How does Québec limit investment interest deductions?

Québec Schedule N generally limits deductible investment expenses to qualifying investment income. The federal deduction follows federal rules. The estimate uses its own portfolio distributions and any final taxable gain for the Québec cap and excludes carryovers.

Can I pay off my mortgage earlier and still lose money?

Yes. Prepayments can shorten the mortgage while the HELOC remains outstanding. Compare after-tax sale proceeds and cash paid against the remaining debts. A higher HELOC rate or a weaker portfolio can turn an apparent gain into a loss.
