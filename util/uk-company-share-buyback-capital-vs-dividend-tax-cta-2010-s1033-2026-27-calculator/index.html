Key takeaways

Capital treatment can mean 18% Business Asset Disposal Relief (BADR) or ordinary CGT at 18% or 24%. Under default distribution treatment, the excess above originally subscribed capital is generally taxed as dividends at 10.75%, 35.75% or 39.35%. A buyback can also reduce your Personal Allowance.

Delaying a qualifying disposal could add up to £30,000 in CGT

The Budget 2026 proposal would cut the lifetime BADR cap from £1 million to £500,000 for qualifying disposals from 6 April 2027. The maximum applies when the full £500,000 difference moves from 18% to 24%. Check the disposal date and proposed anti-forestalling rules before relying on the 2026/27 cap. HMRC proposal

    Frequently asked questions

    When can a company buyback qualify for capital treatment?

    Under Condition A, an unquoted trading company or qualifying holding company must buy its shares mainly or wholly to benefit the trade. UK residence, the holding period, reduction of interests, post-buyback connection and scheme rules also apply. A separate inheritance-tax route exists.

    Does exactly 30% fail the post-buyback connection test?

    The statutory percentage threshold is more than 30%. Exactly 30% clears that percentage threshold but falls outside the calculator’s strict under-30% screen. Include associates and check ordinary capital, loan capital, voting power and winding-up rights.

    How could the April 2027 BADR proposal add £30,000 to CGT?

    Budget 2026 proposes a £500,000 lifetime cap for qualifying disposals from 6 April 2027. If £500,000 of taxable gain moves from 18% BADR to 24% ordinary CGT, the extra tax is £30,000. Prior claims, unused exemptions and the rate band can change the result.

    How is the default dividend route taxed?

    The distribution is the excess of the purchase price over originally subscribed capital. It is added to existing dividends and can reduce the seller’s Personal Allowance. The remaining proceeds also enter a separate CGT calculation.

    Who pays Stamp Duty on form SH03?

    The company pays Stamp Duty on the aggregate consideration shown on the form. Above £1,000 the usual charge is 0.5%, rounded up to a multiple of £5. Seller net cash in this calculator excludes company duty.
