UK Mixed-Use & Commercial SDLT Calculator

Compare stamp duty on a commercial or qualifying mixed-use purchase with the tax due under residential rates. Include rent when calculating tax on a new lease.

Key takeaways

Commercial and qualifying mixed-use purchases use marginal rates of 0% up to £150,000, 2% to £250,000 and 5% above that. A £600,000 purchase costs £19,500 under Table B, compared with £50,000 for a qualifying additional residential property. A new lease can also trigger a separate charge on the rent’s net present value (NPV).

Frequently asked questions

What counts as mixed-use property for SDLT?

A transaction can qualify when it includes both a dwelling and a genuine non-residential element, such as a shop with a flat above it. A farmhouse with commercially farmed land can qualify depending on the facts. Land forming the dwelling’s garden or grounds is residential.

How does SDLT treat six or more dwellings?

Six or more separate dwellings acquired in a single transaction involving the transfer of a major interest or the grant of a lease are treated as non-residential for SDLT. This is a separate statutory rule from mixed use.

Do the 5% and 2% surcharges apply to Table B transactions?

The residential additional-property and non-UK resident surcharges do not apply to transactions taxed wholly under the ordinary non-residential Table B rules. Special corporate rules can apply to some acquisitions that include a high-value dwelling.

What rent figure do I enter for a commercial lease?

Enter the net present value of the chargeable rent over the new lease, calculated using HMRC’s lease rules. Rent SDLT is calculated separately from premium SDLT, at 0% through £150,000 NPV, 1% to £5 million and 2% above that.

What if HMRC treats the property as residential?

The residential column shows a scenario for the same consideration and the selected buyer profile. If the classification changes, the actual amount also depends on the transaction facts and any reliefs. Budget for a possible shortfall and ask your conveyancer to review the classification before filing.
