Canadian mortgage renewal FAQ

Why do Canadian mortgages compound semi-annually?

Section 6 of the Canadian Interest Act mandates that no interest is recoverable on a mortgage unless the statement indicates the rate of interest and that it is compounded per annum or semi-annually, not in advance. Consequently, Canadian fixed mortgage monthly payments use (1 + r/2)^(1/6) - 1.

What is the expected mortgage payment shock in Canada in 2026?

Borrowers renewing 5-year fixed loans taken out in 2020-2021 at pandemic-era lows (1.7% to 2.4%) into 2026 rates (4.5% to 5.2%) face payment shocks averaging 20% to 35%, increasing monthly outlays by $500 to $900 on typical Canadian home loans.

How does the OSFI straight-switch stress test exemption work?

The Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) removed the Minimum Qualifying Rate (stress test of contract rate + 2%) for straight-switch renewals across federally regulated lenders. If you keep your existing loan balance and remaining amortization unchanged, you can switch to a lower competitor rate without requalifying.

Should I extend my amortization period to reduce my renewal payment?

Extending your amortization (e.g. from 20 years back to 25 or 30 years) suppresses monthly payments, but dramatically increases total lifetime interest paid to the lender. On a $450,000 balance, adding 5 years of amortization adds over $45,000 in interest.

How much does making a lump-sum prepayment before renewal save?

Making a lump-sum prepayment (e.g. $25,000 from savings or TFSA) directly reduces the renewed principal balance, permanently lowering your ongoing monthly payment shock by over $160 per month and saving thousands in compound interest.

Calculation assumptions and methodology

Calculations strictly implement Canadian Interest Act Section 6 semi-annual compounding for fixed-rate Canadian residential mortgages. Property taxes, heating costs, and condo fees are not modeled in P&I amortisation.
