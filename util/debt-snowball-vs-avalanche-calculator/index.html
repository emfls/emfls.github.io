What this estimate includes

  
The monthly budget combines the starting minimum payments on active debts and your extra budget. Each month the calculator adds interest at APR divided by 12, rounded to the nearest cent, then pays each active debt up to its minimum. Any money left goes to the highest-priority remaining debt, with unused payments applied to the next debt that same month.

  
Snowball ranks debts by their smallest balance at the start of each month. Avalanche ranks debts by their highest APR. Ties follow the order you entered the debts. The estimate assumes fixed rates and payments and excludes fees, new charges, and daily interest.

FAQ

  
How do the payoff strategies differ?
Snowball prioritizes clearing smaller balances. Avalanche prioritizes higher-rate balances to reduce interest costs. The comparison shows the interest difference for the payments you entered.

  
What if my minimum payment is lower than monthly interest?
The balance can grow while that debt receives only its minimum. Extra payments and freed minimum payments can still pay it off. The calculator reports an error if a strategy cannot finish within 600 months.




How the Calculation Works & Mathematical Formula
This Debt Snowball vs Debt Avalanche calculator helps you evaluate two strategies for paying off debt: Snowball and Avalanche. Each method prioritizes different debts based on either the balance or the interest rate.
For each debt, you will need to input the following:
  • Name or label: The name or description of the debt.
  • Balance ($): The current amount owed on the debt.
  • APR (%): The annual percentage rate, which represents the cost of borrowing on the debt.
  • Minimum monthly payment ($): The least amount you must pay each month.
Once you have entered your debts, the calculator will process them as follows:
The Snowball Method pays off debts starting from the smallest balance, while the Avalanche Method focuses on the debt with the highest APR to save on interest. 
The formula used for calculating monthly payments includes: Interest = (APR / 100) / 12 * Balance. This interest is added before calculating how much of the payment goes to principal.
Example Calculation:
Consider the following debts:
  • Debt 1: $500 balance, 5% APR, $50 minimum payment.
  • Debt 2: $1,500 balance, 10% APR, $150 minimum payment.
Using the Avalanche method, you would pay off Debt 2 first to minimize interest cost. After entering these details, the calculator will show you how long each method takes to pay off the debts and how much interest you will save.
Decision-making Tips: Consider your emotional and financial situation; the Snowball method may offer motivational wins, while the Avalanche method is mathematically optimal for interest savings.

  

    
Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

    

      

        What is the main difference between the Snowball and Avalanche methods?
        
The Snowball method prioritizes paying off the smallest debts first for quick wins, while the Avalanche method focuses on the highest interest debts to minimize total interest paid over time.

      

      

        How does the calculator determine the payment order?
        
The calculator ranks debts based on the selected strategy. For Snowball, it sorts by the smallest balance. For Avalanche, it sorts by the highest APR, applying any remaining budget towards these debts accordingly.

      

      

        Can I pay less than the minimum payment on a debt?
        
If your payment falls below the minimum, the balance may grow due to accruing interest. However, extra payments on other debts will still help pay them off faster in the long term.
