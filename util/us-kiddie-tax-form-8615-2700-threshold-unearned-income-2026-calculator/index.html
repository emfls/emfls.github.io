Compare a child’s separate return with the Form 8814 parent election. Check how a planned UTMA or UGMA sale changes federal tax.

Key takeaways

With no wages, the dependent deduction covers the first $1,350 of unearned income. Through $2,700, taxable ordinary income starts at 10% and qualifying long-term gains and dividends can be taxed at 0% on a separate return. Above $2,700, Form 8615 can apply the parent’s rates. A qualified gain from selling shares can use the remaining 0% room and generally rules out Form 8814.

Filing eligibility

The age test covers children under 18, age 18 whose earned income is at most half their support, and full-time students ages 19–23 whose earned income is at most half their support. Those tests use age at year-end.

Check Form 8814 eligibility

The child must otherwise be required to file and be under 19, or under 24 and a full-time student. Only interest and dividends, including eligible fund capital gain distributions, qualify. The child must have filed no joint return and had no federal withholding, estimated payments or prior-year overpayment applied. The electing parent must be the qualifying parent under the IRS instructions.

A year-end distribution can use up your gain room

Include expected interest, dividends, and fund distributions through December 31 before selling. For example, $1,600 of existing unearned income plus a later $300 distribution leaves $800 of room under $2,700. Selling for a $1,100 gain before counting that distribution puts $300 above the threshold. A separate return can still owe tax on existing ordinary income, and state taxes can apply to the sale.

Selling an appreciated investment realizes the gain. If you buy it back, the purchase price generally becomes the basis of the new shares for a later sale.

Frequently asked questions

How do the $1,350 and $2,700 thresholds work?

With no earned income and the dependent standard deduction, the first $1,350 of unearned income is covered. In the next $1,350, ordinary income starts at 10% and eligible gains and qualified dividends can fall in the 0% bracket. Above $2,700, Form 8615 can apply the parent's rates to net unearned income, limited to the child's taxable income.

Who generally needs Form 8615?

The child must have over $2,700 of unearned income, be required to file, have a living parent at year-end and file without a spouse. The child must also be under 18, age 18 with earned income at most half of support, or a full-time student age 19 through 23 with earned income at most half of support.

Can a parent use Form 8814 after selling investments in a UTMA?

A child's own sale of shares that generates a gain generally disqualifies the election. Eligible income consists of interest and dividends, including fund capital gain distributions. The child's gross income must exceed $1,350 and be under $13,500, and the parent and child must meet the remaining IRS eligibility rules.

Can Form 8814 increase the parent's tax?

Yes. Form 8814 applies 10% to up to $1,350 above the initial $1,350, even if the child would have paid 0% on qualified income. The amount included in the parent's adjusted gross income can also reduce income-based credits or deductions and may increase net investment income tax by up to 3.8% of that added investment income in the calculator's scenario.

How much long-term gain can I harvest in a UTMA or UGMA?

With no earned income, subtract existing unearned income from $2,700 and stop at zero for the remaining gain room under that threshold. This estimates how much eligible long-term gain can be added without increasing regular federal income tax in the modeled separate return. With wages, the child's 0% bracket can further limit the room. Use realized gain after basis rather than gross sale proceeds.
