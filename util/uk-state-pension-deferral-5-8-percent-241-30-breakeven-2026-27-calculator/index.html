Key takeaways

Deferring from age 66 to 67 breaks even at age 84.31 before tax, or 79.98 if your pension is taxed at 40% while working and 20% after retiring. In this example you give up £12,547.60 gross to gain about £13.94 a week for life. An ISA return can push the break-even age later or prevent a crossover.

What you give up by waiting

The higher weekly payment starts only after the delay. If you die before the break-even age, you may receive less in total, and the missed payments are unavailable for spending during the delay. Some benefits can also prevent you from building up extra pension. Weigh that loss of cash flow against the value of a higher lifetime income.

How the comparison works

Before tax, divide the total missed payments by the annual uplift and add that time to the delayed claim age. After tax, apply the working rate to the missed payments and the retirement rate to the extra pension. Personal Allowance, income that spans tax bands and means-tested benefits require a separate calculation.

For the ISA comparison, the early claim invests the missed after-tax payments at the end of each modelled week. After the delayed claim starts, the pensions' common base payments cancel and the extra pension is invested at the same rate. The break-even age interpolates between weeks. This assumes enough unused ISA allowance and a steady real return. Actual investments can fall in value.

All amounts are in 2026/27 pounds, with the base and uplift held flat in real terms. Future triple-lock increases above CPI can change the result. The model uses 52 weeks per year, assumes all deferred weeks are eligible and excludes backdating. The estimate uses qualifying years divided by 35. Transitional rules, including pre-2016 contracting out, can change your amount. Check your State Pension forecast and official pension age before relying on those inputs.

Frequently asked questions

How much extra State Pension does a one-year delay buy?

A 52-week eligible delay adds about 5.78%, or £13.94 per week on the £241.30 full rate, at constant 2026/27 values. Once you meet the nine-week minimum, each eligible week earns one-ninth of 1%.

How long does it take to break even if I defer for one year?

In the constant-rate model, it takes 17.31 years after the delayed payments start to recover the missed gross pension. If you are eligible at 66 and start at 67, that is age 84.31.

How does falling from 40% tax to 20% affect the result?

Each pound of pension missed while working would have paid 60p after tax. Each pound of extra pension in retirement pays 80p. With one year of deferral from age 66, that moves break-even from age 84.31 to 79.98 in this model.

What happens if I claim at pension age and invest in a Stocks and Shares ISA?

The missed after-tax payments could have built an ISA balance. The calculator grows that balance and the later extra payments at the same assumed real return. If growth on the starting ISA balance covers the entire weekly uplift, deferring never catches up at that return.

Can I take a lump sum if I defer the new State Pension?

The old interest-bearing deferral lump sum is unavailable under the new State Pension rules. You can generally backdate up to 12 months and receive arrears. Only the remaining eligible weeks count towards any uplift.
