How the estimate works

The standard Personal Allowance is £12,570. The 20% basic band covers £37,700 of taxable income (£50,270 of gross income with the full allowance). The 40% rate applies above that band up to £125,140 of taxable income, and 45% applies above £125,140. The taper rounds the allowance withdrawn down to a whole pound. Above £125,140, the extra income is taxed at 45%, so the tool calculates tax on both sides of the contribution. Check pension relief eligibility and available annual allowance before contributing.

Frequently asked questions

Is 60% a separate Income Tax rate?

No. In the taper band, the 40% higher rate combines with the withdrawal of Personal Allowance to produce an effective 60% Income Tax rate. Standard employee National Insurance can add 2% on these earnings, for about 62% combined. The displayed net pay pension estimate excludes NI savings.

How do I recover my full Personal Allowance?

Under the model assumptions, make a qualifying gross pension contribution equal to the amount by which your adjusted net income exceeds £100,000. Confirm how your pension takes contributions and avoid subtracting deductions already included in your pay figure.

Will the contribution restore childcare eligibility?

Tax-Free Childcare and England’s Free Childcare for Working Parents normally exclude a household if either partner’s expected adjusted net income exceeds £100,000. Getting to £100,000 can satisfy the income ceiling for that person. Other eligibility rules still apply.
