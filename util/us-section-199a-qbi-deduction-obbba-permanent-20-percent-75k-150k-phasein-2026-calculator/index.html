How much deduction could you lose as income rises?

A single filer with $100,000 in QBI from an SSTB, $20,000 in allocable wages, and $200,000 in qualified property basis could see the deduction drop from $20,000 at the $201,750 threshold to $7,500 at $239,250, then reach $0 at $276,750. Run both income scenarios before relying on the deduction for estimated payments.

Frequently asked questions

What changed for the QBI deduction in 2026?

OBBBA made Section 199A permanent at up to 20% of QBI. It expanded the phase-in window to $75,000, or $150,000 on a joint return, and added a $400 minimum for eligible taxpayers with active QBI.

How does the W-2 wage and property limit work?

The limit is the greater of 50% of allocable W-2 wages or 25% of those wages plus 2.5% of qualified property UBIA. In the phase-in range, only the excess of the tentative deduction over that limit is reduced in proportion to how far income is into the range.

How does the SSTB phase-out work?

Within the phase-in range, subtract the phase-in percentage from 100% and multiply SSTB QBI, wages, and UBIA by that percentage before applying the wage and property limit. At the upper limit and beyond, that SSTB contributes zero.

Who qualifies for the $400 active QBI minimum?

For 2026, taxpayers with at least $1,000 of eligible aggregate QBI from businesses in which they materially participate can claim the greater of the regular deduction or $400. Passive income cannot qualify for the minimum.

Which income determines my phase-in status?

Use your return-level taxable income before the Section 199A deduction and the Section 68 itemized-deduction limitation. Net capital gain and qualified dividends reduce the base for the separate overall deduction cap.
