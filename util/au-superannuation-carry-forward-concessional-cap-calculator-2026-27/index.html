How the tax estimate works

We compare progressive resident income tax and the full 2% Medicare levy before and after the deductible contribution, then deduct 15% fund tax and any increase in Division 293 tax. Division 293 uses taxable income after the deduction plus eligible concessional contributions. The deduction reduces taxable income as it increases concessional contributions.

The estimate assumes a full-year Australian resident and applies the standard levy to all taxable income. Lower incomes, family circumstances, and exemptions can reduce the levy. Tax offsets, the Medicare levy surcharge, other Division 293 income adjustments, and excess contributions assessments are outside this estimate.

To claim a personal deduction, check your eligibility and give your fund a valid notice of intent. Obtain its acknowledgement before claiming. ATO personal super contribution guidance

Frequently asked questions

Who can use carry-forward concessional cap in 2026–27?

Your total super balance across all funds must be below $500,000 on 30 June 2026, and you must have unused cap from one or more of the previous five financial years. Exactly $500,000 fails the balance test. The general $32,500 cap still applies.

Which unused super cap expires on 30 June 2027?

Any remaining 2021–22 unused cap expires after 30 June 2027. Your fund generally needs to receive the contribution by 30 June for it to count that year. Check the fund’s processing cutoff and the remaining balance in ATO online services.

Which super contributions use the cap, and in what order?

Employer super, salary sacrifice and eligible personal contributions you claim as a deduction all count across your funds. The current $32,500 cap is used first. Eligible amounts above it draw on the oldest available cap first, starting with 2021–22.

How does Division 293 affect tax savings?

The rate is 15% of the smaller of eligible concessional contributions and the amount above the $250,000 combined income and contributions threshold. The estimate compares Division 293 before and after the proposed deduction. ATO income adjustments can change the assessed result.

What happens if I exceed my available concessional cap?

The ATO generally adds excess concessional contributions to your assessable income and applies a 15% tax offset for fund tax already paid. This calculator flags the excess and limits its tax comparison to the deductible amount within your cap.
