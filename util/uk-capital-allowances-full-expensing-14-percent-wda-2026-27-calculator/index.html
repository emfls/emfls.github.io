Key takeaways

Companies can deduct 100% upfront on qualifying new main-rate equipment under full expensing, with no cap. The £1 million AIA can cover qualifying new and used equipment for companies, sole traders and partnerships made up entirely of individuals. Above your available AIA, a first-year allowance or writing-down allowance may apply.

Budget for tax when you sell

Spending all of the upfront tax saving can leave a cash shortfall at disposal. If you fully expense an £85,000 machine and its capital allowances disposal value is £30,000, that £30,000 is added to taxable profits. At the same 25% marginal rate, that means £7,500 of extra tax. Frequently asked questions

When should a company use full expensing or AIA?

Both can give a 100% deduction. Full expensing preserves AIA for other eligible purchases such as used equipment. Compare disposal treatment if you expect to sell the asset.

Can I claim AIA and a first-year allowance on one purchase?

Yes, on different portions of eligible expenditure. If you claim a 40% or 50% first-year allowance, the unrelieved balance of that portion can go into its pool from the following chargeable period.

How do the 14% and 18% comparisons work?

The two columns hold the upfront claim and tax rate constant and change only the main-pool writing-down rate. The special pool keeps its 6% rate. The separate projection can include the proposed 12% main-pool rate from April 2027.

Which business cars qualify for capital allowances?

A qualifying new zero-emission car can get a 100% first-year allowance through 31 March 2027 for Corporation Tax or 5 April 2027 for Income Tax. Used electric cars and other cars at 50 g/km or less use the main pool. Cars above 50 g/km use the special pool. Cars are excluded from AIA.

What happens if I sell an asset after full expensing?

A company that fully expensed the entire cost generally adds the full capital allowances disposal value to taxable profits as a balancing charge. Partial full expensing and the 50% special-rate first-year allowance use a proportion of the disposal value.
