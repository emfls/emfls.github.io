See how regular contributions, conversions and earnings cover your withdrawal. Estimate federal income tax, any 10% additional tax and whether Form 8606 Part III applies.

Key takeaways

  • Regular contributions come out first, tax- and penalty-free. Conversions follow by year, with earnings last.
  • Two five-year clocks apply. The first Roth tax year governs earnings qualification. Each conversion tax year has its own recapture clock.
  • Age 59½ ends the early-distribution penalty. Earnings can still be taxable until the account clock clears.

    Frequently asked questions

    What comes out of a Roth IRA first?

    Remaining regular contributions across your own Roth IRAs come out first. Conversions and eligible rollovers follow, oldest calendar year first, with the previously taxable portion ahead of nontaxable basis in each year. Earnings come out last.

    When do the two five-year clocks end?

    Each conversion clock starts January 1 of the conversion year. The earnings clock starts January 1 of the first tax year for which you funded a Roth IRA. Both clear on January 1 of the starting year plus five. A 2022 clock clears January 1, 2027.

    Can I owe a penalty on a conversion I already paid tax on?

    Yes. Before age 59½ and within the conversion’s five-year period, the previously taxable portion withdrawn can trigger the 10% additional tax unless an exception applies. The conversion principal does not generate ordinary income again.

    How does the first-time homebuyer exception work?

    The lifetime cap is $10,000 in qualifying distributions, reduced by prior qualifying amounts. After the account’s five-year period, Form 8606 subtracts the qualified home amount before applying contribution and conversion basis. Before that period ends, the exception can reduce the additional tax, but withdrawn earnings remain taxable.

    Do I report a withdrawal that only returns contribution basis?

    Generally yes on Form 8606 Part III if the distribution is nonqualified. A distribution due to disability or made at age 59½ or older after the account clock clears is generally excluded from Part III. Review Form 5329 separately if an additional tax or exception applies.
