Frequently asked questions

Who qualifies for the $6,000 senior deduction in 2026?

Each claimant needs a valid Social Security number and generally must have been born before January 2, 1962. Married taxpayers must file jointly. On a joint return, each spouse qualifies separately, so the maximum is $6,000 for one eligible spouse or $12,000 for two.

Can I claim the senior deduction if I itemize?

Yes. The enhanced senior deduction is claimed on Schedule 1-A and can be added to either the standard deduction or eligible itemized deductions. The traditional age or blindness addition applies only when taking the standard deduction.

At what income does the senior deduction phase out?

The deduction drops by $60 per $1,000 of MAGI above $75,000 for a qualifying non-joint return or $150,000 for a joint return, per eligible senior. It reaches zero at $175,000 or $250,000 respectively, including when two spouses qualify.

How much income can the combined deduction cover?

With the full bonus and no blindness additions, the deductions can shelter $24,150 of ordinary AGI for a single eligible senior or $47,500 for two eligible spouses filing jointly. These examples estimate regular federal income tax before credits using the listed deductions.

How does a Roth conversion or QCD affect the phase-out?

The taxable portion of a Roth conversion generally increases AGI and can shrink the senior deduction. An eligible, excludable qualified charitable distribution from an IRA at age 70½ or older can keep MAGI lower than receiving the same amount as a taxable distribution.
