Understanding the Bill C-15 Canadian SR&ED Tax Reform (2026 Guide)

The Scientific Research and Experimental Development (SR&ED) tax incentive is Canada’s cornerstone federal research funding program, delivering billions annually in direct tax credits. Through landmark legislation enacted under Bill C-15, the Canadian government modernized the program to supercharge scaling domestic tech companies and high-growth innovators.

1. Doubling the Enhanced Limit to $6,000,000

For decades, Canadian-Controlled Private Corporations (CCPCs) were restricted to an enhanced expenditure limit of $3,000,000. Bill C-15 doubled this threshold to $6,000,000. Small-to-medium Canadian tech firms can now earn up to $2,100,000 in pure cash refunds (at the 35% refundable rate) every fiscal year, providing massive non-dilutive liquidity to scale software, biotechnology, clean energy, and advanced manufacturing initiatives.

2. Expanded Taxable Capital Phase-Out ($15M to $75M)

Under prior rules, fast-growing tech companies faced a severe "cliff-edge" between $10 million and $50 million of taxable capital. Bill C-15 expanded this buffer dramatically:

  • Full 100% Limit ($6M): Preserved for corporations with taxable capital under $15,000,000.
  • Gradual Phase-Out: The expenditure limit scales down linearly between $15 million and $75 million of taxable capital.
  • General Corporate Rate: Once taxable capital exceeds $75 million, the company receives the 15% non-refundable basic rate.

3. The Prescribed Proxy Method (55% Overhead Multiplier)

Rather than tracking and auditing individual utility receipts, office cleaning, and administrative overhead, the CRA permits claimants to utilize the Prescribed Proxy Amount (PPA). Under this statutory method, an automatic 55% overhead factor is added directly to all eligible Canadian R&D employee salaries, drastically increasing your qualified claim pool.
