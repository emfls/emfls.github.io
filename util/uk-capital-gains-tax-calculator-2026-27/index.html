How the estimate works

Planning estimate for individuals with disposals from 6 April 2026 to 5 April 2027 and a full Annual Exempt Amount. Enter gains after asset costs and any disposal-specific relief, including Private Residence Relief if applicable. This estimate excludes trusts, carried interest, prior-year losses, special allowances and basic-rate band extensions. Use taxable income mode when you know taxable income after allowances. It applies the standard £37,700 band for CGT and is for planning only.

With the full £12,570 Personal Allowance, gross income of £50,270 uses the entire £37,700 basic band. The allowance falls by £1 for every £2 of adjusted net income over £100,000 and reaches zero at £125,140. Any unused basic band can take gains at 18%. Gains above it generally face 24%.

Relieved BADR gains are taxed at 18% from 6 April 2026 and use the basic band before other gains. The model applies the annual exemption to the highest-rate gains first. For 2026/27, the BADR lifetime limit is £1 million of qualifying gains. Budget 2026 proposes a reduction to £500,000 for qualifying disposals from 6 April 2027, subject to legislation.

Frequently asked questions

Do shares and residential property use different CGT rates in 2026/27?

Individuals generally pay 18% within their available basic band and 24% above it for both types of asset. Property-specific relief can affect the underlying gain.

How much can I deduct as the Annual Exempt Amount?

Eligible individuals can deduct up to £3,000. Most trustees have a £1,500 allowance. This estimate is for individuals.

Does BADR apply to ordinary investment share sales?

BADR has business ownership and other eligibility requirements. Enter a qualifying amount only after checking eligibility and accounting for prior claims and any losses allocated to that gain.
