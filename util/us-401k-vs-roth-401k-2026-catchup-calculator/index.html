Compare what you could keep after taxes. See how the SECURE 2.0 super catch-up, Roth wage rule and employer match affect your retirement savings.

Key takeaways

  • The 2026 employee maximum is $24,500 under age 50, $32,500 at ages 50–59 and 64+, and $35,750 at ages 60–63 when your plan allows catch-ups.
  • At age 50 or older, 2025 FICA wages above $150,000 from your plan sponsor make any 2026 catch-up Roth. Your regular deferral can still be pre-tax.
  • Traditional can leave you more after tax when your retirement tax rate is below your break-even rate and you invest the tax savings. Roth withdrawals are tax-free when qualified.

How the comparison works

Same annual cash budget

Every strategy makes the same gross employee deferral. The Roth deferral sets the common cash budget, plus any current income tax on an elected Roth match. Traditional deferrals free up cash equal to the pre-tax contribution times your current marginal income tax rate. That cash is either invested in a taxable brokerage or counted as current spending.

Employer match and taxes

The estimate applies the match rate to the lesser of your regular deferral and the eligible share of pay, then caps it at remaining §415(c) room. It assumes a single plan offering both Roth and catch-up contributions, with employer match on regular deferrals only. Other annual additions and plan-specific limits can reduce available room. Employer match defaults to pre-tax. A Roth election generally includes the vested match in current taxable income.

Growth and break-even

The projection repeats your 2026 contribution amounts at the end of each year from a zero starting balance. It uses a constant return and flat tax rates, assumes qualified Roth withdrawals, taxes pre-tax balances at your future rate and taxes positive brokerage gains at retirement. It omits annual brokerage tax drag, loss deductions, fees, penalties, and changes to your age-based limits or future law. With flat rates, each optional pre-tax dollar has the same marginal result. The optimal strategy uses the better tax treatment for optional dollars and routes mandatory catch-up to Roth. At a tie it splits optional dollars evenly. The break-even rate is where the Traditional-first and all-Roth projections tie.

Frequently asked questions

Who qualifies for the 2026 super catch-up?

If you turn 60, 61, 62 or 63 in 2026 and your 401(k) plan permits catch-ups, you can contribute up to $11,250 above the $24,500 regular limit. Employees aged 50–59 or 64 and older have an $8,000 catch-up limit.

When must my catch-up contributions be Roth?

If you are catch-up eligible and your 2025 FICA wages from the employer sponsoring your plan exceeded $150,000, any 2026 catch-up you make must be Roth. Exactly $150,000 does not trigger this rule. You may still make pre-tax regular deferrals.

Does an employer match count toward my $24,500 deferral limit?

Employer contributions do not use your personal elective deferral limit. They count toward the overall annual additions limit, generally the lesser of $72,000 or 100% of compensation for 2026. Eligible catch-up contributions are excluded from annual additions.

When are Roth 401(k) withdrawals tax-free, and are there lifetime RMDs?

Roth 401(k) withdrawals are generally qualified after the five-tax-year requirement and when made at age 59½ or older, after disability, or after death. Roth 401(k) balances have no required minimum distributions during the account owner's lifetime. Beneficiary rules still apply.

How does investing the Traditional tax savings change the break-even rate?

Investing the tax savings increases the after-tax wealth of the Traditional-first strategy. The calculator adds brokerage proceeds after tax on gains. When the tax savings are spent, the brokerage contribution is zero and the two strategies tie at a 0% future withdrawal tax rate if there are eligible pre-tax deferrals.
