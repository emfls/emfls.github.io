How the Calculation Works & Mathematical Formula

The Text Diff Comparison Tool works by evaluating two separate text inputs: the Left Text and the Right Text. The tool compares each character and word in both inputs to identify and highlight:

  • Added text (present in the Right Text but not in the Left Text)
  • Deleted text (present in the Left Text but not in the Right Text)
  • Unchanged text (identical in both inputs)

The primary mathematical operation here is a comparison algorithm, typically a variation of the Levenshtein Distance, which measures the difference between two sequences. The formula can be expressed as:

Distance(A, B) = d[m][n]

Where:

  • A = Left Text
  • B = Right Text
  • d[m][n] = minimum number of single-character edits needed to change one word into the other

Example:

Let's say:

Left Text: "Hello World!"

Right Text: "Hello Universe!"

Step 1: The tool evaluates both texts for differences.

Step 2: It identifies that:

  • "World" is replaced with "Universe" (highlighted red as deleted text)
  • "Universe" is newly added (highlighted green as added text)

Final Result: The word differences will be highlighted accordingly, helping users see exactly what has changed.

Tips & Limitations:

While this tool is great for quickly comparing text, it may not handle complex formats such as rich text or embedded links optimally. Make sure to always verify important outcomes before making decisions based on the tool's output.
