IRS Section 1244 Stock Loss Calculator 2026

Compare the $50,000 or $100,000 ordinary-loss limit with standard Schedule D treatment for a startup stock loss.

Key takeaways

Qualifying stock losses can offset taxable wages and other ordinary income up to $50,000 per year or $100,000 on a joint return. Capital losses first offset capital gains dollar for dollar. After that, the annual deduction against other income is generally $3,000, or $1,500 if married filing separately.

Five-point Section 1244 eligibility check

  1. Partnership interests and interests in LLCs taxed as partnerships or disregarded entities do not meet this stock requirement.

  2. Exclude services, stock or securities exchanged for the shares, and secondary purchases.

  3. Include earlier and current issuances, capital contributions and paid-in surplus. Value contributed property at the issuer’s adjusted basis less associated liabilities. Shares from the threshold year may require designation or pro-rata allocation.

  4. Use “Yes” for an individual original holder or an individual who was a partner when the partnership acquired the original shares and remained a partner through the loss. Gifts and inheritances generally lose this treatment. Trusts, estates and corporate holders do not qualify.

  5. Use the five tax years ending before the loss, or the shorter statutory period for a newer corporation. Exclude royalties, rents, dividends, interest, annuities and gains from stock or securities from the qualifying share of receipts. The deduction exception excludes amounts under §§172, 243 and 245.

Frequently asked questions

Can Section 1244 losses reduce taxable wages?

Yes. A qualifying ordinary loss can offset ordinary income such as wages, up to $50,000 per year or $100,000 on a joint return, even when only one spouse owned the shares.

How is the $1 million issuance limit measured?

Count money and qualifying property received for the current and prior shares, capital contributions, and paid-in surplus. In the first tax year the issuer exceeds $1 million, check the designation of qualifying shares or the regulatory pro-rata allocation.

How long can a standard $100,000 capital loss take to use?

With no capital gains and enough taxable income, $100,000 represents about 33.3 years of $3,000 deduction capacity. The last $1,000 is used on the 34th return. Married filing separately generally has a $1,500 annual limit instead.

Where do I report a Section 1244 loss?

Report the eligible ordinary portion on Form 4797, Part II, line 10 and attach the loss computation. Report the capital portion on Form 8949 as applicable and Schedule D. Keep records of issuance, basis and qualifying shares.

Can I split a sale across December and January?

Two bona fide sales in separate tax years can use two annual limits when the shares sold in each year qualify. A wholly worthless lot is tied to the year it became worthless. For example, two $80,000 losses on still-saleable qualifying stock could fit within two $100,000 joint limits.
