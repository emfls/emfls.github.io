Canada Swap ETF vs Dividend ETF After-Tax Calculator (2026)

Compare what you keep after fees, annual distribution tax and the final sale in a personal non-registered or corporate account.

Key takeaways

A zero-distribution swap ETF can defer investor-level tax until sale under this scenario. It comes out ahead only when that deferral outweighs the extra costs and sale tax. A distributing ETF reinvests its after-tax cash distributions, increasing its adjusted cost base.

In a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC), deferring distributions can preserve the federal small business limit during the holding period. The taxable gain can still reduce the limit after a sale.

What the projection assumes

Each year the opening balance earns the entered gross total return, less annual fees. The regular ETF pays the entered yield on the opening balance, capped at available fund value in extreme loss scenarios. The after-tax cash is reinvested and added to adjusted cost base (ACB). New contributions are added at year-end before hypothetical liquidation. The swap scenario pays zero annual distributions.

Rates and OAS inputs stay constant across the projection. The calculator assumes one distribution type and excludes return of capital, capital gains distributions, trading costs, inflation and tax benefits from capital losses. CCPC results exclude RDTOH refunds, shareholder-level withdrawals, and the cash tax effect of a change in the small business limit. Actual limit reductions depend on the corporate group’s prior-period AAII, taxable capital and business-limit allocation.

Frequently asked questions

When does a corporate-class swap ETF beat a distributing ETF?

It can pull ahead when the compounding benefit from deferring distribution tax outweighs its higher fees and the tax due at sale. This calculator compares after-tax proceeds at each year-end and reports the first year the swap ETF leads by at least one cent.

How does the calculator prevent double taxation of reinvested distributions?

It adds each net reinvested distribution and each contribution to adjusted cost base. At sale, half of a positive gain enters taxable income at the applicable rate. A capital loss gives no immediate tax benefit in this model.

How do eligible Canadian dividends affect OAS recovery tax?

An eligible dividend increases the income used for recovery by 138% of the cash dividend. The 2026 recovery rate is 15% above $95,323, capped by your OAS benefit. With the OAS option enabled, the model also applies recovery to the taxable portion of the gain in the sale year.

Can a swap ETF preserve a CCPC’s small business limit?

A swap ETF with zero distributions adds no annual ETF income to AAII while held. A realized taxable gain can still increase AAII in the sale year. Under the federal passive-income rule, associated-group AAII above $50,000 reduces the next applicable business limit by $5 per dollar, up to $500,000.

Does corporate after-tax wealth include refunds or cash paid to shareholders?

The comparison shows cash inside the corporation after upfront tax, before any refundable dividend tax on hand (RDTOH) refund or shareholder tax. The non-taxable portion of a realized gain may increase the capital dividend account (CDA), which can support an elected tax-free capital dividend to eligible shareholders.
