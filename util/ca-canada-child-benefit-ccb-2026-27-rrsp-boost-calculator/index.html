Canada Child Benefit Calculator

Compare your tax-free CCB before and after a 2025 RRSP or FHSA deduction, then see how much the deduction could save in income tax.

Key takeaways

  • The maximum annual CCB is $8,157 per child under 6 and $6,883 per child aged 6 to 17.
  • CCB starts to fall above $38,237 of 2025 adjusted family net income. A second set of reduction rates applies to income above $82,847.
  • At $75,000 of family income with three eligible children, a $5,000 deduction could add $950 in annual CCB and save $1,500 in income tax at a 30% tax rate.

The calculator carries the exact reduction at $82,847 into the second band. The CRA publishes whole-dollar bases above that threshold ($3,123, $6,022, $8,476 and $10,260), so estimates can differ by up to $0.35 annually from that rounding alone.

To estimate the annual benefit, subtract both bands’ reductions from the age-based maximum, with a floor of zero. The monthly estimate divides that amount by 12. The deduction lowers the modelled AFNI dollar for dollar down to zero.

Frequently asked questions

Which income year sets my July 2026 to June 2027 CCB?

The CRA uses 2025 adjusted family net income. Start with line 23600 for you and your spouse or common-law partner, if applicable, then apply the CRA adjustments for UCCB and RDSP income and repayments if they apply to your household.

Can a new contribution increase my CCB for this period?

Only an eligible deduction claimed for 2025 affects the income used here. Eligible early-2026 RRSP contributions made by March 2, 2026 can qualify for the 2025 deduction. FHSA contributions made in 2026 first qualify for a deduction in 2026.

Why can the boost be smaller than the published clawback rate?

The calculator compares two benefit amounts. A deduction can span two income bands, reach the full benefit at $38,237, or leave income high enough that the benefit is still zero. Each portion of the deduction can therefore have a different effect.

What does the combined savings percentage measure?

It divides the estimated income tax savings plus the extra annual federal CCB by the deduction. A $5,000 deduction at a 30% tax rate with three eligible children and $75,000 of starting AFNI saves an estimated $1,500 in tax and adds $950 in annual CCB, or 49% of the deduction. This measures cash-flow effects before future withdrawal taxes and investment performance.

Will my CRA monthly payment match the estimate?

The monthly figure is an average across 12 months with the same eligible children and age bands. Birthdays, custody changes and CRA rounding can affect payments. CRA generally issues a single July payment when the total annual benefit is below $240. The estimate covers the federal base CCB and excludes disability and provincial or territorial benefits.
