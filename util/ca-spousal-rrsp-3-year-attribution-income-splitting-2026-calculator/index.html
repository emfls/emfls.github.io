Key takeaways

A one-day shift across New Year’s can delay an attribution-free withdrawal by a full calendar year. The CRA checks contributions in the withdrawal year and the two preceding calendar years. Both examples below can qualify for a 2026 deduction if you have room.

Early withdrawal can erase the rate benefit. On a C$20,000 withdrawal, attributing the full amount to a contributor at 43% instead of an annuitant at 25% adds C$3,600 in estimated family income tax. A later spousal contribution can move the clearance date again.

How the estimate works

The calculator multiplies the deductible contribution by your contributor rate. The tax saved through splitting equals the amount taxable to the annuitant multiplied by the difference between your rates, compared with paying contributor-rate tax on the whole withdrawal. The extra tax from attribution applies the same rate difference to the contributor’s portion, compared with taxing the entire withdrawal to the annuitant.

This models one withdrawal type at a time and assumes none of the listed contributions were previously attributed. If you have prior attribution, mixed RRSP/RRIF withdrawals, or a statutory exception such as separation or non-residency, use Form T2205 and the CRA guidance. The deduction benefit estimates the reduction in tax, and the cash refund depends on the full return. Estimates use constant rates and exclude withholding, benefit changes, and tax on excess contributions.

Spousal RRSP FAQ

Can a February 2027 contribution count for 2026?

Yes. A contribution from January 1 through March 1, 2027 can qualify for a 2026 deduction if the contributor has remaining room. For attribution, the contribution was made in 2027. With no later contributions, the window clears on January 1, 2030.

Why does a December 31, 2026 contribution clear on January 1, 2029?

A withdrawal in 2029 looks back to calendar years 2027, 2028 and 2029. A December 31, 2026 contribution falls outside that window. A contribution in any of those later years can make some or all of the withdrawal attributable.

What if we contribute to several spousal RRSPs?

Count this contributor’s spousal contributions across the annuitant’s plans for the withdrawal year and the prior two calendar years. A contribution later in the withdrawal year can count. Form T2205 accounts for contributions already attributed in an earlier year.

How much can I deduct for a 2026 spousal RRSP contribution?

The deduction uses the contributor’s available RRSP deduction room. The 2026 dollar cap on new room is C$33,810. Your actual limit can be lower because of income or pension adjustments, or higher because of unused room.

Does converting to a spousal RRIF avoid attribution?

The required annual minimum stays taxable to the annuitant. Payments above the minimum can still be attributed to the contributor. The minimum is zero in the year the RRIF is established.
