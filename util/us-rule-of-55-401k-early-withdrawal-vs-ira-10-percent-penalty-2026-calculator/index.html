IRS Rule of 55 Calculator 2026

Compare keeping your 401(k) or 403(b), rolling it to an IRA, or moving eligible old savings into the plan before you leave.

Key takeaways

If you leave in or after the calendar year you turn 55, eligible withdrawals from that employer’s plan can avoid the 10% federal additional tax. The threshold can be age 50 or 25 years of credited plan service for qualifying public safety workers. Ordinary income tax still applies to taxable withdrawals.

A direct rollover to a traditional IRA can preserve tax deferral, but later IRA withdrawals lose the separation exception. Eligible pre-tax savings rolled into the separating employer’s plan before you leave can increase the balance available for the exception if the plan accepts the rollover and permits the withdrawals.

What an IRA rollover could cost you

Before authorizing a rollover, check how much you will need before age 59½ and whether your plan allows partial withdrawals. The direct rollover itself generally preserves tax deferral. A later taxable IRA withdrawal can incur the 10% additional tax unless a separate IRA exception applies.

How this estimate works

The calculator prorates gross withdrawals by whole months through age 59½ using your birthday offset. It spends the separating plan first and then draws from the outside balance. The roll-in scenario assumes all entered outside dollars are eligible pre-tax funds, the plan accepts them before separation, and the plan permits access to the combined balance. An old employer’s plan may have its own separation exception based on when you left that employer. Enter outside funds assuming they have no separate exception.

All modeled withdrawals are fully taxable. The estimate holds balances and the marginal rate constant and excludes investment returns, fees, other exceptions, and state-specific distribution or penalty rules. The standard age input covers ages 50–59. Select public safety to model a separation before 50 under the 25-year service rule.

Frequently asked questions

Can I leave in January at age 54 if I turn 55 in December?

Yes. If you separate in the calendar year you turn 55, eligible distributions from that employer’s plan after separation can qualify even before your birthday. A separation in the preceding year does not qualify based on that later birthday.

Who can use the age 50 or 25-year public safety rule?

Qualified public safety employees with eligible governmental plans and covered private-sector firefighters can use the earlier of age 50 or 25 years of service under the plan. The service test uses credited plan service, which can differ from total years in the profession.

Does moving my 401(k) into a traditional IRA immediately cost 10%?

A properly completed direct rollover of pre-tax money to a traditional IRA generally preserves tax deferral. The separation exception is unavailable on later IRA withdrawals. Before age 59½, taxable IRA withdrawals generally incur the 10% additional tax unless another exception applies.

Can I roll an old 401(k) or traditional IRA into my current plan?

Ask the current plan administrator which eligible funds it accepts and what withdrawal schedule it offers after separation. A completed roll-in of eligible pre-tax dollars before departure may increase the balance available under the separation exception when the receiving plan allows those withdrawals.

Will the plan’s 20% withholding cover what I owe?

The federal withholding is generally 20% on taxable eligible rollover distributions paid to you. Actual tax depends on your return, other income, and state rules. A higher combined marginal rate leaves an estimated gap in the calculator. A lower rate shows excess withholding relative to the estimate.
