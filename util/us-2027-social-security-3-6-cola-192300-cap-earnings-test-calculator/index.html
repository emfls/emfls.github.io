Frequently asked questions

What does the 3.6% COLA add to a $2,200 monthly benefit?

A $2,200 gross monthly benefit becomes about $2,279.20. Subtract the 2027 standard Part B premium of $209.40 to estimate a $2,069.80 monthly check before other deductions. With the $202.90 standard premium in 2026, the comparable check was $1,997.10. SSA rounding can change the actual amount.

How much could Social Security withhold if I earn $40,000 in 2027?

If you are under full retirement age all year, $40,000 is $14,440 above the $25,560 annual limit. At $1 withheld for every $2 over the limit, the annual test calls for $7,220 of withholding, up to the benefits subject to the test.

Which earnings count in the year I reach full retirement age?

For the 2027 annual test, count wages and net self-employment earnings from months before the month you reach full retirement age. SSA withholds $1 for every $3 over $67,920. Starting with your full retirement age month, the earnings test stops.

Do benefits withheld under the earnings test count later?

At full retirement age, SSA recalculates your monthly benefit to credit months affected by earnings-test withholding. The adjustment increases future monthly payments when applicable. SSA may temporarily hold whole checks to collect an annual withholding amount.

What is the most I can pay in Social Security tax for 2027?

The 2027 cap is $192,300 of covered earnings. The maximum Social Security tax is $11,922.60 for an employee at 6.2% or $23,845.20 for someone paying the 12.4% self-employment rate. Regular Medicare tax applies to covered earnings above the Social Security cap.
