Pension splitting questions

Which pension income can I split before age 65?

Qualifying lifetime payments from a registered workplace pension, such as a DB pension, can generally be split at any age. RRIF and LIF amounts generally qualify when the transferring spouse is 65 or older at year-end, with an exception for qualifying payments received because of a spouse or common-law partner’s death. CPP, QPP and OAS are outside Form T1032.

How do we report the election on our returns?

Both spouses or common-law partners complete and sign Form T1032 for the year. The transferring spouse claims the split amount at line 21000 and the receiving spouse reports it at line 11600. Québec filers use a separate Schedule Q when eligible.

Can my spouse receive a split and still miss the $280 credit?

Yes. A spouse under 65 may receive split income, but their pension income amount depends on the pension source and the adjustment in Step 4 of Form T1032. A regular RRIF or LIF split alone generally gives an under-65 recipient no federal pension amount. The credit is non-refundable.

Does splitting pension income always reduce OAS recovery?

No. The 15% recovery applies individually above $95,323 in 2026 and is capped at each person’s gross OAS. Shifting income may reduce one spouse’s recovery while increasing the other’s. The optimizer compares both amounts along with federal and provincial income taxes.

Can splitting help preserve the age amount?

Yes. The 2026 federal age amount for a person at least 65 is reduced by 15% of net income over $46,432. A transfer can restore some of that amount for the transferring spouse while reducing it for the receiving spouse, so compare both results.
