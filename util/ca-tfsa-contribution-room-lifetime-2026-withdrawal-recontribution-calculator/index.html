  • $7,000 is the 2026 annual TFSA dollar limit. The limits from 2009 through 2026 total $109,000 if you were eligible every year.

  • The CRA charges 1% of the highest excess in each calendar month, including the month you remove it.

Avoid the same-year recontribution trap

If you have $7,000 of room left and withdraw $4,000, putting $11,000 back in 2026 creates a $4,000 excess. That can cost $40 per calendar month while the same excess remains. Withdrawing the excess promptly prevents that amount from generating tax in later months.

TFSA questions

Can everyone contribute $109,000 to a TFSA in 2026?

The annual limits total $109,000 from 2009 through 2026 for someone eligible each year. Subtract contributions across all your TFSAs and account for prior withdrawals to determine how much room you have left.

What if I turned 18 later or moved to Canada?

You earn room starting in 2009 or the calendar year you reach age 18 and have Canadian tax residency, whichever is later. An eligible partial resident year earns the full annual limit. A year spent entirely as a non-resident earns zero.

When can I recontribute a TFSA withdrawal from 2026?

Ordinary 2026 withdrawals return as contribution room on January 1, 2027. You can recontribute before then if you already have enough room. The portion used to remove an excess does not generate new room.

How does CRA calculate the 1% TFSA excess tax?

CRA adds 1% of the highest excess in each calendar month. If you have an excess of $1,200 for three months, that is $36. Removing it during the third month still leaves that month taxable. Report the tax using Form RC243 and Schedule A.

How is 2027 room projected?

The projection adds unused 2026 room, ordinary 2026 withdrawals, and a $7,000 assumed annual limit if you are eligible in 2027. The main projection assumes any remaining excess is removed in 2026. An excess carried into 2027 uses up room first.
