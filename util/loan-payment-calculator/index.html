FAQ

Does a zero-interest loan work?

Yes. When annual interest is 0%, principal is divided equally across the total number of monthly payments with zero interest.

How is remaining balance at my exit year handled in Offer Comparison?

At your chosen exit month, Offer Comparison sums monthly payments made, upfront fees/points paid, and the remaining principal payoff balance, then subtracts initial loan principal so both offers are measured on total net borrowing cost through the same exit date.

Why can a lender quote differ?

Real offers can include daily simple interest accrual, escrow reserves, PMI, prepaid interest, and lender-specific rounding rules.

How the Calculation Works & Mathematical Formula

The Loan Payment Calculator determines monthly payments on a fixed-rate loan using the following key input variables:

  • Loan Amount: The total sum borrowed.
  • Annual Note Interest Rate (%): The yearly interest charged on the loan.
  • Term (Years): The duration over which the loan is to be repaid.
  • Upfront Fees / Points: Optional fees paid at the loan initiation.
  • Currency Symbol: The symbol representing the currency used.
  • Offer B Interest Rate (%): An alternative interest rate for comparison.
  • Offer B Upfront Fees / Points: Fees associated with Offer B.
  • Planned Exit / Horizon (Years): The time frame for stopping payments or refinancing.

The formula used in this calculator for monthly payment calculation (M) is based on the principal loan amount (P), monthly interest rate (r), and number of payments (n):

M = P[r(1+r)^n] / [(1+r)^n – 1]

For instance, if you input a loan amount of $200,000, an annual interest rate of 5%, and a term of 30 years:

  • The monthly interest rate: 0.05 / 12 = 0.004167
  • Total number of payments: 30 * 12 = 360
Substitute into the formula:

M = 200000[0.004167(1+0.004167)^360] / [(1+0.004167)^360 – 1]

The calculated monthly payment would be approximately $1,073.64.

When evaluating multiple offers, analyze the break-even point where total costs of each offer are equal. Remember that the presence of upfront fees can influence your overall cost significantly. It’s crucial to consider these factors while making your decision.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Does a zero-interest loan work?

Yes, in a zero-interest loan, the principal is divided equally across monthly payments with no additional interest added. Therefore, the payment amount equals the total amount divided by the number of payments.

How is the remaining balance calculated in Offer Comparison?

In the Offer Comparison, at your selected exit year, the remaining balance is computed based on the outstanding principal at that time, allowing for a clearer analysis of costs between two offers.

How do upfront fees affect my loan?

Upfront fees increase the overall cost of a loan but can lower your interest rate. Calculating their impact helps determine if you save more in the long run by paying these fees upfront.
