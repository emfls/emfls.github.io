2026 NSO Exercise Tax & Withholding Gap Calculator

Estimate the tax on exercising US employee non-qualified stock options and compare the cash from a same-day sale with the after-tax profit from holding.

Key takeaways

  • The taxable W-2 spread is the exercise-day fair market value minus your strike price, multiplied by shares, with a floor of $0.
  • If $30,000 of spread falls entirely in the 32% bracket, 22% withholding leaves a $3,000 federal income-tax gap before other payments or credits.
  • After more than one year, gains above your exercise basis generally qualify for long-term capital gains rates. The W-2 tax on the exercise spread still applies.

What the estimates include

The calculator models a typical vested employee NSO without a readily determinable value at grant. It applies regular federal brackets to the added exercise income, without an ISO-style AMT adjustment. The model excludes overall AMT, credits, deductions or benefits that change with income, self-employment income, and Social Security reconciliation across employers.

Same-day sale assumes execution at exercise-day fair market value. Later sales use 2026 rates and thresholds for comparison, with no other preferential gains, investment income or capital losses. Sale profit subtracts strike cost and estimated taxes. The calculation excludes fees, the time value of money and any tax benefit from losses.

For exercise and reporting rules, see IRS Publication 525 · IRS capital gains and losses

Frequently asked questions

How is an NSO exercise reported on Form W-2?

For a typical vested employee NSO, the spread at exercise is ordinary wage income. It is generally reported in Box 12 with code V and included in Boxes 1, 3 up to the Social Security cap, and 5.

Why can 22% withholding leave a tax bill?

The flat supplemental withholding method takes 22% through $1 million of cumulative supplemental wages and 37% above that amount. The income tax on the exercise applies your regular brackets to the spread. If the entire spread falls in the 35% bracket and is withheld at 22%, the federal income-tax gap is 13% of the spread.

Why can Additional Medicare withholding differ from the tax I owe?

An employer starts withholding 0.9% once it pays you more than $200,000 in Medicare wages. On a joint return, the 0.9% tax applies above $250,000 in combined Medicare wages, so the amount withheld can differ from the liability.

When does an NSO stock sale qualify for long-term rates?

Shares from a typical vested NSO exercise must be held for more than one year. Only the gain above your basis qualifies. Basis is the strike cost plus wages recognized at exercise, which equals exercise-day fair market value for an in-the-money exercise.

What if the stock falls after I exercise?

Selling below your basis creates a capital loss. It does not reverse the W-2 income from exercise. Capital losses generally offset capital gains first, and separate limits apply to deductions against other income.
