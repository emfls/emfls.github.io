Key takeaways

An HSA can save federal income tax when you contribute, shelter investment growth, and reimburse qualified medical expenses tax-free. A Section 125 payroll election may also save 7.65% in employee payroll taxes while the elected wages fall below the Social Security cap and Additional Medicare threshold.

Paying eligible bills out of pocket and keeping the receipts lets you leave your HSA invested. Your documented, unreimbursed expenses set the amount you can later withdraw tax-free.

What delaying reimbursement can cost

Paying a bill today ties up cash while your investments can fall. Keep receipts and proof of payment. If your receipts cover only part of a withdrawal, the rest generally incurs ordinary federal income tax plus a 20% additional tax before age 65. The 20% tax generally stops at 65.

How the comparison works

The scenarios start at zero and use the same gross pay and return each year. Deposits arrive at year-end. Income and payroll taxes are paid from that pay slice. Employer HSA contributions reduce the amount you can elect for 2026. Employer funding and 401(k) matches are outside the paycheck comparison.

The projection starts at your entered year-end age and holds 2026 contribution room, eligibility, wages and assumed tax rates constant. It does not model partial-year eligibility, enrolling in Medicare during the projection, future catch-ups, early withdrawal exceptions, or changes in tax law. Medical bills are paid separately and held equal across scenarios. The Roth IRA clock starts with the modeled contributions.

Taxable distributions are reinvested after tax. Remaining positive price gains are taxed at the end, with no modeled benefit for losses. The California and New Jersey scenarios use this same approach for state tax on HSA investments. State treatment uses a single rate per period and excludes special exemptions and adjustments.

HSA questions

How much can I contribute to an HSA in 2026?

The 2026 base limit is $4,400 with self-only coverage and $8,750 with family coverage. Employer deposits and employee payroll elections count toward the limit. Each eligible spouse age 55 or older can contribute a $1,000 catch-up to their own HSA. The family base limit is shared.

Do HSA contributions through payroll save FICA?

An eligible Section 125 salary reduction generally avoids 6.2% Social Security tax on wages up to $184,500 in 2026 and 1.45% Medicare tax on all wages. It may also avoid the 0.9% Additional Medicare Tax on wages above the applicable filing threshold. Direct HSA contributions and traditional 401(k) deferrals do not reduce employee FICA.

Can I reimburse a medical bill decades later?

Federal rules impose no reimbursement deadline for qualified expenses incurred after you established the HSA. Keep records of the bill and payment. Use only expenses that have not already been reimbursed or claimed as an itemized deduction.

What happens if I use my HSA for non-medical spending?

A non-medical distribution generally counts as ordinary federal income. A 20% additional federal tax generally applies before age 65, with exceptions for qualifying disability or death. Qualified medical reimbursements remain federally tax-free.

How do California and New Jersey affect HSA savings?

The calculator treats both states as taxing HSA contributions and taxable investment income. It removes the upfront state income tax break and estimates tax on distributions and realized gains, including gains at the final sale. Federal HSA treatment still applies.

See IRS Notice 2004-50, Q&A 39 for the delayed reimbursement rules.
