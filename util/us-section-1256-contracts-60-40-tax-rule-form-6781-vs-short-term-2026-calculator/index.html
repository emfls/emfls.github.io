Form 6781 and Section 1256 FAQ

How does the Section 1256 60/40 rule work for day trades?

An eligible contract’s net gain or loss is 60% long-term and 40% short-term, even on a one-minute trade. For a gain, the short-term portion adds to ordinary taxable income first. The long-term portion is then stacked above it at the applicable 0%, 15%, or 20% rates.

Do SPX and SPY options receive the same tax treatment?

Qualifying broad-based index options such as SPX, NDX and RUT, and regulated futures such as ES, NQ and MES, generally receive Section 1256 treatment. This calculator treats short-term SPY and QQQ ETF shares or ETF options as entirely short-term. Confirm the exact contract and any elections with your broker and tax preparer.

How do year-end mark-to-market and wash sales work?

An open Section 1256 contract is generally treated as sold at fair market value on the last business day of the tax year, with an adjustment when it closes. Section 1256(f)(5) exempts losses recognized under that year-end rule from the wash-sale rule. Special straddle and hedging rules can still apply.

How does the 3.8% net investment income tax affect the comparison?

NIIT applies to the smaller of net investment income and the amount by which modified adjusted gross income exceeds the filing-status threshold. Equal investment gains produce the same modeled NIIT under either scenario. The comparison subtracts NIIT already due before the trade.

How does the Form 6781 Box D loss carryback work?

Eligible individual filers can elect to apply the qualifying loss to the earliest of the three prior tax years first. Each year is limited by eligible prior Section 1256 gains and the net operating loss restriction. A taxpayer may claim a refund with Form 1045 or Form 1040-X, as applicable. Ordinary investor capital losses generally offset current capital gains, then up to $3,000 of ordinary income ($1,500 if married filing separately), with eligible excess carried forward.

What the estimate covers

The gain comparison assumes the basic 2026 standard deduction and treats pretrade income as ordinary income. It assumes MAGI equals AGI, the trading gain is investment income, the equity loss is deductible after wash-sale adjustments, and the trade is your only current capital gain or loss. It uses rate schedules and excludes IRS tax-table rounding, qualified dividends, credits, AMT, state tax, and special straddle or hedging rules.

Use this estimate for planning. For a carryback claim, recompute the affected returns with the applicable Form 6781 and Schedule D. Calculator inputs are processed in your browser.
