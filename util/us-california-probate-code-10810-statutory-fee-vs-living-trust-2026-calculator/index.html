1. The California Statutory Probate Fee Schedule (§10810 & §10800)

Unlike most US states where probate attorneys bill on an hourly or reasonable flat-fee basis, California operates under a mandatory statutory percentage fee system established in California Probate Code §10810 for attorney compensation and §10800 for personal representative (executor or administrator) compensation:

Gross Estate Tier Statutory Percentage Rate Cumulative Maximum Fee per Role
First $100,000 4.0% $4,000
Next $100,000 ($100k to $200k) 3.0% $7,000

Next $9,000,000 ($1M to $10M) 1.0% $113,000

2. The Critical Gross Value Trap: Why Mortgages Multiply the Cost

The single most dangerous financial trap in California probate law is that statutory fees are calculated on the gross fair market value of the assets, without deducting mortgages, liens, or encumbrances (Cal. Prob. Code §10810(b)).

For example, if a decedent passes away owning a San Francisco or Los Angeles home appraised at $1,500,000 with an outstanding mortgage of $1,200,000, the family's actual equity is only $300,000. However, the statutory probate fees are computed on the full $1,500,000 gross value:

  • Attorney Statutory Fee: $28,000
  • Executor Statutory Fee: $28,000
  • Court, Referee, and Filing Costs: ~$3,870
  • Total Probate Depletion: $59,870 — consuming nearly 20% of the entire family equity!
