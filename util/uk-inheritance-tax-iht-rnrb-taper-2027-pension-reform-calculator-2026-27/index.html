Compare inheritance tax (IHT) before and from 6 April 2027. See how unused pension savings, the £2 million residence allowance taper and lifetime gifts affect your estimate.

Key takeaways

  • A £325,000 nil-rate band and up to £175,000 for a qualifying home can shelter £500,000. Full transfers from a late spouse or civil partner can raise this to £1 million.
  • Above £2 million, the residence allowance falls £1 per £2 of estate value. Where that loss increases the amount taxed at 40%, an extra £1,000 can cost £600 in IHT.
  • Most unused pension funds enter the estate for deaths from 6 April 2027. In the example below, adding £300,000 of pension increases estimated IHT by £160,000 and reduces the residence allowance by £100,000.

A pension can cost your heirs part of the home allowance

At the 40% rate, losing £100,000 of residence allowance adds £40,000 of IHT on top of the tax caused by the pension itself. If pension benefits are taxable after a death at age 75 or older, Income Tax can compound the loss. For example, 40% IHT attributable to a pension and 45% Income Tax on the remaining benefit would leave £33 per £100. The table estimates IHT only.

What this estimate covers

The comparison uses a single general estate component and a pension that falls outside IHT before the reform, such as an eligible discretionary DC pot. From April 2027, registered-scheme death-in-service benefits remain outside IHT and applicable spouse or civil partner exemptions still apply. Include pension amounts already subject to IHT in the net estate for both scenarios.

The estimate excludes downsizing additions, business and agricultural reliefs, settled property, gifts with reservation and chargeable lifetime transfers. Budget 2026 proposed an option to treat the pension as a separate component for the charity rate. The comparison uses the general-component treatment. Check the applicable rules before relying on an estimate for estate administration.

Frequently asked questions

Who qualifies for the £500,000 or £1 million IHT threshold?

The ordinary £325,000 nil-rate band can be combined with up to £175,000 when a qualifying home share passes to direct descendants. A surviving spouse or civil partner can reach £1 million if both unused bands are fully transferable and the home and estate qualify. Gifts within seven years can use some or all of the ordinary band.

How does the £2 million taper create a 60% marginal IHT rate?

The residence band falls by £1 for every £2 of estate value over £2 million, after debts and before exemptions. Where the residence band is falling and taxable estate is charged at 40%, £2 of extra estate and £1 of lost allowance result in £1.20 extra tax. A full single band disappears at £2.35 million and two full bands disappear at £2.7 million.

Does taper relief reduce tax on all gifts made within seven years?

Outright gifts within seven years are set against the ordinary nil-rate band from oldest to newest. Taper relief only reduces the tax on the part above the available band, with rates falling from 40% to 8% as the gift gets older. An outright gift made at least seven years before death generally falls outside IHT.

How much must I leave to charity for the 36% rate?

A qualifying charity legacy must reach at least 10% of the baseline of the relevant estate component. The calculation takes account of exemptions and the available ordinary nil-rate band but leaves out the residence band. Apply the reduced rate to the taxable part of the qualifying component after the charitable gift.

How do unused pensions affect IHT from 6 April 2027?

Most unused pension funds and death benefits are included for deaths from 6 April 2027. This can increase the taxable estate and reduce the residence band. If a beneficiary also owes Income Tax on the pension, that tax generally applies to the benefit after the IHT the beneficiary bears. The calculator compares an in-scope pension with its prior discretionary treatment.
