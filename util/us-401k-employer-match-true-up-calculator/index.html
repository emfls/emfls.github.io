How the estimate works

Federal planning estimate for one traditional or Roth 401(k) with a single-tier match, equal gross checks and no other annual additions. This model uses capped plan pay for employee deferrals and employer matching. Confirm eligible pay, percentage precision, treatment of catch-ups and eligibility for a true-up with your plan.

The regular 2026 elective limit is $24,500. If permitted, age 50 or older adds $8,000, or $11,250 for ages 60–63, based on age at year-end. Matching is based on at most $360,000 of pay. The model applies the $72,000 annual additions ceiling or 100% of compensation if lower, with catch-up amounts above the regular limit outside that ceiling.

Set how eligible pay is apportioned under your plan. The paycheck schedule stops deferrals at the elective limit and combines cent-rounded amounts over the year. Annual matching uses actual deferrals and annual eligible pay. A modeled true-up pays the difference between annual and paycheck matching when the plan reconciles the two.

For $150,000 over 26 paychecks, maxing out $24,500 evenly averages $942.31 per check, or 16.33% of eligible pay when rounded. The table apportions the exact cents. With 20% elected and a 50% match up to 6%, the limit is reached on check 22 and $730.77 in employer match is at risk without a true-up. A rounded payroll election may produce slightly different results.

A contribution above the match threshold can help reach the IRS limit. If the full formula requires more deferrals than the IRS permits, the steady schedule reports the shortfall. The model excludes multi-tier matches, midyear changes, other plans, nonelective contributions, forfeitures and vesting.

Frequently asked questions

Does every 401(k) plan offer a true-up?

A true-up depends on the plan document. If your plan reconciles matching annually, it may pay the difference between annual matching and matching already paid each payday. Check eligibility and payment timing with your plan.

How much should I contribute each biweekly paycheck to reach $24,500?

For 26 eligible biweekly checks, the average is $942.31. An exact cent schedule uses 20 checks at $942.31 and six at $942.30; actual payroll elections and rounding may vary.

What if I earned more than $150,000 from my employer last year?

The 2026 Roth catch-up wage test generally uses 2025 wages from the sponsoring employer. If those wages exceeded $150,000 and the rule applies to you, eligible catch-up contributions must generally be Roth. Ask payroll how your plan handles the rule.
