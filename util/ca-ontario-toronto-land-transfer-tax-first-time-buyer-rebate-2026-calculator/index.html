Key takeaways

An eligible first-time buyer with a 100% qualifying ownership share can receive up to $8,475 in Toronto: $4,000 from Ontario plus $4,475 from the city.

Toronto charges its municipal tax in addition to Ontario’s. For registrations from April 1, 2026, the city’s new marginal rates range from 4.40% to 8.60% on portions above $3 million.

Frequently asked questions

How much transfer tax does an $850,000 Toronto purchase incur?

With a 100% qualifying share and no foreign-buyer surcharges, the estimate is $18,475 in Toronto after rebates. The same purchase outside the City of Toronto is $9,475, a difference of $9,000.

Does the April 2026 luxury rate apply to the entire purchase price?

Each rate applies only to the portion in its band. With a $3.5 million purchase registered from April 1, Toronto taxes the $500,000 above $3 million at 4.40% and taxes the lower portions at their own rates.

How much do first-time buyer rebates cover with an ineligible co-buyer?

A qualifying 50% ownership share can receive up to $2,000 from Ontario and $2,237.50 from Toronto. The provincial cap covers the estimated Ontario tax on a fully qualifying purchase up to about $368,333. The city rebate covers the Toronto tax up to $400,000.

Who qualifies for the first-time buyer rebates at closing?

Qualifying buyers must be at least 18, be Canadian citizens or permanent residents at registration, and occupy the home as their principal residence within nine months. Previous home ownership anywhere in the world disqualifies a buyer, as does a spouse’s ownership during the relationship. A spouse who sold before the relationship may be treated differently.

Can Ontario and Toronto foreign-buyer taxes both apply?

Yes. When both apply, Ontario’s 25% and Toronto’s 10% together add 35% of the taxable consideration. Applicability depends on the purchasers and any exemption. The first-time buyer rebates reduce regular transfer taxes and do not reduce these surcharges.
