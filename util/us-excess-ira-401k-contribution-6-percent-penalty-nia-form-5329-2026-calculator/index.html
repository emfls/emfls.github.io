Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate net income attributable (NIA) on an excess IRA contribution?

Multiply the excess being returned by (adjusted closing value minus adjusted opening value) divided by adjusted opening value. Include deposits and transfers in when adjusting the opening value. Include withdrawals and transfers out when adjusting the closing value. A loss can reduce the amount your custodian returns.

When can I correct an excess 2026 IRA contribution?

The usual federal deadline is April 15, 2027, or October 15, 2027 with a valid filing extension. If you filed on time without correcting the excess, IRS rules generally allow a six-month correction window measured from the original due date. That relief requires an amended return.

Do I owe the 10% early withdrawal tax on a timely IRA correction?

A qualifying timely return of the excess and its allocable earnings avoids the 6% excise tax and the 10% early distribution tax, including before age 59½. Positive IRA earnings are income for the contribution year. A returned traditional IRA contribution must be left out of the IRA deduction.

Does the 6% Form 5329 tax apply every year?

Yes, while the IRA excess remains subject to the tax. Each year, apply 6% to the smaller of the remaining excess or the year-end value of the applicable IRA type, including contributions made after year-end for that year. The projection assumes no removal or absorption by unused contribution room and a constant year-end value.

What happens if I miss the 401(k) April 15 correction deadline?

The usual 2026 excess deferral correction deadline is April 15, 2027. A personal filing extension does not extend it. A pre-tax excess can be taxed in 2026 and again when distributed. Designated Roth principal is already included in wages and is excluded from the double-tax estimate. Ask the plan administrator for the allocable earnings and its request cutoff.
