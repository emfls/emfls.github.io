Beware the bridging property market discount trap

If your existing property fails to sell within the bank's 6-month bridging window, lenders may force a rapid price reduction. Selling at a 10% discount ($120,000 less) increases your permanent End Debt by over $117,000, adding hundreds in ongoing monthly repayments for the next 30 years.

Australia bridging finance FAQ

How does an Australian bridging loan work?

A bridging loan allows an Australian homebuyer to purchase a new property before selling their existing home. The lender finances the new property purchase while holding both properties as security, creating a temporary Peak Debt balance until the existing home is sold.

What is capitalised interest in bridging finance?

Most major Australian banks (CBA, Westpac, NAB, ANZ) capitalise the bridging interest rather than requiring monthly payments. Monthly interest charges are added directly to the Peak Debt balance each month, compounding until the existing property is settled.

What is the maximum allowable Peak Debt LVR under APRA guidelines?

Most Australian lenders require the initial Peak Debt to remain at or below 80% of the combined valuation of both the existing and new properties. Exceeding 80% Peak LVR generally triggers Lenders Mortgage Insurance (LMI) or outright loan rejection.

How long do Australian banks give you to sell your existing property?

Australian lenders typically grant a 6-month bridging period for established home purchases, or up to 12 months if constructing a new home. If the property remains unsold after the bridging term, standard variable penalties or forced sale clauses can apply.

How is the ongoing End Debt calculated?

End Debt equals the Final Peak Debt (initial peak debt plus all accrued capitalised interest) minus the Net Sale Proceeds (gross sale price minus real estate agent commissions, marketing, and legal conveyancing fees).

Calculation assumptions and methodology

Bridging interest is compounded on a monthly basis across the designated term. Stamp duty rates and legal conveyancing costs vary across Australian states (NSW, VIC, QLD, WA, SA) and should be validated against state revenue office calculators.
