Mortgage recast and PMI removal FAQ

What is a mortgage recast and how does it work?

A mortgage recast (or re-amortization) occurs when you make a large lump-sum principal payment (e.g. $10,000 to $50,000) and your lender recalculates your monthly payments based on the new reduced balance while preserving your existing interest rate and remaining term for a small fee ($250 to $500).

How does a recast remove private mortgage insurance (PMI)?

Under the federal Homeowners Protection Act of 1998, once your lump-sum payment drops your mortgage loan-to-value (LTV) ratio to 80% or below based on the original home value, you have the legal right to request immediate PMI cancellation, eliminating that monthly premium.

Why is a recast better than a refinance when you have a low interest rate?

If your current mortgage rate is 3% to 5.75% and prevailing market rates are higher, refinancing would force you into a higher interest rate and cost $4,500 to $7,500 in closing costs. A recast retains your existing low rate, costs only $250 to $500, and permanently lowers your monthly payment.

What is the difference between recasting and principal curtailment?

A recast lowers your required monthly payment while keeping your payoff date unchanged. Principal curtailment keeps your monthly payment the same, applying the lump sum to shorten your loan term by several years and maximizing total interest saved.

Can FHA or VA loans be recast?

Generally no. Federal Housing Administration (FHA) and Department of Veterans Affairs (VA) loans do not permit mortgage recasting under program guidelines. Recasting is predominantly available on conventional conforming Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac loans.
