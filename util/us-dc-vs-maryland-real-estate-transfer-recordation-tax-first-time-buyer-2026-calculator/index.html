Frequently asked questions

Who qualifies for DC’s 0.725% recordation rate?

The home must qualify for DC’s Homestead Deduction, and the buyer must meet DC first-time buyer requirements. From October 1, 2026, the price cap is $794,000 and the combined federal AGI cap is $239,220 for two people. Both caps are inclusive.

Does past ownership in Maryland or Virginia affect DC eligibility?

Past ownership outside DC does not by itself disqualify a buyer. DC looks at prior ownership of a DC principal residence that qualified for its Homestead Deduction. DC domicile and the current home’s eligibility still matter.

What happens to DC deed taxes at $400,000?

The standard recordation and transfer rates each rise from 1.10% to 1.45% on the full price. A qualifying buyer still pays 0.725% recordation tax, while the seller’s transfer rate follows the standard schedule.

Does first-time Maryland buyer relief remove county deed taxes?

The statewide relief reduces state transfer tax to 0.25% and assigns it entirely to the seller. Local transfer and recordation taxes follow their own rules. Use the local adjustment fields for amounts confirmed by your settlement agent.

How does Montgomery County’s recordation exemption work?

An eligible individual buyer can exempt the first $100,000 of consideration by occupying the home as a principal residence for at least seven of the first 12 months after conveyance. Higher portions of the sale price still follow the county’s marginal tiers.
