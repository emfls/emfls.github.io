Key takeaways

  • Age 62 pays 70% of your full benefit. Age 70 pays 124%. With a full retirement age of 67 and flat gross payments, the age-70 strategy overtakes age 62 at about 80.4.
  • Up to 85% of Social Security can enter taxable income. OBBBA preserved the $25,000 single and $32,000 joint provisional-income thresholds.
  • The $6,000 senior deduction lowers taxable income. The phase-out starts at $75,000 of modified adjusted gross income (MAGI) for single filers and $150,000 for joint filers.

The cost of claiming early or waiting

Claiming at 62 gives you checks sooner and generally locks in a lower monthly benefit. Waiting raises the monthly payment but uses savings or other income to cover the gap. Consider cash needs, longevity and survivor benefits before choosing based on a single break-even age.

Frequently asked questions

Did OBBBA eliminate federal tax on Social Security?

No. IRC §86 still uses provisional income to determine taxable benefits. The temporary Schedule 1-A deduction lowers taxable income by up to $6,000 per eligible senior for 2025–2028.

How is provisional income calculated?

In this calculator, add ordinary income, taxable traditional IRA withdrawals, tax-exempt municipal interest and half of annual Social Security. The result determines how much of the benefit is included in taxable income, up to 85%.

How can a $1,000 IRA withdrawal cost $407 in federal tax?

If the entire withdrawal stays in the 85% benefit phase-in and the 22% tax bracket, it adds $850 of taxable benefits and $1,000 of IRA income. That adds $407 of tax before any deduction phase-out. A qualified Roth IRA distribution adds no federal income tax in this model.

How does the senior deduction phase out for a joint return?

For each eligible spouse, subtract 6% of joint MAGI above $150,000 from $6,000, with a floor of zero. If both qualify, the combined deduction is $10,800 at $160,000 and zero at $250,000.

When does delaying Social Security break even?

With full retirement age 67 and flat gross benefits, age 67 catches age 62 at about 78.7, age 70 catches age 67 at 82.5, and age 70 catches age 62 at about 80.4. Future COLAs and discounting change these ages.
