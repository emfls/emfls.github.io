2026 Section 179 & Heavy SUV Bonus Depreciation Calculator

Estimate your first-year write-off and cash tax savings for equipment, software or a qualifying heavy vehicle placed in service in 2026.

Key takeaways

  • Section 179 lets you expense up to $2,560,000 in 2026. The limit falls dollar for dollar once qualifying investment exceeds $4,090,000.
  • Qualifying heavy SUVs have a separate $32,000 Section 179 cap. The business share of remaining eligible basis may qualify for 100% bonus depreciation.
  • Section 179 is limited by active taxable business income. Bonus depreciation can create a loss and its cash value depends on when you can use it.

The 50% business-use trap

A vehicle at 50% qualified business use loses both accelerated deductions in the listed-property scenario. A later drop to that level can require you to add part of an earlier deduction back to taxable income. Keep a mileage log and budget from the purchase price until your tax savings are known.

How the estimate works

Business basis is purchase cost times qualified business use. Qualifying business basis and other qualifying property determine the annual phase-out. The model assumes you elect the largest available Section 179 amount for this asset and have no competing elections or prior carryforwards. Bonus applies to basis outside that election. The Section 179 income test uses active taxable income after modeled bonus depreciation.

The truck and cargo van option assumes the vehicle qualifies for the heavy SUV cap exception and is modeled as listed property. Check vehicle eligibility before using this option. Ordinary MACRS, straight-line ADS, prior acquisitions eligible for transition rates, separate state tax rules and the future value of losses or carryforwards are outside this estimate.

Frequently asked questions

When does the 2026 Section 179 deduction phase out?

The $2,560,000 annual limit falls by one dollar for every dollar of qualifying investment above $4,090,000. At $6,650,000, the annual Section 179 limit is zero. Eligible basis can still qualify for bonus depreciation.

Which vehicles are subject to the $32,000 heavy SUV limit?

This scenario covers qualifying passenger-oriented SUVs over 6,000 and up to 14,000 pounds GVWR. The estimate prorates the $32,000 SUV cap by qualified business use. Vehicles above 14,000 pounds and qualifying designs such as a cargo bed at least six feet long that is inaccessible from the passenger compartment can fall outside this cap.

Can bonus depreciation create a loss while Section 179 carries forward?

Yes. Section 179 is limited by income from actively conducted trades or businesses. Elected basis disallowed by that limit may carry forward and stays out of bonus basis. Bonus depreciation has no Section 179 income limit and can contribute to a net operating loss.

What if business use is exactly 50% or drops later?

Section 179 requires more than 50% business use. At 50% or below, listed property also loses bonus depreciation and generally uses straight-line ADS. A later drop to 50% or below can trigger recapture. Qualifying non-listed property may still receive bonus depreciation on its business basis.

Can used equipment qualify for 100% bonus depreciation?

Yes, eligible used property can qualify, subject to acquisition rules such as the related-party and prior-use restrictions. The 100% rate generally applies to qualified assets acquired and placed in service after January 19, 2025, including qualifying MACRS property with a recovery period of 20 years or less.
