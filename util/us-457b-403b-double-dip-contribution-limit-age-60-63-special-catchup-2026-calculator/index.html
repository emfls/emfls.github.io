Key takeaways

The two plans offer $49,000 in combined base room. With the age catch-up in both plans, that rises to $65,000 at ages 50–59 and 64+, or $71,500 at ages 60–63.

A qualifying special catch-up can bring the governmental 457(b) alone to $49,000. An eligible 403(b) participant can add up to $3,000 under the 15-year service rule.

Frequently asked questions

Can I contribute to both a 457(b) and a 403(b) or 401(k) in 2026?

Yes. The governmental 457(b) has its own $24,500 base limit. The 403(b) and 401(k) share a separate $24,500 elective deferral limit. The combined base is $49,000 when you have access to both buckets and sufficient plan and payroll room.

How much can I contribute if I turn 60, 61, 62 or 63 in 2026?

If both plans permit the age-based catch-up, each can add $11,250 to its $24,500 base, for $71,500 combined. Use the age you reach by December 31. The standard $8,000 catch-up applies at ages 50–59 and 64 or older.

How do the special 457(b) and 403(b) catch-ups work?

The 457(b) special rule can add eligible unused prior-year room, up to another $24,500, in the three calendar years before you reach the plan’s normal retirement age. Use the higher allowed 457(b) catch-up without stacking them. In an eligible 403(b), the 15-year rule is applied before the age-based catch-up. Its amount is the lowest of $3,000, the remaining $15,000 lifetime allowance, and $5,000 per service year minus prior elective deferrals to the organization’s plans.

Which 2026 catch-up dollars must be Roth?

When applicable 2025 FICA wages from the sponsoring employer exceed $150,000, the 2026 age-based catch-up in that plan generally must be Roth. Exactly $150,000 does not trigger the requirement. The 457(b) special catch-up and the 403(b) 15-year service catch-up can still be pre-tax. Confirm the wage basis and Roth availability with the plan.

When can I avoid the 10% early-withdrawal tax with a governmental 457(b)?

A permitted withdrawal of native governmental 457(b) funds after separation generally avoids the 10% additional federal tax, even before age 59½. Roll-in amounts from other types of plans or IRAs can be subject to it. A 403(b) or 401(k) distribution can also qualify if you separated in or after the calendar year you reached age 55. The age-55 separation exception does not apply to a traditional IRA.
