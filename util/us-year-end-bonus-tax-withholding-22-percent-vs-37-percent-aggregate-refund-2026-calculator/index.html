Frequently Asked Questions: Bonus Tax Withholding

Why is my bonus taxed at a higher rate than my normal paycheck?

In reality, bonuses are taxed at the exact same statutory income tax rates as regular wages when you file your annual return. The perception of higher taxes comes from withholding methods: either the 22% flat supplemental rate or the aggregate method, which annualizes your single paycheck and withholds as if you made that amount 26 times a year.

Can I ask my employer to withhold using the Flat 22% method instead of Aggregate?

Employers have the legal option under IRS rules to choose either method. If paid as a separate check or itemized separately, most payroll providers (like ADP, Gusto, Workday) default to the flat 22% rate. Check with your HR or payroll department before bonus processing.

What happens if my bonus pushes me into the 0.9% Additional Medicare Tax threshold?

Employers are required to begin withholding the 0.9% Additional Medicare tax once your compensation from that specific employer exceeds $200,000 in a calendar year, regardless of your marital status. If you are Married Filing Jointly with a $250,000 threshold, any excess withheld is reconciled on IRS Form 8959 in April.

If I earn more than $184,500, why does my bonus take-home jump higher?

Because the 2026 Social Security tax wage base caps out at $184,500. Once your year-to-date earnings hit that ceiling, your employer stops deducting the 6.2% Social Security tax, instantly giving you an extra $62 in cash for every $1,000 earned.

How can I legally reduce tax withholding on my upcoming December bonus?

The most effective strategy is increasing your pre-tax 401(k) or 403(b) contribution percentage for that specific bonus paycheck, up to the remaining annual limit ($24,500 for 2026). This completely shields that portion from federal income tax withholding.
