Budget for the A$1,455,000 duty cliff

If your dutiable value moves from A$1,455,000 to A$1,455,001, standard owner-occupier duty rises from A$63,078 to A$66,057.05. Allow A$2,979.05 more for duty if your offer or valuation crosses that line. Investor duty moves from A$66,070 to A$66,057.05. An eligible HBCS claim keeps duty at A$0 on both sides.

Frequently asked questions

Can an eligible buyer pay zero ACT stamp duty on an A$1.8 million home?

Yes. From 1 July 2026 the Home Buyer Concession Scheme has no property price or income cap. On an A$1.8 million purchase, an eligible buyer who claims the concession can pay A$0 and save A$81,720 against the standard owner-occupier schedule.

Does the five-year property test cover domestic partners and overseas property?

Yes. The five-year lookback applies to all buyers and their domestic partners and covers legal or equitable property interests worldwide. Limited exceptions apply. Buyers ordinarily must be individuals aged 18 or older buying in their personal capacity.

When does the ACT residence period start?

At least one eligible buyer must own and live in the home as their principal residence for a continuous year. They generally must move in within one year of settlement, or within one year of the certificate of occupancy for a home built on vacant land.

What happens when the dutiable value exceeds A$1,455,000?

Both standard schedules apply 4.54% to the entire value. At A$1,455,001 an owner-occupier pays A$66,057.05, up A$2,979.05 from A$1,455,000. The investor estimate falls by A$12.95 at that same step and the two schedules converge above the threshold.

How are Sydney and Melbourne duty compared?

The comparison uses the same dutiable value for all three places. Sydney uses NSW general and residential premium rates. Melbourne uses Victorian general rates and the principal-place-of-residence rates where applicable. The interstate estimates exclude first-home concessions and buyer surcharges.
