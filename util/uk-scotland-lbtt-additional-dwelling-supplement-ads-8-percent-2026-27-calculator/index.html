England and Northern Ireland comparison

Standard SDLT rates are 0%, 2%, 5%, 10% and 12%, with bands ending at £125,000, £250,000, £925,000 and £1.5 million before the top rate. The additional-dwelling rates add 5 percentage points to each band. Qualifying first-time buyers pay 0% to £300,000 and 5% above that on purchases up to £500,000. The non-residential bands end at £150,000 and £250,000, with rates of 0%, 2% and 5%.

Frequently asked questions

When does Scotland’s 8% ADS start?

ADS starts at £40,000 inclusive when the purchase meets the additional-dwelling conditions. At exactly £40,000, Scottish ADS is £3,200 on the full amount. Below £40,000, ADS is £0.

Who usually pays ADS on a second home or buy-to-let?

ADS usually applies if you buy an additional dwelling while keeping another qualifying home, including property abroad. Companies generally pay ADS on qualifying dwelling purchases even when it is their first dwelling. Joint purchases can also trigger ADS.

How much can a first-time buyer save?

Scotland raises the nil-rate band from £145,000 to £175,000 for eligible main-home purchases, saving up to £600. In England and Northern Ireland, qualifying buyers pay no SDLT up to £300,000, with relief available only when the price is £500,000 or less.

Can I reclaim ADS after replacing my main home?

You generally pay ADS upfront if you buy before disposing of your previous main home. A full ADS repayment normally requires disposal within 36 months, prior use of the old property as your main home within the 36 months before purchase, and occupation of the new one as your main home. Joint-buyer rules and claim requirements also apply.

What tax applies to a commercial purchase?

A pure non-residential purchase uses 0%, 1% and 5% LBTT bands in Scotland and 0%, 2% and 5% SDLT bands in England and Northern Ireland, with the same £150,000 and £250,000 thresholds. For example, at £275,000 the bills are £2,250 and £3,250 respectively.
