True Cost of an Australian Commercial Lease in 2026–27: Beyond Headline Rent per SQM

Business owners, medical practices, and CFOs negotiating office, retail, or industrial space in Sydney, Melbourne, Brisbane, Perth, or Adelaide routinely anchor their budgets on a single quoted figure: the headline net base rent per square metre (for example, A$550/sqm). In reality, Australian commercial leases are structured around four compounding cost layers—base rent escalations, variable building outgoings, 10% Goods and Services Tax (GST) cash-flow timing, and end-of-lease make-good restoration—plus an upfront unconditional bank guarantee that ties up working capital for the entire lease term.

On a modest 250 sqm metro office leased for five years at A$550/sqm net, headline base rent looks like A$137,500 per year. Yet once A$120/sqm in outgoings, 3.5% annual compounding increases, a 3-month incentive, and a A$350/sqm strip-out make-good obligation are combined, the true ex-GST occupancy commitment reaches A$949,738—and the cash outlay including 10% GST crosses A$1.04 million before you buy a single desk or pay for fit-out depreciation. Running a full-term occupancy model before signing a non-binding Heads of Agreement (HOA) is the single highest-ROI discipline in Australian commercial tenant representation.

Net Leases vs. Gross Leases and State Retail Lease Outgoings Rules

Commercial landlords in Australia quote rent on either a Net basis (tenant pays base rent plus its pro-rata share of statutory and operating outgoings) or a Gross basis (base outgoings are bundled into the rent, though tenants still frequently pay increases over a "base year"). Typical outgoings in 2026–27 range from A$80–A$140/sqm in suburban commercial and industrial assets up to A$180–A$260/sqm in prime Sydney and Melbourne CBD A-grade towers, covering council rates, water rates, building insurance, air-conditioning maintenance, lift servicing, common-area cleaning, and property management fees.

Crucially, if your lease falls under state Retail Lease legislation—such as the Retail Leases Act 2003 (Victoria), the Retail Leases Act 1994 (NSW), or the Retail Shop Leases Act 1994 (Queensland)—landlords face strict statutory limits on what can be recovered. In Victoria, retail landlords are prohibited from recovering land tax or capital expenditure from tenants, and in all states landlords must provide an itemized annual outgoings budget and audited reconciliation statement. By contrast, non-retail commercial and industrial leases generally allow landlords to pass through single-holding land tax and Victoria's Commercial and Industrial Property Tax (CIPT) once transitioned, making legal classification of your lease a major financial variable.

Fixed Escalations vs. Uncapped CPI Rent Reviews

Every anniversary of an Australian commercial lease triggers an annual rent review—most commonly a fixed percentage increase (3.0% to 4.5%) or an adjustment linked to the Australian Bureau of Statistics (ABS) All Groups Consumer Price Index (CPI) for the relevant capital city. When inflation spikes, an uncapped CPI clause (or worse, a "greater of CPI or 4%" hybrid clause) creates severe budget volatility. Our calculator automatically stress-tests your entered escalation rate against a 5.0% CPI environment so you can quantify the exact dollar risk of signing an uncapped CPI review.

  • Negotiate a Collar (Cap and Floor): If a landlord insists on CPI reviews, push for a symmetrical collar such as "CPI capped at 4.0%, minimum 2.5%."
  • Watch for Market Review Ratchet Clauses: On exercising an option term (e.g., Year 6 of a 5+5 year lease), rent normally resets to current market rent. Ensure there is no unlawful "ratchet clause" preventing rent from falling if market rents soften (ratchet clauses are void under most state retail Acts, but can still appear in pure commercial or industrial contracts).
  • Check Incentive Clawback Triggers: Landlords in soft office markets often offer 20% to 35% lease incentives—structured as rent-free months, rent abatement spread across the term, or a fit-out cash contribution. Beware of "clawback" clauses requiring you to repay the unamortised incentive if you default or assign the lease; note also that cash fit-out contributions have distinct ATO income tax assessability rules compared with pure rent abatement.

The Hidden Exit Bomb: End-of-Lease Make-Good Obligations

Make-good is the single most litigated clause in Australian commercial leasing. A standard "back to base building" make-good clause forces an outgoing tenant to demolish all internal partitions, remove workstations and structured data cabling back to the riser, restore ceiling tiles and lighting grids, re-balance HVAC ductwork, strip floor coverings, re-polish or re-carpet, and repaint every wall to original specification—often at a cost of A$250 to A$550 per square metre plus 10% GST. On a 250 sqm office, that translates to an A$87,500+ lump-sum bill right when you are also funding a new office move and double-rent overlap.

Experienced tenant advisors limit make-good exposure before lease execution using four concrete safeguards:

  1. Photographic Condition Report: Attach a signed, date-stamped Premises Condition Report to the lease so you are never forced to restore defects that existed before handover.
  2. Cap Make-Good to "Clean and Tidy" or Negotiated Cash Settlement: If you are taking over an existing landlord-built spec suite, stipulate in the HOA that you only need to leave the suite clean, remove loose furniture and branded signage, and patch wall fixings—never demolish the landlord's fit-out.
  3. Early Cash-Out Negotiation: Six to nine months before expiry, ask whether the landlord intends to refurbish or re-let the existing fit-out. Settling make-good for a negotiated cash payment (often 40%–60% of contractor strip-out quotes) saves both parties waste and avoids holdover rent penalties.
  4. IFRS 16 / AASB 16 Accounting Provision: Under Australian Accounting Standard AASB 16, businesses must recognise a Right-of-Use (ROU) asset, a lease liability, and a present-valued make-good restoration provision on the balance sheet from day one.

Bank Guarantees and 10% GST Working Capital Impact

Unlike residential bonds held by a state bond authority, Australian commercial landlords almost universally require an irrevocable, unconditional Bank Guarantee equal to 3 to 6 months of Year-1 gross rent including 10% GST (and sometimes escalating over time). To issue a A$61,417 bank guarantee, your business bank will typically freeze an equivalent A$61,417 in a locked term deposit (or consume equivalent commercial lending facility headroom) plus charge an ongoing facility fee of 1.5% to 3.0% per annum. Furthermore, while GST-registered businesses claim back the 10% GST on commercial rent and outgoings via their quarterly or monthly Business Activity Statement (BAS), you must still fund the gross 110% cash outlay upfront on the first of every month. Confirming both your upfront bank guarantee capital lockup and monthly GST cash-flow gap prevents liquidity surprises in Year 1.

Before signing any commercial Heads of Agreement, request three written disclosures from the leasing agent: (1) the prior two years' audited outgoings reconciliation statements, (2) explicit confirmation of whether land tax or Victoria's CIPT is recoverable under your lease classification, and (3) a capped "clean and tidy" make-good clause backed by a photographic handover report. Those three clauses alone routinely save Australian commercial tenants A$50,000 to A$150,000 over a single five-year term.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much is a standard commercial lease bank guarantee in Australia?

Most Australian commercial landlords require an unconditional bank guarantee equal to 3 to 6 months of Year-1 gross rent (base rent plus outgoings) including 10% GST. The bank normally holds matching cash in a locked term deposit or against your business property security, plus charges an annual bank guarantee fee of roughly 1.5% to 3.0%.

What does a typical make-good cost per square metre in Australia in 2026–27?

A full strip-out make-good back to base-building condition typically costs between A$200 and A$550 per square metre (ex GST), depending on ceiling grid repairs, HVAC rebalancing, data cabling removal, flooring replacement, and painting. Negotiating a 'clean and tidy' clause or photographic condition baseline at the Heads of Agreement stage can cut this cost drastically.

Is GST payable on commercial rent and outgoings in Australia?

Yes. Commercial property leasing is a taxable supply in Australia, so landlords charge 10% GST on base rent, recoverable outgoings, and car parking. GST-registered businesses can generally claim an Input Tax Credit for the full 10% GST on their Business Activity Statement (BAS), making ex-GST figures the true economic cost.

Should I choose a fixed annual increase or a CPI rent review?

Fixed increases (commonly 3.0% to 4.0% per year) provide complete budget certainty for cash-flow forecasting. Uncapped CPI reviews can become very expensive during inflationary periods; if a landlord insists on CPI, negotiate an explicit cap (for example, 'CPI capped at 4.0%').
