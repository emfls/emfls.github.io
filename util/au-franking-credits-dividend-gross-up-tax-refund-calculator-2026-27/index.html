Key takeaways

  • Fully franked dividends gross up by 1.428571× at 30% or 1.333333× at 25%.

  • Eligible individuals and complying SMSFs can receive excess credits as cash after tax liabilities are covered. Ordinary companies can use credits against tax and may convert excess credits to a carried-forward tax loss.

How the calculation works

Franking credit equals cash dividend × the franked fraction × imputation rate ÷ (1 − imputation rate). Add the eligible credit to the cash dividend to get the grossed-up dividend. For individuals, the calculator compares annual bracket tax with and without the grossed-up dividend and does the same for Medicare levy when selected.

An accumulation SMSF with no other taxable income gets a $15 refund on $70 fully franked at 30%, or $10 on $75 at 25%. For the same $100 cash dividend, the refunds are $21.43 and $13.33 respectively. A fully exempt retirement-phase SMSF can receive its full eligible credit in the absence of other tax liabilities.

The individual estimate uses bracket tax and excludes LITO, SAPTO, other offsets, the Medicare levy surcharge and family thresholds. The SMSF scenarios assume a complying fund wholly in the selected phase. The company estimate excludes deductions beyond those already reflected in other taxable income, as well as tax loss and franking account adjustments. Use the actual credit from your dividend statement when preparing a return.

Frequently asked questions

How do I gross up a partly franked dividend?

Multiply the cash dividend by the franked percentage and then by the payer’s imputation rate divided by one minus that rate. Add the eligible credit to the cash dividend. A $700 dividend that is 50% franked at 30% has a $150 credit and an $850 grossed-up amount.

How does the 15% resident tax bracket affect my dividend?

From 1 July 2026 the resident rate on the portion from $18,201 to $45,000 is 15%. The grossed-up dividend is added to your other taxable income, so it can span more than one bracket. Any cash refund depends on credits left after the total applicable tax and Medicare levy are covered.

How much can a complying SMSF get back?

For a fully franked dividend paid as $70 at 30% or $75 at 25%, the credits are $30 and $25 respectively. A fully exempt retirement-phase fund can receive the entire eligible credit if it has no other tax liability. A 15% accumulation fund with no other taxable income has $15 of tax on either $100 grossed-up dividend, leaving $15 or $10 for refund.

Can a company shareholder get a cash refund?

An ordinary company shareholder can use eligible franking credits to reduce its income tax to zero. Excess credits may be converted to a carry-forward tax loss under the company loss rules. The dividend payer’s imputation rate and the shareholder company’s own tax rate are separate inputs.

When does the 45-day holding rule apply?

When the rule applies, hold ordinary shares continuously at risk for at least 45 days, excluding acquisition and disposal days. Certain preference shares require 90 days. The small-shareholder exemption generally applies to eligible individuals with total annual franking credit entitlement below $5,000, subject to related-payment rules.
