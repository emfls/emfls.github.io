UK CGT and BADR questions

How do the 18% and 24% CGT rates work?

Taxable gains on ordinary shares, crypto and UK residential property use the same standard rates. The portion that fits in the unused basic-rate band is taxed at 18% and the rest at 24%. Taxable BADR gains use band space first. Gross Gift Aid and relief-at-source pension payments can extend the band.

How does the proposed April 2027 BADR cut affect a sale?

The Finance Bill proposes replacing the £1 million lifetime limit with £500,000 for disposals from 6 April 2027. Previous qualifying gains count against both limits. A full £500,000 moving from 18% to 24% can cost £30,000 extra for one seller. Check the transitional rules for contracts and reorganisations.

How do capital losses interact with the £3,000 exemption?

Allowable current-year losses are deducted first, even when they use up part of the annual exemption. The exemption is applied next. Brought-forward losses are then used only against gains still above the exemption. This estimate prioritises deductions against gains that would otherwise be taxed at 24%.

Can a transfer to my spouse or civil partner reduce CGT?

An eligible no-gain/no-loss transfer generally passes over part of the original tax basis. A genuine 50/50 ownership split before disposal lets each person use their own £3,000 exemption and available basic-rate band. BADR must be checked separately for each seller. For typical non-EMI shares that includes two years meeting the employment or office, trading-company and 5% personal-company tests, including voting and economic rights.

When do I have to report a UK residential property sale?

Report and pay any CGT due within 60 days of completion. Non-residents generally must report UK property disposals even when there is no tax to pay. Each co-owner reports their own share where required.
