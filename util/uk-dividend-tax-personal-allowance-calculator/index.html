The first £500 of dividends remaining after any Personal Allowance is taxed at 0% and still occupies band space. The rest is taxed from the basic band upwards based on your taxable income. HMRC's example with £29,570 in wages and £3,000 in dividends has £2,500 of dividends taxed at 10.75%, giving £268.75 in dividend tax.

How the Personal Allowance taper works

The standard Personal Allowance is £12,570. It falls by £1 for every £2 of adjusted net income above £100,000 and reaches zero at £125,140. The calculator rounds the reduction down to a whole pound and allocates the remaining allowance across the two income types to minimise total Income Tax. Dividends are taxed after the non-dividend income.

For example, £99,000 in wages and £11,000 in dividends give adjusted net income of £110,000 in this model. The £5,000 reduction leaves a Personal Allowance of £7,570.
