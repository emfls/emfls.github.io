Frequently asked questions

How is provisional income calculated in 2026?

Add adjusted gross income before taxable Social Security, tax-exempt interest, and half of net Social Security benefits. Include any other adjustments required by IRS Publication 915 Worksheet 1 in the non-Social Security income field. Joint filers combine both spouses’ income and benefits.

How do the 50% and 85% Social Security tiers work?

In the first tier, half the combined income above the lower threshold is taxable, capped at half your benefits. In the second tier, the worksheet adds 85% of the income above the upper threshold to the first-tier amount. Overall inclusion is capped at 85% of benefits.

What happens if I file separately and lived with my spouse?

If you lived with your spouse at any time during 2026 and file separately, your base amount is $0. The worksheet takes 85% of positive combined income, capped at 85% of your benefits. If you lived apart all year, use the $25,000 and $34,000 thresholds.

Can a 22% bracket produce a 40.7% tax rate on a withdrawal?

Yes. If the entire next $1,000 is in the 85% inclusion tier before the benefit cap, it adds $850 of taxable Social Security. At a constant 22% federal marginal rate, the $1,850 of additional taxable income generates $407 of tax. Crossing a threshold or reaching a cap changes the result.

Can a Roth IRA withdrawal or QCD avoid the tax torpedo?

A qualified Roth IRA distribution stays out of combined income. An eligible qualified charitable distribution (QCD) can also stay out of combined income when the IRA trustee pays an eligible charity directly. You must be at least 70½ for a QCD, and the charity receives the money. The comparison assumes the full distribution qualifies.
