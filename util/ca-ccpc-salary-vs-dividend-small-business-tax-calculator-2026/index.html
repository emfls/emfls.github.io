What you could give up by choosing dividends

A higher current payout can cost you new RRSP room and another year of CPP or QPP pension accrual. Keeping profits in the corporation preserves business cash and postpones personal tax until a distribution. Compare those effects before choosing how much to pay yourself.

How the estimate works

The corporation deducts gross salary and employer payroll contributions before applying the federal and provincial small-business limits separately. After corporate tax, cash can be distributed as a non-eligible dividend or retained. The salary baseline budgets for employer payroll costs, so gross salary can be lower than the starting profit.

The cash optimizer tests $100 salary increments, pension and business-limit breakpoints, and both all-pay endpoints, then checks $1 increments near the best result. It can choose all salary or all dividends. Use a fixed salary target to model a specific salary and retain part of the remainder. Pension and RRSP targets use the highest affordable salary when the pool falls short.

This planning estimate uses one resident owner, one corporation in the same province, one year of pensionable pay and no other income. It applies standard personal and employment amounts, the enhanced pension deduction, Ontario health premium and surtax, BC tax reduction and Québec abatement. The provincial dividend credits follow the published return rates used in the estimate.

The model excludes eligible dividends, investment-income tax, dividend refunds, tax on split income, alternative minimum tax, EI, other employer levies, income-tested credits and benefits, pension adjustments and existing RRSP room. All dividends are treated as non-eligible, including any payout of general-rate profit. Confirm your SBD eligibility and dividend designation before acting.

Frequently asked questions

Is salary or a non-eligible dividend better for a CCPC owner in 2026?

The larger personal payout depends on province, corporate profit, the available business limits and your salary. Compare current cash alongside RRSP room, pension contributions and the cash your business needs to keep.

What happens when corporate profit exceeds the $500,000 small-business limit?

Salary and employer payroll contributions first reduce active business profit. Each government applies its own available business limit to the remainder. Profit above either limit is taxed at that government’s general corporate rate.

How much CPP or QPP can an owner-manager pay on salary?

At $85,000 of pensionable salary, CPP and CPP2 together reach $4,646.45 per side for $9,292.90 total. At the same salary, QPP and QPP2 reach $4,895.30 per side. Cash dividends have no CPP or QPP contributions.

How much RRSP room does 2026 salary create?

Salary earned in 2026 generally creates 2027 room equal to 18% of earned income, up to $35,390 before pension adjustments. The $33,810 limit applies to the 2026 contribution year. Non-eligible dividends do not create RRSP room.

How does retaining profit defer taxes?

The corporation pays tax on the profit it keeps. Retaining the remaining cash postpones the personal dividend tax. The calculator estimates the extra personal tax that would be due if the same retained cash were paid out this year.
