A SEP balance can increase the tax bill on a Backdoor Roth IRA

Before funding a traditional SEP IRA, consider any Roth IRA conversion you plan to make in the same year. Form 8606 generally combines your traditional, traditional SEP and traditional SIMPLE IRA balances, including the December 31 value. A pre-tax SEP balance can make part of a conversion taxable even if the IRA contribution you just made was nondeductible. Assets held in a Solo 401(k) stay outside this pro-rata calculation.

How the estimate works

Schedule C. The model applies the 92.35% Schedule SE multiplier, then subtracts half of regular self-employment tax from net profit. The 12.4% Social Security portion uses the remaining wage base after other covered wages, and the 2.9% Medicare portion uses the full SE tax base. The maximum employer rate starts at 20% of adjusted profit. The Solo 401(k) applies the Publication 560 worksheet’s additional earned-income constraints when compensation is low.

S or C corporation. Employer contributions start at 25% of eligible W-2 wages, capped at $360,000 of wages. The calculator allocates regular employee deferrals first, then employer profit sharing. Eligible catch-up is calculated separately.

Tax savings. The comparison assumes a traditional SEP IRA and pre-tax Solo 401(k) contributions except where Roth catch-up is required. Voluntary after-tax contributions provide no up-front deduction. The estimate excludes bracket changes, payroll tax savings, QBI interactions, credits and state-specific treatment.

This comparison assumes one owner, unrelated outside employers and one plan choice for the business. Plan terms, plan aggregation, payroll withholding and deadlines can affect the amount you can actually fund.

Frequently asked questions

Who can reach $83,250 in a Solo 401(k) in 2026?

If you turn 60, 61, 62 or 63 in 2026, the enhanced catch-up limit is $11,250. With sufficient compensation and a plan that permits catch-up, you can combine it with up to $72,000 of regular annual additions. At ages 50–59 and 64 or older, the catch-up limit is $8,000.

Why can my Solo 401(k) limit be higher than my SEP IRA limit?

A standard SEP IRA takes employer plan contributions only. A Solo 401(k) can also take employee elective deferrals and eligible catch-up contributions. Your regular $24,500 deferral limit is shared across applicable plans, so a day-job 401(k) can reduce the amount available to your Solo 401(k).

How does a Mega Backdoor Roth use the remaining Solo 401(k) room?

Subtract regular employee deferrals and employer contributions from the lesser of $72,000 or eligible compensation. Eligible catch-up contributions are outside that calculation. Your plan must accept voluntary after-tax contributions and allow a Roth conversion or an eligible distribution for a Roth rollover. Any pre-tax earnings converted may be taxable.

How does a traditional SEP IRA affect a Backdoor Roth IRA?

Form 8606 generally aggregates your traditional, traditional SEP and traditional SIMPLE IRAs, including their December 31 balances, when you convert. A pre-tax SEP balance can make part of the conversion taxable even if the new IRA contribution was nondeductible. Assets that stay in a Solo 401(k) are outside that IRA calculation.

When are 2026 Solo 401(k) catch-up contributions required to be Roth?

The 2026 threshold applies when your 2025 FICA wages from the employer sponsoring the plan exceeded $150,000. Required Roth catch-up contributions use contribution room but provide no current-year income tax savings. Schedule C earnings and wages from an unrelated employer do not count toward this sponsor threshold.
