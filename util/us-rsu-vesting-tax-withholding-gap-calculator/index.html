What the estimate covers

The model treats shares as delivered and taxable at vest. It stacks their value above annual wages, applies the deduction to total taxable income, and calculates the difference in regular income tax. Social Security, regular Medicare and any selected SDI are attributed to the vest using its payroll amounts. The all-in gap also accounts for any difference in Additional Medicare Tax and state income tax.

The model uses marginal schedules and excludes AMT, credit or deduction phaseouts, multistate allocation, local taxes and gains or losses after vest. For this estimate, the NY and MA state rates are editable scenarios. The MA 4% surtax above $1,107,750 and any related withholding can be reflected through those rate overrides. Frequently asked questions

Why can I owe more tax when my employer withheld 22%?

The 22% flat rate is a federal payroll withholding method for eligible supplemental wages up to $1 million. Your regular federal tax on the vest is the difference between federal income tax with and without the RSU wages. One vest can cross several brackets.

What happens if my supplemental wages cross $1 million?

Under the flat-rate method, the portion of the vest that brings cumulative supplemental wages to $1 million uses 22%. The portion above $1 million must use 37%. Include applicable prior bonuses, equity pay and other supplemental wages from the employer and related employers.

How does sell-to-cover affect my delivered shares?

Estimated sell-to-cover divides total payroll withholding by the share price and rounds up to whole shares. Net shares are the vesting shares left after that sale. An actual brokerage sale can differ because of execution prices, fees or fractional-share policies.

Why can Additional Medicare Tax differ from payroll withholding?

An employer starts withholding 0.9% after paying you $200,000 in Medicare wages. The return threshold is $250,000 for joint filers, $125,000 for separate filers and $200,000 for most other filers. Joint filers combine spouses’ Medicare wages on the return.

How does the 110% safe harbor affect estimated payments?

The general federal payment target is the lower of 90% of current tax and 100% of eligible prior-year tax. The prior-year percentage rises to 110% if 2025 AGI exceeded $150,000, or $75,000 for 2026 married filing separately. Timing and the under-$1,000 exception also affect whether payments are required.
