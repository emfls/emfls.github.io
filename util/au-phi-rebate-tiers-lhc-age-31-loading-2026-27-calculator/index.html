What missing your LHC base day can cost

At a joining reference age of 40, a 20% loading adds $420 a year to a $2,100 hospital premium. That adds up to $4,200 over ten years at the same base premium. Compare this with the premiums you would pay to keep cover during those years.

Frequently asked questions

Which income and age determine my private health insurance rebate?

Use income for Medicare levy surcharge purposes to find your tier, then use the age of the oldest person on the policy to find the rate. Couples and single parents use the family thresholds, which rise by $1,500 for each eligible dependent child after the first.

How do I work out my Lifetime Health Cover loading age?

The usual base day is the 1 July after your 31st birthday, with 1 July 2000 as the earliest possible base day. If you miss it, use your age on the 1 July before joining. Each year of age above 30 adds 2 percentage points, up to 70%. A reference age of 40 gives 20%. Some people have a different base day or an exemption.

How does LHC work for couples and when does it end?

The usual two-adult policy averages the individual loadings. A 20% loading and a 0% loading give a 10% policy loading. An individual's loading is removed after 10 continuous years of hospital cover with the loading. Permitted breaks can preserve continuity, but the time without cover does not count towards the 10 years.

Can I claim the rebate on extras or LHC loading?

Eligible premiums for complying hospital and extras cover can attract the rebate. The LHC charge is added to the base hospital premium and receives $0 in rebate.

Will buying private health insurance save me the Medicare levy surcharge?

Appropriate private hospital cover for you and any spouse or dependants can avoid the surcharge for the days everyone is covered. Extras alone do not qualify. The estimate uses your surcharge income as a proxy for the amount subject to MLS and assumes no personal exemptions.
