2026 Section 83(b) Election Calculator

Compare IRS Form 15620 with standard vesting for founder restricted stock and shares received from early-exercised nonstatutory stock options (NSOs).

Key takeaways

  • A timely election taxes the spread at stock transfer and generally eliminates compensation tax as those shares vest. A small spread today can mean a much smaller cash outlay than tax on appreciated, illiquid stock at vesting.
  • Count 30 calendar days from the actual stock transfer. For an early exercise, use the date the shares transferred. The IRS instructions allow the next eligible day when day 30 falls on a weekend or legal holiday.
  • Compare the tax through sale with the cash you could lose upfront if you forfeit the shares or the company fails.

How the estimate works

At transfer or vesting, compensation equals the positive spread above your purchase price times the applicable shares. The estimate applies the ordinary and effective payroll rates to that spread. Basis equals purchase price plus compensation recognized per share.

Without the election, the model allocates 25% of the shares per year, rounding cumulative vesting to whole shares. At sale it subtracts each lot’s basis and nets gains and losses from those lots. Short-term gains use the entered ordinary rate. Net Exit Tax Saved equals the difference between the two paths’ combined compensation and sale taxes.

The estimate uses constant marginal rates and nominal USD, with a single sale after full vesting. It excludes state tax on long-term gains, QSBS exclusions, progressive bracket stacking, AMT, outside gains or losses, withholding credits and time value. The result is a planning estimate for comparing scenarios.

Section 83(b) FAQ

Which startup equity qualifies for this 83(b) comparison?

Founder restricted stock and stock transferred on early exercise of an NSO can qualify when the shares remain substantially nonvested. Standard unexercised NSOs, unexercised ISOs and RSUs provide no qualifying stock transfer for this calculator. Shares from early-exercised ISOs require separate regular-tax and AMT analysis.

When and how do I file Form 15620?

File within 30 calendar days after the stock transfer, subject to the IRS weekend and legal-holiday rule. Submit through an IRS Online Account or mail the signed election to the IRS office where you file your federal return. Certified mail provides a mailing record. Give a copy to the employer or other service recipient, and to the transferee if different.

Why can standard vesting generate a cash tax bill before sale?

Each lot's positive spread over the purchase price generally becomes compensation when the shares substantially vest. Income and applicable payroll taxes can be due while the shares are still illiquid. A timely election generally shifts that compensation event to the transfer date.

Does the election start the capital gains and QSBS holding periods?

The federal holding period for restricted stock generally begins just after transfer with a timely election and just after vesting without it. Long-term capital gains normally require more than one year. Section 1202 applies separately to qualifying stock and has three-, four- and five-year exclusion tiers for stock acquired in 2026.

What happens to the upfront tax if I leave or the startup fails?

Forfeiting unvested shares does not reverse the election's compensation income. The forfeiture loss generally equals the amount you paid minus any recovery. If your vested shares instead become worthless, adjusted basis generally determines the loss and capital-loss rules determine how much you can use.
