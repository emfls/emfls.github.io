Canada RRSP Meltdown vs. Deferred RRIF Calculator

Compare drawing down your RRSP in your 60s and delaying CPP and OAS to 70 with keeping the RRSP intact until conversion at 71. See how each plan affects tax, OAS recovery and your estate under 2026 rules.

Key takeaways

  • Early withdrawals can fill the 14% federal bracket up to $58,523 before CPP, OAS and mandatory RRIF income overlap. Provincial tax also applies.
  • OAS recovery starts above $95,323 of 2026 individual net income. Starting benefits at 70 raises CPP by 42% and OAS by 36% compared with taking both at 65.
  • Converting at 71 usually starts mandatory RRIF withdrawals the following year. A large remaining balance may raise the tax on the final return unless a rollover applies.

Watch for loss aversion

Paying tax on an early withdrawal can feel like a loss, while a growing RRSP can feel like a gain even though taxes are due later. Compare spendable cash and the after-tax estate together. Try an earlier death age or a lower return to see how giving up five years of pension payments or running short on cash changes the result.

How the projection works

  • One person, using 2026 brackets and benefits in constant 2026 dollars throughout. Benefits are modeled for the full year at the listed age. The model assumes your January 1 age is one year below the age you turn that year, so the first minimum uses the age-71 factor on the opening balance in the year you turn 72.
  • Registered withdrawals precede annual growth. Annual spending is funded by after-tax income, then other savings, then TFSA withdrawals. The model keeps the selected RRSP withdrawal schedule and shows a funding gap if those sources run out. Gaps reduce net wealth dollar for dollar, without assumed borrowing costs.
  • Surplus first goes into available TFSA room, then other savings. TFSA room carries forward and withdrawals restore room the next year. Other savings grow at the after-tax return you enter.
  • Regular income tax uses federal and provincial basic personal amounts. Age and pension credits, pension splitting, GIS, fees, and other credits or deductions are outside the estimate. OAS recovery is deducted when computing taxable income and then added to annual tax. The model treats recovery as a liability for the income year. CRA repayment rules
  • The model assumes death at year-end and includes the remaining registered balance on the final return. It assumes no eligible rollover or other estate costs. The age-70 QPP deferral uplift matches CPP for this comparison, while QPP can be deferred further. Retraite Québec timing

Frequently asked questions

What does an RRSP meltdown do?

It uses early retirement years to withdraw RRSP money within a target income band. The withdrawals are taxable. Surplus after spending can go to a TFSA if you have contribution room.

Do RRIF minimum withdrawals begin at age 71?

An RRSP must mature by the end of the year you turn 71. If you open a RRIF that year, your first mandatory withdrawal is the following year, based on the opening account value and applicable opening-year age.

How is the 2026 OAS clawback calculated?

The annual estimate is 15% of individual net income above $95,323, capped at the OAS received. CRA deducts the repayment from taxable income and adds it to tax payable.

How much does waiting until 70 raise CPP and OAS?

Compared with starting at 65, waiting until 70 raises CPP by 42% and OAS by 36%. OAS increases a further 10% at 75. Deferral gives up the payments you could have received at ages 65 through 69.

How does an RRSP or RRIF affect the final return?

Without an eligible rollover, the remaining account value is generally included in income on the final return. The incremental bill depends on other income, provincial and federal brackets, and any remaining OAS subject to recovery.
