Marriage Allowance FAQ

How does the £1,260 Marriage Allowance transfer work?

The lower earner transfers the full £1,260, leaving a Personal Allowance of £11,310. The recipient gets a tax reduction of up to £252, capped by their Income Tax bill. The donor’s PAYE code normally ends in N and the recipient’s in M.

Who qualifies for Marriage Allowance in 2026/27?

You must be married or in a civil partnership and cannot also claim Married Couple’s Allowance. The donor normally earns up to £12,570. With a standard Personal Allowance, a recipient with whole-pound earnings typically qualifies from £12,571 to £50,270 in England, Wales and Northern Ireland, or to £43,662 in Scotland. Qualifying pension contributions or Gift Aid can affect which tax band applies.

Can transferring still save money if the donor earns £12,000?

Yes, if the recipient can use all £252. The donor pays tax on £690. At 20% that costs £138, leaving a £114 gain for the couple. At Scotland’s 19% starter rate it costs £131.10, leaving a £120.90 gain.

How much can I backdate, and when does 2022/23 expire?

Eligible claims for 2022/23 through 2025/26 can reduce the recipient’s tax by up to £1,008 in total. The 2022/23 deadline is 5 April 2027. Add up to £252 for 2026/27 for a maximum of £1,260 across five years. Any donor tax reduces the couple’s net saving.

How do I claim, and how will HMRC apply the reduction?

The lower earner makes the claim. Follow HMRC’s application instructions for online, Self Assessment or backdated claims. HMRC can reflect current relief through a tax code or Self Assessment. The prior-year figure estimates the recipient’s tax reduction. The amount refunded depends on their tax account.
