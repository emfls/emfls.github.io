2026 RRSP vs TFSA and OAS Clawback Calculator

Compare how much you keep in retirement after income tax and Old Age Security (OAS) recovery tax.

Key takeaways

  • Compare the rates. With the same after-tax cash and return, the RRSP with its tax refund reinvested wins when your working tax rate exceeds your effective retirement withdrawal rate. Equal rates produce a tie.
  • TFSA withdrawals are tax-free. They do not increase net world income for OAS recovery. The TFSA wins if the effective retirement rate on the RRSP is higher than your working tax rate.
  • OAS can change the winner. The 2026 recovery rate is 15% on income above $95,323 until your OAS has been fully recovered. In the additive model, a 30% retirement tax rate can become 45%.

How the comparison works

  1. The calculator converts a pre-tax budget to after-tax cash using your working rate. It reduces the common cash budget when necessary to keep both contributions within available room.
  2. For the reinvested-refund RRSP, it divides the common cash by one minus the working rate. The resulting contribution uses the same cash after the refund. This assumes you can fund the gross contribution up front or reinvest an equivalent refund immediately.
  3. Each account compounds at the same rate up to retirement. The calculator divides the balance into equal yearly withdrawals with no growth during withdrawals.
  4. Additional OAS recovery is the difference between recovery with the RRSP withdrawal and recovery on your baseline income alone. The effective withdrawal rate is incremental income tax plus incremental OAS recovery divided by gross withdrawals.

This planning estimate holds the 2026 OAS threshold, benefit, baseline income and your entered tax rates constant. It excludes future indexation, changes in tax brackets, GIS, other credits, and RRIF minimum withdrawals. The default additive option approximates the combined cost. The deduction option reflects the effect of the CRA Line 23500 deduction at a flat rate. Verify your actual available room before contributing.

Frequently asked questions

What are the 2026 RRSP and TFSA contribution limits?

New RRSP room is generally 18% of 2025 earned income up to $33,810, plus unused room and subject to applicable adjustments. The TFSA adds $7,000 in annual room for eligible savers. The $109,000 sum since 2009 assumes eligibility every year; your remaining room depends on your contribution and withdrawal history.

How does the $95,323 OAS clawback threshold work?

For 2026 income, the recovery tax is 15% of adjusted net world income above $95,323, capped at the recoverable benefit. The income year determines the July 2027 to June 2028 payment period. The calculator charges each RRSP scenario only for the extra recovery its withdrawals cause.

Do TFSA withdrawals affect my OAS payments?

No. TFSA withdrawals are generally tax-free and are excluded from income used for federal income-tested benefits such as OAS. In this comparison, a TFSA withdrawal adds zero income tax and zero OAS recovery.

When do an RRSP and a TFSA produce the same net wealth?

They tie when the tax rate saved on an RRSP contribution equals the effective tax and OAS recovery rate on its withdrawal, assuming the same after-tax cash, identical returns and a fully reinvested RRSP refund. With no incremental OAS recovery, equal working and retirement tax rates give parity.

What changes if I spend the RRSP tax refund?

The refund-spent scenario starts with the same cash as the TFSA and leaves the tax refund available to spend today. That reduces the invested balance and foregoes growth. The calculator shows the difference in retirement wealth after allowing for each RRSP scenario’s own income tax and OAS recovery.
