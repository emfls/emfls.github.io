Key takeaways

  • A £100,000 single premium accrues £5,000 of tax-deferred withdrawal allowance per policy year for up to 20 years. Earlier withdrawals can use some of that allowance.

  • The full UK-taxable gain still counts toward the £60,000 Child Benefit charge threshold and the £100,000 Personal Allowance taper.

Check the cost before you withdraw

A large part withdrawal can produce a taxable excess even if the bond has fallen in value. Compare a part withdrawal and a full surrender before instructing your insurer. An excess normally arises at the end of the policy year, and deferred withdrawals are included when calculating the final gain.

Frequently asked questions

How much can I withdraw under the 5% rule?

A single premium accrues a 5% tax-deferred allowance each policy year for up to 20 years, including the policy year of payment. Subtract allowance already used. Unused allowance carries forward while the bond remains in force.

When is a part withdrawal taxable?

An excess over the available allowance normally gives rise to a gain at the end of the policy year. If the policy ends in the same final insurance year, the withdrawals are included in the final gain calculation.

How is the full surrender gain calculated?

Add the surrender proceeds and earlier withdrawals, then deduct the original premium and prior chargeable gains included in earlier tax years. A negative result can warrant a separate check for deficiency relief.

Does top slicing restore my Personal Allowance?

The annual-equivalent calculation recalculates the Personal Allowance, Personal Savings Allowance and savings starting rate with the slice included. Full adjusted net income still determines the tax-year Personal Allowance and potential High Income Child Benefit Charge.

How do non-UK resident years affect the calculation?

Where time apportionment applies, reduce the gain by the proportion of foreign days in the material interest period. Reduce the certificate's complete years separately by whole years of non-UK residence.

Scope and HMRC guidance

This estimate covers one chargeable event for an individual with a single-premium offshore policy, standard allowance rules and no tax treated as paid. It excludes multiple gains in the same tax year, dividends, deductions such as Gift Aid and relief-at-source pension contributions, deficiency relief, Scottish rates and special residency rules. Use the insurer’s chargeable event certificate for reporting figures.
