2026 Rule 72(t) SEPP Calculator

Compare three IRS withdrawal methods, size an IRA carve-out and see when the Rule of 55 may give you more flexibility.

Key takeaways

The 2026 fixed-method rate ceiling is at least 5%. It rises when either eligible month’s 120% mid-term AFR is higher. RMD payments are recalculated each year.

SEPP generally locks the account until the later of five full years or age 59½. The Rule of 55 has no SEPP schedule and applies to qualifying withdrawals from the employer plan tied to your separation.

Use a reasonable valuation for the fixed methods. The IRS treats balances from the prior December 31 through the first distribution date as reasonable. For RMD, the prior December 31 balance generally applies.

Early modification can undo years of tax savings

Taking an extra withdrawal, missing a payment or changing the scheduled account balance can trigger the 10% recapture tax on prior eligible taxable payments, plus interest. The modification year’s taxable distributions may also face a separate 10% additional tax.

During the lock, you may switch once from fixed amortization or annuitization to RMD. The switch is irrevocable and the original lock end date still applies.

Frequently asked questions

Is 5% the minimum rate I must use for SEPP?

No. For the two fixed methods, Notice 2022-6 lets you choose a rate up to the greater of 5% or the annual 120% federal mid-term AFR for either of the two months immediately before your first distribution. A lower rate is permitted. RMD uses no interest rate.

How do the three methods calculate my annual payment?

RMD divides the applicable balance by a life expectancy factor recalculated each year. Fixed amortization uses a selected life expectancy and rate. Fixed annuitization divides the starting balance by the present value of a single-life annuity based on IRS mortality rates. Both fixed methods generally repeat the same annual amount.

Can I stop after five annual payments or switch to RMD?

The lock ends on the later of the fifth anniversary of your first payment or the day you reach age 59½. You can switch once from either fixed method to RMD without recapture. The switch preserves the original lock date and commits you to the RMD method through the rest of the required period.

What happens if I modify the schedule too early?

A disqualifying modification can recapture the 10% additional tax that would have applied to eligible taxable payments in prior years, plus interest for the deferral periods. Taxable withdrawals in the modification year can separately face the 10% tax when no other exception applies.

Does Rule of 55 replace SEPP for my IRA?

The general Rule of 55 applies to eligible employer-plan withdrawals after separation during or after the calendar year you turn 55. It has no SEPP payment schedule and does not apply to IRAs. Plan withdrawal terms still apply, and separate rules can apply to certain public safety employees and firefighters.
