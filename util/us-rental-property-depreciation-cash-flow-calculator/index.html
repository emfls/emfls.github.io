How the estimate works

Building basis equals the purchase price plus capitalizable acquisition costs minus the land allocation. The first-year rate comes from IRS Table A-6. Each full year uses basis ÷ 27.5, or about 3.636%, and the final year absorbs the remaining cents. January through June placements finish in year 28. July through December placements finish in year 29 and include a full year 28.

Effective rent reflects vacancy. NOI subtracts operating expenses before debt. Cap rate divides NOI by purchase price. Cash-on-cash return divides cash flow after debt service by invested cash. Taxable rental income subtracts interest and depreciation from NOI. Use rent and expenses for the modeled period. The loan schedule assumes the first 12 scheduled payments fall in projection year 1 and retains monthly cent rounding. A partial calendar tax year may require adjusting the interest from your lender statement separately.

Scope and assumptions

Federal planning estimate for one standard GDS residential rental held through the modeled year. Assumes the loss clears basis and at-risk restrictions, the entered passive income is available and the full special allowance has no competing claims. Personal use, ADS, improvements, disposal-year proration, state tax and prior losses require separate calculations. A sale can have unrecaptured §1250 gain taxed federally at up to 25%, depending on allowable depreciation and realized gain.

Frequently asked questions

How much first-year depreciation does MACRS allow?

Table A-6 ranges from 3.485% for January to 0.152% for December under the mid-month convention. Use the month the building became available for rent.

How does the active-participation allowance phase out?

For most filers, the $25,000 allowance falls by 50 cents per dollar of modified AGI over $100,000 and ends at $150,000. MFS filers who lived apart all year phase out from $12,500 at $50,000 to zero at $75,000. Other MFS filers receive no special allowance.

Why can a rental with positive cash flow report a tax loss?

Depreciation reduces taxable income without a current cash payment. Conversely, mortgage principal uses cash but is excluded from the interest deduction.
