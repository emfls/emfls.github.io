Frequently Asked Questions: HMRC Self Assessment Penalties

Can I appeal an HMRC late filing penalty?

Yes. If you had a "reasonable excuse" (such as unexpected hospitalization, serious bereavement, severe postal delays, or major IT failure on HMRC's digital service), you can lodge an appeal within 30 days using Form SA370 or your online tax account.

What qualifies for HMRC's online Time to Pay service?

You can set up a Time to Pay arrangement online without speaking to an adviser if: you owe £30,000 or less, have filed your latest return, are within 60 days of the payment deadline, and can pay off the debt within 12 months.

Why did the HMRC interest rate increase to 8.00%?

By law, HMRC charges interest pegged at the Bank of England base rate plus 4.00 percentage points. With the base rate at 4.00%, the statutory late payment interest rate is 8.00% per annum.

Does HMRC charge interest on penalties themselves?

Yes. If late filing penalties or late payment surcharges are issued and not paid within 30 days of the penalty notice, HMRC begins charging 8.00% interest on the unpaid penalties as well.

If I submit a draft return with estimated figures before 31 January, will I be penalized?

No. HMRC accepts provisional figures on your return to meet the 31 January deadline, provided you declare they are provisional and amend them as soon as exact figures become available. This protects you from the £100 late filing fine.
