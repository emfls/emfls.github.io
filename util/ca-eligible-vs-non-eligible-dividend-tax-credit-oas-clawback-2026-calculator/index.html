  • $10,000 in eligible dividends adds $13,800 to income before OAS repayment. Non-eligible dividends of the same amount add $11,500.

How the estimate works

Income tax is the difference between two estimates, with and without the dividend. Both use progressive brackets and basic personal credits. The model includes the Quebec abatement, Ontario surtax and Health Premium, and basic Ontario and B.C. tax reductions. It omits other credits and tax reductions, including age and pension credits, alternative minimum tax and tax on split income. It treats the other-income input as both the taxable and OAS income baseline before OAS repayment.

OAS repayment is 15% of income above $95,323, capped at the gross pension. Income tax accounts for the repayment deduction. The gross-up-only figure compares capped OAS repayments using the grossed-up amount and a hypothetical cash-only amount. The all-in rate adds the entire incremental repayment to the incremental income tax and divides by cash received.

TFSA and capital-gain rows assume the same dollar amount of dividends or realized gain. TFSA treatment assumes qualified investments and available contribution room. The estimate excludes transaction costs and taxes from transferring existing holdings. The 2026 OAS income threshold corresponds to the July 2027–June 2028 withholding period.

Frequently asked questions

Which dividends qualify for the 38% gross-up?

Use the designation on your T5 or other information slip. Eligible dividends often come from listed Canadian corporations and can also come from a private corporation. Dividends paid from small-business-rate earnings are commonly other than eligible.

Where do I report Canadian dividends on my tax return?

Line 12000 includes taxable eligible and other-than-eligible Canadian dividends. Line 12010 also reports the other-than-eligible portion separately. Claim the federal dividend tax credit on Line 40425 and the provincial or territorial credit on the applicable return.

When can eligible dividends produce zero income tax?

When basic personal credits and dividend tax credits cover both levels of tax. For example, the model estimates zero regular income tax on $40,000 of eligible Canadian dividends for a B.C. resident with zero other income and no OAS. The result depends on your jurisdiction and other income.

Why can a dividend tax credit still leave me with an OAS repayment?

The taxable dividend enters income before OAS repayment. The dividend tax credit reduces income tax and leaves that income figure unchanged. Each dollar above the 2026 threshold of $95,323 triggers 15 cents of recovery until the pension cap is reached.

How do a TFSA and a realized capital gain compare for OAS?

Qualified investment income inside a TFSA generally adds zero to OAS recovery income. A realized capital gain in a taxable account generally adds half the gain. In a taxable account, each dollar of eligible Canadian dividends adds $1.38.
