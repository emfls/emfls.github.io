Key takeaways

  • A $2,500 eligible deposit earns $500 in Basic CESG. With enough unused grant room, a $5,000 deposit can earn $1,000 in Basic CESG.
  • Families that meet the income rules can receive up to $100 extra CESG per year and up to $2,000 in CLB. The bond requires no personal deposit.
  • The $50,000 lifetime limit applies to personal contributions across all RESPs for one beneficiary. Government grants and investment growth sit outside that limit.

Projection assumptions

  • Your plan repeats your deposit through age 17, within available RESP room. The grant-focused plan targets the available annual match, including partial catch-up, and may spread smaller deposits to capture more Additional CESG when contribution room is tight.
  • The hybrid starts at $16,500, then uses $2,500 for up to 13 years and $1,000 in year 15 if time and room permit. The day-one lump sum fills available room immediately. A late start or existing contributions can prevent the hybrid from reaching $7,200.
  • Income, family size, the expected rate of return and the 2026 program settings stay constant. The scenario assumes qualifying Canadian residency, SIN, RESP and caregiver conditions. For 17+ children, future CLB is omitted pending confirmation.
  • Deposits, grants and confirmed unclaimed CLB are credited at the start of each modeled year. Growth is applied annually through the year the child turns 17. Amounts are nominal and before withdrawal taxes, fees and over-contribution tax. Provincial incentives are excluded.

Frequently asked questions

How do I get the full $7,200 CESG?

At the Basic CESG rate of 20%, you need $36,000 of matched contributions spread across eligible years. Additional CESG shares the $7,200 lifetime limit and can reduce the contributions needed.

Can I catch up on missed CESG years?

Yes. With at least $500 of unused Basic CESG room, $5,000 of contributions can receive $1,000 in Basic CESG in one calendar year. Partial room supports a smaller catch-up. Additional CESG expires each year.

Can a child receive CESG at age 16 or 17?

Yes, if at least $2,000 was contributed and retained by the end of the calendar year the child turned 15, or at least $100 was contributed and retained in each of any four earlier calendar years, including the year the child turned 15.

Can I get the Canada Learning Bond without contributing?

Yes. An eligible child can receive $500 for the first eligible year and $100 per later eligible year through the year they turn 15, up to $2,000. Past eligible amounts can be claimed retroactively.

Why compare a $16,500 hybrid with a $50,000 lump sum?

Starting from birth with Basic CESG only, $16,500 in year one, $2,500 in each of the next 13 years, and $1,000 in year 15 totals $50,000 and can earn $7,200 in CESG. A single $50,000 birth-year deposit earns $500 in Basic CESG. Earlier compounding can still favour the lump sum at some returns.
