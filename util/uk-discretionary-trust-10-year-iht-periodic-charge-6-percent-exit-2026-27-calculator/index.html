UK Discretionary Trust IHT Calculator 2026/27

Estimate the lifetime entry charge, the 10-year periodic charge of up to 6%, and the tax on a capital distribution.

Key takeaways

  • Entry: the amount above the available £325,000 nil-rate band is taxed at 20% if trustees pay, or an effective 25% if the settlor pays.
  • Every 10 years: the periodic rate is up to 6%. Prior chargeable transfers and qualifying exits can reduce the available band. Time relief can reduce the payable rate.
  • On exit: the rate scales with completed three-month quarters. A qualifying exit within the first three months of a cycle has no exit charge.

Plan for cash to pay the anniversary bill

A periodic charge can fall due while the trust holds illiquid assets. If trustees also pay exit tax from remaining relevant property, more cash leaves the trust than the beneficiary receives. A settlor’s death within seven years can trigger additional IHT at death rates, after credit for lifetime tax and any applicable taper relief.

Frequently asked questions

When does the 10-year charge reach 6%?

In this model, the payable rate reaches 6% when the available nil-rate band is zero and the assets qualify for all 40 quarters. With an available band, the rate is lower, and assets that became relevant property later may qualify for time relief.

Why does the entry charge rise when the settlor pays?

Tax paid by the settlor increases the loss to their estate. Grossing up the amount above the available band gives an equivalent rate of 25% on that excess. If the settlor dies within seven years, the transfer can be reassessed at death rates, with credit for lifetime tax and taper relief where applicable.

How do exits before and after the first anniversary differ?

A first-cycle exit uses a rate based on historic values settled and the available band. A later exit generally starts from the last anniversary rate before time relief, with adjustments in some cases. The base rate is then scaled by the number of completed quarters out of 40.

When is an exit charge grossed up?

Grossing applies when trustees pay the tax from remaining relevant property. The tax then equals the distribution to the beneficiary multiplied by the exit rate and divided by one minus that rate. The first three months after setup or an anniversary are generally exempt.

What does the 30-year comparison include?

Both routes start with the same total budget and grow at the same assumed rate. The trust route pays entry tax and three periodic charges. The estate route pays 40% on year-30 assets above £325,000. The illustration holds rates and the band constant and assumes no interim distributions or additional transfers.
