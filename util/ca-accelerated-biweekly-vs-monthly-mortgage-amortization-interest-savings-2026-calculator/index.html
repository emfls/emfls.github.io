Canadian Mortgage Regulations & Interest Act

Why is Regular Bi-Weekly different from Accelerated Bi-Weekly?

Banks calculate regular bi-weekly payments as (Monthly Payment × 12) ÷ 26. This means you pay the exact same annual total as a monthly mortgage, saving virtually no interest. In contrast, accelerated bi-weekly simply divides the monthly payment by 2, meaning you make 26 half-payments (equal to 13 full monthly payments) each year.

How does the Canadian Interest Act calculate semi-annual compounding?

Under Section 6 of the Interest Act, Canadian mortgages calculate effective interest rates as (1 + annual_rate/2)^(1/6) - 1 for monthly payments, and (1 + annual_rate/2)^(1/13) - 1 for bi-weekly payments. This prevents lenders from charging continuous or monthly compound interest.

Can you combine accelerated bi-weekly with annual lump sums?

Yes. Most Canadian closed mortgages allow annual lump-sum prepayments between 10% and 20% of the original principal without penalty. Applying even $5,000 to $10,000 per year alongside accelerated bi-weekly payments can cut your 25-year mortgage down to under 16 years.

Assumptions & Statutory Notes

Calculations adhere to Canadian federal Interest Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. I-15) s.6 semi-annual compounding standards. Estimates do not include property taxes, CMHC insurance premiums, or lender discharge fees.
