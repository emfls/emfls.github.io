Key takeaways

  • At 2024 adjusted family net income of $117,045 or less, $1,500 can earn a $3,500 regular 2026 Canada Disability Savings Grant (CDSG).
  • The Canada Disability Savings Bond (CDSB) can pay $1,000 for 2026 with no contribution when that income is $38,237 or less.
  • Eligible unused entitlements can raise 2026 payments to $10,500 in grants and $11,000 in bonds. The lifetime ceilings are $70,000 and $20,000.

Frequently asked questions

Which tax return determines my 2026 RDSP grant and bond?

Use 2024 adjusted family net income. Through the year the beneficiary turns 18, use parent or guardian family income. From the year they turn 19, use the beneficiary’s family income, including a spouse or common-law partner where applicable. The beneficiary should start filing in the year they turn 17.

How much should I contribute to get the full regular 2026 grant?

With available lifetime room and income of $117,045 or less, $1,500 in contributions can earn $3,500. Above that income, $1,000 can earn $1,000. Unused grant entitlement can change the contribution needed, while an eligible bond requires no contribution.

How does RDSP carry-forward work?

Unused entitlements from up to 10 preceding eligible years may be paid through the year the beneficiary turns 49. The grant uses the highest matching rate first and the oldest entitlement first within each rate. With carry-forward, annual payments can reach $10,500 in grants and $11,000 in bonds, subject to lifetime limits.

How much grant or bond will I repay if I withdraw early?

For a standard withdrawal before the calendar year the beneficiary turns 60, multiply the withdrawal by three and cap it at the issuer’s assistance holdback amount for that date. The holdback generally reflects grants and bonds paid in the preceding 10 years, less applicable repayments. A loss of DTC eligibility can change the period.

Is the projected RDSP balance at age 60 tax-free?

The projection shows the account balance before withdrawals and tax. Personal contributions generally come out tax-free. Grants, bonds, investment earnings and applicable rollovers are generally taxable to the beneficiary when paid out.
