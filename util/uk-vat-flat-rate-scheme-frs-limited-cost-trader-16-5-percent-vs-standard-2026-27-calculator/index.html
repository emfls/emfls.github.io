Key takeaways

At 16.5%, the Flat Rate Scheme (FRS) takes 19.8% of net sales when you charge 20% VAT. Standard VAT can leave you with more cash if you regularly reclaim VAT on expenses. The annual goods test uses the higher of 2% of VAT-inclusive turnover and £1,000.

VAT Flat Rate Scheme FAQs

When does the 16.5% limited cost rate apply?

For an annual VAT return, you are a limited cost trader if VAT-inclusive relevant goods cost less than the higher of 2% of VAT-inclusive flat-rate turnover and £1,000. Test each return period separately. For a quarterly return the cash floor is £250.

Which purchases count as relevant goods?

Count eligible goods used exclusively for business, with VAT included. Services, software downloads, rent, broadband, capital goods and food or drink for you or your staff are excluded. Stock for resale can count when selling those goods is your main business activity.

Can I still claim the first-year VAT discount?

Yes. Eligible businesses reduce the applicable flat rate by one percentage point until the first anniversary of VAT registration. This can reduce 16.5% to 15.5%. The 16.5% limited cost rate was introduced in April 2017.

Can several capital purchases be combined to reach £2,000?

The minimum is £2,000 including VAT for each qualifying single purchase. A package bought together may be a single purchase under normal VAT rules. Separate purchases are tested individually.

Why can standard VAT leave more cash even at a 16.5% flat rate?

The flat rate is applied to gross turnover. At 20% output VAT, 16.5% of gross sales equals 19.8% of net sales, leaving only £2 per £1,000 of net sales before accounting for input VAT lost under the scheme.
