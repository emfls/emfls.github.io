Key takeaways

In the default four-year example, setting aside A$15,000 of gross salary each year produces an estimated A$12,661 more for your deposit through FHSS and bank savings combined.

  • Eligible voluntary contributions are capped at A$15,000 per financial year and A$50,000 in total per buyer.
  • Two individually eligible buyers can count up to A$30,000 a year and A$100,000 in total between them.
  • A 30% marginal rate plus a 2% Medicare levy gives an estimated 2% tax rate on the assessable FHSS release after the offset.

How the comparison works

Both strategies start with the same gross salary budget. The bank receives after-tax money, with interest compounded monthly after estimated tax. On the FHSS path, the chosen before-tax share is directed to super and the remaining share becomes an after-tax contribution. Cash above the modeled limits goes to the same bank account. Prior eligible contributions use up cap space and are excluded from both projected balances.

The model assumes monthly deposits from July 2026 and a determination at the end of June in the selected year. It compounds one assumed SIC rate daily on the releasable contribution amounts, rounding deemed earnings down per contribution. The 7.1% default is a projection assumption. Actual deemed earnings depend on ATO quarterly rates and contribution timing.

Estimated release tax applies the release-year marginal rate plus Medicare levy minus the 30% offset, floored at zero, to 85% of eligible before-tax contributions plus all deemed earnings. The comparison uses flat rates and holds caps constant. It excludes changes in tax brackets, additional super taxes, carry-forward cap amounts and personal after-tax cap adjustments. Frequently asked questions

Can a couple use the FHSS scheme together?

Yes. Each eligible buyer applies separately and can count up to A$15,000 in voluntary contributions per financial year and A$50,000 overall. Two eligible buyers can combine their releases for one home, but each buyer uses their own limits.

Which super contributions can I release?

You can release 85% of eligible salary sacrifice or personal deductible contributions and 100% of eligible personal after-tax contributions, plus associated earnings. Compulsory employer Super Guarantee contributions are excluded from FHSS and still count toward the general concessional cap.

Is the deemed earnings rate my super fund’s return?

No. The ATO uses the Shortfall Interest Charge rate for each day of the calculation period. For new contributions, the period runs from the first day of the contribution month through the determination day. The calculator applies one editable assumed rate across the projection.

How does the 30% FHSS tax offset work?

The assessable amount includes 85% of eligible before-tax contributions and all associated earnings. With a 30% marginal income tax rate and a 2% Medicare levy, the flat-rate estimate is 2% of the assessable amount after the offset. At 37% plus a 2% levy, it is 9%. Final tax depends on your release-year assessment.

What happens if I release money and do not buy a home?

If the purchase or construction falls through, check the ATO deadlines for recontributing the required amount and notifying the ATO. If you keep the money without meeting the scheme requirements, an additional FHSS tax of 20% of the assessable released amount can apply.
