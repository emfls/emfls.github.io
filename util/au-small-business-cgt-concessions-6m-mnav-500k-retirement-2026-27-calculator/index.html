Australia Small Business CGT Concessions Guide (ATO Division 152)

Selling a small business in Australia can trigger enormous capital gains tax liabilities. Fortunately, Division 152 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1997 (ITAA 1997) provides four interconnected statutory concessions that can reduce, defer, or completely eliminate capital gains on active business assets.

1. The Two Gateway Qualification Tests

Before any concession can be accessed, your enterprise must pass one of the two strict statutory gateway hurdles:

  • Small Business Entity Test: Aggregated annual turnover of your business and its affiliates must be less than $2,000,000 for the financial year.
  • Maximum Net Asset Value (MNAV) Test: The total market value of net assets owned by you, your affiliates, and connected entities cannot exceed $6,000,000 immediately prior to the disposal event. Personal superannuation balances and your primary family home are legally excluded from this calculation.

2. The Four Sequential Relief Concessions

  • 15-Year Exemption: If you are aged 55 or older, retiring, and your business has continuously owned the active asset for at least 15 years, the entire capital gain is 100% tax-free. You do not need to use any other concessions or touch your retirement cap.
  • 50% Active Asset Reduction: Reduces the capital gain by an additional 50%. When combined with the 50% general discount for individuals and trusts, the taxable gain is slashed to 25% of the original figure.
  • $500,000 Lifetime Retirement Exemption: Allows up to $500,000 of capital gains per individual to be sheltered. If you are under 55 years old, this money must be contributed directly into a complying superannuation fund (which counts toward your $1,935,000 lifetime CGT cap, not your standard non-concessional caps). If aged 55 or older, you can receive the funds as tax-free cash.
  • Small Business Rollover: Allows you to defer any remaining taxable gain for up to two years while you acquire replacement commercial assets.
