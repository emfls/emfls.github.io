Frequently asked questions

Can a household get both benefits in 2026–27?

Yes. If you qualify for both, an A$1,000 personal after-tax contribution and a separate A$3,000 contribution to your spouse’s super can produce up to A$1,040 in combined benefit. The ATO pays up to A$500 into your super, and the spouse offset can reduce your tax by up to A$540.

When does the A$20 minimum co-contribution apply?

If you meet the eligibility rules and your calculated co-contribution is positive but below A$20, the ATO pays A$20. The income cut-out of A$64,293 still applies. The ATO rounds positive payments up to a multiple of five cents.

Which income figures should I enter?

For both tests, start with assessable income, reportable fringe benefits and reportable employer super contributions, and exclude assessable First Home Super Saver releases. For the co-contribution threshold, also subtract allowable business deductions and reduce reportable employer super contributions by excess concessional contributions, down to zero. The 10% work or business income test uses income before allowable business deductions.

Who gets the spouse tax offset and when?

The contributing spouse claims the non-refundable offset on their tax return. The contribution goes into the receiving spouse’s super. The offset can reduce eligible tax owed, and unused offset cannot create a refund by itself.

When does my fund need to receive the contribution?

Your fund needs to receive the contribution by 30 June 2027 for it to count in 2026–27. Check its processing cut-off and allow time for the transfer. Enter personal after-tax contributions you will leave undeducted, and keep spouse contributions separate from rollovers or contribution splits.
