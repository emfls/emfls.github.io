How the estimate works

The age schedule uses illustrative annual funding benchmarks at full eligible pay. It interpolates between ages and scales the result by eligible compensation divided by $360,000. At full pay and with enough funding room, the estimate reaches $345,000 at 62 and $365,000 at 65.

The model discounts an illustrative $3.6 million retirement balance at 5%, subtracts the opening balance grown to year-end, and uses the remaining room to limit each year’s pay credit. It holds compensation and limits constant, advances age annually and applies interest before adding each year-end credit. Credits stop after the retirement year, with at least one modeled funding year. The balance keeps earning hypothetical interest through year 10.

The 401(k) estimate assumes one plan with traditional regular deferrals, available catch-up features and employer profit sharing up to 6% of eligible pay ($21,600 at the compensation cap). It assigns regular deferrals first when compensation limits annual additions. Combined pre-tax shelter adds estimated employer deductions and employee pre-tax deferrals. Estimated tax savings apply the entered marginal rates to that total. Actual savings vary with taxable income, entity structure, state rules and tax brackets.

Frequently asked questions

Does the $290,000 benefit limit cap the annual cash balance contribution?

The $290,000 limit applies to the annual pension benefit. Funding and deductible contributions depend on plan assets, liabilities and actuarial assumptions. Section 415 also generally limits the annual benefit based on high-three average compensation and can reduce limits for shorter service or participation.

How should I interpret the $3.6 million figure?

It represents an illustrative retirement balance, approximately 12.4 times the $290,000 annual benefit. A plan’s actual lump-sum equivalent depends on its benefit terms and applicable interest and mortality assumptions.

Can employer profit sharing exceed 6% with a cash balance plan?

Yes. For applicable overlapping plans, the combined deduction calculation generally considers only employer defined contribution amounts above 6% of aggregate covered compensation. PBGC-covered defined benefit plans are generally excluded from that combined limit. This calculator models a 6% allocation and omits matching and contributions above that rate.

When does the 401(k) catch-up have to go to Roth?

For 2026, when 2025 Social Security wages from the relevant employer exceed $150,000, eligible catch-up contributions generally must be Roth. Employees reaching ages 60–63 can contribute up to $11,250 in catch-up if the plan permits it. Other eligible employees age 50 and up can contribute up to $8,000.

What does the 10-year cash balance projection represent?

It shows a hypothetical benefit balance with 5% annual interest and estimated year-end pay credits through retirement, subject to modeling assumptions. Plan assets and actual employer funding can differ from the hypothetical account.
