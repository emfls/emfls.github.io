Canada Section 116 Withholding Calculator 2026

Compare closing holdbacks, CRA T2062 and Québec TP-1097-V certificate payments, and estimated tax refunds when a non-resident sells Canadian real estate.

Key takeaways

On a C$780,000 non-depreciable Québec sale with a C$510,000 adjusted cost base, the buyer may hold back C$295,425 without certificates. The combined certificate payment is C$102,262.50, a potential C$193,162.50 difference in closing cash.

For ordinary capital property, CRA uses 25% of gross proceeds for the holdback and 25% of the gain before selling costs for the certificate. Québec adds 12.875% on the corresponding base. Depreciable buildings and inventory have separate rules.

Frequently asked questions

Why can a Québec closing freeze 37.875% of the sale price?

Without applicable certificates, the ordinary purchaser holdback combines 25% federal and 12.875% Québec on gross proceeds. Closing professionals may hold the money in trust to cover the purchaser’s liability.

Do commissions and legal fees reduce the T2062 payment?

Selling commissions and legal fees reduce the capital gain reported on the income tax return. The ordinary certificate calculation uses proceeds minus adjusted cost base before those selling costs.

How does selling a depreciable rental building change the estimate?

The higher purchaser rates of 50% federal and 30% Québec apply to the depreciable portion. Land keeps the ordinary rates. CRA handles recapture on T2062A, Québec uses TP-1102.1-V for the building, and recapture can increase taxable income at filing.

What happens if I file the required notice late?

The standard deadline is 10 days after disposition when notice is required. CRA charges C$25 per late day with a C$100 minimum. Québec charges C$25 per late day. Each penalty is capped at C$2,500.

When can I recover excess remittances?

After assessment of the federal T1 and, where applicable, the separate Québec return, the agency can refund payments that exceed the assessed tax. A holdback still in trust becomes a tax credit only if it is remitted.
