2026 Dependent Care FSA ($7,500) vs. Child Care Credit: Which Saves More?

Compare a $7,500 dependent care FSA, a child care credit of up to 50%, and a separate Child Tax Credit worth up to $2,200 per qualifying child.

Key takeaways

  • The One Big Beautiful Bill Act raises the regular dependent care FSA limit from $5,000 to $7,500 in 2026, its first permanent increase since 1986. Check how much your employer actually allows.
  • Each excluded FSA dollar reduces both your unreimbursed care expenses and the $3,000 or $6,000 Form 2441 expense cap. A full $7,500 exclusion uses up the cap.
  • The separate $2,200 Child Tax Credit can stack with either care strategy. The best election depends on your income, tax liability, and eligible expenses.

Frequently asked questions

Can you use a dependent care FSA and claim the child care credit in the same year?

Yes. Subtract the excluded FSA amount from both your qualifying expenses and the Form 2441 cap. With two qualifying people and $10,000 of costs, a $3,000 exclusion can leave $3,000 of expenses eligible for the credit. A $7,500 exclusion leaves zero under the cap.

How does AGI affect the 2026 child care credit?

The rate is 50% through $15,000 of AGI and declines in one-percentage-point steps. For example, exactly $43,000 gives 36%; just above that gives 35%. The 20% floor starts above $103,000 for non-joint filers and $206,000 for joint filers.

Does a dependent care FSA save the full 7.65% in payroll taxes?

It can if the excluded dollars would otherwise be subject to both Social Security and Medicare. Social Security applies only up to $184,500 in covered wages in 2026. If your election takes wages below that cap, only the dollars that reduce Social Security-taxable wages save the 6.2% portion.

How does the $2,200 Child Tax Credit stack with child care benefits?

The Child Tax Credit is a separate benefit for eligible children under 17. Form 2441 can use some of your available federal tax liability first. Schedule 8812 then applies the remaining liability to the nonrefundable CTC and may allow up to $1,700 per child as a refundable credit, subject to its own rules.

Can a married person filing separately use both benefits?

Married filing separately generally allows a dependent care exclusion of up to $3,750 if the plan and earnings requirements are met. The care credit is generally unavailable. If you meet the Form 2441 rules for married people living apart, you can generally use the $7,500 exclusion limit and may qualify for the care credit.
