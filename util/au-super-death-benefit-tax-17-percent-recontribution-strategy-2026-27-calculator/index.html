Compare 17% and 32% death benefit tax, payment via a deceased estate, and the $390,000 super recontribution strategy.

Key takeaways

  • A lump sum paid to a tax dependant is tax-free. For a non-dependant adult child receiving it directly, this calculator uses 17% on the taxed element and 32% on the untaxed element, including the standard 2% Medicare levy.
  • Via the deceased estate, the model uses 15% and 30% for a non-dependant beneficiary. The difference is two percentage points on the taxable elements.
  • Withdrawing and recontributing $390,000 from a fully taxable balance made up entirely of the taxed element can reduce the modelled direct death tax by $66,300. The amount converted depends on the account’s component proportions.

    How the recontribution estimate works

    Each withdrawal uses the account’s proportions immediately before that cycle. If the taxed element is 80% of the account, a $390,000 withdrawal draws $312,000 from it. Replacing the entire withdrawal as an after-tax contribution adds $390,000 to the tax-free component, after the original tax-free share of the withdrawal has been deducted. The net conversion from the taxed element is $312,000, reducing direct death tax by $53,040.

    The model caps each withdrawal at the smallest of the target, available modelled cap and account balance. It assumes unused contribution capacity across all funds, no active earlier bring-forward arrangement and fund acceptance before age 75. It keeps the account balance constant and excludes growth, fees, inflation, withdrawal tax and pension effects. The optional second cycle starts three financial years later and uses your projected prior 30 June total super balance. Check the rules in force at that time.

    Frequently asked questions

    Who counts as a tax dependant for a super death benefit?

    A current or former spouse or de facto partner, a child under 18, a financial dependant or someone in an interdependency relationship may qualify. An adult child generally needs to meet a dependency test to receive the lump sum tax-free.

    Does paying an adult child via the estate save 2%?

    In this model the estate route saves two percentage points on the taxed and untaxed elements compared with direct payment at the maximum rates plus the standard Medicare levy. The amount depends on the actual levy, the beneficiary’s tax circumstances and whether the fund pays the estate.

    Can I withdraw just the taxable part of my super?

    The fund applies the account’s component proportions to a lump-sum withdrawal. When you put back the gross amount as a non-concessional contribution, the new contribution goes into the tax-free component.

    Can I use the full $390,000 cap twice?

    The 2026–27 $390,000 cap requires a 30 June 2026 total super balance below $1.84 million and eligibility for a fresh bring-forward period. The second cycle is illustrative and assumes eligibility and unused capacity at the start of 2029–30. Future caps and thresholds may change.

    Does turning 60 make every super withdrawal tax-free?

    You must meet a condition of release to withdraw an unrestricted lump sum. At age 60 or older, its tax-free component and taxed element generally have no withdrawal tax. An untaxed element can still be taxed, affecting the cash available to recontribute.
