Key takeaways

The 2026 federal credit is 14% of eligible expenses above the lesser of 3% of the claimant’s net income or $2,890. An eligible claimant may also receive up to $1,534 through the refundable supplement.

A smaller threshold can still cost you savings. If the lower-income spouse has little tax left to offset, the other spouse can come out ahead. Compare both claims before filing.

Medical expense credit FAQ

What is the federal medical expense tax credit threshold for 2026?

The threshold is the lesser of 3% of the claimant’s Line 23600 net income and $2,890. It reaches the ceiling at about $96,333.33 of net income. The base federal credit is 14% of the eligible expenses above the threshold, limited by available federal tax.

Should the lower-income spouse claim all the medical expenses?

A lower net income usually means a smaller threshold, but the non-refundable credit can only reduce tax the claimant still owes. Compare both spouses’ remaining federal and provincial or territorial tax, and check each spouse’s refundable supplement eligibility.

How does the 2026 refundable medical expense supplement work?

Line 45200 starts at 25% of the allowable medical expense amount after the federal threshold, plus any eligible Line 21500 disability supports deduction, up to $1,534. The claimant must have at least $4,478 of qualifying working income, be at least 18 at year-end, and have lived in Canada throughout the year. The supplement falls by 5% of adjusted family net income above $33,960 and reaches zero by $64,640. Smaller claims phase out sooner.

Which medical expenses can I include, and which dates count?

Choose any 12 consecutive months ending in 2026 and keep receipts for eligible expenses paid in that window. Examples include eligible dental care, prescription drugs, vision care, private health plan premiums and eligible therapy. Transportation for care may qualify at 40 km or more one way when CRA conditions are met. Exclude reimbursements and amounts already claimed, subject to CRA exceptions.

How does the Québec medical expense credit differ?

Québec applies a 20% credit to eligible expenses above 3% of the spouses’ combined Québec net income on Line 275, without a fixed ceiling. The federal threshold still uses each claimant’s own federal net income. The comparison includes the effect of Québec’s federal abatement.

Compare rolling claim periods using the dates you paid. For example, July 1, 2025 through June 30, 2026 can capture a series of bills on either side of New Year’s Day. Use each expense once.
