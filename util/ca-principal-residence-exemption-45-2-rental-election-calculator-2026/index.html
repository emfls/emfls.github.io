Canada Principal Residence Exemption & 45(2) Rental Election Calculator 2026

Compare the capital gains tax on a former home with a subsection 45(2) election and with a fair market value reset when you start renting it out.

Key takeaways

  • A 45(2) election defers the deemed sale and can cover up to four additional eligible rental tax years under the principal residence exemption (PRE).
  • The plus-one rule adds one year to the PRE numerator if you were resident in Canada in the acquisition year and designate the home for at least one year.
  • The fair market value (FMV) reset can cost less if most of the appreciation occurred before conversion and the PRE fully shelters that gain.
  • 50% of the remaining capital gain is included in taxable income for this 2026 estimate.

How the estimates differ

With an election in force through the sale, the gain starts at the original adjusted cost base and the PRE applies across the ownership period. Without an election, the property is deemed sold and reacquired at conversion-date FMV. The rental-stage gain uses that FMV as its starting point. Both strategies subtract eligible selling expenses from actual sale proceeds.

This comparison covers capital gains tax on this property at a single marginal rate. It excludes CCA recapture, capital-loss relief, post-conversion capital additions, bracket changes, rental income tax, and any effect of allocating designation years away from another property. Special rules can apply to employment relocations, partial changes of use, non-resident dispositions and flipped property.

Frequently asked questions

How does the principal residence plus-one rule work?

If you were resident in Canada in the acquisition year and designate the home for at least one eligible year, add one to the designated-year count. Divide by the total tax years owned and cap the result at 100%. For example, five designated years out of six can fully exempt the gain.

How do I file a subsection 45(2) rental election?

File a signed letter describing the property and electing under subsection 45(2) with your return for the year of the change of use. Keep reporting your net rental income. For an actual or deemed sale of a principal residence, review the Schedule 3 and T2091 reporting requirements.

Can I designate both my old home and my new home?

Your family unit can ordinarily designate only one property per tax year. With the election in force, you can generally designate up to four eligible rental-only years for the old home. Using a year for the old home can reduce the exemption on your new home.

What happens if I claim CCA on the rental building?

CRA treats an existing election as rescinded on the first day of the year you claim CCA. That can trigger a deemed sale and reacquisition at fair market value. Previously claimed CCA can be recaptured separately when the building is disposed of.

When can skipping the election result in less tax?

An FMV reset can give the lower estimate if the exemption fully covers a large pre-conversion gain and the home gains little value after it becomes a rental. With an election, every year outside your exemption share can expose part of the entire ownership-period gain to tax.
