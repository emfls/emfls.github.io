Key takeaways

Expect 25% withholding on gross rent by default. Once CRA approves Form NR6 in writing, your Canadian agent can remit 25% on net rent after eligible cash expenses. The remittance is generally due by the 15th of the following month.

Withholding and final tax can differ. Section 216 applies federal brackets to taxable net rent, then adds 48% of the basic federal tax. A valid return reconciles that tax against your remittances.

Frequently asked questions

How do I switch from gross withholding to net withholding?

You and your Canadian-resident agent generally send Form NR6 to CRA by January 1 or before the first rental payment is due. The payer continues withholding 25% of gross rent until CRA gives written approval. After approval, the agent can withhold 25% of rent remaining after eligible cash expenses.

What is the 2026 T1159 deadline with and without an approved NR6?

With approved NR6, the usual filing deadline is June 30, 2027. With gross withholding throughout 2026 and no approved NR6, the usual deadline to elect is within two years after the end of 2026. Reporting CCA recapture on a property sale can trigger an April 30, 2027 filing deadline.

Can I deduct CCA when estimating NR6 withholding?

Form NR6 excludes CCA, depreciation and amortization. An allowable CCA claim may reduce taxable net rent on T1159, up to the amount of positive net rental income. CCA cannot create or increase a rental loss.

How does the 48% non-resident federal surtax work?

Multiply the basic federal tax by 48% and add that amount to the basic federal tax. For example, the first federal bracket of 14% becomes 20.72% on that portion of taxable net rent. The model applies the federal surtax in place of provincial income tax and does not claim a basic personal amount.

When will I get a refund or owe a balance?

On a valid T1159 return, compare the tax payable with the tax withheld and remitted for the year. Excess remittances may be refunded, while a shortfall produces a balance owing. NR6 reduces remittances during the year, so it can leave a smaller refund or a larger balance than gross withholding.
