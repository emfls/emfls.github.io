How tribunal compensation is calculated

The usual unfair dismissal calculation in England, Wales and Scotland has two parts. The basic award uses statutory weekly pay, age and continuous service. The compensatory award reflects financial loss attributable to the dismissal, subject to tribunal findings and any applicable limit. A successful Equality Act claim can bring separate compensation, including injury to feelings. Northern Ireland has separate legislation and institutions. Use this page to compare remedies on stated assumptions once you have identified the claim and the effective date of termination.

Financial loss, mitigation and percentage adjustments

Build the loss figure from the pay and benefits you would probably have received if the dismissal had not happened, including reasonably evidenced pension or benefit loss where applicable. Use net earnings loss in the loss estimate and statutory gross weekly pay for the pay-based ceiling. Deduct replacement earnings from the periods they cover and account for loss you could reasonably have avoided. Evidence such as payslips, job applications, offers, pension statements and records of new earnings helps explain both the amount and the time period claimed.

Past loss typically runs from dismissal to a relevant assessment date. Future loss covers the period for which the tribunal expects the financial shortfall to continue, if supported by evidence. A comparable new job may end the loss period. A lower-paid role may leave a continuing difference. Pension loss can require a separate calculation and should reflect the relevant pension rights. Small amounts for loss of statutory employment protection or reasonable expenses attributable to dismissal may also be relevant. Check any notice payment, settlement sum, benefit recoupment or offset before treating the total as money you will receive.

The calculator first reduces the loss for any Polkey percentage. This models a tribunal finding that a fair dismissal might have happened in any event. Evidence may instead indicate that a fair process would have taken a specific further period, in which case calculate the relevant loss period before entering the amount. A percentage represents a finding about the likely outcome and is independent of whether the employee caused or contributed to their dismissal.

The Acas Code adjustment follows Polkey in this model. Enter between −25% and +25% to model a discretionary adjustment for unreasonable failure to comply with an applicable Code. A positive figure generally models an employer's failure and a negative figure models an employee's failure. The disciplinary and grievance Code excludes redundancy dismissals and the expiry of fixed-term contracts. In an unfair dismissal award the adjustment applies to the compensatory amount, immediately before the relevant contributory fault deduction. The calculator then applies the compensatory cap where it still exists. It leaves separate Equality Act compensation at the values entered because adjustments on that claim depend on its own facts and legal findings. Income Tax and the £30,000 threshold

Section 401 of the Income Tax (Earnings and Pensions) Act 2003 identifies qualifying termination-connected receipts and section 403 provides the £30,000 threshold. The first combined £30,000 of qualifying payments is generally free of Income Tax. Amounts above the available threshold are subject to Income Tax at the rates applicable to your income, which may span more than one band. Earlier qualifying payments for the same termination, including qualifying severance or redundancy pay, can use up part of the threshold. Taxable wages, holiday pay, notice pay, payment in lieu of notice (PILON) and post-employment notice pay (PENP) are generally taxed as earnings under the relevant rules.

A £45,000 qualifying termination award with the full threshold available leaves £15,000 potentially taxable. A separate £5,000 of taxable notice earnings is taxed under the earnings rules and does not use the £30,000 threshold. Injury to feelings requires its own analysis. HMRC distinguishes sums attributable solely to pre-termination discrimination from those connected to termination. Worked award examples

High earner with £200,000 of adjusted financial loss

Assume £3,000 gross weekly pay, age 62, 20 complete years of service and no offsets or percentage adjustments. The formula basic award is £22,530 in both periods. For a 31 December 2026 effective date, 52 weeks of pay gives £156,000, so the fixed £123,543 limit applies and the combined unfair dismissal award is £146,073. For a 1 January 2027 effective date, the same £200,000 of loss survives the removal of the cap and the total is £222,530. The difference is £76,457. A shorter evidenced loss period would reduce that difference and could eliminate it.

Mixed age bands and sequential reductions

An employee aged 43 has worked eight complete years on £600 a week. Two years in the oldest band earn three weeks and six years in the middle band earn six weeks, for a £5,400 basic award. Suppose mitigated loss is £50,000. A 20% Polkey deduction leaves £40,000, a 10% Acas uplift takes it to £44,000, and a 25% contributory fault deduction leaves £33,000. The old 52-week ceiling reduces it to £31,200 for a total of £36,600. Under the 2027 rules the total is £38,400 with those same facts.

Separate discrimination claim

Assume a discrimination-only claim supports £18,000 of missed earnings and £14,000 for injury to feelings. The £14,000 falls into the middle Vento band for Ninth Addendum claims. The model gives £32,000 before tax and any applicable interest or other adjustments. If an unfair dismissal claim is also made for the same missed earnings, identify the award head that accounts for each part of that loss so the schedule does not seek payment twice.

Frequently asked questions

Which date decides whether the compensation cap applies?

The effective date of termination controls the transition. For ordinary unfair dismissal with an effective date from 6 April through 31 December 2026, the compensatory award is limited to the lower of £123,543 and 52 weeks’ statutory gross pay. The cap is removed for effective dates from 1 January 2027. Where the employer gave notice before January and employment ends in January, the commencement regulations can bring the case within the new rules.

Are all automatically unfair dismissals uncapped before January 2027?

The old cap has specific exceptions, including qualifying whistleblowing and health and safety dismissals and corresponding protected redundancy selections. Other automatically unfair claims can remain capped before January 2027 even where they have no minimum service requirement. An Equality Act discrimination claim uses separate remedies with no unfair dismissal cap. If both claims recover the same financial loss, it is only payable once.

Can the Acas adjustment take a 2026 award over the cap?

The unfair dismissal compensatory cap applies after the model’s adjustments. The calculator first applies the Polkey deduction, then the Acas adjustment and then the compensatory contributory fault deduction. It applies the cap to the result where the cap is in force and the claim is subject to it. The Acas adjustment depends on unreasonable non-compliance with a relevant Code and what the tribunal considers just and equitable.

Can I claim both unfair dismissal compensation and a Vento award?

A successful discrimination claim can support a distinct injury-to-feelings award alongside unfair dismissal compensation. A finding of ordinary unfair dismissal alone does not support a Vento award. Use the Vento guidance for the date the discrimination claim was presented and evidence the impact of the discriminatory conduct. Where both claims cover the same earnings loss, include that loss only once in your schedule.

Is the first £30,000 of a tribunal award always tax-free?

The £30,000 threshold generally applies to the combined qualifying termination payments under sections 401 and 403 of ITEPA 2003. It is shared with earlier qualifying payments relating to the termination. Taxable earnings such as notice pay, PILON or PENP are dealt with separately. The tax treatment of injury-to-feelings compensation depends in part on its connection with termination, so consider the different components individually.
