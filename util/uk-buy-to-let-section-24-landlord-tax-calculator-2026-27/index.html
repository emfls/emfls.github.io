Key takeaways

A rental can make a cash profit and still lose money after tax. Section 24 leaves qualifying mortgage interest in an individual’s taxable profit calculation and gives a capped tax credit instead.

From 6 April 2027, Budget 2026 announced property rates of 24% / 44% / 49%, up four percentage points from 2026/27. The finance-cost credit also rises from 20% to 24%. An eligible company generally deducts qualifying interest before Corporation Tax.

Frequently asked questions

How does Section 24 mortgage interest relief work?

Individual residential landlords calculate taxable property profit before qualifying interest. The tax reducer is 20% in 2026/27, applied to the lowest of eligible finance costs, taxable property profit after losses and adjusted total income above the Personal Allowance. It cannot create a tax refund. Finance costs restricted by the profit or income cap can be carried forward.

What changes on 6 April 2027?

Budget 2026 announced property tax rates of 24%, 44% and 49% for England, Wales and Northern Ireland, four percentage points above 2026/27. The finance-cost credit tracks the 24% property basic rate. Other income such as wages keeps its own bands and rates, so the change in net tax depends on both taxable property profit and the credit you can use.

How can landlord tax exceed 100% of cash profit?

If rental income minus running costs is £20,000 and interest is £15,000, pre-tax cash profit is £5,000. With £90,000 of other income, the 2026/27 extra tax after Section 24 relief is £7,000, including the effect of the allowance taper. That is 140% of cash profit and leaves a £2,000 shortfall before any mortgage principal repayment.

How can rental profit trigger the £100,000 Personal Allowance taper?

Taxable rental profit before mortgage interest enters adjusted net income. Above £100,000, the allowance falls by £1 for every £2 of excess income and reaches zero at £125,140. The finance-cost credit reduces the final tax bill, leaving adjusted net income unchanged.

How is the limited company buy-to-let comparison calculated?

The benchmark deducts allowable operating costs and qualifying interest before Corporation Tax. It uses the 19% small profits rate, marginal relief between £50,000 and £250,000, and the 25% main rate from £250,000. The cash stays in the SPV. Dividend tax, incorporation or transfer costs and differences in mortgage terms can change the comparison.
