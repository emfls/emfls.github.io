How the tax estimate works

The calculator compares personal tax before and after your proposed deduction using the 2026–27 resident brackets. Where the full Medicare levy applies, the combined marginal rates above A$45,000 are 32%, 39% and 47%. A deduction that crosses a bracket saves tax at each applicable rate. The personal tax figure estimates the change in tax liability, which may reduce a bill or increase a refund.

Fund tax is 15% of the proposed concessional contribution. Any increase in Division 293 is added to that cost. The calculation includes the excess concessional contribution income add-back and associated 15% offset. Net gain is the personal tax saving less the extra fund tax and Division 293.

The model assumes a fully eligible deduction, a full-year Australian resident with the full tax-free threshold, an ordinary taxed fund, and a flat 2% Medicare levy. It excludes levy reductions and exemptions, the Medicare levy surcharge, other tax offsets, and Division 293 income adjustments such as reportable fringe benefits and net investment losses. It does not model second-order consequences of exceeding the cap.

See the ATO guidance on personal super deductions and notices of intent. Confirm your unused balances and fund receipt dates before making a contribution.

Frequently asked questions

How much can I contribute concessionally in 2026–27?

The standard cap is A$32,500 from 1 July 2026. If you qualify and have all five prior caps unused, your combined cap can reach A$175,000. Employer super, salary sacrifice and personal contributions you deduct all count towards that cap.

Can I carry forward unused cap if my total super balance is exactly A$500,000?

No. Your total super balance across all funds must be below A$500,000 on 30 June 2026. At or above that balance, your 2026–27 cap is A$32,500 and any unused 2021–22 amount expires at the end of 30 June 2027.

When does unused 2021–22 concessional cap expire?

Any unused 2021–22 amount expires at the end of 30 June 2027. The current A$32,500 cap is used first. Once eligible contributions exceed it, the ATO automatically applies the oldest available unused amounts first. Check when your fund will receive the payment.

How does Division 293 affect the tax benefit of a deductible contribution?

Division 293 adds 15% on the lesser of eligible contributions and the amount by which Division 293 income plus eligible contributions exceeds A$250,000. A personal deduction reduces taxable income, so the calculator compares the surcharge before and after the contribution.

What happens if I exceed my cap or want to claim the deduction?

Excess concessional contributions are added to assessable income and generally receive a 15% tax offset. Excess retained in super can also count towards the non-concessional cap. To claim an eligible personal contribution, give your fund a valid notice of intent and get its acknowledgement before claiming.
