UK Short-Term Bridging Finance Mechanics & Interest Structure

Bridging finance is a flexible, short-term property loan (typically 1 to 18 months) used to secure auction purchases, fund property refurbishments, or bridge the gap between buying and selling a property.

1. Retained vs Rolled-Up Interest Mechanics

  • Retained Interest: The lender deducts the full facility term's interest upfront from your gross loan advance. You make zero monthly payments during the loan term. Crucially, if you redeem early (e.g., selling or refinancing in Month 6 of a 12-month facility), the unused 6 months of retained interest are refunded/rebated to you.
  • Rolled-Up Interest: No interest is deducted upfront on Day One, maximizing your immediate cash in hand. Instead, interest accrues monthly (often on a compound basis) and is repaid in a single lump sum when the loan is redeemed.

2. Fee Stacking & Gross LTV Boundaries

Lenders calculate maximum loan limits (typically 75% LTV) based on the gross loan, which includes facility arrangement fees (usually 2.0%), broker fees, and retained interest. When opting for rolled-up interest, developers must ensure that compounding interest at exit does not breach the lender's final redemption LTV thresholds.
