Key takeaways

  • Stocks & Shares ISA. Invest up to £20,000 across adult ISAs and withdraw from a standard ISA without UK tax.
  • Lifetime ISA. A £4,000 contribution earns a £1,000 bonus and leaves £16,000 of the shared ISA allowance. Check the withdrawal charge before relying on the money.
  • SIPP or workplace pension. £1,000 of take-home pay can fund £1,250 at 20% relief or £1,666.67 at 40% relief. Pension access age and withdrawal tax can change which strategy leaves you with the most to spend.

Frequently asked questions

Does the Lifetime ISA limit come out of the £20,000 ISA allowance?

Yes. A £4,000 Lifetime ISA contribution leaves £16,000 of your adult ISA allowance. The £1,000 government bonus does not use up any ISA allowance.

What happens if I use my Lifetime ISA for a home over £450,000?

An otherwise qualifying purchase above £450,000 has a 20% withdrawal charge from 29 October 2026. Before that date in 2026/27 the charge was 25%. A qualifying purchase at or below £450,000 is charge-free.

When can a Lifetime ISA beat a SIPP for retirement?

For each £1 invested with 20% pension relief and 20% retirement tax, the SIPP leaves about £1.06 if 25% is tax-free. A charge-free Lifetime ISA leaves £1.25. At 40% pension relief and 20% retirement tax the SIPP leaves about £1.42. These examples assume equal growth, available allowances and no salary sacrifice.

How do higher-rate relief and the 60% Personal Allowance taper work?

A relief-at-source pension typically adds 20% basic-rate relief to the gross contribution. You may claim extra relief on the eligible slice. Gross contributions can also recover some Personal Allowance when adjusted net income falls into or through the £100,000 to £125,140 taper range. The effective 60% applies only to the portion in that range.

Is the first 25% of a large pension always tax-free?

The standard £268,275 lump sum allowance caps how much tax-free cash you can take, even if 25% of the pot is more. Earlier tax-free payments can reduce the allowance available. Tax on the rest depends on your taxable income in the years you make withdrawals.
