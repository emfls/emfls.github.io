Key takeaways

  • On covered sales above $1 million, the seller pays 1% to 3.5% on the entire deed consideration.
  • A nonresident’s estimated closing payment is generally the greater of 2% of consideration or 10.75% of federal reportable gain. GIT/REP-3 can remove that prepayment if a seller’s assurance applies.
  • The standard Realty Transfer Fee is a separate seller charge. Any excess income tax prepayment may be refundable after filing.

New Jersey seller tax FAQ

Who pays the New Jersey mansion tax in 2026?

The seller pays the graduated percent fee on covered deed transfers above $1 million. The applicable rate applies to the entire consideration. For example, $2.75 million at 2.5% produces a $68,750 fee.

What happens when the sale price crosses a mansion tax threshold?

The rate increases on the full sale price. At $2 million the mansion fee is $20,000. At $2,000,001 it is $40,000.02, before the separate Realty Transfer Fee.

Who can use GIT/REP-3 to avoid the closing payment?

A seller who remains a New Jersey resident can generally use Box 1. A nonresident who meets the exclusive principal-residence requirements and can exclude all gain under Section 121 clears the Box 2 screen. If any gain remains, confirm the form with the settlement agent because the form instructions also address some over-limit principal-residence sales. GIT/REP-3 has other assurances, including consideration of $1,000 or less.

Is the 2% payment due if a nonresident sells at a loss?

The 2% floor usually applies even when reportable gain is zero. A qualifying assurance or an approved waiver can change the closing payment. Selling at a loss alone does not guarantee a waiver.

How can I estimate a refund of the New Jersey closing payment?

Subtract the estimated New Jersey liability covered by the payment from the amount paid at closing. If there is an excess, a nonresident can generally claim it on Form NJ-1040NR or request an early refund on Form A-3128 if eligible. The actual refund depends on the filed return or approved refund claim.
