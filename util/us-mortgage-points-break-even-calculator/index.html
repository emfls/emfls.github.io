How the estimate works

The payment break-even is the first month the accumulated payment differences cover the points. The exit break-even also accounts for the difference in remaining principal at payoff, so it compares interest paid to date with the points cost. The calculation uses exact fixed-rate amortization before rounding displays to cents. A 0% loan divides principal evenly over its term. Taxes, insurance, other fees, prepayments, and the time value of money are excluded; lender rounding can differ.

Frequently asked questions

How much does one mortgage discount point cost?

One point costs 1% of the loan principal. For example, 1.5 points on a $450,000 loan cost $6,750. Use the rate reduction quoted by your lender.

Why are there two break-even months?

Payment break-even counts the monthly payment savings. Sale or refinance break-even also accounts for the difference in the remaining loan balance, which makes the comparison reflect interest paid through that month.

What happens at 0% interest or with no discount?

A 0% loan has no interest, and its monthly payment is principal divided by the number of months. Paying for points that leave the rate unchanged does not generate savings.
