Key takeaways

  • Standard employer NI is 15% of annual pay above £5,000 per employee. On £30,000, that is about £3,750 before allowance.
  • Eligible employers can offset up to £10,500. The prior-year £100,000 NI eligibility cap was removed from 6 April 2025.
  • For eligible under-21s and qualifying apprentices under 25, employer NI starts at 15% above £50,270 a year.

Avoid budgeting for an allowance you cannot claim

A limited company loses up to £10,500 in potential relief when its sole director is its only employee paid above the secondary threshold. A qualifying hire can change eligibility if the other rules are met. The available saving is limited to your eligible employer NI bill.

How to read the estimate

Each group models full-year staff on the same annual pay. For a mixed-pay workforce, a group average can distort the result if salaries cross the applicable threshold. The effective rate is annual net employer NI divided by gross payroll.

The monthly output averages annual net NI over 12 months. Actual payroll uses earnings-period thresholds, and the allowance is used as liabilities arise. Director appointments, pay changes and age or apprenticeship changes can affect the calculation.

The 2024/25 comparison uses the same pay and staff, a 13.8% standard rate, a £9,100 secondary threshold and up to £5,000 in allowance. It assumes the same employer status and other eligibility facts unless you clear the prior-year cap checkbox.

This budget estimate covers employer secondary Class 1 NI. It excludes employee deductions, employer pensions, Apprenticeship Levy, benefits and other NI reliefs. Check HMRC eligibility for connected employers, public-sector work and excluded workers before claiming.

Frequently asked questions

How much employer NI is due on a £30,000 standard salary?

On an annual basis, 15% of £25,000 is £3,750 before Employment Allowance. Actual payroll uses the employee’s earnings-period thresholds.

Can employers with a prior-year NI bill of £100,000 or more claim?

Yes, if they meet the other eligibility rules. From 6 April 2025 the previous-year NI cap was removed. In 2026/27, the allowance offsets up to £10,500 of qualifying employer Class 1 NI.

When can a single-director company claim the allowance?

If the sole director is the only employee paid above the secondary threshold, the company is excluded. A qualifying additional employee paid above the applicable earnings-period threshold can make the company eligible for the whole tax year if the other rules are met.

How does employer NI relief work for younger employees and apprentices?

For employees under 21 and apprentices under 25 on a recognised apprenticeship, the employer rate is 0% up to £50,270 a year and 15% on earnings above it. Apply the appropriate payroll category to each employee.

Why can a new hire reduce the employer’s NI bill?

If the hire removes the sole-director exclusion, the allowance can offset NI on the existing payroll as well as the new hire. The simulator subtracts the current net NI from the projected net NI, so a negative result is a saving on the employer’s total NI bill.
