Key takeaways

For a typical Victorian dwelling vacant for more than six months in the previous calendar year, VRLT starts at 1% of capital improved value (CIV). It rises to 2% in the second consecutive liable year and 3% from the third. The tax applies from the first dollar of CIV.

From 1 January 2026, qualifying undeveloped residential land in metropolitan Melbourne can incur flat 1% VRLT after five continuous years.

Cost of keeping a home vacant. At A$1.2 million CIV, three consecutive liable years cost A$12,000, A$24,000 and A$36,000. That is A$72,000 in VRLT alone, before ordinary land tax or any absentee surcharge.

Victoria VRLT questions

What does the 1%–3% VRLT escalator tax?

It taxes the full assessed capital improved value of an eligible Victorian dwelling, including land and improvements. The rates are 1% for the first consecutive year liable, 2% for the second and 3% from the third.

When does the five-year rule apply to undeveloped Melbourne land?

From 1 January 2026, metropolitan Melbourne land outside a non-residential zone that can be developed for housing may owe 1% of CIV after five uninterrupted years undeveloped. Earlier years count. A genuine ownership change can restart the period and adjoining land may qualify for an exemption.

Can separate visits count towards the holiday-home exemption?

Yes. You can combine distinct days of use by the owner and eligible relatives to reach four weeks in the previous calendar year. Each owner needs a principal home in Australia. Notify the SRO when required and meet its genuine holiday-home test.

How does the 4% absentee surcharge interact with VRLT?

The surcharge is part of the ordinary land tax assessment and uses aggregate taxable site value. At the general rates it starts at A$50,000 of taxable land value and applies to the full taxable site value. VRLT is assessed separately using CIV.

Does listing the home for rent avoid VRLT?

A listing alone does not count as occupation. At least six months of qualifying use in the preceding calendar year can take an existing dwelling out of the ordinary vacancy rule. Actual occupancy under genuine leases or short-term letting can count across separate stays.
