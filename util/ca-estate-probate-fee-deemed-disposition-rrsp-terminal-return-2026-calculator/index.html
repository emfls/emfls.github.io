A small probate saving can leave a large tax bill

In Ontario, naming an adult child as an RRSP beneficiary can save up to about $15 per $1,000 in probate tax. On the same dollars, high-bracket combined marginal income tax can reach roughly 45%–53.5%. The final return generally includes the RRSP’s value even when the money goes straight to the child.

The estate generally pays the final-return tax. CRA can assess the recipient directly for the applicable tax on received RRSP or RRIF proceeds under the joint and several liability rules, including when the estate runs short. Income Tax Act, section 160.2

How the estimate works

The final-return estimate is the tax on other taxable income plus death-triggered inclusions, less the tax on other taxable income alone. It includes basic personal amounts, Ontario surtax and health premium, and basic Ontario and B.C. tax reductions. It excludes individual credits and deductions beyond those listed, capital-loss relief, rental depreciation recapture, estate debts, encumbrances, post-death gains, and professional or administrative costs. Probate and income tax are assumed to be in the same province. Outside Québec, a zero probate value assumes no grant is needed.

The home setting models a full exemption or none. Use the rollover switch when both the capital transfer and any registered-plan transfer meet their respective rules. Mixed transfers and partial exemptions require a separate calculation. The net estimate covers the estate and direct recipients together and subtracts only the incremental income tax and probate or verification fee.

Sources include CRA basic amounts and Ontario adjustments, the B.C. tax reduction, Income Tax Act section 38, CRA guidance on gains at death, registered retirement plans and TFSAs.

Frequently asked questions

Can naming an adult child avoid RRSP tax at death?

A valid direct designation can keep the proceeds outside the probate estate. The RRSP value is generally still included on the deceased’s final return, and CRA can assess the recipient for their applicable share of the tax under section 160.2.

What happens to capital gains when someone dies in 2026?

Capital property is generally deemed disposed of at fair market value immediately before death. This estimate includes 50% of positive gains in taxable income, subject to a qualifying rollover or a full principal residence exemption.

When does a spousal rollover defer tax?

Qualifying capital property can generally roll to a spouse, common-law partner or qualifying spousal trust. A separate set of conditions applies to RRSP and RRIF transfers to an eligible spouse or partner. Tax may arise on a later sale, withdrawal or death.

Does a TFSA or principal residence count for probate?

The TFSA value at death is tax-free and can bypass probate when validly paid directly to a beneficiary or successor holder. A home may qualify for a full principal residence exemption and still count for probate if it passes through the estate.

How does Québec handle a notarial will?

A Québec notarial will does not require verification. A holograph or witnessed will generally requires verification by a court or notary. This calculator uses the $241 court application tariff, before professional fees and other charges.
