Canada CPP Survivor’s Pension & Combined Cap Calculator 2026

Compare your CPP at 60, 65 and 70 and see how a combined-payment ceiling changes the total.

Key takeaways

  • Under 65: $238.17 plus 37.5% of the deceased contributor’s age-65 CPP equivalent, up to $803.54 a month.
  • Age 65 or older: 60% of that CPP equivalent, up to $904.59 a month. The flat payment ends.
  • Combined at 65: Service Canada publishes a January 2026 benchmark of $1,531.56 a month ($18,378.72 annualized). CPP enhancement can sit above the base ceiling.

Budget for the combined payment

Adding the two standalone amounts can overstate the income you’ll receive. Service Canada combines the benefits and applies component-level reductions that can matter even below the published age-65 maximum. Use the cap-only figures below to see how much room the scenario leaves in your budget, and confirm your award with Service Canada.

How the cap scenario works

While you wait to start your own CPP, the model pays the standalone survivor pension and switches to the 65+ formula at age 65. After you start, the combined payment is the smaller of the two standalone payments added together and the scenario ceiling. For claims at 60 and 65 it uses $1,531.56. At 70 it adds the 42% dollar increase on your own CPP to that ceiling. For example, if your age-65 estimate is $1,000, the age-70 scenario ceiling is $1,951.56.

Service Canada calculates combined benefits using the base and additional CPP components, earnings ceilings and age adjustments under the Canada Pension Plan. The fixed ceiling and its age-70 adjustment are assumptions for comparing timing. Service Canada survivor rules · CPP start ages · CPP Act, section 46 · CPP Act, section 58

Frequently asked questions

How is the standalone CPP survivor’s pension calculated?

For the January 2026 benchmark, an eligible survivor under 65 gets $238.17 plus 37.5% of the deceased contributor’s age-65 pension equivalent, up to $803.54 monthly. From age 65 the rate is 60%, up to $904.59 monthly, and the flat amount ends.

Is $1,531.56 a universal combined CPP cap?

Service Canada lists $1,531.56 per month as the January 2026 combined maximum at age 65. CPP enhancement components sit outside the base ceiling, and survivor reductions can occur even when the two standalone amounts sum to less than $1,531.56.

What does the modeled cap reduction measure?

It is the difference between adding the two modeled standalone pensions and applying the scenario ceiling. The annual amount uses the first modeled payment month times 12. The 20-year amount includes months on survivor CPP alone and changes at age 65.

Which CPP start age gives me the highest lifetime payout?

The answer depends on the age you project to, how much CPP you can claim, and your survivor pension. The table includes survivor payments while you wait and compares gross cumulative dollars at the same end age. Service Canada’s personal estimates are needed for an actual claiming decision.

Who can receive the $5,000 CPP death benefit?

The $2,500 top-up requires death-benefit eligibility and a death on or after January 1, 2025. The contributor must never have received a CPP or QPP retirement, disability or post-retirement disability benefit and must leave no spouse or common-law partner eligible for a survivor’s pension.
