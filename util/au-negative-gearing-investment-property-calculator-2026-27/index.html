Frequently asked questions

How is the 2026–27 negative gearing tax saving calculated?

The calculator compares resident income tax plus a flat 2% Medicare levy on your other taxable income with the same calculation after adding your allowable rental result. It applies the 15% rate to the portion of taxable income above $18,200 up to $45,000, then applies higher rates where relevant. The difference estimates the property’s tax effect. Your actual refund also depends on tax withheld and your full return.

Which properties keep the rental loss offset from 1 July 2027?

For an individual investor, the offset is generally preserved for an ownership interest last acquired before 7:30 pm AEST on 12 May 2026, including a contract entered into before that time. Eligible new builds also retain the offset. Choose the new-build category only if the dwelling qualifies under the rules.

What happens to established property losses after the transition year?

An established property acquired at or after the cut-off can generally offset eligible rental losses against other income in 2026–27 if it produces a deductible loss that year. From 1 July 2027, excess deductions can generally be used against eligible residential rental income or residential capital gains under the law. Unused amounts carry forward. This calculator assumes one property and no other eligible property gains.

Can depreciation make after-tax cash flow positive?

Yes. If rent is $200 a year below cash expenses and eligible depreciation is $6,000, the tax loss is $6,200. At a combined marginal rate of 32%, an allowable loss saves $1,984 of tax, leaving $1,784 of after-tax cash flow. Capital works under Division 43 commonly use 2.5% of eligible construction costs each year. Division 40 covers eligible plant and equipment, and most second-hand assets in residential rentals are excluded.

What does the comparison assume about the 2027–28 tax rates?

Both columns use 2026–27 rates and identical property figures to isolate the quarantine effect. The legislated second-bracket rate drops to 14% from 1 July 2027. The comparison assumes the full 2% Medicare levy, and excludes levy reductions, exemptions, the surcharge, tax offsets and other personal adjustments.
