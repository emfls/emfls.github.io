Key takeaways

Start with the smaller of 90% of your estimated 2026 tax or 100% of your eligible 2025 tax. Use 110% for the prior-year option if 2025 AGI exceeded $150,000 ($75,000 if married filing separately for 2026).

If your 2026 tax after refundable credits and withholding is below $1,000, the penalty generally does not apply. Otherwise, compare what you’ve paid with what was due at each deadline.

Frequently asked questions

How do I find my 2026 safe harbor?

For the general rule, compare 90% of your estimated 2026 tax with 100% or 110% of the eligible 2025 tax. Use the smaller amount. If your 2026 tax after refundable credits and withholding is under $1,000, the underpayment penalty generally does not apply.

When does the 110% prior-year rule apply?

Use 110% when 2025 AGI exceeds $150,000, or $75,000 if you are married filing separately for 2026. Exactly at the applicable threshold, use 100%. The prior-year comparison generally requires a return covering 12 months.

When are the four 2026 estimated tax payments due?

The general deadlines are April 15, June 15 and September 15, 2026, and January 15, 2027. You may skip the January installment if you file your 2026 return and pay all tax due by February 1, 2027.

Can extra Q4 withholding reduce an earlier underpayment penalty?

Yes. Under the default Form 2210 allocation, federal income tax actually withheld during 2026 is credited evenly across the four installment dates. Extra Q4 payroll withholding can reduce or eliminate earlier shortfalls if it is large enough. An estimated tax payment instead reduces an outstanding shortfall as of its payment date.

How is the underpayment penalty estimated?

The calculator applies simple daily interest to the unpaid balance for each period through April 15, 2027 or earlier payment. The default is a 7% annual rate for illustration. Switch to calendar-quarter rates to account for rate changes. The IRS computes the actual penalty under Form 2210.
