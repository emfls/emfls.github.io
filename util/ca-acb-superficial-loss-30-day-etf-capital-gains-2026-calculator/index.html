Key takeaways

  • Canada uses weighted-average cost for identical units. Return of capital reduces ACB, and reinvested non-cash distributions increase it.
  • The superficial loss window includes day −30, the sale date and day +30. Day +31 is the first day outside this sale’s window.
  • The standard 2026 inclusion rate is 50% for taxable capital gains and allowable capital losses.

Frequently asked questions

Does Canada use FIFO to calculate ACB?

For identical shares or ETF units held by the same taxpayer in taxable accounts, pool the acquisition costs and commissions. Allocate the weighted-average ACB per unit to each unit sold.

What if ETF return of capital exceeds my ACB?

Reduce the pool ACB to zero and recognize the excess as a capital gain when it occurs. Later purchases or basis increases do not reverse the earlier gain.

How do I record a phantom ETF distribution?

Add the reinvested non-cash amount to ACB when unit count stays unchanged. Report the taxable amount from the tax slip separately. For a cash distribution reinvested into extra units, enter the new units as a purchase instead of adding the amount twice.

Does a partial repurchase deny the whole loss?

The CRA administrative approach multiplies the loss by the smallest of units sold, units acquired during the inclusive 61-day window, and identical units held on day +30, divided by units sold. Day +31 is outside the window for this sale.

What happens if I repurchase in a TFSA, RRSP or FHSA?

If the registered plan acquisition triggers the superficial loss rule, the denied portion generally cannot be recovered. A qualifying taxable replacement can generally carry the denied amount in the replacement property’s ACB.
