Key takeaways

Eligible employees can get SSP from their first full qualifying sick day. The weekly rate is the lower of £123.25 and 80% of average weekly earnings, for up to 28 weeks. The £125 earnings threshold and three waiting days ended on 6 April 2026.

Frequently asked questions

When does day-one Statutory Sick Pay start?

Since 6 April 2026, eligible employees can get SSP for their first full qualifying day of sickness. Qualifying days are normally the days they work. The old three qualifying waiting days and £125 weekly earnings threshold have been removed.

How much SSP will I get if I earn £110 a week?

If you are eligible, one full qualifying week pays £88 gross, or 80% of £110. Under the 2025/26 rules, £110 was below the £125 weekly earnings threshold, so SSP would have been £0.

Can Statutory Sick Pay be lower under the new rules?

Yes. For example, earnings of £125 a week give a new weekly rate of £100, compared with the old £118.75 rate after waiting days. The new rules can pay more for a short spell and less over a longer one. Absences already in progress on 6 April 2026 can have transitional protections.

Is SSP paid on top of company sick pay?

Company sick pay can include SSP. In this calculator, full-pay days include any SSP due for those days and use up the corresponding statutory entitlement. Check your contract to see when company pay steps down.

How long can I get SSP and are there deductions?

SSP is payable for up to 28 weeks, including linked absences. It is processed through payroll with Income Tax and employee National Insurance where due. Your actual deductions depend on your pay period, tax code and other earnings.
