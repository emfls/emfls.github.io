Key takeaways

Most taxable estates pay 40% above their available allowances. The standard band is £325,000, and a qualifying home can add up to £175,000. The home allowance starts tapering above a £2 million net estate. Gifts within seven years can use up the basic band, and a qualifying charity legacy can bring the estate rate down to 36%.

    Common Tax Trap / What You Could Lose

    A £2.2 million net estate loses £100,000 of its maximum residence allowance before the home-value cap. If the estate could otherwise use that full £100,000 and pays the 40% rate, the taper can add £40,000 to the tax bill. A spouse or charity legacy does not lower the estate value used for the taper.

    Frequently asked questions

    How much can a UK estate pass on free of inheritance tax in 2026/27?

    The basic nil-rate band is £325,000. A qualifying home left to direct descendants can add up to £175,000. A surviving spouse or civil partner may claim unused percentages from the first death, bringing the combined bands to as much as £1 million if both transfers are 100% and the residence allowance is fully available.

    How does the £2 million residence nil-rate band taper work?

    The maximum residence allowance falls by £1 for every £2 of net estate over £2 million, before spouse and charity exemptions. The remaining allowance is capped at the net home share left to direct descendants. A full £175,000 allowance reaches zero at £2.35 million, and a full £350,000 allowance reaches zero at £2.7 million.

    When does a charity legacy reduce the estate tax rate to 36%?

    For a single estate component, eligible charity legacies must total at least 10% of the positive baseline. In this estimate the baseline is the net estate less the exempt spouse legacy and the basic nil-rate band remaining after failed gifts. The baseline includes the charity legacy and excludes the residence allowance from the deductions.

    Which lifetime gifts receive taper relief?

    Outright potentially exempt transfers normally fall outside inheritance tax once the donor survives seven years. Within seven years, the oldest gifts use the basic nil-rate band first. The part above the remaining band is taxed at 40%, 32%, 24%, 16% or 8%, depending on how long the donor survived.

    How does a spouse or civil partner legacy affect the estimate?

    An outright legacy that qualifies for full spouse or civil partner exemption comes out of the taxable estate and the charity baseline. It still counts in the estate value used for the £2 million taper. Full exemption generally applies when both parties are long-term UK residents, and cross-border cases can have different limits.
