UK Mortgage Overpayment vs Cash ISA & Savings Calculator

Compare mortgage interest saved with tax-free ISA growth and after-tax savings. Includes 2026/27 and April 2027 savings tax rates, plus an early repayment charge (ERC) check.

Key takeaways

  • Compare all three options at your original mortgage payoff date. The taxable savings result accounts for your remaining Personal Savings Allowance (PSA).
  • Overpayments reduce the interest you owe and can shorten the term if your normal payment stays the same. Cash ISA interest is tax-free within the subscription rules.
  • At a 44% savings tax rate with your PSA used up, matching a 5% mortgage rate takes 8.93% gross on a simple rate comparison, or 9.14% AER with monthly mortgage compounding.

Avoid paying a charge that eats into your savings

A 3% charge on £5,000 above your lender’s cap costs £150 immediately. Check the balance your lender uses, the reset date and any separate charge for full redemption before sending an overpayment. Money paid into the mortgage can also be hard to get back if you need it for an emergency.

How the comparison works

All routes use the same lump sum, scheduled mortgage payment and monthly spare cash. The model applies the lump sum immediately, then charges mortgage interest at one-twelfth of the entered rate and applies scheduled payments before regular overpayments. The model holds unused and freed payments in cash at 0% on the mortgage route and subtracts ERCs from that route’s cash. Both mortgage balances are cleared by the original payoff date, so the comparison shows the cash and savings left over.

The scenario starts on 6 April of the selected tax year. Savings compound monthly at the equivalent AER and receive deposits at month-end. The model deducts tax at each tax-year end and settles tax at the comparison date for a partial year. It applies the selected savings band to interest above your available PSA, assumes the same amount of other interest each year, and carries forward the announced April 2027 rules and selected age group. Allow for any starting rate for savings, unused Personal Allowance or tax-band change separately.

The ERC model treats each 12-month period as a lender year. It calculates a new cap from the balance at the start of that year, uses the current balance for the first cap, and applies one ERC rate to actual overpayments above the cap. Rates and lender terms are held constant. Lenders can calculate interest daily, recalculate monthly payments, use a different allowance basis or charge for full redemption.

Frequently asked questions

How do I compare an overpayment with a savings rate?

Compare the interest the overpayment avoids with the interest the same money earns after tax. Use the matching time period and account for any early repayment charge. A 5% nominal mortgage rate compounds to about 5.12% annually under this calculator’s monthly model.

How does the Personal Savings Allowance affect my return?

Basic-rate taxpayers can generally earn £1,000 of savings interest at 0% and higher-rate taxpayers £500. Additional-rate taxpayers have no PSA. Existing taxable interest can use up some or all of that allowance. The model deducts tax only on projected interest above the available PSA.

What changes on 6 April 2027?

The savings tax rates rise to 24%, 44% and 49%. The Cash ISA subscription limit is set to be £12,000 for under-65s, within the £20,000 overall ISA allowance. The Cash ISA limit for people aged 65 and over is set to remain at £20,000.

Does Cash ISA interest use my Personal Savings Allowance?

Cash ISA interest is tax-free and leaves your PSA available for non-ISA interest. Paying into other ISAs can reduce the overall room you have left for new Cash ISA subscriptions. The model directs new cash above the available ISA room into taxable savings.

How does the calculator estimate early repayment charges?

It subtracts any allowance already used from the first year’s cap and counts both the actual lump sum and regular overpayments against the available allowance. It applies the specified ERC rate to the excess. Check your mortgage offer for the balance basis, reset date and any full-redemption charge.
