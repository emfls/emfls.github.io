Lower repayments can come with a larger buyout

Budget for the government’s share at the value used when you repay it. Every A$100,000 rise adds A$30,000 to a 30% buyout or A$40,000 to a 40% buyout. Falling prices reduce the buyout and the equity you hold in the home. Refinancing to fund the buyout depends on your lender’s assessment and available equity.

How the comparison works

All three mortgages use the same constant annual rate, monthly payments and a 30-year term. Help to Buy assumes the maximum government share and no interest charged on that share. The 5% loan models an editable LMI estimate of 3.8% of the base loan by default. A financed premium increases the loan balance and interest, while an upfront premium increases your cash at purchase. The 20% loan assumes zero LMI.

The buyout multiplies the purchase price by compounded annual growth and then by the original government percentage. The net wealth difference is interest saved through exit, plus LMI avoided, minus the government’s share of the change in home value. It accounts for the different deposits and outstanding loan balances through that cost comparison.

Estimates hold the government share constant and exclude taxes, stamp duty, purchase and sale costs, valuation and refinance fees, later buybacks, investment returns on cash savings and any interest after the modeled exit. Frequently asked questions

Which income caps apply to Help to Buy in 2026–27?

From 1 July 2026 to 30 June 2027, the cap is A$103,000 for a single applicant and A$165,000 for joint applicants or a single parent or legal guardian. Use taxable income from the FY2026 ATO Notice of Assessment and combine both applicants’ incomes for a joint application.

Can I buy with a 2% deposit and no LMI?

Help to Buy requires at least 2% from savings and the maximum reasonable deposit you can afford. The government can contribute up to 30% for an existing home or 40% for an eligible new home. The initial Help to Buy purchase loan has no lenders mortgage insurance. The participating lender assesses what you can borrow.

How is the government buyout calculated?

Multiply the government’s remaining equity percentage by the home value used for repayment at the time. For example, a 30% share originally worth A$240,000 costs A$300,000 to buy out if the applicable value rises to A$1 million. If the value falls to A$700,000, the same share costs A$210,000. Actual repayments follow scheme valuation and equity adjustment rules.

What price cap applies in Sydney or Darwin?

Housing Australia lists a Help to Buy cap of A$1.3 million for Sydney and A$600,000 for Darwin. Confirm the cap for the property’s exact suburb and postcode using the official Help to Buy postcode tool.

When can a conventional 5% deposit loan leave me ahead?

In this model a 5% deposit loan leaves you ahead when the government’s share of price growth exceeds the mortgage interest and LMI you save with Help to Buy. Enter a lender’s LMI estimate or test 0% if you qualify for a no-LMI loan. The result assumes you keep cash savings without earning a return.
