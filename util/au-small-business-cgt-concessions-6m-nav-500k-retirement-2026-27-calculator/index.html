2026–27 Australia ATO Small Business CGT Concessions (ITAA 1997 Division 152)

Selling a profitable Australian small business, goodwill, or commercial premises can easily trigger a seven-figure capital gain. Yet under Division 152 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1997, eligible Australian business owners can stack up to four extraordinarily generous ATO Small Business CGT Concessions—frequently slashing Capital Gains Tax all the way down to zero on exits worth A$1.5 million to A$2.0 million or more per stakeholder.

Before accessing any concession, the taxpayer must clear two threshold gates: the Active Asset Test (the asset was used in carrying on a business for at least half the ownership period, or 7.5 years if owned longer than 15 years) AND at least one of the two size gates—either the $6,000,000 Maximum Net Asset Value (MNAV) Test (aggregating net market value of business and investment assets across you, connected entities, and affiliates, while crucially excluding your main residence, personal-use assets, and superannuation balances!) OR the $2,000,000 Aggregated Turnover Test as a CGT Small Business Entity.

Exact Statutory Ordering of Division 152 Concessions

The ATO mandates a strict sequence when applying Small Business CGT Concessions—getting the order right can save hundreds of thousands of dollars:

  1. Step 1 — 15-Year Exemption (Subdivision 152-B): The undisputed crown jewel. If you continuously owned the active asset for at least 15 years, are aged 55 or older (and the sale happens in connection with your retirement), or are permanently incapacitated, 100% of the capital gain is completely disregarded. You do not even have to apply prior capital losses against it, and proceeds can be contributed into superannuation under your lifetime CGT Cap (indexed above A$1.865 million+) without breaching standard non-concessional super limits.
  2. Step 2 — Offset Capital Losses: If the 15-Year Exemption does not apply, any current-year or carried-forward capital losses must be subtracted from the gross capital gain before percentage discounts apply.
  3. Step 3 — General 50% CGT Discount (Subdivision 115-A): Individuals and complying family trusts holding the asset for more than 12 months cut the remaining gain in half (50%). Private companies do not receive the general 50% CGT discount, which is why Australian accountants strongly prefer holding appreciating business goodwill and commercial real estate in discretionary trusts rather than Pty Ltd companies.
  4. Step 4 — 50% Active Asset Reduction (Subdivision 152-C): Eligible taxpayers then reduce the remaining gain by a further 50%—meaning an individual or trust stacks two 50% cuts to shrink a A$1,600,000 gross gain straight down to just A$400,000 (a 75% combined reduction!). Note that you can optionally elect out of the 50% Active Asset Reduction if you want to push a larger dollar amount into superannuation tax-free under the $500,000 Retirement Exemption.
  5. Step 5 — $500,000 Lifetime Retirement Exemption (Subdivision 152-D): You can exempt up to A$500,000 (lifetime limit per CGT concession stakeholder) of the remaining taxable gain. On a A$1,600,000 sale by a family trust, applying the 50% General Discount (A$800,000), 50% Active Asset Reduction (A$400,000), and A$400,000 of Retirement Exemption reduces net taxable gain to A$0—saving A$376,000 in CGT at the 47% top marginal rate!

Critical Age-55 Trap: Mandatory Superannuation Contribution vs. Tax-Free Cash

Despite its name, you do not have to retire to claim the A$500,000 Retirement Exemption! However, your age on the day you make the written choice (or receive the proceeds from a company/trust) triggers an absolute ATO rule: if you are aged 55 or older, you can pocket the exempted gain directly as tax-free personal cash. If you are under age 55, you are legally forced to contribute 100% of the Retirement Exemption amount into a complying superannuation fund or SMSF (by the later of receiving the proceeds or making the choice). Failing to deposit the funds into super on time invalidates the exemption retroactively—triggering full 47% CGT plus ATO shortfall penalties. The silver lining: a properly documented Retirement Exemption super roll-in counts toward your lifetime CGT Super Cap rather than your annual A$120,000 non-concessional cap, letting under-55 founders supercharge their SMSF tax-free.

Why Selling Shares or Units Requires Extra Caution

When selling shares in a Pty Ltd company or units in a unit trust (rather than selling the underlying business assets directly), Subdivision 152-A adds an extra 80% Active Asset test inside the company plus requires you to be a CGT Concession Stakeholder (generally holding at least a 20% small business participation percentage directly or through interposed entities, or being the spouse of a significant individual with a direct participation >0%). Additionally, extracting the 50% Active Asset Reduction out of a Pty Ltd company later often triggers an unfranked dividend trap, whereas a family trust can distribute the tax-free 50% Active Asset portion directly to adult beneficiaries with zero extra tax. Reviewing entity ownership and MNAV balance sheets 12 months before listing your business for sale protects every cent of Division 152 relief.

Worked 2026–27 ATO Benchmark: Under-55 Founder vs. 15-Year Retiree

Compare two common Australian business exits to see how Division 152 transforms after-tax wealth. In Scenario 1, a 52-year-old business owner sells goodwill held in a family trust for 8 years, realizing a A$1,600,000 gross capital gain with A$4.2 million in net business assets (passing the A$6M MNAV test). Without Division 152, the general 50% CGT discount leaves A$800,000 taxable—costing A$376,000 in CGT at 47%. By stacking the 50% Active Asset Reduction (cutting the gain to A$400,000) and applying A$400,000 of the A$500,000 Lifetime Retirement Exemption, net taxable gain drops to A$0—saving the entire A$376,000! Because the owner is under age 55, they must deposit that A$400,000 directly into their complying superannuation fund or SMSF, where it enters tax-free under the lifetime CGT Super Cap.

In Scenario 2, a 60-year-old retiring founder sells a commercial workshop owned continuously for 16 years with a A$3,000,000 gross gain (MNAV A$5.0 million). Because the Subdivision 152-B 15-Year Exemption applies first, 100% of the A$3,000,000 gain is completely disregarded—saving over A$705,000 in CGT with zero forced superannuation deposit and full optional access to the lifetime CGT super cap. Finally, always consider whether Subdivision 152-E Small Business Rollover (deferring a remaining capital gain for 2 years while acquiring a replacement active asset) fits your growth roadmap if you plan to reinvest straight into another Australian enterprise.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does my family home or superannuation count toward the $6 million MNAV test?

No. Under ITAA 1997 s.152-20, your main residence (to the extent not used to produce assessable income), personal-use assets (furniture, personal cars), and superannuation balances are strictly excluded from the $6,000,000 Maximum Net Asset Value test.

Do I actually have to retire to claim the $500,000 Small Business Retirement Exemption?

No. You do not need to stop working or retire to claim the $500,000 Retirement Exemption. However, if you are under age 55 when making the choice, you MUST pay the exempted amount directly into a complying superannuation fund or SMSF.

Can a Pty Ltd company claim the General 50% CGT Discount before Division 152?

No. Companies are ineligible for the general 50% CGT discount (Subdiv 115-A). A company can still claim the 15-Year Exemption, the 50% Active Asset Reduction, and the $500,000 Retirement Exemption (for each significant individual), though extracting the 50% Active Asset portion from a company can create unfranked dividend issues.

What if I have multiple family members in a discretionary trust selling a business?

Each CGT concession stakeholder who receives a qualifying distribution from the trust gets their own separate $500,000 lifetime Retirement Exemption cap—meaning a couple (two stakeholders) can shield up to A$1,000,000 of post-discount gain (equivalent to A$4,000,000 of gross capital gain after stacking both 50% discounts!).
