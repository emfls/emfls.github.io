Avoid losing retirement-phase tax treatment

Missing the minimum payment or exceeding the TTR maximum can cause the income stream to cease for tax purposes. Check your fund’s actual payments before 30 June. A spending target above the minimum can also shorten how long the account lasts, especially after early investment losses.

Frequently asked questions

How is my 2026–27 minimum worked out?

Multiply your pension balance by the rate for your age. Use your age and balance on 1 July for an existing pension, or on the start date for a new pension. Round to the nearest A$10, with exactly A$5 rounded up. The monthly figure is the annual requirement divided by 12.

What if I start my pension during the financial year?

For a start before 1 June, multiply the full-year minimum by the share of financial-year days from your start date through 30 June, including both dates, before rounding. A pension starting on or after 1 June has no minimum payment for that financial year.

How does proportional cap indexation work?

Divide your highest pre-indexation transfer balance by your personal cap on the first day you reached that balance. Round the used percentage down, subtract it from 100% and multiply the result by A$100,000. If you previously reached or exceeded your personal cap, you receive no increase.

Does drawing down my pension restore transfer cap space?

Regular pension payments and investment losses generally leave your transfer balance account unchanged. A properly reported commutation can create a debit. Remaining cap space depends on your current transfer balance account across all retirement-phase income streams.

Are retirement-phase and TTR investment earnings taxed the same way?

Investment earnings and gains supporting an eligible retirement-phase pension are generally exempt from fund tax. Earnings supporting a TTR income stream outside retirement phase are generally taxed in the fund at up to 15%. While TTR payment restrictions apply, the annual maximum is 10% of the 1 July or commencement balance.
