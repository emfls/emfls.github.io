Key takeaways

Seven years can take an outright gift out of Inheritance Tax. Before then, earlier chargeable gifts use the £325,000 basic nil-rate band first.

Taper relief only reduces tax on the part of a gift above the available band. If the band covers the gift, taper relief saves £0. The gift can still leave less band for the estate, where the standard rate is 40%.

A £0 gift tax bill can still cost the estate

For example, a £100,000 gift after exemptions can use £100,000 of the nil-rate band. If the estate would have used that whole amount, it could owe £40,000 more when death occurs within seven years. Keep dates, values and exemption records so the executor can calculate what remains.

Frequently asked questions

Does taper relief help if my gifts are within the nil-rate band?

No. Taper relief reduces the tax due on the part of a failed gift above the available nil-rate band. A gift covered by the band can still leave less of it for the estate.

What happens on the seventh anniversary of a gift?

An outright potentially exempt transfer becomes exempt after seven years. Earlier outright gifts also age out by that point. Different rules apply to trusts and gifts from which the donor still benefits.

Can I combine annual, wedding and income exemptions?

A qualifying wedding gift can use both the wedding exemption and unused annual exemption. The annual allowance is £3,000 per tax year, with unused allowance carried forward for one year only. Regular gifts from income may be fully exempt if they meet HMRC’s conditions.

Who pays Inheritance Tax on a failed gift?

The recipient normally pays the tax attributable to their gift. The executor or administrator pays the estate’s own tax out of estate funds. A will can affect where the money ultimately comes from.

How do transferred and residence nil-rate bands affect the calculation?

A valid claim for unused basic band from a spouse or civil partner can increase the band available to cover failed gifts and the estate. The residence band applies only to the estate and depends on the qualifying home share left to direct descendants and the size of the estate.
