Washington 9.9% Capital Gains Tax vs. Massachusetts 4% Millionaires Tax Calculator 2026

Compare the state tax on a stock exit, salary, or property gain. See how the result changes when you recognize long-term stock gains over several years.

Key takeaways

  • Washington: $290,000 standard deduction in 2026. The first $1 million of taxable Washington long-term gain faces 7%. The portion above that faces 9.9%. Wages and direct real estate gains are outside this tax.
  • Massachusetts: The 4% surtax starts above $1,107,750 of combined taxable income. Marginal rates reach 9% for wages and ordinary long-term gains and 12.5% for short-term gains.
  • California: Capital gains use the same brackets as wages. The top income tax rate is 13.3%. The combined top marginal rate on wages subject to 2026 SDI can reach 14.6%.

    Frequently asked questions

    When does Washington’s 9.9% capital gains rate start in 2026?

    The rate applies to taxable Washington long-term gains above $1 million. With the $290,000 standard deduction and no other deductions, the higher rate starts above $1.29 million of qualifying gains. The first $1 million after deductions faces 7%.

    Do married couples receive two Washington deductions?

    Washington limits spouses and domestic partners to one combined $290,000 standard deduction for 2026, including when they file separately. Qualifying charitable gifts above $290,000 can generate a separate deduction up to $116,000 under Washington’s eligibility rules.

    How does Massachusetts tax wages, vested RSUs and investment gains?

    Wages and RSU vesting compensation generally face the 5% base rate. Subsequent gain on shares is generally taxed at 8.5% if short term or 5% if long term. The 4% Fair Share surtax applies to combined taxable income above $1,107,750.

    How do the states treat gains from selling a home?

    A direct real estate sale is exempt from the Washington capital gains tax, though Washington real estate excise tax can apply separately. Massachusetts and California generally include taxable gain after any applicable home-sale exclusion. The location of the property can create a separate state filing obligation.

    Can I use an installment sale to keep gains below a threshold?

    Eligible installment sales can recognize gain as payments arrive. Publicly traded securities generally require recognition in the sale year, so a staggered plan for those shares would involve separate sales. Washington generally follows eligible federal installment reporting, subject to its own rules, including those for pre-2022 sales.
