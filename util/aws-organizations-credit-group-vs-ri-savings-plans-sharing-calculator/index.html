Month-end governance at 23:59:59 UTC

The final monthly AWS bill uses RI/SP sharing preferences active at 23:59:59 UTC on the last day of the month. AWS also uses the last-day credit sharing preference. Mid-month changes can recalculate allocations for the month to date.

  • Verify the AWS Cost Category, BU membership, and account-level activation for credits and commitments in Billing preferences.
  • Review the inherited credit default, each expiring credit’s override and applicable products, and each commitment’s sharing rule in Inventory.
  • Record the approved settings before cutoff. Check for stranded commitment cost and credits that will expire with a balance.
  • Reconcile the finalized Bills page and Cost and Usage Report with internal chargeback, including any approved BU-A-to-BU-B subsidy.

How the estimate works

RI/SP commitment. The owner gets matching coverage first. Open considers all sharing-enabled accounts together. Prioritized considers BU-A first and offers the remainder to BU-B. Restricted stops at BU-A. Within each tier the model covers the largest percentage savings first, using the input rate, then account order for ties. In AWS, Open represents the organization-wide setting when no commitment-level override applies.

AWS credit. Apply the credit against the owner’s remaining eligible usage charges, then to the highest eligible balance within each allowed tier. The modeled commitment fee is outside the credit pool. The inherited Prioritized default can spill beyond BU-A. The selected credit-group override stays within BU-A. A per-credit override represents an allowed set of accounts subject to account-level activation.

Chargeback. Each net bill equals usage before benefits minus covered On-Demand charges, plus the owner’s commitment fee where applicable, minus credit applied. The BU-B subsidy adds its avoided On-Demand charges and consumed credit. It is the gross benefit to BU-B before any internal reimbursement. Unused commitment cost is included in the owner’s fee.

Scope. Use rates already on your AWS agreement. The model estimates allocation, chargeback, and waste using one monthly pool and one representative rate per account. AWS applies matching rules at the usage-line level, and hourly commitment capacity cannot carry between hours. Monthly pooling can understate waste when usage varies. Taxes, multiple commitments and credits, and upfront cash timing are outside the estimate.

Frequently asked questions

Can a credit override its AWS credit sharing group?

New credits inherit the credit group default. A per-credit preference overrides that default for the selected credit. Shared credits still require eligible service charges and sharing activation for the owner and recipients.

How do Open, Prioritized, and Restricted commitment sharing differ?

The owner gets matching coverage first. Open shares surplus across sharing-enabled accounts. Prioritized covers the designated group first, then lets surplus reach other eligible accounts. Restricted keeps surplus within the designated group, so matching usage elsewhere can remain uncovered.

When do AWS sharing changes affect the monthly bill?

AWS uses RI and Savings Plans sharing preferences in effect at 23:59:59 UTC on the last day of the month. AWS also uses the last-day credit sharing preference. Changes before close can reallocate benefits for the month to date.

Does the calculator estimate a new AWS discount rate?

The simulator uses the existing rates you enter to estimate benefit allocation, chargeback, and unused commitment risk. It models one monthly commitment pool, so hourly usage variation can increase actual waste.
