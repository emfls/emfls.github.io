Australia Stamp Duty & First Home Buyer Calculator 2026–27

Compare transfer duty in NSW, Victoria and Queensland at the same property value.

Key takeaways

  • NSW: Eligible first homes pay zero duty through A$800,000. Vacant residential land has a separate A$350,000 exemption threshold.
  • VIC: Eligible first home buyers pay zero through A$600,000 and receive partial relief up to A$750,000.
  • QLD: Eligible new first homes and vacant residential land have no price cap. Eligible established first homes pay zero through A$700,000.

Frequently asked questions

Which value should I enter for stamp duty?

Use the purchase price if it reflects market value. If market value is higher, use that higher amount. Check with your conveyancer if an off-the-plan concession changes your dutiable value.

How much duty does a NSW first home buyer pay at $900,000?

An eligible buyer of a new or established home pays A$19,593.50 in transfer duty in 2026–27. The full exemption ends at A$800,000, and standard duty applies from A$1 million.

Does Victoria's first home buyer relief cover vacant land?

Yes, eligible buyers can claim relief on vacant land intended for their first home. The exemption applies up to A$600,000 of dutiable land value, with a partial concession above that amount through A$750,000. Construction and occupancy deadlines apply.

Does Queensland have a price cap for new first homes?

Queensland has no price cap for full transfer duty relief on an eligible first new home or vacant residential land to build it on. Established first homes use a separate schedule with zero duty through A$700,000 and stepped deductions below A$800,000.

Does a first home concession remove the foreign buyer surcharge?

It depends on the state. A NSW first home exemption can still leave surcharge duty payable. Victoria waives its additional duty on exempt transfers. In Queensland, buyers subject to full AFAD generally use standard transfer duty for contracts from 1 August 2026. Confirm foreign status and any exemption before signing.
