Frequently asked questions

How much can I roll from a 529 to a Roth IRA in 2026?

Start with the lower of taxable IRA compensation and the 2026 IRA limit of $7,500, or $8,600 at age 50 or older by year-end. Subtract other IRA contributions and earlier 2026 529-to-Roth transfers. The result is limited by eligible 529 funds and the beneficiary’s remaining $35,000 lifetime allowance.

How do the 15-year rule and five-year lookback work?

The 529 must have been maintained for the beneficiary for at least 15 years as of the transfer date. Only contributions made before the five-year period ending on that date and their attributable earnings can transfer. Ask the 529 administrator to confirm eligible dollars and the account history, especially after a beneficiary or plan change.

Can a high-income beneficiary make a 529-to-Roth rollover?

Yes. The regular Roth IRA modified adjusted gross income phase-out does not reduce an eligible 529 rollover. The taxable compensation and shared IRA limits still apply. The 529 trustee must send the money directly to a Roth IRA for the same beneficiary.

What is the federal cost of a nonqualified 529 withdrawal?

Ordinary federal income tax generally applies to the taxable earnings included in the distribution. A 10% additional federal tax usually applies to those taxable earnings unless an exception applies. The plan’s reported earnings and basis determine the withdrawal’s earnings share.

Do California and New York follow the federal rollover rules?

California does not follow the federal exclusion, so state income tax and a 2.5% additional tax can apply to taxable earnings. New York treats eligible direct rollovers from a New York 529 plan as qualified on or after September 5, 2024. A nonqualified New York 529 withdrawal can trigger a state add-back.
