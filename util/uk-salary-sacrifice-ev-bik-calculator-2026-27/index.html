How the estimate works

For a fully electric company car, taxable benefit is 4% of P11D value in 2026/27. The schedule shows taxable benefit at 5% in 2027/28, 7% in 2028/29 and 9% in 2029/30. The 2026/27 benefit tax is the difference between annual income tax with and without the benefit at the reduced cash salary.

The standard personal allowance is £12,570, reduced by £1 per £2 of adjusted net income over £100,000 and exhausted at £125,140. Taxable income uses 20% on the first £37,700, then 40% through £125,140 and 45% above. Standard employee Class 1 NI is 8% between £12,570 and £50,270 and 2% above £50,270, calculated on cash pay.

For £70,000 salary, a £45,000 EV and £600 monthly gross sacrifice, the taxable benefit is £1,800, benefit tax £720, and annual take-home cost £4,896. For the slice of pay from £110,000 down to £100,000, the gross relief before EV benefit tax is 60% income tax plus 2% employee NI.

This full-year estimate assumes one salary, standard employee NI, full-year availability, no other income, adjustments or taxable benefits, and no separate employee payments for the car. Actual NI can vary by pay period. Check the employer’s minimum-wage assessment for the relevant pay reference period. The calculation excludes Scottish income tax, other lease costs, benefits and future payroll tax rates.

Frequently asked questions

Does the car benefit count toward adjusted net income?

Yes. The calculator adds the taxable car benefit to reduced cash pay. On £110,000 salary, a £12,000 sacrifice and a £55,000 EV, estimated adjusted net income is £100,200, still above the Tax-Free Childcare income ceiling.

Does dropping to £100,000 guarantee Tax-Free Childcare?

No. £100,000 or less meets the upper income test for this person, but your partner’s income and other conditions also matter. Check the eligibility rules before making a salary sacrifice decision.

Can I sacrifice pay below the National Minimum Wage?

No. A salary sacrifice arrangement must keep cash earnings at or above the applicable minimum wage. Your employer needs to check your pay and hours for the relevant pay period.
