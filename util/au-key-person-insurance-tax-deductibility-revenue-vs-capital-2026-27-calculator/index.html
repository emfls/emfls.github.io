ATO Revenue Purpose vs. Capital Purpose: The Core Australian Key Person Tax Rule

In Australia, the tax treatment of Key Person business insurance is governed not by the marketing label on the policy brochure, but by the documented commercial purpose for which the company holds the cover—specifically under ATO guidance (including IT 155 principles) and the Income Tax Assessment Act 1997 (ITAA 1997). Every board of directors and SME accountant must document in formal board minutes at policy inception whether the insurance is held for a Revenue Purpose or a Capital Purpose—and review that purpose annually.

Under a Revenue Purpose policy, the business insures a rainmaker, technical founder, or senior executive to replace lost gross trading profits, maintain working capital, and fund executive recruitment while operations recover. Because the outgoing relates directly to protecting assessable trading income, the company can generally claim annual premiums as a tax deduction under ITAA 1997 s.8-1. However, the symmetry rule cuts both ways: if a claim is paid, the entire insurance payout is treated as assessable ordinary income under s.6-5 and taxed at the company's marginal corporate rate (25% for base rate entities under A$50m aggregated turnover, or 30% for standard companies).

Under a Capital Purpose policy, the business insures the key person to retire bank debt, protect enterprise goodwill/credit standing, or preserve capital value on the balance sheet. Because the outgoing is capital in nature, annual premiums are not tax-deductible under s.8-1(2)(a). In return, life insurance claim proceeds paid to the original beneficial owner are generally exempt from Capital Gains Tax (CGT) under ITAA 1997 s.118-300 (and TPD/trauma capital proceeds have specific statutory CGT exemptions under s.118-37 where conditions are satisfied, though specialist tax advice is critical for TPD/trauma owned by a company).

The Mathematical Equivalence—and the Hidden Gross-Up Trap

Many SME owners instinctively select Revenue Purpose because "we get a tax deduction on the premiums every year." Look closely at the after-tax mathematics in the calculator above, however: when a company properly grosses up its Revenue-purpose sum insured by 1 / (1 - t) so that the net post-tax payout equals the exact same target cash, the after-tax annual premium cost is algebraically identical to a non-deductible Capital-purpose policy—assuming a constant flat premium rate per A$100,000 of cover!

For example, suppose a base-rate Australian company (25% tax rate) needs exactly A$1,200,000 net cash if its managing director dies. Under Capital Purpose, it insures A$1,200,000 at A$420 per A$100,000 = A$5,040 non-deductible per year. Under Revenue Purpose, it must insure A$1,200,000 / 0.75 = A$1,600,000 so that after paying A$400,000 in ATO s.6-5 tax on claim, exactly A$1,200,000 remains. Its gross deductible premium is A$6,720, saving A$1,680 in corporate tax—leaving an exact net after-tax premium of A$5,040/year.

Where do companies lose hundreds of thousands of dollars? In two real-world scenarios:

  1. Forgetting to Gross Up a Revenue Policy: The accountant deducts the premiums under s.8-1, nobody grosses up the sum insured, and upon claim the ATO collects 25% or 30% tax—leaving a devastating A$250,000–A$450,000 funding hole precisely when the business is most vulnerable.
  2. Splitting Mixed Needs Into Two Separate Policies: If a business has A$600,000 in revenue-protection exposure and A$800,000 in bank-debt repayment exposure, lumping both into one blended policy invites ATO audit challenges. Best practice is holding two distinct policies with explicit annual board resolutions recording each policy's sole commercial purpose.

Board Resolution & Audit-Proofing Checklist for 2026–27

  • Document Purpose Before Policy Issue: Record a signed directors' minute specifying whether the policy replaces assessable trading revenue (Revenue) or retires capital debt / protects goodwill (Capital).
  • Verify Original Beneficial Ownership: The s.118-300 CGT exemption on life insurance requires the recipient to be the original beneficial owner (or to have acquired the rights for nil consideration). Transferring an existing policy between related entities for consideration can destroy the CGT exemption!
  • Watch Company-Owned TPD & Trauma Policies: Unlike life cover under s.118-300, TPD and trauma capital payouts to a company entity can trigger CGT under s.118-37 because a company is not the injured individual or a relative. Always consult a registered Australian tax adviser before placing TPD/trauma cover inside a company structure.
  • Re-Index Sum Insured Annually: Revisit gross profit contribution and guaranteed bank facilities every financial year as turnover grows.

Frequently Asked Questions (2026/27)

Are key person insurance premiums tax-deductible in Australia?

Only when the policy is held for a documented Revenue Purpose (protecting assessable trading profit or funding executive replacement) under ITAA 1997 s.8-1. If held for a Capital Purpose (repaying business loans or protecting goodwill), premiums are non-deductible.

How much extra cover is needed under ATO Revenue Purpose rules?

At the 25% base rate entity company tax rate, you must gross up the sum insured by +33.33% (target divided by 0.75). At the 30% standard corporate tax rate, you must gross up by +42.86% (target divided by 0.70) so net claim cash meets your target after s.6-5 tax.

Are Capital Purpose key person life insurance payouts tax-free?

Generally yes for life cover paid to the original beneficial owner under ITAA 1997 s.118-300, which exempts the payout from Capital Gains Tax. However, transferring an existing policy for consideration or holding TPD/trauma inside a company can trigger CGT.

Can an Australian company hold both a Revenue policy and a Capital policy on the same founder?

Yes. Splitting cover into two separate policies—one documented for profit replacement and one documented for bank debt extinguishment—is widely recommended to maintain clean ATO audit evidence.

Does the 25% or 30% company tax rate apply in 2026–27?

Companies with aggregated annual turnover under A$50 million and no more than 80% base rate entity passive income pay 25%. Companies above A$50 million turnover or passive investment companies pay the 30% corporate rate.
