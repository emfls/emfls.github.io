Key takeaways

  • A qualifying sale must clear both clocks: two years from grant and one year from transfer. Ordinary income is capped at the smaller of your actual gain or the grant-date discount. Any remaining gain is long-term.
  • An early sale generally creates ordinary income equal to the purchase-date spread, even when the shares sell at a loss.
  • Add ESPP compensation to the purchase cost when reconciling Form 1099-B. This keeps the same amount from being taxed again as a capital gain.

What could erase the tax savings?

Waiting can expose both your investment and your paycheck to the same company. A lower share price can wipe out the estimated tax savings. In an early sale, you can still owe ordinary income tax on the purchase spread even if shares sell below your purchase price.

Watch for double taxation on Form 1099-B

Check Form W-2 Box 1 for Section 423 compensation. If it is missing, IRS guidance directs you to Schedule 1, line 8k. Add the compensation to your purchase cost for capital gain or loss. If your broker reported only your purchase cost to the IRS, use code B in Form 8949 column (f) and enter the basis increase as a negative amount in column (g). If basis was not reported to the IRS, generally enter corrected basis in column (e).

ESPP tax questions

What makes an ESPP sale a qualifying disposition?

Both holding periods must pass: two years from the grant date and one year from the date the stock was transferred to you. Selling early can still produce long-term capital gain or loss if only the grant test fails.

How do lookback pricing and the $25,000 ESPP limit work?

Lookback pricing applies the discount to the lower of grant-date and purchase-date fair market value. For a single year with the entire limit available, divide $25,000 by grant-date fair market value and round down to whole shares. The actual accrual test considers your employer and related-corporation plans and the calendar years the option is outstanding.

Can I owe ordinary income tax on an ESPP sale at a loss?

Yes. A disqualifying sale generally creates ordinary income equal to the purchase-date fair market value minus the discounted purchase price. A sale below purchase-date fair market value can also generate a capital loss. A qualifying sale at or below what you paid generally creates no ordinary income.

Can ESPP gain trigger NIIT or Social Security and Medicare tax?

Positive capital gains can add to net investment income, and the ordinary compensation counts toward MAGI without itself becoming investment income. The 3.8% NIIT applies to the lesser of net investment income or MAGI above the filing threshold. Section 423 disposition income is exempt from Social Security and Medicare taxes.

How do Form 3922 and Form 8949 prevent double taxation?

Use Form 3922 to verify the lot dates, values and purchase price. Add Section 423 compensation to your basis. If the IRS received an understated basis, correct it on Form 8949 with code B and a negative column (g) adjustment for the difference. If basis was not reported to the IRS, generally enter corrected basis in column (e).
