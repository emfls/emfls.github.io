Which rules apply?

The State Pension Debits and Credits (Revaluation) Order 2026 (SI 2026/1226) was laid before Parliament on 25 November 2026. Under article 1(2), it commences on 22 December 2026 for advance awards under regulation 15(1) of the Social Security (Claims and Payments) Regulations 1987 and on 12 April 2027 generally. The State Pension age threshold is 13 April 2027. Article 1(3) limits the Order to England, Wales and Scotland. Read SI 2026/1226 and its explanatory note.

Article 2 and the Schedule set the percentage increases under paragraph 3 of Schedules 8 and 10 to the Pensions Act 2014. These provisions use the last applicable revaluation order in force before you reach State Pension age. This page models SI 2026/1226 only. For a later pension-age date, check whether a later instrument applies. The check assumes the relevant sharing order took effect before you reached State Pension age. Frequently asked questions

Who can use the 2027 revaluation rates?

The calculator checks relevant new State Pension sharing debits and credits for England, Wales and Scotland where State Pension age is reached on or after 13 April 2027. Northern Ireland has separate rules. Check whether a later order applies before using the 2026 Order for a later pension age date.

Which tax year should I select?

Use the UK tax year in which the pension sharing order took effect. A UK tax year runs from 6 April through 5 April of the following calendar year. The Schedule lists 2016/17 through 2026/27.

How does a £10 weekly debit or credit from 2026/27 change?

The 3.5% increase takes £10.00 to £10.35 a week, or £538.20 over 52 weeks. A debit reduces the pension by that amount and a credit adds to it.

Where do I get an official valuation for divorce or dissolution?

Use DWP Form BR20, currently published as BR20NSP, to request a State Pension valuation. Each party completes a separate form. This calculator estimates an existing debit or credit only. A court valuation, full State Pension award and protected payment calculation are outside its scope.
