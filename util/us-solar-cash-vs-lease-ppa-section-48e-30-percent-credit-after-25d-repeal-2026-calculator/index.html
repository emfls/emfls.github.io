Answer-First: 2026 Solar Financing Policy Rules

  • Section 25D Repeal (0% Cash Credit): Under 2025 federal legislation (H.R. 1), the homeowner residential solar credit expired December 31, 2025. Homeowners who buy with cash or solar loans receive zero federal tax credit in 2026.
  • Section 48E Commercial Credit (30% Active): Third-party owned (TPO) systems (leases and PPAs) are owned by commercial entities and remain eligible for the 30% Clean Electricity Investment Credit through 2027.
  • The TPO + Year-5 Buyout Arbitrage: By entering a third-party lease, the solar developer captures the 30% §48E credit and MACRS depreciation. If the contract includes a Year-5 Fair Market Value (FMV) buyout, the homeowner can purchase the system at a steep discount, capturing the tax credit indirectly.
  • Cash Payback Extension: Without the 30% federal credit, direct cash purchase break-even periods have lengthened by 3 to 5 years, making utility escalation rates the decisive variable in solar ROI.

Frequently Asked Questions

Did the Section 25D residential solar tax credit expire in 2026?

Under the 2025 federal tax reform act (H.R. 1), the Section 25D residential clean energy tax credit for homeowner-owned cash and loan solar systems expired on December 31, 2025, dropping federal direct credits to 0% for residential purchases in 2026.

Why do solar leases and PPAs still receive the 30% tax credit in 2026?

Third-party owned (TPO) systems such as solar leases and Power Purchase Agreements (PPAs) are commercial installations owned by the solar provider. They qualify under Section 48E (Clean Electricity Investment Credit), which remains active at 30% through December 31, 2027.

Is a solar lease better than buying cash in 2026 without the 25D credit?

Because solar companies can monetize the 30% Section 48E commercial credit and MACRS depreciation, they can offer Day-1 monthly lease rates lower than local utility electric bills. However, escalating leases (1.9%–2.9%/yr) can become expensive in later years unless a Year-5 buyout option is exercised.

How does a Year-5 Fair Market Value (FMV) buyout work?

Many prepaid and custom TPO lease structures allow the homeowner to purchase the system after Year 5 at Fair Market Value (typically 35%–40% of original cost). This allows the homeowner to capture the benefit of the commercial credit before taking full long-term ownership.

What is the typical break-even payback period for a 2026 cash solar system?

Without the 30% Section 25D federal credit, typical cash solar payback periods have lengthened from 6–8 years to approximately 9–13 years, depending on local utility electricity rates and annual net metering export values.

Calculation assumptions and methodology

Calculations assume standard PV solar degradation of 0.5% per year and local utility rate inflation. Section 48E credits reflect statutory rates for commercial clean energy properties placed in service before 2028.
