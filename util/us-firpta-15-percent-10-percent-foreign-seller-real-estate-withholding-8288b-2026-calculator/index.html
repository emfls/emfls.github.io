Buyer liability and the 20-day deadline

When withholding is due, the buyer generally files Form 8288 with copies A and B of Form 8288-A and pays the IRS by the 20th day after transfer. The buyer can be liable for tax, interest, and penalties if withholding or remittance is missed.

Submit Form 8288-B on or before the transfer to use the pending-application timing rule. A seller who applies must notify the buyer in writing by the transfer date. While the application is pending, the buyer holds the standard amount and generally files and remits by the 20th day after the IRS mails the certificate or denial to the buyer. The certificate controls the amount due.

How the estimate works

  1. Add capital improvements to purchase price and subtract accumulated depreciation to get adjusted basis.
  2. Subtract selling costs and adjusted basis from the gross amount realized. For the tax estimate, floor the result at zero.
  3. Apply 25% to the lesser of positive gain and accumulated real property depreciation. Apply the selected 15% or 20% rate to the remaining gain.
  4. Subtract the modeled tax from standard withholding and floor the potential release at zero.

The tax model assumes a nonresident alien individual selling U.S. real estate held for more than one year, with simplified basis and straight-line real-property depreciation. It omits other income, bracket stacking, exclusions, loss offsets, other recapture, state taxes, and special transaction rules. Nonresident aliens generally are exempt from the 3.8% net investment income tax.

Frequently asked questions

When do the 0%, 10%, and 15% FIRPTA rates apply?

For a qualifying individual residence buyer, withholding is 0% through $300,000, 10% above $300,000 through $1 million, and 15% above $1 million. The general rate for a nonqualifying purchase is 15% at every price. The applicable percentage applies to the whole withholding base.

How does the buyer qualify for the residence tiers?

The individual buyer or a family member must plan to live there on at least half the days anyone occupies the property, measured separately in each of the first two 12-month periods. Vacant days are excluded. A residence affidavit can document those plans for closing.

Why can withholding be due when the seller has a loss?

Standard withholding is based on gross amount realized before selling costs and basis. The seller’s income tax depends on taxable gain. A loss can bring the modeled tax to zero while the entire standard holdback is still required pending an IRS certificate.

Does filing Form 8288-B release cash at closing?

An IRS-issued certificate can reduce the holdback if issued in time for closing. For a timely application still pending at closing, the buyer holds the standard amount. The IRS determination controls how much can be released and how much must be sent to the IRS.

When must the buyer file Forms 8288 and 8288-A?

When withholding applies, the buyer generally files and pays by day 20 after transfer. With a timely certificate application pending at transfer, the deadline is generally day 20 after the IRS mails the buyer the certificate or denial. The buyer can be liable for underwithholding.
