Key takeaways

  • Main home: Welsh LTT is £0 up to £225,000. First-time buyers in Wales use the same main rates when eligible.
  • Additional home: When higher rates apply, a £40,000 purchase triggers £2,000 of Welsh LTT. The first £180,000 is taxed at 5%.
  • Across the border: A £300,000 main home costs £4,500 in Welsh LTT versus £5,000 in standard SDLT, or £0 in SDLT for an eligible first-time buyer.

Frequently asked questions

Does Wales offer first-time buyer LTT relief?

Wales has no separate first-time buyer relief. If the main residential rates apply, the first £225,000 is taxed at 0%. At £300,000, Welsh LTT is £4,500, while a qualifying first-time buyer in England or Northern Ireland pays £0 in SDLT.

How does the £40,000 threshold work for an additional home?

In a standard higher-rate purchase below £40,000, the Welsh higher rates do not apply. At exactly £40,000, if the higher-rate conditions are met, 5% applies to the whole £40,000, giving a £2,000 LTT bill. Above £180,000, additional bands apply to slices of the price.

Can I reclaim higher LTT when replacing my main home?

If you qualify and sell your former main home within three years of buying its replacement, you can usually claim the higher-rate LTT paid minus the main-rate LTT for the same purchase. Some cases qualify for longer. At £300,000, the potential refund is £15,450, from a £19,950 higher-rate bill.

What happens to SDLT relief just above £500,000?

At exactly £500,000, an eligible first-time buyer in England or Northern Ireland pays £10,000 in SDLT. Above £500,000, the entire purchase follows the standard SDLT bands. At £500,001, SDLT is £15,000 after HMRC whole-pound rounding.

How much purchase tax applies to a £275,000 shop?

For a standard freehold purchase at £275,000, Welsh non-residential LTT is £1,500 and England or Northern Ireland SDLT is £3,250. A new lease can also trigger tax on rent and different rules for the Welsh lease premium.
