Australia Division 296 Super Tax Calculator 2026–27

Estimate the additional tax on fund-reported super earnings with the A$3 million and A$10 million thresholds.

Key takeaways

  • From 1 July 2026, Division 296 adds 15% to the share of earnings attributable to super above A$3 million and a further 10% to the share above A$10 million.
  • For 2026–27, a total super balance of A$3 million or less on 30 June 2027 means zero Division 296 tax. From 2027–28, the higher of your opening and closing balances applies.
  • At A$4 million with A$100,000 in relevant earnings, the estimate is A$3,750.

Frequently asked questions

Which balance counts in 2026–27?

Your total super balance at 30 June 2027 determines the threshold and the taxable share of earnings. If an eligible withdrawal brings that balance to A$3 million or less by that date, Division 296 tax for the year is zero.

What changes in 2027–28?

The higher of the opening and closing balances drives both tiers. If your opening balance exceeds the applicable threshold, reducing the closing balance below it can still leave you with tax for that year. Check the ATO’s indexed thresholds for the year you are modelling.

What if my super balance rises without a sale?

The balance is used to allocate relevant earnings to each tier. Enter the earnings attributed to you and reported by your funds for Division 296. A rise in the market value of an unsold asset can affect the balance used in the formula.

Do negative earnings create a personal carry-forward balance?

Non-positive relevant super earnings produce zero Division 296 tax in this estimate. The fund’s own tax-loss treatment can affect the earnings it reports. The enacted Division 296 calculation provides no separate member-level carry-forward deduction.

Does the 25% rate apply to all my earnings?

The 15% charge applies to earnings attributed to the balance above the large threshold. The extra 10% applies to earnings attributed to the balance above the very large threshold, so only that portion is subject to both rates.
