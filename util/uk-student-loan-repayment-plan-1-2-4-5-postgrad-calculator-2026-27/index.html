Forecast assumptions

In England, the Department for Education projected full repayment for 32% of full-time Plan 2 starters in 2022/23 and 55% of full-time Plan 5 starters in 2025/26. Your forecast depends on your balance, earnings and time left. DfE student loan forecasts, table 4.3

The projection starts in tax year 2026/27 and holds its repayment thresholds constant to isolate your salary and interest assumptions. It applies salary growth each tax year, compounds the effective annual interest monthly, and caps the final payroll payment at the balance. It writes off the remaining balance at the end of your selected final year. Each loan runs on its own clock.

The interest inputs are fixed scenario assumptions. Plan 5 normally uses RPI only. Plan 2 usually uses RPI plus up to 3% based on income after study, and the England or Wales Postgraduate Loan normally uses RPI plus 3%. A temporary 6% cap applies to Plan 2 and Postgraduate Loans from 1 September 2026 to 31 August 2027. DfE and SLC terms and conditions

Actual lifetime results depend on future thresholds, interest caps, earnings and your SLC cancellation date. Frequently asked questions

How are 2026/27 monthly student loan deductions calculated?

For each active loan, subtract its monthly threshold from monthly earnings subject to Class 1 National Insurance. Apply 9% for Plan 1, 2, 4 or 5, or 6% for the Postgraduate Loan, then round each positive deduction down to a whole pound. Bonuses and unequal pay can change the annual payroll total.

Can I pay 15% with an undergraduate and a postgraduate loan?

Yes. While both loans are active, earnings above both thresholds face a combined marginal student loan deduction of 15% before payroll rounding. Between the two thresholds, only the 6% Postgraduate Loan rate applies. Income Tax and National Insurance are separate.

When did Plan 5 repayments start and how is interest set?

The earliest Plan 5 repayments began on 6 April 2026. The 2026/27 threshold is £25,000 a year. Plan 5 interest is normally set at RPI and can be lower under a market-rate cap. The usual write-off is 40 years after the April you first became due to repay.

How do I work out how many years I have until write-off?

Use the April you first became due to repay, which can precede your first actual deduction. Plan 2, most Plan 4 and England or Wales Postgraduate Loans use 30 years, and Plan 5 uses 40. Plan 1 uses 25 years if your first loan was paid on or after 1 September 2006. Older Plan 1 and Plan 4 loans have age-65 rules. Check your date with the Student Loans Company.

Is it worth making a voluntary overpayment?

Compare total projected cash paid with and without the lump sum. If both scenarios reach write-off with the same payroll deductions, the entire lump sum increases your cost. If the lump sum cuts enough future payroll deductions, it can reduce total cost. GOV.UK says voluntary overpayments cannot be refunded.
