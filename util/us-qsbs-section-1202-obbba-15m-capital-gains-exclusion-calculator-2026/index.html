2026 QSBS Section 1202 Tax Exclusion Calculator

Estimate capital gains tax on a qualified small business stock exit. Compare the legacy $10 million limit with the OBBBA $15 million limit and 3-, 4- and 5-year tiers.

Key takeaways

  • After July 4, 2025: Eligible stock can receive a 50%, 75% or 100% exclusion once held for 3, 4 or 5 years, respectively.
  • Legacy stock: The 100% tier for stock acquired September 28, 2010 through July 4, 2025 requires a hold of more than five years.
  • Apply the cap first: Start with the greater of the remaining $10M or $15M dollar limit, as applicable, and 10 times qualifying basis. Then multiply the eligible gain by the holding-period percentage.

QSBS questions

Which QSBS holding period applies?

Stock acquired after July 4, 2025 can qualify for 50%, 75% and 100% exclusions at the third, fourth and fifth anniversaries. Legacy stock acquired September 28, 2010 through July 4, 2025 can qualify for 100% after more than five years.

Does the $15 million cap apply before the exclusion percentage?

Yes. Apply the greater of the remaining dollar cap or 10 times qualifying adjusted basis to the gain first. A $20 million gain at the 50% tier with a $15 million cap produces a $7.5 million exclusion, $7.5 million of taxable Section 1202 gain and $5 million of regular taxable gain.

Why can the taxable portion be subject to a 28% rate?

The taxable portion within a partial Section 1202 exclusion falls in the federal 28% maximum-rate category. With the full 3.8% NIIT and all gain within the cap, the modeled effective rates on the total gain are 15.9% at three years and 7.95% at four years.

How does state conformity affect the estimate?

The conforming scenario applies a selected state rate to federally taxable gain. California and Pennsylvania scenarios apply their selected rates to the entire gain, assuming the same basis. California uses a top marginal 13.3% illustration and Pennsylvania uses its 3.07% personal rate.

Can multiple taxpayers or non-grantor trusts increase the cap?

Each independently qualifying taxpayer can have a separate per-issuer limit. This scenario assumes equal gain, basis and previously counted eligible gain per taxpayer. Trust classification, ownership, transfer timing and anti-abuse rules require review before relying on a separate limit.
