How the estimate works

  • Eligibility. Use the same HSA-eligible coverage tier for all 12 months. Medicare enrollment, disqualifying other coverage such as a general-purpose health FSA, or being claimable as a dependent can prevent contributions. For a midyear change or retroactive Medicare enrollment, check the monthly limit and last-month rule in IRS Publication 969. Run separate self-only estimates if both spouses have self-only coverage.
  • Contribution room. Employer deposits use up part of the limit. The family estimate applies each spouse’s employer deposit to their own catch-up first, then allocates shared base room to the spouse as needed for their employer deposit. It assigns the remaining base room to you. Agree on the split before funding separate HSAs. Count any employee deposits already made against the displayed annual employee room. Archer MSA contributions and qualified IRA-to-HSA funding distributions can also reduce room.
  • Tax savings. The estimate assumes you fill the displayed employee room. It applies the entered marginal income tax rates to those deposits and estimates payroll tax savings only on your own Section 125 contributions. Any remaining spouse catch-up is modeled as a direct deposit. If the contribution crosses a tax bracket or payroll tax threshold, use an effective marginal rate for those dollars. California and New Jersey contribution savings are set to zero.
  • Triple-tax treatment and growth. Eligible deposits get a federal deduction or exclusion, earnings compound without federal tax inside the HSA, and qualified medical withdrawals are federally tax-free. The projection grows one fully funded 2027 deposit, including employer money, at the entered annual rate for the chosen period. It excludes future deposits, withdrawals, fees, inflation, and state tax on growth. Nonmedical retirement withdrawals remain subject to ordinary federal income tax.

HSA questions

Do employer deposits count toward the 2027 HSA limit?

Yes. Employer and employee deposits share the same annual limit. For example, a $1,000 employer deposit leaves $3,500 of the $4,500 self-only limit for an eligible employee under age 55.

Can two spouses age 55 or older contribute $11,000 in 2027?

Yes, if both spouses are HSA-eligible all year and at least one has family coverage. They share the $9,000 family limit. Each can add $1,000 to their own HSA by qualifying at age 55 or older at year-end.

How much extra can Section 125 payroll save compared with a direct deposit?

On $4,500 subject to the standard 7.65% employee FICA rate, Section 125 salary reduction saves an extra $344.25. Eligible direct deposits can be deducted on the federal return. The paycheck taxes already paid on those dollars stay paid.

Does an HSA lower California or New Jersey income tax?

California and New Jersey generally do not give HSA contributions the federal income tax break and can tax account earnings. Select California or New Jersey to set state contribution savings to zero. Federal HSA rules still apply.

Can I use my HSA tax-free in retirement?

Qualified medical withdrawals are federally tax-free at any age. From age 65, nonmedical withdrawals are federally taxable as ordinary income, with the 20% additional tax waived. Keep receipts for eligible unreimbursed expenses incurred after you opened the HSA.
