Frequently asked questions

Can I take the standard deduction and still deduct student loan interest?

Yes. Eligible student loan interest is an adjustment to income on Schedule 1. You can claim up to $2,500 of qualified interest per return before the income phase-out, even if you take the standard deduction.

Do I need Form 1098-E to claim interest below $600?

No. The $600 threshold generally tells a lender when to issue Form 1098-E. Use your servicer’s records to verify any qualified interest you paid. Include interest on qualified loans you were legally obligated to repay and exclude principal and amounts covered by tax-free employer education assistance.

How does the 2026 MAGI phase-out work?

For single, head of household, and qualifying surviving spouse filers, the deduction starts shrinking above $85,000 and reaches zero at $100,000. For joint filers, it shrinks above $175,000 and reaches zero at $205,000. Halfway through either range, half of the capped interest is deductible.

Can I claim the deduction if I file separately or someone claims me as a dependent?

Married filing separately disqualifies you. You also cannot claim the deduction if someone claims you as a dependent, or claims your spouse when you file jointly. Lowering MAGI cannot remove these restrictions.

How do traditional 401(k) or HSA contributions affect the deduction?

Eligible additional pre-tax traditional 401(k) contributions or qualifying HSA contributions can lower MAGI and restore part or all of the deduction. Enter only amounts that reduce MAGI and have yet to be counted in your estimate. Check your contribution limits and eligibility. Roth 401(k) contributions do not reduce MAGI in this scenario.
