Key takeaways

The first £2.5 million of qualifying agricultural and business property can receive 100% relief. A surviving spouse or civil partner can claim up to £5 million with a full unused transfer.

Qualifying value above the cap and qualifying AIM shares receive 50% relief. At the standard 40% inheritance tax rate, that is 20% before estate-wide allowances. AIM shares are outside the cap.

Check the cash due before selling assets

An estate can have substantial value tied up in farmland, a trading business or shares and still need cash for its first tax payment. Allow for both the first instalment and any tax outside the plan. A sale can bring forward the remaining tax on the asset, and late instalments can incur interest.

How the estimate works

The calculator applies the shared 100% allowance to agricultural and private business property first. It adds half of any excess and half of the designated shares to other assets, then deducts the available NRB and tapered RNRB before applying 40%. The historical comparison assumes the entered relief-eligible assets previously qualified for unlimited 100% relief and holds the other inputs constant.

The payment estimate allocates tax to qualifying assets in proportion to their share of the chargeable estate after asset reliefs. It assumes those assets are retained and the executor elects to pay by instalments. The first payment combines the first instalment with the amount outside this plan. Other assets may have separate instalment options. HMRC apportionment example · GOV.UK instalment guidance

Frequently asked questions

How does the £2.5 million allowance work?

Agricultural and private business property that would otherwise qualify for 100% relief share one £2.5 million allowance. Qualifying value above it receives 50% relief. Eligible lifetime gifts can use part of that allowance.

When can the allowance reach £5 million?

A surviving spouse or civil partner can claim unused allowance from the first estate. A full £2.5 million transfer raises the surviving estate's allowance to £5 million. A partial transfer produces a lower cap.

Do AIM shares use the £2.5 million allowance?

Qualifying AIM shares and similar designated market shares receive 50% Business Relief outside the allowance. Qualifying private-company shares that are eligible for the 100% rate can use the allowance. Check that each holding qualifies before entering it.

How do the nil-rate bands affect the estimate?

The available basic nil-rate band and the tapered residence nil-rate band reduce the chargeable estate. The residence band usually requires a qualifying home to pass to direct descendants. The taper is £1 per £2 above £2 million of estate value before asset reliefs.

What part of the bill can be paid interest-free over 10 years?

The interest-free option applies to tax attributable to qualifying property when the instalments are paid on time. Elect on IHT400. The first instalment is due at the end of the sixth month after death. A sale may accelerate payment of the tax still due on that asset.
