Key takeaways

183 countable UK days make you UK tax resident. With fewer days, the automatic overseas and UK tests can decide the result before the sufficient ties test applies.

For example, 78 UK days and two ties leave an arriver with 42 more countable days under the ties test, provided the other inputs stay the same. A leaver with the same two ties has 12 days left under the ties test.

Frequently asked questions

Can I be UK tax resident with fewer than 183 days?

Yes. The UK home or full-time UK work test can apply after the automatic overseas tests are checked. If no automatic test decides the result, residence depends on the sufficient ties table.

Am I an arriver or a leaver for the sufficient ties test?

For 2026/27, use the leaver column if you were UK resident in any of 2023/24, 2024/25 or 2025/26. Use the arriver column if you were non-resident in all three. Only leavers can have a country tie.

How do I count UK days and UK workdays?

UK days normally follow where you are at midnight. Qualifying transit, exceptional circumstances and the deeming rule can change the statutory total. A UK workday requires more than three hours of UK work, and can occur on a day trip.

Does the remaining-days figure guarantee non-residence?

The figure assumes your other answers stay the same and each additional UK midnight adds one countable day to both day totals. Recalculate if a visit creates a new tie, increases UK workdays or changes a home or work test.

How do exceptional circumstances and split-year treatment affect the result?

Qualifying exceptional circumstances can exclude up to 60 days for certain tests. Split-year treatment can apply in one of eight statutory cases when arriving or leaving and can affect which income is taxed in each part of the year. Check both separately under HMRC guidance.
