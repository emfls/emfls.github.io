What the estimate covers

The model uses ordinary taxable trust income and assumes eligible full-year Australian resident adults, a valid trust resolution and ordinary company income tax without offsets. It excludes family Medicare reductions and exemptions, Medicare levy surcharge, tax offsets, HELP repayments, capital gains and franked distributions from the trust. It compares current-year tax only. Check the trust deed, beneficiary eligibility and tax treatment with a registered tax agent before making a distribution.

Frequently asked questions

Why does the model stop personal allocations at A$45,000 when using a bucket company?

The next personal bracket costs 30% income tax plus 2% Medicare levy. A company paying 30% or 25% therefore costs less on the next dollar under the model assumptions.

When does a bucket company qualify for 25%?

Its aggregated turnover must be below A$50 million and no more than 80% of its assessable income can be base rate entity passive income. The source of the income behind a trust distribution can affect that test.

ATO ruling on base rate entity passive income

What did the High Court decide in Bendel?

On 10 June 2026, the High Court held that the unpaid present entitlements in Bendel [2026] HCA 18 were not loans under section 109D. A company merely leaving its entitlement unpaid does not itself create a loan. Shareholder or associate benefits may still trigger Subdivision EA, and Section 100A can separately apply.

High Court judgment ATO decision impact statement

When can Section 100A apply to a family trust distribution?

Section 100A can apply when a beneficiary's entitlement arises from an agreement that benefits someone else and has a tax reduction purpose, subject to exceptions such as ordinary family or commercial dealings. An agreed gift back to parents can raise that risk. The model illustrates trustee tax at 47% on the affected income.

How are estimated franking credits used later?

Company income tax paid generally adds to the franking account. Eligible shareholders include the grossed-up franked dividend in their income and may use the credits as a tax offset. The franking account balance and the applicable imputation rate govern how much the company can attach.

For example, a 30% company that pays A$30,000 of income tax on A$100,000 could later pay a fully franked A$70,000 dividend with A$30,000 attached, if the 30% imputation rate and enough franking credits still apply.
