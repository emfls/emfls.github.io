Canada Employer Health Tax Calculator 2026

Compare Ontario EHT, Québec HSF (FSS), British Columbia EHT and Alberta. See the annual tax and what planned hires add to the bill.

Key takeaways

  • Ontario: Eligible private employers can deduct $1 million. At $5,000,001 of Ontario payroll, the exemption disappears and tax jumps from $78,000.00 to $97,500.02.
  • Québec: The standard HSF schedule taxes subject wages from the first dollar. Rates start at 1.65% for general employers or 1.25% for eligible primary and manufacturing employers.
  • British Columbia: Regular employers pay 5.85% on payroll above $1 million within the notch band, then 1.95% on all BC payroll above the band.
  • Alberta: $0 provincial employer health payroll tax.

Budget for the Ontario cliff and BC notch

Ontario’s $5 million cliff adds $19,500 from the lost exemption. The first whole dollar over the threshold raises the tax bill by $19,500.02.

BC’s 5.85% notch costs $585 on $10,000 of added payroll entirely within the regular band. The marginal rate drops to 1.95% above $1.5 million.

How to read the estimate

Annual tax uses projected payroll. An effective rate is the annual tax divided by projected payroll. Tax added by hires is the difference between the projected and current annual tax bills.

The model assumes a full year, one independent employer with the full applicable exemption available, and one qualifying location for BC charities and non-profits. For Québec it uses the same payroll as both the rate-setting total and subject wages, and keeps the formula at full precision until the liability is rounded to cents. Associated groups, part-year establishments, public-sector Québec rates, credits, and temporary Québec HSF relief require separate calculations.

Frequently asked questions

What happens when Ontario payroll exceeds $5 million by one dollar?

An otherwise eligible private employer pays $78,000 at $5 million and $97,500.02 at $5,000,001. Losing the $1 million exemption adds $19,500, and the extra payroll dollar adds two cents after rounding.

Can a registered charity keep the Ontario exemption above $5 million?

Yes. Eligible registered charities can claim the Ontario exemption even when annual Ontario remuneration exceeds $5 million. Special rules apply to qualifying campuses and the amount available may depend on the employer’s circumstances.

Does Québec HSF have a general payroll exemption?

Under the standard schedule, HSF applies to subject wages from the first dollar. At total payroll of $1 million or less, the general rate is 1.65% and the eligible primary and manufacturing rate is 1.25%. A temporary exemption applies to certain employers with qualifying activities in 2026 and 2027.

Why is the BC notch rate 5.85% when the full rate is 1.95%?

Between $1 million and $1.5 million, BC applies 5.85% only to remuneration above $1 million. At $1.5 million this is $29,250, the same amount as 1.95% of $1.5 million. Above the band, 1.95% applies to all BC remuneration.

What does the Alberta comparison include?

Alberta has no provincial employer health payroll tax, so its benchmark is $0. Other employer costs, including pension contributions, employment insurance and workers’ compensation, are outside this comparison.
