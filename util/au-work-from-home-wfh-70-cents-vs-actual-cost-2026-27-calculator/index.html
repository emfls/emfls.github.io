Frequently asked questions

Is 70¢ an official ATO rate for 2026–27?

The ATO lists 70¢ per hour for 2024–25 and 2025–26. This calculator uses 70¢ as a 2026–27 planning assumption. At that rate, 1,152 hours gives A$806.40 before eligible equipment, repairs and dedicated-office cleaning. Check the ATO rate for your income year before lodging.

What records do I need for the fixed rate?

Keep a contemporaneous record of your actual working-from-home hours covering the full income year. A four-week sample cannot substitute for that hours log. Keep at least one record for each covered expense you incurred, such as one internet bill and one electricity bill if you paid both, plus evidence for separate deductions.

Which costs can I add to either method?

Both methods allow eligible work-related equipment decline in value, repairs and dedicated-office cleaning. The fixed hourly amount already covers energy, internet, phone and work supplies, so those costs cannot be added again. The actual cost method uses supported work-use shares of those expenses.

When can I deduct equipment costing A$300 or less immediately?

The cost test applies to the whole item before apportionment. Claim only the work share in the eligible first year. The asset must be used mainly to produce non-business assessable income, and same-year sets or substantially identical items costing more than A$300 together are excluded. Other assets may qualify for decline in value.

How does a deduction affect my refund?

The calculator applies progressive resident income tax before and after the deduction and optionally includes the standard 2% Medicare levy. The difference is the estimated tax and levy saved. Your final refund or tax bill depends on withholding, offsets, levy reductions and the rest of your return.
