How the estimates work

  • Current service. At the model's $192,500 salary scaling point, annual funding is $33,810 at age 35, $37,100 at 40, $40,800 at 45, $44,900 at 50, $49,300 at 55, $54,100 at 60 and $56,300 at 65. The model interpolates between age points, holds the age-65 amount through 71 and scales down in proportion to salary below $192,500.
  • Past service. The estimate multiplies eligible years since the first corporate T4 year or 1991, whichever is later, by the current-age funding estimate scaled to average historical T4 salary. It deducts the qualifying transfer to estimate the new corporate lump sum. Actual service and transfer eligibility depend on plan terms and the CRA past-service certification process.
  • Triennial funding scenario. Both asset paths start with gross past-service funding and add the same current-service deposits at year-end. In years 3, 6 and 9, the model funds any shortfall versus the 7.5% path and starts the next interval from the resulting asset balance. A plan actuary determines actual liabilities and eligible contributions.
  • Tax and fee treatment. The IPP scenario assumes all modeled employer contributions and employer-paid setup and annual fees qualify for a full corporate deduction with enough taxable income to use it. Corporate tax saved is the assumed eligible amount multiplied by the entered rate. The RRSP baseline uses a personally funded RRSP and leaves personal income tax outside the comparison. See CRA employer pension contribution rules.
  • Projection. Year 1 uses 2025 salary for RRSP room. Later years use the entered 2026 salary, hold the 2026 limits constant and omit carryforward and pension adjustments. Regular IPP and personal RRSP deposits stop after the age-71 year. The scenario continues fees and modeled valuation checkpoints and omits pension payments, inflation and withdrawal taxes.

IPP and RRSP questions

How much T4 salary reaches the 2026 RRSP limit?

About $187,833.33 of 2025 earned income generates $33,810 at 18%, before pension adjustments and carryforward. If entering whole dollars, $187,834 is the first salary at or above the mathematical threshold. Check your CRA notice of assessment for your personal deduction limit.

Is the age-based IPP amount an official contribution limit?

The schedule is an illustrative funding estimate. Actual employer contributions depend on plan benefits, compensation history, assets, liabilities and the actuarial valuation. IPP participation can also reduce future RRSP room through a pension adjustment.

How does a qualifying RRSP transfer affect past-service funding?

A qualifying transfer uses existing registered savings to fund part of the past-service benefit. The calculator subtracts it from gross funding, down to zero, to estimate the new employer contribution. Confirm recognized service, transfer requirements and past-service pension adjustment certification with the plan actuary.

Does earning less than 7.5% automatically allow a deductible top-up?

A return below 7.5% creates a modeled asset gap. Deductible contributions depend on applicable funding rules, actual assets and liabilities, and actuarial approval requirements. The 7.5% rate is a designated-plan maximum-funding valuation assumption under regulation 8515(7)(b).

What does fee break-even measure?

It is the first modeled year when cumulative extra current-service funding versus the RRSP baseline covers setup and annual fees after their assumed tax deduction. This measure excludes the past-service lump sum and deficit top-ups.
