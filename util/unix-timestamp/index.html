10-digit seconds or 13-digit milliseconds?

A 10-digit Unix timestamp, such as 1786233600, usually represents seconds. A 13-digit Unix timestamp, such as 1786233600000, represents milliseconds and is common in JavaScript and many APIs.

How the Calculation Works & Mathematical Formula

The Date to Milliseconds Converter allows users to convert local date and time into Unix timestamps (in seconds or milliseconds) and vice versa. This tool accepts a local date/time and converts it based on the Unix epoch.

Definitions of Input Variables:

  • Local Date and Time: The specific calendar date and time that you wish to convert to Unix timestamp.
  • Unix Timestamp: A numeric representation of time that counts seconds or milliseconds since the Unix epoch (January 1, 1970).
  • Input Unit: Specify whether the input timestamp is in seconds or milliseconds.

Mathematical Formula:

The conversions are based on the following formulas:

  • From Local Date and Time to Unix Timestamp:
    Unix Timestamp = (Local Date and Time in milliseconds) / 1000 (if in seconds)
  • From Unix Timestamp to Local Date and Time:
    Local Date and Time = (Unix Timestamp * 1000)

Example:

Suppose you want to convert December 31, 2023, 23:59:59 (local time) to milliseconds:

  1. Convert this local date and time to milliseconds (let's say it converts to 1704067199000 milliseconds).
  2. To convert to a Unix timestamp in seconds: 1704067199000 / 1000 = 1704067199 seconds.

Conversely, for a Unix timestamp 1704067199:

  1. Convert it to milliseconds: 1704067199 * 1000 = 1704067199000 milliseconds.
  2. Then, if you want to see the local date and time, simply reverse the process.

Important Tips:

  • Always check your browser's time zone settings, as local date and time can differ based on time zones.
  • A common mistake is confusing 10-digit and 13-digit timestamps; remember: 10 digits represent seconds, while 13 digits represent milliseconds.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

What is the Unix epoch?

The Unix epoch begins at 00:00:00 UTC on January 1, 1970. It is the standard for Unix timestamps, representing the number of seconds that have elapsed since this point in time.

Why do local and UTC values differ?

Local time depends on your geographical time zone, which may include adjustments for Daylight Saving Time. UTC is a global time standard and does not change.

How do I know if my timestamp is in seconds or milliseconds?

A Unix timestamp with 10 digits typically represents seconds, while 13 digits indicate milliseconds. Ensure you select the correct input unit in the converter.
