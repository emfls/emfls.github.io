How the estimate works

Federal planning estimate only. The model uses the beneficiary’s own taxable compensation and the currently eligible balance. Confirm account or beneficiary changes, state taxes or recapture, and transfer procedures with your plan and IRA custodian.

For 2026 the IRA limit is $7,500, or $8,600 for a beneficiary age 50 or older. Start with the smaller of that limit and IRA-eligible compensation, then subtract direct IRA contributions and earlier 529-to-Roth rollovers for 2026. The proposed transfer also cannot exceed the eligible 529 balance or the beneficiary’s remaining $35,000 lifetime room.

The account must have completed 15 years by the transfer date. For a 2011 opening, compare the full date with the 2026 transfer date. Contributions made within the five-year lookback period and their earnings are excluded. The transfer must go directly to the same beneficiary’s Roth IRA.

The projection holds compensation, direct contributions and IRA limits constant across years and uses only the currently eligible balance. IRS Notice 2026-66 confirms the same IRA limits for 2027. Later limits, investment returns, amounts aging into eligibility and future transfers from other accounts can change the schedule. The projection runs for up to ten years and requires an eligible 2026 transfer date.

Frequently asked questions

Does the Roth IRA MAGI phase-out apply?

The Roth IRA MAGI phase-out does not limit an otherwise qualifying 529-to-Roth transfer. The beneficiary still needs eligible compensation and room under the applicable annual and lifetime limits.

Can an account opened in 2011 qualify in 2026?

Yes, once it has been open for the full 15-year period as of the 2026 transfer date. The contributions and earnings transferred must also satisfy the five-year rule.

Can I roll over the full $35,000 in 2026?

No. At the standard annual limit with sufficient compensation and eligible balance, no other IRA contributions, and no previous 529 rollovers, the estimate is $7,500 each year from 2026 to 2029 and $5,000 in 2030.
