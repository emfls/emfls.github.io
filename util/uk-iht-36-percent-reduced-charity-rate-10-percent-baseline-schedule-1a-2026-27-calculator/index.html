UK IHT 36% Reduced Charity Rate Calculator 2026/27

Find the minimum qualifying charity legacy under IHTA 1984 Schedule 1A and compare how much goes to heirs, charity and HMRC.

Key takeaways

Leave at least 10% of the Schedule 1A baseline to qualifying charity to cut IHT from 40% to 36% on the qualifying component’s remaining taxable amount.

Exclude the residence nil-rate band (RNRB) from the 10% test. Apply it when working out tax after the legacy. For example, with a £1 million estate and £325,000 of available ordinary nil-rate band, the target is £67,500 before and after applying any RNRB.

A gift just short of 10% can cost heirs more

With no residence band, a gift above 4% but below 10% can miss the 36% rate and leave less for heirs than giving the full qualifying amount. At a £675,000 baseline, giving £67,500 instead of £33,750 can add £4,050 to heirs’ inheritance while giving charity £33,750 more.

Check the comparison using your own residence allowance. If there is little or no IHT left to save, a larger gift can reduce what heirs receive. With no residence band, the qualifying gift costs heirs about 2.4% of the baseline compared with giving nothing.

The comparison assumes the balance passes to family. It excludes lifetime-gift tax, other exemptions and reliefs, estate components outside the general component, tax grossing-up and interactions between beneficiaries. Frequently asked questions

What is the Schedule 1A baseline amount?

For the single general component modelled here, subtract the available ordinary nil-rate band from the net estate before the qualifying charity gift, with a floor of zero. Schedule 1A paragraph 5 starts with the chargeable value after charity exemption, deducts the apportioned nil-rate band and adds the donated amount back. In estates with multiple components the test generally applies to each component separately, subject to any valid merger election.

Does the residence nil-rate band lower the 10% charity target?

No. The residence nil-rate band can reduce taxable inheritance but does not enter the charity baseline. Its 2026/27 limit is £175,000 before any eligible transfer. Taper it by £1 per £2 above £2 million of net estate value before exemptions and reliefs. The value of the qualifying share passing to direct descendants can further limit the allowance.

When can topping up to 10% leave the family with more?

With no residence nil-rate band and a taxable component, a gift just above 4% and below 10% can leave less to heirs than a qualifying 10% gift. At 4% the unrounded family outcomes tie. For example, on a £675,000 baseline, increasing a 5% gift to 10% gives charity £33,750 more and heirs £4,050 more. A residence nil-rate band can raise the break-even percentage or remove the gain.

Can a Deed of Variation increase the charity legacy after death?

Affected beneficiaries may be able to redirect part of their inheritance to a qualifying charity using a written variation within two years of death. Beneficiaries who receive less must agree. For tax treatment, the variation must meet the applicable requirements, including the relevant tax statement and notification to the charity. The calculator shows the potential change in inheritance, charity and tax.

How are pension pots treated in the April 2027 preview?

For deaths from 6 April 2027, most unused pension funds and death benefits come into scope. In-scope notional pension property forms part of the general component and can raise the baseline and the residence-band taper. The preview adds only the amount you enter. Pension exemptions, who receives the benefits, and any beneficiary income tax need separate consideration.
