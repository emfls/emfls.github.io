Calculate your 38.33% corporate dividend refund and see how much cash the corporation and shareholder would keep after tax.

Key takeaways

  • Every $2.61 of qualifying taxable dividends can recover about $1 of available refundable dividend tax on hand (RDTOH).
  • Eligible dividends draw only from ERDTOH. Non-eligible dividends draw from NERDTOH first, then from ERDTOH still available.
  • A larger refund can still leave you with less combined cash today if personal dividend tax exceeds the corporate refund.

Frequently asked questions

How much dividend do I need for a $1 refund?

At the exact 38⅓% rate, $1 of available RDTOH takes about $2.61 in qualifying taxable dividends to recover. The calculator rounds each payout target up to the next cent when needed.

Can eligible dividends recover NERDTOH?

Eligible dividends recover ERDTOH only. Non-eligible dividends first recover NERDTOH and may then recover ERDTOH that remains after the eligible dividend refund.

What adds to each RDTOH balance?

The refundable portion of Part I tax on aggregate investment income and Part IV tax on non-eligible Canadian portfolio dividends add to NERDTOH in this model. Part IV tax on eligible Canadian portfolio dividends adds to ERDTOH.

Can I pay the suggested eligible dividend if my GRIP is too low?

The payout target measures the dividend needed to recover ERDTOH. Check your general rate income pool and make a valid designation before paying. An excessive eligible dividend designation can trigger Part III.1 tax.

Does the refund equal cash saved by paying a dividend?

The refund goes to the corporation. Personal dividend tax reduces what the shareholder keeps. The cash comparison adds the shareholder’s after-tax dividend to the corporation’s modeled remaining cash and compares that sum with paying no dividend this year.
