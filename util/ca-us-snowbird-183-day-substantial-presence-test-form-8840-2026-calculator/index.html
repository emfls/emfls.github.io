See how many U.S. days you have left before you meet the Substantial Presence Test, then check your visitor and provincial health coverage thresholds.

Key takeaways

  • 183 weighted days can be reached before 183 actual days. With 121 days in each of 2024 and 2025, the last day below the test in 2026 is day 122.
  • At 183 current-year U.S. days, Form 8840 is barred. Below that cap, the exception also requires a foreign tax home, closer ties abroad, and timely filing.
  • 121 U.S. days per year stays below the test when the same count repeats across all three years. Check your authorized stay and provincial health rules separately.

One extra day can change your filing options

Missing the Form 8840 deadline can cost you the closer-connection exception. At 183 countable days in 2026, the exception is unavailable even if your closest ties remain in Canada. U.S. residency can bring worldwide income reporting. Depending on your circumstances, review FBAR and Form 8938, the U.S. treatment of TFSAs, and PFIC reporting for Canadian mutual funds. Extended absences can also affect provincial coverage.

Frequently asked questions

What counts as a U.S. day for the Substantial Presence Test?

Presence at any time during a calendar day generally counts as one day. Arrival and departure on different dates normally count separately. IRS exclusions include qualifying regular commutes from Canada, certain transit under 24 hours, and qualifying exempt-individual or medical days.

Why can 122 days be safe one year but too many every year?

With 121 U.S. days in both prior years, 122 days in 2026 produces a weighted total of 182.50. With 122 in all three years, the total is exactly 183. At 121 in all three years, the total is 181.50.

When can I use Form 8840 and when is it due?

If you meet the SPT and have fewer than 183 countable U.S. days in 2026, you can claim the exception if you meet the foreign tax-home and closer-connection conditions and have taken no affirmative green-card steps. File by your applicable Form 1040-NR deadline, generally June 15, 2027 for calendar-year filers without wages subject to U.S. withholding.

What happens after 183 U.S. days in 2026?

The count meets the SPT and bars Form 8840. If you qualify for Canadian residency under the treaty tie-breaker and claim it, the IRS directs you to file Form 1040-NR with Form 8833. Otherwise review U.S. resident filing. For immigration, follow your CBP admission terms separately.

Can Québec and Ontario count the same trip differently?

Yes. Québec checks counted absences over the calendar year for established residents, leaving out travel days at each end and absences of 21 consecutive days or less. Ontario checks physical presence in any 12-month period. Use your plan's own dates and include trips outside your province in Canada.
