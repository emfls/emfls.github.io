How the estimate works

If the original owner died on or after their required beginning date, annual minimums continue, and the entire remaining balance is due by December 31 of the 10th year after death. For a pre-RBD death, this beneficiary has no annual minimum in years 1–9 and the same Year 10 deadline.

The first post-RBD divisor is the greater of the beneficiary’s Table I factor for the year after death and the owner’s Table I factor for the death year minus one. Subtract one each following year. The model distributes the available balance when the divisor reaches one or lower. At age 45 the first beneficiary factor is 41.0. A $500,000 prior-year balance and divisor 41.0 produce an estimated $12,195.12 first distribution.

The minimum plan withdraws the projected minimum each year. The smoothed plan finds the smallest level target that leaves a final withdrawal no higher than that target, subject to annual RMD floors and available balances. Years 1–9 model withdrawal before growth and Year 10 models growth before liquidation. The comparison holds 2026 federal rates, the standard deduction and other ordinary income constant. Different drawdowns can change both investment growth and the dollars available for distribution.

Planning estimate for fully taxable traditional IRAs. It excludes the owner’s death-year RMD, relief or shortfalls before 2026, Roth and mixed-basis accounts, successor beneficiaries, trusts, spouses and eligible designated beneficiaries. Eligibility can change if the beneficiary is disabled, chronically ill, the owner’s minor child, or not more than 10 years younger than the owner. The tax estimate excludes AMT, credits, state tax and interactions with other federal benefits or surcharges.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need annual distributions before Year 10?

For the beneficiary modelled here, yes if the original owner died on or after RBD. A death before RBD requires only that the account be emptied by the Year 10 deadline.

Do I look up a new life expectancy factor each year?

Use the initial applicable single-life divisor, then subtract one each year. For a post-RBD death the initial divisor also considers the owner’s remaining single life expectancy.

What if I missed a required distribution?

The federal excise tax is generally 25% of the shortfall. The rate can fall to 10% if you withdraw the shortfall and file the required return within the statutory correction window. It generally closes at the earliest of a deficiency notice, assessment or the end of the second following tax year. The IRS may waive it for reasonable error if you take corrective steps. Check Form 5329 for filing.
