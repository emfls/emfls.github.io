Key takeaways

The charge takes back 1% of Child Benefit for every full £200 over £60,000 of the higher individual adjusted net income (ANI). At £80,000 the rate reaches 100%. For two children, £70,000 ANI gives a £1,168 charge on £2,337.40 of Child Benefit over 52 weeks.

For a relief-at-source pension, the provider adds basic-rate relief to the pension and you claim any higher-rate relief due. The gross amount goes into the pension. With Gift Aid, the charity receives the gross amount and you claim any additional relief due. Net cost is the gross target less total Income Tax relief and saved HICBC.

The cash estimate assumes 40% total relief across the entire gross target. Actual relief depends on taxable income, tax bands (including Scotland), eligibility and pension allowances. It excludes salary-sacrifice NI savings, personal allowance effects and any tax on later pension withdrawals. The offset percentage measures the share of the gross target covered by relief and saved tax.

Frequently asked questions

Whose adjusted net income determines the charge?

Use the higher individual adjusted net income of you and your partner. Include taxable income such as bonuses, benefits from work, interest and dividends before Personal Allowances, and deduct qualifying reliefs when working out ANI.

Why is there no charge at £60,199.99?

The charge rises by one percentage point for each full £200 above £60,000. The first 1% step starts at £60,200. The rate reaches 100% at £80,000 and the charge is rounded down to whole pounds.

How do pension contributions and Gift Aid lower ANI?

Under relief at source, £80 paid into a pension becomes £100 gross. A qualifying £80 Gift Aid donation also reduces ANI by £100. Use the gross amount once. If workplace pension deductions have already reduced your taxable pay, avoid subtracting them again.

Why might my pension contribution leave a household charge?

If you reduce your ANI below your partner’s and your partner is still above a chargeable step, the remaining charge is based on your partner’s income. The £60,000 target is shown separately for each person.

Can I keep State Pension credits if I opt out of payments?

Yes. A parent or guardian registered for Child Benefit for a child under 12 automatically gets Class 3 National Insurance credits, including while opting out of payments. A charge can still be due for payments received before the opt-out.
