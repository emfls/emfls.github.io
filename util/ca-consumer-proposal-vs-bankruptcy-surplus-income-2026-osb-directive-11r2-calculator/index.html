The home equity forfeiture risk in personal bankruptcy

In personal bankruptcy, any home equity exceeding your provincial exemption (e.g. $10,000 in Ontario or $40,000 in Alberta) is legally vested in the Licensed Insolvency Trustee and must be sold to pay creditors. A Consumer Proposal avoids bankruptcy, allowing you to settle debt without losing your family home.

Frequently asked questions

What are the 2026 OSB Directive 11R2 thresholds?

The Superintendent of Bankruptcy establishes annual net income thresholds. For 2026, a family of four can earn up to $5,047 net monthly before surplus income payments apply.

How much surplus income do I have to pay?

You pay exactly 50% of the net income that exceeds the threshold. If your surplus income is $200/month or more, your bankruptcy duration extends to 21 months.

Can creditors reject a Consumer Proposal?

Creditors vote on the proposal based on dollar amount. If creditors representing 50%+ of proven debt approve, it becomes legally binding on all unsecured creditors.

Do I lose my car in a Consumer Proposal?

No. You retain your personal vehicle, and can continue making normal financing or lease payments without disruption.

What is a Stay of Proceedings?

Filing a Consumer Proposal or Bankruptcy initiates an immediate legal Stay of Proceedings, halting all wage garnishments, collection calls, and lawsuit actions.
