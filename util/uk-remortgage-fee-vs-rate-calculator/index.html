What this estimate includes

  
The calculator assumes fixed rates and equal monthly principal-and-interest repayments. It rounds years to the nearest month, uses a minimum comparison horizon of one month, and stops at mortgage payoff. It excludes early repayment charges, legal fees, and other remortgaging costs.

FAQ

  

    Should you add a UK mortgage product fee to the loan or pay it upfront?
    
Adding the fee increases the balance you pay interest on. Paying upfront leaves the starting loan balance unchanged. Both options include the entire fee in net borrowing cost.

  

  

    What if the product fee takes longer than the fixed deal period to recover?
    
The break-even month is the first month when the higher-fee deal's cumulative interest savings cover its extra fee. If that month falls beyond the comparison horizon, the result shows whether the fee could still be recovered by payoff at the entered rates.

  

  

    How is net borrowing cost calculated?
    
It is the product fee plus interest paid through the comparison month. That equals repayments made plus any upfront fee and the remaining balance, minus your original outstanding mortgage balance.

How the Calculation Works & Mathematical Formula

  
The UK Remortgage Fee vs Rate Calculator helps you evaluate two different mortgage offers. The calculator takes into account the outstanding mortgage balance, amortization term, interest rates, and product fees of each deal.

  
Input Variables:

  
    
    
  • Outstanding mortgage balance (£): Total amount still owed on your mortgage.
    • 
    
  • Amortization term (years): Total duration over which your mortgage will be repaid, measured in years.
    • 
    
  • Deal A interest rate (%): Annual interest rate for the first mortgage option.
    • 
    
  • Deal A product fee (£): Upfront fee associated with the first mortgage option.
    • 
    
  • Deal B interest rate (%): Annual interest rate for the second mortgage option.
    • 
    
  • Deal B product fee (£): Upfront fee associated with the second mortgage option.
    • 
    
  • Fee payment mode: Choose whether to pay the product fee upfront or add it to the loan balance.
    • 
    
  • Fixed deal comparison horizon (years): Duration over which deals are compared.
    • 
  

  
Mathematical Formula:

  
Net Borrowing Cost = Product Fee + Total Interest Paid through Comparison Month

  
To decide, compare:

  
    
    
  • Monthly payments for both deals.
    • 
    
  • Cumulative costs over the chosen period.
    • 
  

  
Example Calculation:

  
Assuming:

  
    
    
  • Outstanding mortgage balance: £150,000
    • 
    
  • Amortization term: 20 years
    • 
    
  • Deal A: 2% interest rate, £0 fee
    • 
    
  • Deal B: 1.8% interest rate, £1,000 fee
    • 
    
  • Comparison horizon: 5 years
    • 
  

  
1. Calculate monthly payments for each deal using the formula 
  M = P[r(1 + r)^n] / [(1 + r)^n – 1] (where M is the monthly payment, P is the principal, r is the monthly interest rate, and n is the number of payments).

  
2. Determine cumulative costs for 5 years including any fees.

  
3. The calculator will indicate the break-even point, showing you when Deal B's lower rate compensates for the fee, helping you make an informed decision.

  
Decision-Making Tips: Always consider the duration of your stay in the property against the terms of each deal. Also, be cautious about missing additional costs not included in the comparison.



  

    
Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

    

      

        Should I add a UK mortgage product fee to the loan or pay it upfront?
        
Adding the fee to the loan increases the balance you'll pay interest on, while paying upfront keeps your loan balance unchanged. Both choices should be weighed to assess overall borrowing costs.

      

      

        What if the product fee takes longer than the fixed deal period to recover?
        
The break-even point occurs when the cumulative interest savings of a higher-fee deal surpass the total fees paid. If this exceeds your selected comparison horizon, the calculator will show if it can still be recovered by your mortgage payoff.

      

      

        How is net borrowing cost calculated?
        
Net borrowing cost is determined by adding the product fee to total interest paid through the comparison month. This includes repayments made, any upfront fee, and the remaining balance, minus your original mortgage balance.
