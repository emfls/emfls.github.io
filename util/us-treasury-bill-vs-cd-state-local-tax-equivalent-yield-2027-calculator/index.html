How the comparison works

Interest on direct U.S. Treasury obligations is exempt from state and local income taxes under 31 U.S.C. § 3124. Enter the marginal rates that apply to the next dollar of interest in a taxable account. The NY + NYC preset combines a 6.85% state bracket and a 3.876% city bracket. California’s 13.3% example combines the 12.3% bracket and a 1% surtax where it applies.

Annual bank-equivalent yield
T-bill yield × (1 − federal rate − NIIT) ÷ (1 − federal rate − state and local rate − NIIT)

For a shorter term, gross interest equals cash × [(1 + APY)months ÷ 12 − 1]. The calculator applies tax to each offer’s gross interest, then solves for the bank APY that produces the same after-tax dollars. The exact term threshold and the annual formula coincide at 12 months.

The model uses constant yields and one marginal tax rate per jurisdiction for the holding period. It omits rate changes, fees, early withdrawal penalties, tax deduction effects and bracket crossings. Use the bill’s purchase cost and redemption value to derive its APY when starting from an auction quote. Use the year the interest is taxable when choosing rates.

Treasury and bank interest FAQ

Which taxes apply to T-bill, CD and savings interest?

Interest on directly held U.S. Treasury bills, notes and bonds is federally taxable and fully exempt from state and local income tax. Bank CD and high-yield savings interest, as well as ordinary distributions from commercial paper in a money market fund, generally face federal and applicable state and local income taxes.

What bank APY matches a 4.20% T-bill in California?

At a 24% federal marginal rate, 9.3% California marginal rate and no NIIT, a 4.20% T-bill APY requires about 4.7856% bank APY for a 12-month tie. A bank quote of 4.79% beats it before fees and penalties. Use the calculator for shorter holding periods.

Does the 3.8% NIIT apply to Treasury interest too?

Yes, taxable Treasury interest, bank interest and ordinary money market fund dividends can count as net investment income. NIIT applies to the smaller of net investment income or modified AGI above $200,000 for single and head-of-household filers, $250,000 for joint filers and qualifying surviving spouses, or $125,000 for married filing separately. Turn on the toggle when all of the modeled extra interest is subject to NIIT.

Are SGOV, USFR or BIL dividends state tax-free?

Use the fund issuer’s year-end percentage of eligible U.S. government obligation income for your state and tax year. California, New York and Connecticut generally require at least 50% of a fund’s assets to be in qualifying obligations at each quarter-end before distributions attributable to eligible Treasury interest can qualify. California also counts certain California state and local obligations toward its test. When a fund fails the applicable test, its ordinary Treasury-derived dividends generally do not qualify for that subtraction. Check the issuer’s tax supplement and your state instructions.

Can I enter the Treasury auction discount rate as APY?

Convert the bill price and redemption value to an effective annual yield first. Divide the redemption value by your purchase cost, raise the result to 365 divided by the days you will hold the bill, and subtract 1. Multiply by 100 for a percentage. For a day-matched comparison, enter holding months as 12 times days divided by 365.

The calculator models directly held bills. For fund distributions, see the California Schedule CA instructions, New York Form IT-225 instructions, Connecticut Form CT-1040 instructions and an example issuer tax library.
