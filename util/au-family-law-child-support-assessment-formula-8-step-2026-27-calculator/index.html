How Australia’s eight-step formula works

Australia’s basic child support formula estimates an annual transfer between two parents who share one child support case. It measures each parent’s share of the income available for child support and compares that share with the contribution the law credits them for providing care. The transfer is the difference expressed in dollars, subject to rules about who can receive a payment. This calculator models Formula 1 with one care pattern for all children, no relevant dependent child deduction and no other child support cases. Child Support (Assessment) Act 1989

1. Deduct the self-support amount from each ATI

Start with each parent’s annual adjusted taxable income. ATI can include taxable income, reportable fringe benefits, reportable superannuation contributions, total net investment losses, certain foreign income and some tax-free pensions or benefits. The applicable income year is generally the last relevant financial year before the child support period began. If you have an assessment notice, use the income figure on it when checking how its calculation was made. An income estimate accepted by Services Australia can affect the inputs used and may later be reconciled.

Deduct $31,046 separately from each parent’s ATI for a period starting in 2026. This is the statutory one-third share of annualised male total average weekly earnings (MTAWE), expressed in whole dollars. A parent’s child support income is floored at zero. For example, an ATI of $27,000 gives zero child support income for that parent. That parent’s unused self-support amount cannot be transferred to the other parent. Each ATI entered here is rounded to a whole dollar before the deduction. Services Australia income guidance

2. Add both child support incomes

Add the amounts left after the two separate deductions. This sum is the combined child support income. If one parent’s amount is zero, the other parent’s amount is the whole combined figure. If both amounts are zero, the cost table yields zero and the basic transfer is zero. The percentage shares are shown as unavailable for that scenario because dividing by zero gives no meaningful income share. Any minimum or fixed assessment is a separate question.

3. Divide to find each parent’s income share

Divide each parent’s child support income by the combined figure, convert the result to a percentage and round to two decimal places, with a third decimal digit of five or more rounding up. In a case with $60,000 and $20,000 in child support income, the shares are 75.00% and 25.00%. These percentages express the parents’ relative financial capacity under the formula. At high incomes, use the full combined figure for this division. The cost cap introduced at step 7 applies to the assessed costs of the children.

4. Convert care nights to percentages

Enter the number of nights each child spends with Parent A over a standard 365-night year. The calculator gives Parent B the remaining nights automatically. Divide each count by 365 and multiply by 100. Round a result below 50% down to the whole percentage and a result above 50% up. For example, 182 and 183 nights become 49% and 51%. Both qualify for the same 50% cost credit. Holidays, rotating schedules and the distribution of nights over the entire care period can matter when counting care.

5. Look up the cost credit

The cost percentage is the share of the children’s formula costs credited to a parent as met directly through care. At 120 nights a parent has 32% care and a 24% cost credit. At 146 nights the parent has 40% care and a 35% cost credit. The bands combine flat credits with sliding scales. A one-night change at a boundary can alter the credit, while a larger move within a flat band can leave it the same. Services Australia care and cost table

6. Subtract cost credit from income share

Subtract each parent’s cost percentage from their income percentage. A positive difference identifies a potential payer. A negative difference shows that the parent meets more of the formula costs in care than their share of the income requires. To receive a Formula 1 transfer, the other parent must have at least 35% care under section 40C. For example, if a parent has 90% of the income share and 76% of the cost credit, their difference is +14 points. When the other parent has only 20% care, the transfer for that child is zero under this rule. DSS guide to child support percentages

7. Apply the registered Costs of the Children table

Use the row for the number and ages of the children and the column for combined child support income. For each of the first five bands, add the printed base to the marginal percentage of income above the preceding band’s upper threshold. The five upper thresholds are $46,569, $93,137, $139,706, $186,274 and $232,843. Above the fifth threshold use the printed cap for that row. A cap on assessed costs still allows the payment to change if income shares or care credits change.

For a case with four or more children, choose the row using the ages of the three oldest. Divide the resulting whole-dollar costs by the actual number of children in the assessment and round the cost per child to the nearest dollar. For instance, if three teenagers and one younger child share the case, select the 13-or-older row in the 3+ category and enter four as the actual count. A mixed row applies when the relevant children cross the age threshold. The table represents standardised costs after Family Tax Benefit. Household spending and Family Tax Benefit entitlements are outside the estimate. DSS methodology for the Costs of the Children table

8. Calculate the annual transfer and budgeting averages

Multiply the eligible payer’s positive child support percentage by the rounded per-child cost. Round the annual payment for each child to the nearest whole dollar, then sum those rates. This can differ by a few dollars from multiplying the total costs first and rounding once. The monthly and fortnightly cards divide the annual estimate by 12 and 26 to give budgeting averages. An assessment notice specifies a daily rate, and actual payment timing can reflect calendar dates, part periods, collection arrangements and other changes. DSS Formula 1 worked example and rounding

When the minimum or fixed rate may change the amount

The minimum annual rate of $551 can apply when the formula assessment is lower and the parent has less than regular care of every child in the case. In this 365-night model, that means fewer than 52 nights for that parent. If the relevant application and income conditions are met, Services Australia can reduce a minimum assessment to nil. There are additional allocation rules when a parent has multiple cases. A zero result in the calculator only describes the basic formula outcome.

The $1,825 fixed annual rate can apply to a low-ATI parent who did not receive qualifying income support during the relevant year and has less than shared care of the child. The total is capped at three fixed rates, or $5,475 in 2026. The Registrar may determine that the fixed rate should be disregarded if the application establishes genuinely low income and that applying the rate would be unfair. This tool’s inputs omit the relevant income-support history and eligibility facts, so both rates are presented as notes. Services Australia guidance on fixed and minimum assessments

Worked examples

One child under 13 with 90 care nights

Parent A has ATI of $91,046 and Parent B has ATI of $51,046. Subtract $31,046 apiece to get $60,000 and $20,000 in child support income. The combined income is $80,000, making the income shares 75.00% and 25.00%. A provides 90 nights, which rounds down to 24% care and gives a 24% cost credit. B provides the other 275 nights, which rounds up to 76% care and earns a 76% cost credit. A’s child support percentage is 51.00% and B meets the minimum care threshold to receive the transfer.

The under-13 one-child cost is $7,917 + 15% × ($80,000 − $46,569), which rounds to $12,932. Multiply by 51% and round to get $6,595 a year from A to B, equivalent to $549.58 a month or $253.65 a fortnight. If A instead provides 182 nights, both cost credits become 50%. The child support percentage for A drops to 25% and the annual transfer becomes $3,233. You can compare these amounts by saving the starting scenario and changing the nights field.

Two children of mixed ages with shared care

Parent A’s ATI is $101,046 and Parent B’s is $61,046, giving child support incomes of $70,000 and $30,000. One child is 10 and the other is 14. A has 146 nights for each child and B has 219. Their care percentages are 40% and 60%, worth 35% and 65% in cost credits. A has a 70% income share and a positive 35% child support percentage. On $100,000 of combined child support income, the mixed-age cost is $24,216 + 22.5% × ($100,000 − $93,137), rounded to $25,760. Each child’s cost is $12,880. At 35%, A pays $4,508 per child, or $9,016 a year to B.

Income above the cost cap

With $240,000 of combined child support income and two children aged 13 or older, the registered cost is $53,554. If A has 80% of the income share and each parent has a 50% cost credit, A’s 30% child support percentage applies to $26,777 per child. The rounded payment is $8,033 per child, or $16,066 a year. Two families with the same capped costs can have different transfers because their income shares or care arrangements differ.

Using this estimate with your assessment notice

Check the period start date, the ATI used for each parent and the care determination for each child. Comparing those figures usually pinpoints where a notice diverges from a scenario entered here. Relevant dependent children, multiple cases, non-parent carers, accepted agreements, court orders and changes of assessment can require different calculations. If the notice covers a change in care or an age milestone, compare the corresponding portions separately. Use the notice and the collection schedule for the amounts actually due. Frequently asked questions

Which rates apply to a case that runs into 2027?

Check the start date of the child support period on your assessment notice. This calculator uses the registered table for periods starting in calendar 2026, including a 2026-start period that continues into 2027. For a new period starting in 2027, use the figures published for that year. The 1 July financial-year change alone does not select a new table.

Can one extra night change the estimate?

Yes. Moving from 51 to 52 nights raises care from 13% to 14% and gives the parent a 24% cost credit. Moving from 127 to 128 nights raises care from 34% to 35%, which also reaches the care threshold for receiving a Formula 1 transfer. Within a flat cost band, extra nights can leave the cash estimate unchanged.

Does shared care eliminate child support?

Each parent receives a 50% cost credit when both parents are in the 48% to 52% care band. A parent with 75% of the combined child support income still has a 25% child support percentage. With equal income shares and those same care credits, the basic annual transfer is zero.

Why can a low-income parent still have an assessed liability?

The 2026 minimum annual rate is $551 for a qualifying case. The fixed annual rate is $1,825 per qualifying child, capped at three children. Payment history, relevant income, care and statutory exceptions affect how these rules apply. The calculator displays the basic estimate and explains the rates separately because its five main inputs cannot establish eligibility.

What if one child turns 13 or has a different care schedule?

A 13th birthday can change the relevant cost row during a child support period. Estimate the configurations before and after the birthday separately. Different care schedules require child-by-child care calculations, with any opposing annual liabilities offset under the applicable rules. This calculator applies a single care schedule across the case.
