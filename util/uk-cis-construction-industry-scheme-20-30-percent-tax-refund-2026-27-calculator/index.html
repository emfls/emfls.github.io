Key takeaways

CIS is a tax prepayment. Contractors withhold 20% for verified registered subcontractors, 30% at the higher rate or 0% with gross payment status. The rate applies after removing VAT charged and eligible direct costs.

If CIS deductions exceed your estimated Income Tax and Class 4 National Insurance, you may be due a refund. With £48,000 paid excluding VAT, £8,000 of eligible direct costs and £6,000 of other expenses, the 20% rate gives an estimated £2,428.20 refund outside Scotland, assuming no other income or credits.

How to reconcile the estimate with your records

For CIS withholding, subtract eligible direct costs from payments excluding VAT, then apply your CIS rate to the remaining amount down to a minimum of £0. Contractors calculate withholding on individual payments. Use their deduction statements for your return, especially if your status changed, your invoices span tax years or individual payment rounding matters.

For Self Assessment, subtract allowable expenses, including eligible direct costs, from turnover to estimate trading profit. The model uses trading profit and the other income you enter to estimate adjusted net income. It assumes standard reliefs and Class 4 rules and excludes loss relief, pension and Gift Aid adjustments, student loans, payments on account and special National Insurance rules.

For earlier years, check which return or amendment route is open and compare each year's statement totals with its own tax calculation. For example, the usual overpayment relief deadline for 2022/23 is 5 April 2027 if the relief applies.

CIS refund FAQ

When does a contractor deduct 20%, 30% or 0% under CIS?

A contractor normally deducts 20% once they verify a registered subcontractor, or 30% if the subcontractor is unregistered or cannot be verified. HMRC-approved gross payment status means 0% is withheld, and the subcontractor pays any tax due through Self Assessment.

What can I exclude from the CIS deduction base?

Start with the payment excluding VAT charged to the contractor. Subtract eligible materials you paid for directly, hired plant and fuel used to operate it. Use actual costs and keep receipts. Travel fuel and a notional hire charge for equipment you own do not qualify for this exclusion.

How do I claim a CIS refund through Self Assessment?

Report your gross trading income and allowable expenses, then enter the CIS deductions recorded on your contractor statements. HMRC credits those deductions against what you owe. If your credits exceed the amount due, HMRC can repay the surplus or use it towards other tax debts.

What changes if I pay Scottish Income Tax or have a second job?

Choose Scotland if you are a Scottish taxpayer. Scottish bands apply to trading profits and employment income. Class 4 uses UK-wide thresholds and is calculated on self-employed profits. The result is before PAYE already paid, and simultaneous employment can affect the final National Insurance calculation.

Can I reclaim CIS deductions from four previous tax years?

Use the return or amendment route where available. If you qualify for overpayment relief, the usual claim deadline is four years after the end of the tax year concerned. Check each year's rates, tax calculation and deduction statements. The calculator's multi-year figure assumes the same estimated refund each year.
