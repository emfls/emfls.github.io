Frequently asked questions

How is Class 4 National Insurance calculated in 2026/27?

You pay 6% on annual trading profit above £12,570 through £50,270, up to £2,262 in that band. Profit above £50,270 is charged at 2%. For example, £60,000 of profit gives £2,456.60 of Class 4.

Has compulsory Class 2 National Insurance been abolished?

The compulsory Class 2 charge ended from April 2024 for self-employed people covered by the standard rules. For 2026/27, eligible self-employed people with profits of £7,105 or more are treated as having paid Class 2 at no cost, which counts towards their National Insurance record. Those below £7,105 may be able to pay voluntarily.

How much could voluntary Class 2 save compared with Class 3?

At 2026/27 rates, 52 weeks of Class 2 cost £189.80 and 52 weeks of Class 3 cost £956.80, a difference of £767. If a missing year would add one thirty-fifth of the full new State Pension, it would add about £358.50 a year at the 2026/27 rate. That would recover £189.80 in under seven months of receiving the extra pension, before tax. Check your forecast first to see if the year would increase your pension and how many weeks you need.

What happens to my Personal Allowance above £100,000?

If your adjusted net income exceeds £100,000, your standard £12,570 allowance falls by £1 for every £2 above that level and reaches zero at £125,140. This calculator treats sole trader profit as your only income and assumes no adjustments such as Gift Aid or qualifying pension contributions.

Why can my first January Self Assessment payment be 150% of my bill?

With no tax deducted at source or prior payments, you pay the full 2026/27 balance plus half the qualifying bill as an advance for 2027/28 by 31 January 2028. The second advance is due by 31 July 2028. HMRC generally requires advances when at least £1,000 of Income Tax and Class 4 remains after source deductions and no more than 80% was paid at source. Voluntary Class 2 is part of the current bill and excluded from both advances.
