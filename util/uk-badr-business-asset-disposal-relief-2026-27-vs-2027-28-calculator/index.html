UK Business Asset Disposal Relief (BADR): The 2026/27 18% Rate and April 2027 £500k Halving

UK company founders, agency owners, and trading partners planning a business exit or Members' Voluntary Liquidation (MVL) face a strict statutory deadline on 5 April 2027. Following the staged Capital Gains Tax reforms—which lifted the Business Asset Disposal Relief (BADR) rate from 10% (2024/25) to 14% (2025/26) and now 18% for 2026/27—the government confirmed a second structural squeeze taking effect on 6 April 2027: halving the individual BADR lifetime limit from £1,000,000 down to £500,000.

Because the standard Higher Rate Capital Gains Tax sits at 24% (after the modest £3,000 Annual Exempt Amount), every pound of qualifying business gain pushed outside the BADR ceiling suffers an immediate 6 percentage point surcharge (24% vs 18%). For a founder realizing a £1,003,000 gain (£1,000,000 taxable after the £3,000 allowance) with zero prior BADR claims, completing the sale on or before 5 April 2027 results in a £180,000 CGT bill (18% across the full £1M). Completing the exact same transaction one day later on 6 April 2027 leaves only £500,000 eligible for 18% BADR (£90,000) while forcing the remaining £500,000 into 24% CGT (£120,000)—jumping total tax to £210,000, an avoidable £30,000 tax hit (or £60,000 for a married couple who both qualify as 5%+ shareholding directors!).

Prior BADR Claims Trap: How Past Exits Wipe Out Your 2027/28 Allowance

The £500,000 cap effective 6 April 2027 is a cumulative lifetime limit, not a fresh allowance. If you previously claimed £500,000 or more of Entrepreneurs' Relief or BADR on an earlier company sale or liquidation years ago, your remaining BADR room on 6 April 2027 drops immediately to £0—whereas in 2026/27 you still retain up to £500,000 of usable 18% headroom! Founders with prior claims between £100,000 and £999,000 should verify their historic Self Assessment filings immediately before deciding whether to accelerate a trade sale, management buyout (MBO), or solvent MVL liquidation ahead of April 2027.

Mandatory 2-Year Qualification Rules for BADR (TCGA 1992 s.169I)

HMRC rigorously audits BADR claims—especially those rushed before a statutory threshold change. To qualify on a share disposal, ALL four statutory conditions must be met continuously throughout the 24 months immediately preceding the date of disposal:

  1. Trading Company Test: The company (or holding company of a trading group) must carry on trading activities with no "substantial" (generally >20%) non-trading investment activity—excess surplus cash invested in property or equities can taint the entire company!
  2. Officer or Employee Test: You must be a statutory director, company secretary, or genuine employee of the company (working hours do not need to be full-time, though bona fide officer status is essential).
  3. Personal Company 5% Test: You must hold at least 5% of ordinary share capital, 5% of voting rights, AND be beneficially entitled to at least 5% of distributable profits and assets on winding up (or 5% of disposal proceeds).
  4. Strict Exchange-of-Contracts Tax Point: For CGT purposes, the disposal date is normally the date an unconditional Share Purchase Agreement (SPA) is exchanged—not the later cash completion date. However, be alert to HMRC's anti-forestalling legislation targeting artificial pre-deadline contracts or share-for-share reorganisations entered solely to lock in the £1,000,000 cap.

Spouse Share Planning & Members' Voluntary Liquidation (MVL) Timing

Because each individual spouse or civil partner receives their own separate BADR lifetime allowance—worth £1,000,000 each (£2,000,000 combined) in 2026/27 and £500,000 each (£1,000,000 combined) from 6 April 2027—gifting at least 5% ordinary voting shares to a spouse (no-gain/no-loss transfer under TCGA 1992 s.58) and appointing them as a director or company secretary at least two full years prior to exit doubles the family's 18% shelter. Likewise, contractors or retiring consultants winding up cash-rich personal service companies via a Members' Voluntary Liquidation (MVL) must ensure the licensed insolvency practitioner distributes capital well ahead of 5 April 2027 (and watch HMRC's Targeted Anti-Avoidance Rule / TAAR, which re-classifies MVL distributions as income dividends if you carry on a similar trade within 2 years of winding up).

Worked UK Exit Scenarios: Single Founder, Married Couple & Earn-Out Structures

To see why M&A tax advisers are urging UK founders to plan 2026/27 completions well before winter, compare three concrete exit scenarios:

  • Single Founder Selling for £1,003,000 Gain (Zero Prior BADR): After deducting the £3,000 Annual Exempt Amount, £1,000,000 is taxable. Exchanging an unconditional contract on or before 5 April 2027 applies 18% BADR across the full £1,000,000 (£180,000 CGT, saving £60,000 versus standard 24% CGT). Exchanging on or after 6 April 2027 caps 18% BADR at £500,000 (£90,000) and pushes the upper £500,000 into 24% CGT (£120,000)—totalling £210,000 and forfeiting exactly £30,000 overnight.
  • Married Co-Founders Selling for £2,006,000 Combined Gain: Where both spouses independently meet the 5% voting/economic and 2-year officer rules, completing in 2026/27 covers £2,000,000 at 18% (£360,000 total CGT). Missing 5 April 2027 cuts their combined BADR cap to £1,000,000, raising total CGT to £420,000—an avoidable £60,000 family tax loss.
  • Deferred Loan Notes & Earn-Out Election Trap (TCGA 1992 s.169Q): When a buyer pays part of the purchase price in qualifying corporate loan notes or share-for-share paper, the gain normally rolls over until redemption—meaning a post-April 2027 redemption could lose the £1,000,000 BADR ceiling! Sellers who want to lock in the 2026/27 £1,000,000 limit can ask their tax adviser about filing an affirmative section 169Q election to disapply rollover treatment and pay 18% CGT on the full £1,000,000 in 2026/27 (provided cash consideration covers the January 31 tax bill).

Finally, never overlook associated disposals (such as selling a personally owned warehouse or office used rent-free by your trading company alongside your share sale). Charging full commercial market rent to your own company can restrict BADR on the personally held building, so reviewing rent histories and partnership/shareholder agreements at least 12 months before marketing your business for sale is vital.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much tax does the April 2027 BADR halving cost a business owner?

Halving the lifetime BADR limit from £1,000,000 to £500,000 on 6 April 2027 pushes up to £500,000 of qualifying gains from the 18% BADR rate into the 24% Higher Rate CGT band—costing up to £30,000 in extra Capital Gains Tax per individual shareholder (or £60,000 for a qualifying couple).

What is the UK Business Asset Disposal Relief tax rate in 2026/27?

From 6 April 2026 (tax year 2026/27), the BADR tax rate is 18% (up from 14% in 2025/26 and 10% in 2024/25). Standard Higher Rate CGT above the BADR cap is 24%.

Does BADR still use up my Basic Rate Income Tax band?

Yes. Even though qualifying BADR gains are taxed at 18%, HMRC ordering rules require BADR gains to consume any remaining Basic Rate band (£37,700 width above the £12,570 Personal Allowance) before non-BADR gains, meaning excess gains above your BADR cap typically fall straight into the 24% Higher Rate band.

How long must I own shares to qualify for Business Asset Disposal Relief?

You must satisfy all qualifying conditions—at least 5% ordinary share capital, 5% voting rights, 5% economic/proceeds entitlement, and director/employee status in a genuine trading company—continuously for at least 2 full years before disposal.
