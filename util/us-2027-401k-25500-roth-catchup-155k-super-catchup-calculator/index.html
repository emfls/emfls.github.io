How the payroll estimate works

The model fills the regular employee limit first and then adds the age-based catch-up, up to your gross salary. It assumes one traditional or safe harbor 401(k), equal gross pay from the first check of the year and a plan that allows the displayed deferrals. Payroll taxes, benefits, plan limits and earlier deferrals can reduce what you can contribute.

The take-home estimate applies your marginal income tax rate to pre-tax deferrals and keeps payroll taxes unchanged. The model treats regular deferrals and optional catch-ups as pre-tax. When Roth is required, it applies Roth treatment to the modeled catch-up. Actual withholding can differ. Treasury and IRS final Roth catch-up regulations

The match estimate applies one match rate to regular deferrals up to your matchable share of pay, using the $375,000 eligible-compensation cap. Regular deferrals and employer match share the annual additions cap. The model assumes an annual match or true-up and no other employer or employee after-tax contributions. Frequently asked questions

Does exactly $155,000 in 2026 Box 3 wages trigger Roth catch-up in 2027?

No. The rule generally applies when your 2026 Social Security wages from the employer sponsoring your 2027 plan exceed $155,000. Exactly $155,000 is at the threshold. The rule applies to catch-up dollars, and you can still elect pre-tax regular deferrals if your plan allows them.

How much can I contribute if I turn 60 or 64 in 2027?

If you turn 60 in 2027, you can contribute up to $37,250, including the $11,750 super catch-up. If you turn 64 in 2027, the $8,500 standard catch-up returns, for a $34,000 total. Use your age on December 31, 2027 and confirm your plan offers catch-ups.

What if I changed employers before contributing in 2027?

Enter $0 if you had no applicable 2026 Social Security wages with the employer sponsoring your 2027 plan. If you joined that employer during 2026, use the applicable wages you earned there in 2026. Ask your plan administrator whether wages from a related employer, common paymaster or predecessor apply.

How much can a required Roth catch-up lower take-home pay?

At a 30% combined marginal income tax rate, an $11,750 Roth catch-up costs about $135.58 more per biweekly paycheck than the same pre-tax catch-up over 26 checks. The estimate keeps Social Security and Medicare taxes unchanged.

Does the employer match count toward my $25,500 limit?

No. The $25,500 regular elective deferral limit is for employee contributions. Employer matches generally share the separate 2027 annual additions limit, the lesser of $75,000 or 100% of eligible compensation. Eligible age-based catch-ups are excluded from that annual additions limit.
