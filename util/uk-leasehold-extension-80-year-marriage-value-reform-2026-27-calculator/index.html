UK Statutory Leasehold Extension Guide (1993 Act & 2024 Reform)

Under the Leasehold Reform, Housing and Urban Development Act 1993, qualifying leaseholders of residential flats in England and Wales have the statutory right to compel their freeholder to grant a 90-year lease extension on top of their remaining term at a peppercorn (zero) ground rent.

1. The Three Components of the 1993 Act Premium

  • Capitalised Ground Rent: Compensates the freeholder for the loss of future annual ground rent payments. Calculated by discounting ground rents at the market capitalisation yield (typically 6.0% to 7.0%).
  • Freehold Reversion: Compensates the freeholder for deferring the right to take vacant possession of the property for an additional 90 years. Fixed at 5.0% for flats and 4.75% for houses by the Lands Tribunal landmark case Cadogan v Sportelli (2007).
  • Marriage Value (The 80-Year Cliff): The combined value of the flat and freehold after the extension is greater than the sum of their parts before extension. Under Section 82 of the 1993 Act, if the unexpired term is below 80 years, 50% of this marriage value must be paid to the landlord. If 80 years or more, marriage value is legally ZERO.

2. Key Reforms Under the Leasehold and Freehold Reform Act 2024

The UK Parliament passed the LFRA in May 2024, introducing transformative protections:

  • Abolition of the 2-Year Ownership Rule (Effective 31 Jan 2025): Flat buyers no longer have to wait 2 years before serving a Section 42 Notice. You can extend on Day 1 of ownership!
  • 990-Year Statutory Term: Future statutory extensions will add 990 years rather than 90 years.
  • Abolition of Marriage Value: Schedulable secondary legislation will remove marriage value calculations, significantly lowering extension costs for short-lease properties.
