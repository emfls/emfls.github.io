Frequently Asked Questions: Canadian RRSP First 60 Days & Limits

What is the 2026 RRSP dollar limit and first 60 days contribution deadline?

For the 2026 tax year, the CRA statutory RRSP annual dollar limit is $33,810 (or 18% of earned income from 2025, whichever is less) plus any unused carry-forward room from your Notice of Assessment. The deadline for contributions deductible in the 2026 tax year is Monday, March 1, 2027.

Can I contribute in the first 60 days of 2027 and defer claiming the tax deduction?

Yes. Under Section 146(5) of the Canadian Income Tax Act, you must report all contributions made in the first 60 days of 2027 on Schedule 7 of your 2026 tax return, but you can choose to defer claiming the deduction to 2027 or any future tax year if you expect to be in a higher marginal tax bracket.

What happens if I overcontribute to my RRSP?

CRA allows a lifetime cumulative overcontribution buffer of $2,000 for individuals aged 18 and older. If your contributions exceed your total deduction limit by more than $2,000, you are subject to a Part X.1 tax penalty of 1% per month on the excess amount until it is withdrawn or new contribution room becomes available.

What is an RRSP Gross-Up loan strategy?

The Gross-Up strategy involves borrowing a short-term 90-day RRSP loan to contribute your intended cash plus the expected tax refund simultaneously (Gross = Cash / (1 - Marginal Tax Rate)). When your tax refund arrives from the CRA in April or May, you use 100% of the refund to pay off the loan in full, permanently boosting your invested retirement capital.

How does a Pension Adjustment (PA) affect my RRSP contribution room?

If you participate in an employer-sponsored Registered Pension Plan (RPP) or Deferred Profit Sharing Plan (DPSP), your employer reports a Pension Adjustment (PA) on your T4 slip. The PA reflects the value of the pension benefits you earned and reduces your new RRSP room for the following tax year dollar-for-dollar.
