Understanding Australia's ATO Division 7A Rules and the 2026–27 8.77% Benchmark Rate

Division 7A of Part III of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 is the Australian Taxation Office's primary anti-avoidance integrity regime preventing private company owners, directors, and family trusts from extracting company cash tax-free disguised as "director loans," unpaid drawings, or unpaid present entitlements (UPEs). Whenever a private company lends money to a shareholder or their associate (including a spouse, child, or related family trust) and fails to either repay the balance in full or place it under a strict written section 109N complying loan agreement before the company's tax return lodgment day, the ATO treats the entire unpaid loan as an unfranked deemed dividend.

For the 2026–27 income year, the ATO statutory Division 7A benchmark interest rate rose to 8.77% per annum (up +0.40 percentage points from 8.37% in 2025–26, tracking the RBA's May indicator lending rate for housing loans). Because Division 7A interest is generally not tax-deductible for personal drawings while remaining fully assessable income inside the lending company (taxed at 25% or 30%), an 8.77% statutory rate creates a painful double-tax cash-flow squeeze for Australian SME owners carrying large debit loan accounts.

7-Year Unsecured vs. 25-Year Real Property Secured Complying Loan Rules (s.109N)

To protect a shareholder loan from triggering an immediate deemed dividend, the written loan agreement executed on or before the company's lodgment day must satisfy three mandatory section 109N criteria:

  • Minimum Interest Rate: The loan must charge interest at least equal to the ATO benchmark rate for each income year following the year the loan is made (8.77% for 2026–27). Note that interest is recalculated every single year using that year's newly published ATO benchmark rate—not locked at inception!
  • Maximum Term — Unsecured (7 Years): Standard director drawings and trust-to-company UPE loans default to a strict 7-year maximum term. On a A$200,000 loan at 8.77%, the Year-1 Minimum Yearly Repayment (MYR) under the statutory s.109E(6) annuity formula is A$39,432 (A$17,540 interest + A$21,892 principal).
  • Maximum Term — Real Property Mortgage Secured (25 Years): A private company loan can extend up to 25 years—cutting Year-1 MYR on A$200,000 down to A$19,983—only if 100% of the loan is secured by a registered first mortgage over real property and the property's market value (less any other liabilities secured over it) is at least 110% of the loan amount when the loan is made.

Why an Unfranked Deemed Dividend Is a 47% Tax Disaster

Missing the written s.109N agreement—or falling even A$5,000 short of your annual 30 June Minimum Yearly Repayment—triggers a brutal tax penalty. Unlike a genuine fully franked dividend declared by the company (which carries 25% or 30% franking credits, capping top-bracket top-up tax at 17% to 22%), a Division 7A deemed dividend is strictly unfranked. The shareholder pays their full marginal tax rate (up to 47% including the 2% Medicare Levy) on the shortfall—on top of the 25%–30% company tax already paid when the company earned the profits—driving combined effective double taxation past 60%! On a A$200,000 non-complying loan, that represents an immediate A$94,000 personal tax bill with zero franking credits.

Practical 2026–27 Strategies to Manage Division 7A Without Going Broke

  1. Set-Off Using Fully Franked Dividends Before 30 June: Rather than finding fresh personal cash to pay the A$39,432 MYR each June, directors routinely declare a fully franked dividend before 30 June and legally set off the net dividend obligation against the required MYR via signed director minutes and accounting journal entries—clearing the statutory repayment while preserving valuable franking credits.
  2. Refinance Out of 8.77% Using Bank Home Equity: Because a 2026–27 Division 7A rate of 8.77% typically exceeds commercial bank owner-occupied or investment mortgage rates (often 6.0%–6.6%) AND creates taxable interest income inside the company, refinancing the director loan using external bank equity to repay the company in full before lodgment day can save thousands in net after-tax cost every year.
  3. Beware of Section 109R "Refinancing / Round-Robin" Traps: You cannot satisfy a 30 June MYR by borrowing fresh money from the same company (or an associated company) on 29 June and paying it back on 30 June. Section 109R explicitly disregards circular repayments made with the intention of re-borrowing similar amounts, causing the MYR to fail retroactively.
  4. Commissioner's Discretion Under s.109RB: If an honest mistake or inadvertent clerical error causes a Division 7A breach, the company and shareholder can apply to the ATO under section 109RB to either disregard the deemed dividend or allow it to be franked—provided prompt corrective catch-up repayments and full voluntary disclosure are made.

Worked 2026–27 Cash-Flow Comparison: 7-Year Unsecured vs. 25-Year Secured Loan

Consider an Australian Pty Ltd company director who drew A$200,000 during the prior income year and signs a written s.109N complying loan agreement before the company's tax return lodgment day. At the 2026–27 ATO benchmark interest rate of 8.77%, an unsecured 7-year loan requires a Year-1 Minimum Yearly Repayment of exactly A$39,432 (comprising A$17,540 in assessable interest income to the company plus A$21,892 in principal reduction)—A$530 higher per year than under 2025–26's 8.37% rate. Over seven years, cumulative interest totals A$76,023 on top of the A$200,000 principal (assuming a steady 8.77% rate).

By contrast, if the director registers a first mortgage over real property worth at least A$220,000 (satisfying the strict 110% security threshold) under a 25-year complying loan, the Year-1 MYR drops almost in half to A$19,983—freeing up nearly A$19,450 per year in immediate personal cash flow while still allowing voluntary lump-sum early repayments whenever cash becomes available. Remember also that if a family trust distributes income to a corporate beneficiary ("bucket company") and leaves that Unpaid Present Entitlement (UPE) unpaid past the trust's lodgment day, ATO Determination TD 2022/11 treats the UPE as financial accommodation subject to these exact Division 7A complying loan rules—making early June cash-flow modeling essential for every Australian SME group.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the ATO Division 7A benchmark interest rate for 2026–27?

The ATO statutory Division 7A benchmark interest rate for the 2026–27 income year is 8.77% per annum, up from 8.37% in 2025–26.

When must a Division 7A Minimum Yearly Repayment (MYR) be paid?

Each annual Minimum Yearly Repayment must be paid on or before 30 June of each income year following the income year in which the loan was made. Note that the initial written s.109N loan agreement must be executed on or before the company's tax return lodgment day.

Can I use a fully franked dividend to satisfy my Division 7A MYR without transferring cash?

Yes. A private company can declare a bona fide fully franked dividend on or before 30 June and execute a documented legal set-off agreement applying the shareholder's dividend entitlement directly against the required MYR (provided it does not breach section 109R anti-avoidance rules).

How do I qualify for a 25-year Division 7A loan instead of 7 years?

Under section 109N(3)(a), 100% of the loan must be secured by a registered mortgage over real property, and the market value of that property (net of prior liabilities) must be at least 110% of the loan amount at the time the loan is made.
