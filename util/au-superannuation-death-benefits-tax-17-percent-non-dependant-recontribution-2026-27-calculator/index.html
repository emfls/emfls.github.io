    How the projection works

    Each withdrawal takes the same proportions of tax-free, taxable taxed and taxable untaxed components found in the source account. The after-tax recontribution goes to a separate clean super interest. Later withdrawals still draw from the original account, so its ratio stays constant in the model while the combined tax-free share rises.

    Two $360,000 cycles from a fully taxed source can reduce the estimated direct tax by $122,400. Two eligible people each completing two such cycles could reduce it by $244,800 in total. Each person needs enough funds, time and their own contribution room.

    Check the cap before withdrawing

    Withdrawing first can leave money outside super if your fund cannot accept the full recontribution. Excess contributions can have tax consequences.     Frequently asked questions

    Who pays tax on a super death benefit?

    A lump sum is tax-free for a tax dependant, such as a spouse or former spouse, a child under 18, or a qualifying financial or interdependency dependant. An independent adult child generally pays tax on the taxable component at up to 17% for a taxed element and 32% for an untaxed element, assuming the standard Medicare levy.

    How does payment through the estate change the estimate?

    For a lump sum through the deceased estate intended for a non-tax dependant, the model applies 15% to the taxed element and 30% to the untaxed element. The estate generally pays no Medicare levy. The modelled difference is 2% of the taxable component. The fund, nomination and estate circumstances determine whether this payment route is available.

    Is the 2026–27 bring-forward cap $360,000 or $390,000?

    The 2026–27 annual cap is $130,000 and a new three-year arrangement can allow $390,000. The $360,000 figure was the three-year maximum for 2025–26 and is the calculator’s default scenario. If you already triggered an arrangement, check your remaining cap and end date in ATO online services.

    How does the proportioning rule affect recontributions?

    A withdrawal from a mixed account takes the same tax-free and taxable proportions as the source account. If the source is 80% taxable, a $360,000 withdrawal removes $288,000 of taxable components. A $360,000 after-tax contribution to a separate clean account adds $360,000 of tax-free contributions. At a 17% rate, the estimated reduction in death-benefit tax is $48,960.

    Can I repeat the strategy every year?

    A new bring-forward arrangement starts when total non-concessional contributions exceed the annual cap and eligibility conditions are met. This calculator models another withdrawal after that arrangement ends. Annual-only withdrawals can repeat a year apart. Before each cycle, check your age, contribution history, prior 30 June total super balance and current ATO thresholds.
