UK Non-Resident SDLT Surcharge & 183-Day Refund Calculator

Estimate your stamp duty at completion and track when you could reclaim the 2% surcharge. For residential purchases in England and Northern Ireland in 2026/27.

Key takeaways

  • The 2% surcharge adds £9,000 to a £450,000 purchase when it applies, including on a first-time buyer’s nil-rate band.
  • When the 5% additional dwelling rates also apply, the combined uplift is 7 percentage points. The top marginal rate is 19%.
  • Individuals may reclaim the 2% surcharge after 183 UK days in an eligible rolling 365-day window. The claim deadline is two years after the effective date.

    Frequently asked questions

    How is SDLT residency determined at completion?

    Count days when each buyer was in the UK at the end of the day, from 364 days before the effective date through that date. The general threshold is 183 days. The effective date usually means completion, but can be earlier if the contract is substantially performed. Qualifying spouses and civil partners can share resident treatment.

    When can I reclaim the 2% surcharge?

    After paying the surcharge, every buyer must be an individual and satisfy the 183-day rule, subject to the rule for qualifying spouses and civil partners. Each can use a different 365-day window within the statutory period. Apply to HMRC within two years of the effective date once the test is met.

    Can the 2% surcharge apply to first-time buyers?

    Yes. If everyone qualifies for first-time buyer relief and the price is £500,000 or less, the surcharge generally makes the rates 2% on the first £300,000 and 7% on the rest. Above £500,000, the standard bands apply to the full price.

    When does the 19% rate apply?

    For an additional dwelling bought by a non-UK resident, 19% applies to the slice above £1.5 million. A non-UK resident company subject to the flat corporate rate pays 19% on the full price of a dwelling costing more than £500,000. Company residency follows a separate test.

    Is the 5% higher-rate refund a separate claim?

    Yes. An eligible individual who paid higher rates when replacing a main home can usually claim that part after disposing of their previous main home within 36 months. For current purchases, HMRC must receive this claim within 12 months of the disposal or 12 months of the return filing date, whichever is later.
