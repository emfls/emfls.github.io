Frequently asked questions

Your RRSP must mature by December 31 of the year you turn 71. You can transfer it into a RRIF, purchase an eligible annuity, or withdraw the funds.

When is the first mandatory RRIF withdrawal?

The opening-year minimum is $0. The first minimum is due by December 31 of the following calendar year. If you open the RRIF in the year you turn 71, the next January 1 age is usually 71, so the standard factor is 5.28%. If you are already 72 on January 1, the factor is 5.40%. An elected spouse age can change the factor.

Can I use my younger spouse’s age to lower my minimum?

You can elect to use your spouse’s or common-law partner’s age before the first payment. The election applies to that RRIF and cannot be changed for the same fund. At age 72, a $400,000 balance requires $21,600 using your age or $16,000 using an elected age of 65.

How is tax withheld on a RRIF lump sum?

Standard withholding on the annual minimum is 0%. For one lump-sum request, the excess above the annual minimum determines the rate for the entire excess. Outside Québec the rates are 10%, 20% and 30%. In Québec, the federal rates are 5%, 10% and 15%, plus 14% provincial withholding on the excess. Periodic Québec payments can use a different method.

How does a RRIF withdrawal affect OAS in 2026?

If you receive OAS, recovery tax generally starts at 15% of your 2026 net world income above $95,323, capped at the OAS you actually received. The 2026 income threshold is used for the July 2027 to June 2028 recovery period. Taxable RRIF cash withdrawals generally count toward that income.

Does RRIF income qualify for the pension income amount?

RRIF income generally qualifies for the federal pension income amount when you are 65 or older on December 31. Up to $2,000 of eligible pension income can count toward the non-refundable credit. RRIF income received because of a spouse’s or common-law partner’s death can also qualify before age 65.
