Frequently Asked Questions: Canadian Tax-Loss Selling

Can my spouse buy the shares while I claim the capital loss?

No. Under ITA s. 54, an "affiliated person" includes your spouse or common-law partner, a corporation you or your spouse control, and a trust where you or your spouse are beneficiaries. If your spouse buys the identical security within the 61-day window, your capital loss is disallowed.

Can I maintain market exposure during the 30-day superficial loss window?

Yes, by purchasing a non-identical replacement asset. For example, if you sell an S&P 500 index ETF (e.g., VFV) at a loss, you can immediately buy a total US market ETF (e.g., VUN) or a NASDAQ-100 ETF. Because the underlying benchmark index is distinct, CRA does not consider them identical property.

How do I receive money from the Form T1A 3-year loss carryback?

When you file your 2026 T1 General Income Tax Return, you include Form T1A (Request for Loss Carryback). You specify how much allowable capital loss to apply against net taxable capital gains in 2025, 2024, or 2023. The CRA recalculates your prior tax returns and issues a refund via direct deposit or cheque.

What is an Allowable Capital Loss (ACL)?

In Canada, capital losses are subject to the capital gains inclusion rate (50%). An Allowable Capital Loss (ACL) is 50% of your total realized capital loss. Only allowable capital losses can be used to offset taxable capital gains.

Do Canadian mutual funds or ETF distributions affect year-end loss harvesting?

Yes. Canadian mutual funds and ETFs frequently distribute annual taxable capital gains in mid-to-late December. If you hold shares on the record date, you receive the distribution regardless of whether you sell before Dec 30. Check the fund issuer's estimated distribution schedule before selling.
