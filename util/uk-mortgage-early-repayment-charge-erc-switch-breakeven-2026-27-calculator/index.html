Never pay an ERC without calculating your remaining fix timeline

If you have fewer than 6 to 9 months remaining on your fixed rate deal, paying a 1% to 2% penalty is rarely profitable. You generally cannot recoup the upfront charge before your existing deal naturally expires without penalty.

UK mortgage early repayment charge FAQ

What is an Early Repayment Charge (ERC) on a UK mortgage?

An Early Repayment Charge (ERC) is a financial penalty charged by UK lenders if you repay, overpay beyond your allowance, or remortgage away from your fixed or discounted mortgage deal before the agreed promotional period ends. It typically ranges from 1% to 5% of the outstanding balance.

How can I use the 10% annual overpayment allowance to reduce my ERC?

Most UK lenders permit up to 10% penalty-free overpayments each calendar year. If you have cash available, making an overpayment up to your 10% allowance immediately before redeeming the mortgage reduces the principal balance on which the ERC percentage is charged, saving hundreds in exit fees.

When does it make financial sense to pay an ERC and switch deals?

Paying an ERC is financially justified if the cumulative monthly interest savings and capital balance improvement over the remaining months of your existing fix exceed the combined sum of the ERC penalty and any new arrangement fees (e.g. £999).

What is the breakeven interest rate for breaking a mortgage?

The breakeven rate is the maximum interest rate on a new mortgage that exactly offsets the total switching cost (ERC plus arrangement fees) through monthly interest savings before your existing fixed deal expires.

Does my current lender waive the ERC if I port my mortgage to a new home?

Most UK fixed mortgages are portable, meaning you can transfer your existing rate and balance to a new property without paying an ERC, provided you meet the lender’s current credit and affordability criteria.
