Key takeaways

Each $1 of group AAII above $50,000 cuts the federal limit by $5. The limit reaches zero at $150,000. If taxable capital also reduces the limit, the larger reduction applies. Ontario and New Brunswick keep separate provincial limits.

How much could the next dollar cost?

With $450,000 of eligible active income and $100,000 of prior-year AAII in British Columbia, $200,000 of active income moves to the general rates. That adds $32,000 to the 2026 tax bill. The next $1 of interest adds $0.80 in active-business tax and about $0.51 of direct cash tax before any dividend refund. If the reduced limits still cover your active income, the extra tax can be zero.

Official 2026 parameters

The federal rates are 9% and 15%. The standard federal limit is $500,000. Federal taxable capital above $10 million reduces that limit by $1 per $80 of excess capital, until the limit reaches zero at $50 million.

Mitigation action plan

  1. Forecast group AAII and gain timing. Compare lawful realization dates using Schedule 7 amounts and the next year's eligible business income. Include portfolio risk and transaction costs.
  2. Evaluate an individual pension plan (IPP). Compare eligible employer contributions, actuarial and administration costs, and liquidity needs with your pension adviser and CPA.
  3. Evaluate corporate-owned exempt life insurance. Start with the insurance need. Compare qualifying policy costs, access to cash, surrender consequences and the potential death-benefit credit to the capital dividend account (CDA).
  4. Review CDA and refundable dividend tax on hand (RDTOH). Confirm the available CDA balance before electing a capital dividend. Model a separate taxable dividend and shareholder tax when estimating a refund of RDTOH. Dividend distributions affect liquidity and future investment income.

Frequently asked questions

What counts as AAII?

Use adjusted aggregate investment income from Part 2 of Schedule 7 for the relevant tax years ending in the preceding calendar year. Combine the amounts for the CCPC and its associated corporations. Interest, portfolio dividends and net taxable gains on passive assets can count. Qualifying active-asset gains are excluded.

How do the two business-limit reductions interact?

The federal limit is reduced by the larger of the passive-income reduction and the taxable-capital reduction. The latter phases out the limit between $10 million and $50 million of applicable group taxable capital.

What happens in Ontario and New Brunswick?

Both provinces retain their separate provincial limit after the federal passive-income reduction. Eligible active income between the two limits moves from the 9% federal rate to 15% while keeping its provincial small-business rate. The provincial capital reduction can still apply.

How can an additional dollar cost more than a dollar in tax?

One extra dollar of ordinary interest can generate direct corporate tax and move up to $5 of active income to the higher federal rate. In British Columbia, at full exposure, the next-year active-income tax adds 80 cents to about 51 cents of upfront investment tax.

Does losing business limit always increase tax?

The limit only affects tax once eligible active income exceeds the reduced limit. A steeper taxable-capital reduction can also absorb some or all of the AAII effect. The calculator compares your scenario with the same capital and active income at $50,000 or less of AAII.
