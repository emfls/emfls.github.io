UK Gift Aid & £100k Tax Trap Calculator 2026/27

See how a Gift Aid donation changes your Income Tax, your Personal Allowance and the High Income Child Benefit Charge in England, Wales and Northern Ireland.

Key takeaways

A £100 Gift Aid donation gives the charity £125. If the entire gift receives relief at your marginal rate, your Income Tax saving is £25 at 40%, £31.25 at 45% or up to £50 within the 60% Personal Allowance taper. A gift that crosses a threshold can receive a mix of rates.

Each £1 paid reduces adjusted net income by £1.25. At £110,000 adjusted net income, a cash gift of £8,000 brings it to £100,000 and restores the full £12,570 Personal Allowance.

Gift Aid questions

How does a £100 Gift Aid donation become £125?

The charity claims £25 at the basic rate. The charity receives £125 in total, and your adjusted net income falls by £125. Give the charity a valid Gift Aid declaration and check your eligible tax for the year.

How do I claim higher-rate or additional-rate Gift Aid relief?

Enter the cash amount you paid on your Self Assessment return, or ask HMRC to adjust your tax code. If the full gross gift receives relief at 40% or 45%, the extra Income Tax saving is £25 or £31.25 per £100 donated. Gifts that cross a band are calculated across the rates they affect.

How can Gift Aid restore my Personal Allowance?

Between £100,000 and £125,140 of adjusted net income, the allowance falls by £1 for each £2 above £100,000. A qualifying cash gift of 80% of the excess over £100,000 can restore the full allowance. At £110,000, an £8,000 gift reduces adjusted net income by £10,000 and can save £4,000 in Income Tax in this model.

Can Gift Aid reduce the High Income Child Benefit Charge?

Yes. It lowers adjusted net income by the gross gift. The charge is 1% of Child Benefit for every full £200 above £60,000 and reaches 100% at £80,000. If you have a partner, check which of you has the higher adjusted net income after the gift.

What if I have paid too little tax to cover Gift Aid?

Your eligible UK Income Tax and Capital Gains Tax for the year must cover the basic-rate reclaim on all your Gift Aid gifts. If the charities claim more than your eligible tax, you can owe the difference to HMRC. National Insurance does not count towards this cover.
