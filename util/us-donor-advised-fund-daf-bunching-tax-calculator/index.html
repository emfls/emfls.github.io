How the estimate works

Enter the same annual gift budget for both strategies. Steady giving makes one gift each year. Bunching contributes the full budget in year 1, then makes no new gifts. The comparison chooses the better standard or itemized tax benefit in every year. Both strategies are measured against making no gifts.

Itemized gifts face the 0.5% AGI floor after the applicable single-asset ceiling. The federal non-itemizer deduction is claimed after AGI and is up to $1,000, or $2,000 on a joint return, for eligible direct cash gifts. Gifts to DAFs, supporting organizations and private non-operating foundations are excluded from that deduction. For gifts above the AGI ceiling, excess may be carried forward for up to five years subject to the carryover rules. The floor itself generally carries only when a percentage limit creates excess in the contribution year.

Scope and assumptions

This federal planning estimate repeats 2026 AGI, rates and deduction amounts across all modeled years. It uses a flat marginal rate, with a 35% itemized benefit in the 37% scenario. Section 68 applies a 2/37 deduction reduction only to the lesser of the affected itemized deductions and income above the top-bracket threshold before itemized deductions. Crossing brackets can change actual savings. This estimate excludes mixed-asset ordering, carryforward use, senior or dependent standard deduction adjustments, AMT, state taxes, fees and timing effects. Stock sale savings apply only when steady giving would be funded by selling the same stock. Gain realization may also alter AGI and deductions.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use the 2026 non-itemizer deduction for a DAF gift?

No. The federal deduction for non-itemizers is for eligible cash gifts made directly to qualifying organizations. Donor-advised fund gifts are excluded.

What are the 2026 basic standard deductions?

The basic amounts are $16,100 for Single, $32,200 for Married Filing Jointly and $24,150 for Head of Household.

What happens if my bunching gift exceeds its AGI ceiling?

For the modeled gifts, the ceiling is 60% of AGI for cash or 30% for appreciated long-term stock. A qualifying excess may carry forward for up to five years, subject to the applicable limits.
