Key takeaways

Canada’s 2026 federal credit is 14% on the first $200 and generally 29% above $200. The 33% tier applies only to the part of the gift above $200 supported by taxable income over $258,482. An eligible direct stock gift can receive a fair-market-value receipt with zero regular capital gains inclusion. Provincial credits and alternative minimum tax can change the result.

Selling before you give can cost you

A sale realizes the gain and usually adds half of it to regular taxable income. Reserving the sale tax leaves less cash for the charity. To give the same amount as the stock’s full value, you would cover the tax separately. Ask the charity how to transfer the shares before placing a sell order.

Frequently asked questions

How does the 2026 federal charitable donation credit work?

The first $200 of the eligible annual claim gets a 14% credit. The portion above $200 gets 29%, except that the 33% rate applies to the lesser of that portion and taxable income exceeding $258,482. The credit reduces tax payable and is non-refundable.

When does a gift of shares qualify for a fair-market-value receipt?

A qualified donee can generally issue an official receipt based on fair market value when it receives eligible shares listed on a designated stock exchange. The regular capital gains inclusion is zero on a qualifying direct gift. A benefit received in exchange for the gift can reduce the eligible amount.

How does selling first affect the gift and its cost?

A sale generally adds 50% of the gain to regular taxable income. Giving the net proceeds after reserving the tax reduces the cash gift and receipt. Giving the full proceeds preserves the receipt amount and requires you to cover the sale tax separately. The calculator shows both results.

Should spouses or common-law partners pool their donations?

The CRA allows eligible receipts to be combined on one return. Pooling can avoid a second first-$200 tier, while Alberta’s 60% provincial first tier can favour splitting eligible receipts. Income, tax payable and claim limits can change the outcome. The comparison assumes equal incomes and a 50/50 split, and shows the federal effect only for Québec.

Does the 30% AMT inclusion mean I owe tax on the gift?

The 30% inclusion adds part of the gain to adjusted income for federal AMT. The 20.5% rate is applied after the exemption. Actual liability depends on the full Form T691 calculation, including applicable credits and the comparison with regular federal tax.
