US Opportunity Zone 2.0 Calculator

Compare taxable reinvestment with a standard Qualified Opportunity Fund (QOF) and a Qualified Rural Opportunity Fund (QROF) under OBBBA.

Key takeaways

  • Five years can reduce the taxable share of the original gain to 90% in a standard QOF or 70% in a qualifying rural fund when the investment has held its value.
  • Ten years can unlock an election to exclude federal tax on qualifying appreciation at exit. Tax on the original deferred gain is calculated separately.
  • January 1, 2027 starts the rolling rules for new QOF investments. The original regime generally recognizes remaining deferred gain by December 31, 2026.

Compare your after-tax outcomes

Check your jurisdiction and tax year. California does not conform. New York's IT-225 requires a deferral addback for covered gains. Massachusetts adopts the federal changes for tax years starting in 2027 and restricts eligible zones to those entirely within Massachusetts. The “No” scenario models full nonconformity, including state exit tax.

Plan for the tax bill before you invest

A fund can lose money or tie up cash. You may owe tax in year five without a distribution to fund it. An early exit can end deferral and forfeit the basis step-up or appreciation exclusion. Fees, funding costs and weaker fund performance can erase a projected tax advantage.

How the estimate works

  • The taxable account invests the gain left after tax today. Each fund invests the full eligible gain. In the full nonconformity scenario, initial state tax is paid from outside the fund.
  • The original gain included for a QOF is the lesser of the deferred gain or fund value at inclusion, reduced by the applicable step-up and floored at zero. The step-up and recognized gain both increase federal basis before exit tax is calculated.
  • Final net wealth subtracts exit tax and the future value of any QOF tax paid earlier, using the assumed annual growth rate to measure the opportunity cost. IRR uses the actual timing of the initial outlay, deferred tax and exit proceeds. The lift is the difference in percentage points versus taxable reinvestment.
  • All scenarios use the same constant growth and flat tax rates. The 10-year savings row shows federal exit tax avoided on the same fund interest. Modeled gains use the selected long-term rate, including a one-year estimate that assumes a sale after the one-year mark. The NIIT switch applies the full 3.8% when enabled.
  • The estimate excludes fees, distributions, capital-loss offsets, short-term and special gain rates, depreciation recapture, and partial state conformity. It assumes a qualifying investment and a timely election. Verify individual rules before filing. See IRS guidance on capital gains and NIIT.

Opportunity Zone 2.0 FAQ

When does the rolling five-year deferral apply?

It applies to amounts invested in qualified opportunity funds after December 31, 2026. The original gain is generally included in the tax year of the earlier of sale or exchange and the fifth anniversary of the investment.

How much original gain is taxable after five years?

When the fund value is at least the deferred gain, a standard QOF includes 90% and a qualifying QROF includes 70%. If fund value has declined, the inclusion calculation starts with the lower fair market value and then subtracts the applicable step-up.

What does the 10-year federal exclusion cover?

A qualifying election after at least 10 years can set the basis to fair market value for an exit before the 30th anniversary. That can eliminate federal tax on eligible appreciation. The original deferred gain still has its own inclusion event, and state treatment can differ.

Does any fund investing in rural real estate get the 30% step-up?

A qualified rural opportunity fund must meet the 90% qualifying rural QOZ property test. The separate 50% substantial-improvement threshold applies to eligible property in a zone comprised entirely of a rural area and must be exceeded within the statutory 30-month period.

What changes if my state does not conform?

The full nonconformity scenario charges state tax on the original gain today and taxes positive fund appreciation at sale. State rules can decouple from deferral or exclusion separately, so check the rules for your state, tax year and fund location.
