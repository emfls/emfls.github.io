Key takeaways

On a £100,000 total loss, a 45% taxpayer with enough tax and qualifying income could lose £27,500 (27.5%) under SEIS or £38,500 (38.5%) under EIS after Income Tax relief and share loss relief.

SEIS could reduce that loss to £15,500 (15.5%) if a separate £100,000 eligible taxable gain also qualifies for 50% reinvestment relief at a 24% CGT rate.

Loss aversion and startup risk

A smaller modelled net loss can make a risky investment feel safer. You still pay the full subscription upfront, the shares can become worthless, and relief can take time to claim. The reliefs depend on your tax position, ongoing qualification and the facts of the disposal. An early sale or loss of eligibility can trigger clawback.

What the estimate assumes

It treats the investment as shares bought at the same unit price, uses one claim year with unused scheme limits, and allocates relief to qualifying shares first. It assumes full disposal after the qualifying period with relief retained. For a carry-back estimate, use the liability and matching SEIS gain for the previous year.

The SEIS and EIS estimates assume the share loss qualifies for Income Tax loss relief and value only the portion you can use in the selected band. The taxable comparison assigns zero tax benefit to losses, although ordinary shares can generate an allowable capital loss and some qualifying trading company shares can receive Income Tax loss relief.

Future and original CGT are estimated at the selected rate. The model applies the entered allowance to gains on these shares and shows the revived EIS gain at the gross tax amount, before any future offsets. It uses nominal amounts and excludes fees, time value, special rates and early-exit clawback calculations.

Frequently asked questions

How much Income Tax relief can I claim in 2026/27?

SEIS offers 50% on up to £200,000 of qualifying subscriptions. EIS offers 30% on up to £1 million, with up to another £1 million of capacity for qualifying knowledge-intensive company (KIC) shares. Your available UK Income Tax liability caps the cash relief.

Can I carry the Income Tax claim back?

You can elect to treat all or part of a qualifying subscription as made in the preceding tax year, subject to that year's available limit and liability. A SEIS reinvestment gain must match the year in which the relevant shares are treated as issued. This calculator models one claim year at a time.

How do the two schemes treat a gain from another asset?

SEIS can exempt half of the gain matched to a qualifying subscription for the same tax year when Income Tax relief is obtained and retained. EIS can defer a qualifying gain if the shares are issued from one year before to three years after the gain arose. The EIS gain normally comes back into charge on disposal or another chargeable event.

Does holding the shares for three years make the exit tax-free?

Disposal relief generally requires a qualifying holding of at least three years and Income Tax relief that remains attributable to the shares. For EIS, the clock can start later than issue if the qualifying trade starts later. A subscription over the annual limit can leave part of the gain taxable. A revived EIS deferred gain is a separate charge.

Can I claim share loss relief if a startup becomes worthless?

A qualifying disposal or valid negligible-value claim can establish a share loss. Deduct any retained upfront Income Tax relief from the share cost before working out the loss. A qualifying loss can be set against income of the loss year or the preceding year. The tax saving depends on the income available in the relevant bands.
