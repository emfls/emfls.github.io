How Negative Gearing Works Under 2026–27 ATO Tax Brackets

In Australia, an investment property is negatively geared when its total tax-deductible expenses—mortgage interest (never loan principal repayments), council and water rates, landlord insurance, property management commissions, repairs, strata levies, and non-cash capital depreciation—exceed gross rental income for the financial year. Under Australian tax law, that net rental loss offsets your regular PAYG salary or business income directly, generating an ATO tax refund proportional to your marginal income tax rate plus the 2% Medicare Levy.

Crucially, accurate 2026–27 modelling must compute tax savings across progressive tax brackets rather than multiplying the entire loss by a single top rate. For example, an investor earning A$155,000 sits in the 39% bracket (37% income tax + 2% Medicare Levy) on the top A$20,000 above A$135,000—but if their net rental loss is A$25,500, the first A$20,000 receives a 39% refund while the remaining A$5,500 drops into the 32% bracket (30% + 2% Medicare). Division 40/43 Non-Cash Depreciation & The Breakeven Capital Growth Rule

The secret weapon that turns a moderately negative pre-tax cash flow into a manageable weekly holding cost—or even an after-tax cash-flow-positive investment—is a quantity surveyor's Tax Depreciation Schedule covering two ATO divisions:

  • Division 43 (Capital Works / Building Structure): Deducts 2.5% per year of original construction cost over 40 years for residential properties built after 15 September 1987 (including subsequent capital renovations).
  • Division 40 (Plant & Equipment): Depreciates removable assets such as carpets, blinds, hot water systems, dishwashers, and air conditioners on brand-new properties or newly installed replacements. (Note: under 2017 law changes, second-hand plant and equipment in previously used residential properties generally cannot be claimed annually, though deferred losses may adjust CGT upon sale.)

Remember two essential financial realities before purchasing a negatively geared asset: first, you are still losing real cash every week—a 47% tax refund means the ATO covers 47 cents while you fund the remaining 53 cents out of pocket. Your investment only builds wealth if long-term capital growth comfortably beats your Breakeven Annual Capital Growth Rate (%) shown above (plus stamp duty and selling costs). Second, every dollar of Division 43 building depreciation you claim reduces your property's CGT cost base upon future sale—though because long-term individuals receive the 50% CGT discount after 12 months, trading a 39%–47% upfront deduction for a discounted 19.5%–23.5% future CGT liability remains a strong net tax arbitrage.

Before committing to a negatively geared metropolitan property, remember two subtle Australian statutory interactions that catch first-time landlords off guard. First, net investment losses are added back when calculating Adjusted Taxable Income (ATI) and Repayment Income—meaning negative gearing does NOT lower your compulsory HECS-HELP repayment rate, Medicare Levy Surcharge (MLS) liability, Division 293 super tax threshold, or Child Care Subsidy income test. Second, maintain an offset account rather than redrawing extra loan repayments for personal spending: drawing personal funds out of an investment loan taints the deductible purpose of that loan portion permanently under ATO TR 2000/2. Always retain receipts for council rates, landlord insurance, body corporate admin funds (note: special sinking fund levies for capital works are depreciated under Div 43 rather than expensed immediately), and borrowing costs (amortized over five years) to ensure full ATO audit compliance.

Interest-Only vs. Principal & Interest Loans and Ownership Structures

Many Australian investors choose a 5-year Interest-Only (IO) repayment period on negatively geared investment properties while directing spare cash into an offset account linked to their non-deductible Principal Place of Residence (PPOR) mortgage. Because every dollar sitting in a PPOR offset saves non-deductible home-loan interest (equivalent to earning 9%–11% pre-tax for top-bracket taxpayers), keeping investment loan principal intact maximizes deductible interest while accelerating non-deductible debt elimination. Similarly, couples planning a multi-property portfolio should evaluate legal ownership percentages before signing the contract of sale: holding a heavily negatively geared growth property 90% in the higher-income spouse's name maximizes upfront ATO refunds, whereas holding a positively geared high-yield regional property in a lower-income spouse's name or discretionary family trust keeps ongoing rental tax leakage to a minimum. Always factor state-by-state land tax thresholds (especially Victoria's lowered A$50,000 threshold and New South Wales's indexed threshold) into your annual operating expense input.

Record Keeping and Capital Gains Tax Cost-Base Adjustments on Property Disposal

The Australian Taxation Office maintains sophisticated data-matching programs with state land titles registries, financial institutions, and property management platforms. Under Section 900-15 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1997, property investors must maintain written evidence for every claimed deduction for a minimum of five years from the date the relevant tax return was lodged. This includes formal loan account statements distinguishing deductible investment borrowing from personal redraws, invoices for maintenance and repairs distinguishing immediate revenue expenses from capital improvements, body corporate statements, and your initial quantity surveyor depreciation report. When disposing of the property in future tax years, capital works deductions claimed under Division 43 must be subtracted from your asset cost base, increasing your gross capital gain. However, if the asset was held for longer than twelve months by an individual or qualifying trust, the remaining net gain qualifies for the 50% CGT discount under Division 115, effectively cutting your ultimate tax rate in half.

Cash-Flow Tip (ATO PAYG Withholding Variation): Instead of waiting until August for a lump-sum ATO refund while funding a weekly shortfall all year, eligible investors can lodge an ATO PAYG Withholding Variation form so their employer reduces weekly payroll tax withheld immediately.

Frequently Asked Questions (2026/27)

Can I claim principal mortgage repayments under Australian negative gearing?

No. Only the interest component of your investment loan is tax-deductible under ITAA 1997 s.8-1. Principal repayments build equity and cannot be claimed as a rental deduction.

How do the 2026–27 ATO tax brackets affect negative gearing refunds?

Following the Stage 3 tax reforms, the 30% bracket spans A$45,001 to A$135,000, the 37% bracket starts at A$135,001, and the top 45% bracket starts at A$190,001 (+2% Medicare Levy). Higher-income investors above A$190k receive 47 cents back per dollar of net rental loss.

Does claiming Division 43 building depreciation increase Capital Gains Tax later?

Yes. Division 43 capital works deductions claimed over the ownership period reduce your CGT cost base when you sell. However, if you hold for over 12 months as an individual, the 50% CGT discount cuts the eventual tax clawback in half.

What if I own the investment property jointly with my spouse?

Rental income and losses must be split strictly according to legal ownership title (e.g., 50/50 for joint tenants, or custom proportions such as 90/10 for tenants in common). You cannot arbitrarily assign 100% of the loss to the higher-earning spouse.

What is a healthy breakeven capital growth rate for a negatively geared property?

Most disciplined Australian property investors target an after-tax holding shortfall below 1.0%–1.5% of property value per year, ensuring realistic 5%–7% long-term metropolitan capital growth delivers strong net compounding.
