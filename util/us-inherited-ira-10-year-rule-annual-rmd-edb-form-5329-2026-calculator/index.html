Form 5329 missed RMD excise tax

25% standard
10% timely correction
0% if full waiver granted

The 10% rate generally requires taking the shortfall and filing a return reflecting the reduced tax within the correction window. The window closes at the earliest of an IRS deficiency notice, an assessment, or the end of the second following tax year. For a reasonable-error waiver, document the error and the steps you’re taking to remedy it on Form 5329. The IRS decides whether to grant it.

How to read this estimate

Year 1 is the calendar year after the owner’s death. Start with the December 31 balance at the end of the death year. The projection applies growth before each year-end withdrawal, calculates RMDs from the prior December 31 balance, and applies 2026 brackets and the same other taxable income to all ten years.

IRA tax is the increase in ordinary federal income tax after adding the withdrawal. Traditional withdrawals are assumed fully taxable. The estimate excludes state taxes, credits, Medicare surcharges, reinvestment of withdrawals, and tax effects from other income. A lower tax total can reflect less growth or a different ending balance, especially for a stretch IRA.

The schedule models one direct individual beneficiary under the current post-2019 inheritance rules. Handle any remaining death-year RMD separately. Historical transition relief, successor beneficiaries, trusts, spouse rollover or delayed starts, and elections to use another distribution method require separate analysis.

Inherited IRA FAQ

Do I need annual RMDs under the 10-year rule?

A non-eligible designated beneficiary generally has annual RMDs in years 1–9 if the traditional IRA owner died on or after the required beginning date. If the owner died before that date, years 1–9 have no annual minimum. Both cases require a full payout by December 31 of year 10.

How is the annual inherited IRA RMD calculated?

Divide the prior December 31 balance by the applicable Table I divisor. The beneficiary generally starts with their age in the year after death and reduces the divisor by one each year. After the owner's required beginning date, use the longer remaining life expectancy. A sole spouse using the inherited-account life-expectancy method recalculates their own divisor each year.

When does an eligible designated beneficiary have to empty the account?

A qualifying spouse, disabled or chronically ill beneficiary, or someone no more than 10 years younger may take life-expectancy distributions. If the owner's own child qualified solely as a minor, annual distributions continue and the balance is due by December 31 of the tenth year after the child reaches age 21.

Do inherited Roth IRAs have RMDs or income tax?

The original Roth IRA owner is treated as dying before the required beginning date. A non-eligible designated beneficiary has no annual minimum in years 1–9 and must empty the account by year 10. Qualified distributions are tax-free. Earnings may be taxable before the original Roth five-tax-year holding period is satisfied.

When can a missed RMD penalty drop from 25% to 10% or zero?

The 10% rate generally requires receiving the shortfall and filing a return reflecting the reduced tax within the correction window. The window can close before the end of the second following tax year if the IRS mails a deficiency notice or assesses the tax. A full waiver requires IRS approval for reasonable error and reasonable steps to remedy the shortfall.
