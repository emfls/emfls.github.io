Key takeaways

  • RRSP, PRPP and SPP. The 1% monthly tax generally applies to excess above the eligible $2,000 cushion at month-end. The cushion provides no deduction room.
  • TFSA. The 1% tax applies to the highest excess in each calendar month, even if you fix it that month. The cushion is $0.
  • 2026 filing dates. File and pay by March 31, 2027 for RRSP excess tax or June 30, 2027 for TFSA tax.

CRA filing and waiver checklist

RRSP, PRPP and SPP

  1. Gather dated contributions and withdrawals plus your CRA deduction limit. Work out the taxable excess at each month-end.
  2. If tax is due, file T1-OVP or T1-OVP-S if eligible, for the 2026 tax year. Pay by March 31, 2027.
  3. Before withdrawing eligible unused contributions, check whether CRA approval of T3012A can prevent withholding. Standard RRSP withholding outside Quebec for Canadian residents is 10%, 20% or 30%, depending on the withdrawal.
  4. If you withdrew without an approved waiver, report the withdrawal on your T1. Claim eligible withholding on line 43700 and use T746 to calculate any eligible offsetting deduction on line 23200. Quebec and non-resident withholding rules differ.
  5. If a reasonable error caused the excess and you took reasonable steps to eliminate it, ask the CRA to review a waiver or cancellation using RC2503. Include transaction dates and evidence of correction.

TFSA

  1. Check the dates and amounts across all TFSAs, then remove the remaining excess. Keep issuer records showing each month’s highest excess.
  2. If tax is due, file RC243 with Schedule A for excess and Schedule B for non-resident contributions as applicable. Pay by June 30, 2027.
  3. Check unused room before contributing again. For eligible holders, the portion of a 2026 withdrawal above the excess at the time can add room on January 1, 2027.
  4. For a possible waiver or cancellation after a reasonable error, send the CRA a letter explaining the error and how promptly you corrected it. Include your statements and transaction history.

Frequently asked questions

Who qualifies for the $2,000 RRSP cushion in 2026?

You generally qualify if you reached age 18 in a prior tax year. For 2026, that means you were 18 by December 31, 2025. The cushion applies to your outstanding excess across eligible plans and does not create additional deduction room.

Does a withdrawal in the contribution month avoid the 1% tax?

An RRSP excess fully corrected before the end of the month it arose can avoid that month’s excess tax. TFSA tax uses the highest excess during the entire month, so a same-month correction still leaves tax for that month.

Can I put a 2026 TFSA withdrawal back in the same year?

Only if you already have enough available contribution room. A 2026 withdrawal does not create room in 2026. For eligible holders, the part above any excess at the time of withdrawal is generally added back on January 1, 2027.

Can a non-resident pay two TFSA taxes in the same month?

Yes. A taxable non-resident contribution can incur its own 1% monthly tax even within available room. If it also creates an excess, the 1% tax on the highest monthly excess can apply as well.

Can I avoid withholding when withdrawing unused RRSP contributions?

An approved T3012A can allow an eligible refund without withholding. If the issuer already withheld tax, report the withdrawal on your T1, claim withholding on line 43700 and use T746 to calculate any eligible deduction.
