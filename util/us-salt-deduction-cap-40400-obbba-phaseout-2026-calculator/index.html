A higher MAGI can cost you part of your deduction

Inside the phase-down, another $1 of MAGI can cost $0.30 of SALT deduction. When that cap binds and itemizing still pays, taxable income can rise by $1.30. A 35% bracket then produces a 45.5% SALT-adjusted marginal rate. For separate returns, the $0.15 deduction loss produces 40.25% at the same bracket.

Frequently asked questions

What is the 2026 SALT cap and when does it start to fall?

The cap is $40,400 for single, joint and head-of-household returns and $20,200 for married filing separately. The reduction begins above $505,000 of SALT MAGI, or $252,500 for separate returns. The maximum cap and threshold rise 1% annually through 2029. In 2030 the cap is scheduled to return to $10,000, or $5,000 for separate returns.

When does the SALT cap reach its floor?

The $10,000 floor is reached at approximately $606,333.33 of SALT MAGI for single, joint and head-of-household returns. For separate returns, the law halves the cap after applying the 30% reduction. That makes the effective reduction 15%, and the $5,000 floor is reached at approximately $353,833.33.

When does the SALT torpedo turn 35% into 45.5%?

When itemizing still gives you the larger deduction and the falling cap limits your SALT, an additional dollar of MAGI can raise taxable income by $1.30. At a 35% bracket, that gives a SALT-only marginal rate of 45.5%. The comparable separate-return example is 40.25% because the effective phase-down is 15%.

How much federal tax could the higher cap save?

The calculator chooses the larger available deduction under each cap, then applies your selected marginal rate to the change in estimated taxable income. The comparison holds other deductions fixed and uses the former $10,000 cap, or $5,000 for separate returns. If the standard deduction wins under both caps, the estimated savings are zero.

Can a 401(k), HSA or donor-advised fund help?

Eligible additional traditional pre-tax 401(k) deferrals or deductible or excluded HSA contributions can lower SALT MAGI and may restore part of your SALT deduction. An eligible donor-advised fund gift can increase itemized charitable deductions. Its deduction is taken on Schedule A and leaves SALT MAGI unchanged.
