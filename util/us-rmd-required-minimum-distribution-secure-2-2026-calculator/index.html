Frequently asked questions

When is my first RMD due if I turn 73 in 2026?

A traditional IRA owner born in 1953 has a 2026 RMD due by April 1, 2027. Taking it in 2027 puts two RMDs in that calendar year, because the 2027 RMD is also due by December 31, 2027.

How do I calculate my RMD, and when do I use the joint table?

Divide your previous December 31 balance by the IRS Table III factor for the age you turn during the distribution year. Use Table II when your sole beneficiary is your spouse and they are more than 10 years younger. Roth IRA and Roth 401(k) owners have no lifetime RMD.

How much can I give through a QCD in 2026?

At age 70½ or older, have an eligible IRA custodian send the gift directly to a qualifying charity. Up to $111,000 per eligible owner can be excluded from income and the distribution can count toward the owner's RMD. Two eligible spouses can exclude up to $222,000 from their separate IRAs. A one-time $55,000 split-interest election counts within the annual cap.

What happens if I miss my RMD deadline?

The standard excise tax is 25% of the shortfall. It can drop to 10% if you withdraw the shortfall and file the required return within the correction window. The window can close early on notice of deficiency or assessment. Form 5329 also provides instructions for requesting a reasonable-error waiver.

Can a 2026 QCD reduce my 2026 Medicare premiums?

Medicare generally uses income from two years earlier, so a 2026 QCD would ordinarily affect 2028 premiums. The 2026 first-tier thresholds of $109,000 for single filers and $218,000 for joint filers are a comparison benchmark for this calculator.
