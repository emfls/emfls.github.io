What you give up when you opt out

Opting out can free up cash now. Each eligible year you skip gives up that year’s additional lifetime PRB. Earlier PRB accruals continue. Investment returns can vary, and the longer you live the more monthly PRB payments you can receive.

GIS can change the decision. The model subtracts 50 cents of GIS per dollar of gross PRB at age 65 and over in the GIS scenario. Actual reductions depend on household income, marital status and benefit thresholds. Self-employed workers pay both CPP shares for the same modeled PRB accrual.

How the comparison works

Contributions and tax. CPP applies the first rate to earnings above the basic exemption up to the first ceiling and the second rate above that ceiling up to the second ceiling. The model credits one base share at 14% federal where assumed usable, and deducts both enhancements and the employer-equivalent base share for self-employed workers. Employee lines are 30800 and 22215. Self-employed lines are 31000 and 22200. Provincial credits, credit limits and any GIS effect of contribution deductions are excluded. The same marginal tax rate applies to deductions and new PRB income.

PRB accrual. Benefits start the January after each work year. The Tier 1 estimate scales by contributory earnings above the basic exemption divided by $71,100. The separate CPP2 increment scales by earnings above $74,600 up to $85,000. The estimate uses the 2023–2027 five-year earnings ceiling average for a payment starting in 2027. The adjustment at first payment is −0.6% per month before age 65 and +0.7% per month after age 65, up to +42% at age 70. Service Canada uses statutory earnings and actual contribution records to calculate your award.

Timing and break-even. The projection applies full calendar years, 2026 contribution rules and constant 2026 dollars throughout. It approximates the age-70 stop and opt-out as whole-year boundaries. Monthly costs, savings and benefits land at month-end. Break-even is the first month-end after funding all optional contributions when the optional benefits cover their costs. Simple break-even ignores growth, while present-value break-even discounts both streams. The recommendation compares discounted cash flows through your chosen life expectancy. The two invested balances use the same return to make the comparison at the same age.

Scope. The projection assumes you receive your CPP pension for the full working period and uses one employment type. A partial year, the year your CPP pension starts, or mixed employment and self-employment can change contributions or benefits.

Frequently asked questions

Who can stop CPP contributions while working and receiving CPP?

Contributions on qualifying earnings are mandatory at ages 60 to 64. Eligible people aged 65 to 69 can elect to stop. Contributions cease after the month you turn 70, and existing post-retirement benefits continue.

When does Form CPT30 take effect for an employee?

Send the original signed form to the CRA and give a copy to each employer. The election generally takes effect on the first day of the month after the employer receives it. You can make an election or revocation once per calendar year.

How do I opt out if I am self-employed?

If you have only self-employment income outside Québec, make the election in the applicable part of Schedule 8 when you file your tax return. If you also have employment earnings, check the CRA timing rules for both types of income.

Can 2026 contributions earn the published $54.69 monthly maximum?

The published $54.69 is the maximum for a new PRB starting at age 65 in 2026, based on maximum 2025 contributions. A benefit earned from 2026 contributions generally starts in January 2027 and depends on earnings, contributions and age at first payment.

Does a post-retirement benefit reduce GIS?

PRB is taxable CPP income and can reduce the Guaranteed Income Supplement. The calculator models a 50% GIS reduction as a scenario. The actual reduction depends on household income, marital status, benefit thresholds and reassessment timing.
