Estimate what you keep and compare the cost of reducing your adjusted net income (ANI) with a SIPP or workplace pension salary sacrifice.

Key takeaways

  • Keep the full benefit at £60,000 ANI or below. The charge rises by 1% per full £200 above £60,000 and reaches 100% at £80,000.
  • Two eligible children receive £2,337.40 over 52 weeks. At £70,000 ANI, the estimated charge is £1,168, leaving £1,169.40.
  • Pension contributions can cut the charge. A partner’s higher ANI can still limit how much you recover.

Protect your State Pension credits

Always claim Child Benefit if eligible. If you choose to forgo payments, register and opt out of payments. A claimant with a child under 12 can keep the National Insurance credits that count towards their State Pension. Check how much benefit you would still keep before opting out. If you already opted out, contact HMRC about restarting payments.

Frequently asked questions

Whose income counts if we both work?

Use the higher individual adjusted net income, even if that person does not claim Child Benefit. If your pension contribution makes your partner the higher-ANI earner, their income sets the remaining charge.

How much Child Benefit do two children receive in 2026/27?

Over 52 weeks, one eldest child and one additional child receive £2,337.40. At £70,000 of higher individual ANI, the taper is 50%. The statutory whole-pound calculation gives a £1,168 charge and leaves £1,169.40.

What income and deductions should I enter?

Include taxable income from all sources before Personal Allowance. Subtract eligible gross pension contributions that are not already deducted, plus grossed-up Gift Aid. For example, £800 paid under Gift Aid reduces ANI by £1,000.

How does salary sacrifice compare with a SIPP at the higher rate?

Each £1,000 gross costs an estimated £580 by salary sacrifice if all the pay saves 40% Income Tax and 2% employee NI. A relief-at-source SIPP costs £600 after the provider adds basic-rate relief and you claim the additional higher-rate relief. Both figures are before any reduction in HICBC.

Can I keep State Pension credits if I opt out of Child Benefit payments?

Yes. Register for Child Benefit and opt out of payments if you choose to forgo them. If your child is under 12, the claimant can still receive National Insurance credits that count towards their State Pension.
