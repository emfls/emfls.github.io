Frequently asked questions

What changed for private cars and vans in 2026/27?

The approved Income Tax rate rose from 45p to 55p for the first 10,000 business miles on 6 April 2026. The rate above 10,000 miles is still 25p. At 10,000 miles the approved amount rises from £4,500 to £5,500.

How much tax can I get back, and where do I claim?

For an own-vehicle claim, deduct employer mileage payments from the approved amount. A £1,000 deduction saves £200 if the entire amount falls in the 20% band and you paid enough Income Tax. Use P87 by post for total eligible job expenses up to £2,500, or Self Assessment above £2,500 or if you already file a return.

Do I owe Class 1 National Insurance on the annual mileage excess?

Class 1 uses the earnings-period qualifying amount. For cars and vans in 2026/27, the disregard is 55p for every qualifying mile in that period. Payroll adds motoring payments above that amount to earnings for Class 1 calculations.

Can I claim an unpaid 5p passenger supplement?

An employer can pay up to 5p per mile for each qualifying fellow employee carried on a business journey in your car or van. If the employer pays less or pays nothing, there is no Mileage Allowance Relief for the passenger shortfall.

Can a company reimburse more than the public EV rate?

Yes, if the employer can show that the cost per business mile is higher. At 72p per kWh and 3.6 miles per kWh, charging costs 20p per mile, leaving a 5p gap at a 15p advisory rate. Use the rates for your journey dates and keep charging records.
