Key takeaways

A 2-1 temporary buydown yields dramatic initial relief by reducing the note rate by 2% in Year 1 and 1% in Year 2, saving over $500 per month in Year 1 on a typical $400,000 loan.

In contrast, permanent discount points require 4.5 to 6 years of holding to reach cash break-even, while an equivalent price cut offers only modest monthly savings (~$48/mo) but slightly lowers upfront down payment cash.

Refinancing before break-even forfeits permanent point value

Paying discount points upfront costs thousands in cash. If mortgage rates drop and you refinance within 3 to 4 years, you will never recoup the cost of the points. Conversely, unspent 2-1 buydown escrow funds are credited against your loan balance when refinancing, protecting your capital.

Mortgage buydown and concession FAQ

What is a 2-1 temporary mortgage buydown?

A 2-1 temporary buydown lowers the borrower’s interest rate by 2% in the first year and 1% in the second year, funded through an upfront seller-paid escrow concession. Starting in year three, payments return to the original note rate for the remaining term.

How does a 2-1 buydown compare to permanent discount points?

A 2-1 buydown maximizes upfront monthly cash flow savings in years one and two, making it ideal for buyers planning to refinance or expecting income growth. Permanent discount points permanently reduce the interest rate (typically 0.25% per 1 point cost) and become more cost-effective if holding the mortgage past the 4 to 6 year break-even period.

What happens to unused 2-1 buydown escrow funds if I refinance?

Under Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac guidelines, any unexpended funds remaining in the buydown escrow account when refinancing or selling the property are credited directly against the borrower’s outstanding principal payoff balance.

Why does a seller price cut provide smaller monthly payment savings?

A direct price cut reduces the loan balance, which slightly lowers monthly payments and down payment cash, but does not alter the note interest rate. A $9,200 price cut saves roughly $48 per month, whereas an equivalent $9,200 2-1 buydown saves over $500 per month in year one.

Are seller concessions for buydowns subject to Fannie Mae limits?

Yes. Interested party contribution (IPC) caps apply: 3% of purchase price for LTV above 90%, 6% for LTV between 75.01% and 90%, and 9% for LTV 75% or lower on conventional loans. FHA loans permit up to 6% seller concessions.
