2026 Jumbo vs. Conforming Mortgage & 80-10-10 Calculator

Compare a single mortgage, a conforming first loan with a second lien, and putting enough extra cash down to reach your county’s conforming limit.

Key takeaways

On a $1,100,000 home with 10% down, you would borrow $990,000. Under the $832,750 baseline limit, you need $157,250 more down to use a single conforming loan. A piggyback can finance that gap with a second lien.

A higher county limit can make the same single loan conforming. At less than 20% down, compare its estimated PMI against the cost of the second lien.

Protect your cash cushion

Extra cash down turns liquid savings into home equity. A smaller payment can still leave you short after closing. Jumbo reserve estimates use 6–12 months of modeled loan payments. Lenders may use a different reserve period and include taxes, insurance and HOA dues in monthly housing expense.

A piggyback preserves cash at closing but adds another lien. A variable-rate HELOC can raise your payment, and a second lien can complicate refinancing. Ask for the complete payment and fee schedule before choosing.

How to read the results

  • Blended interest rate weights the opening note rates by loan balance. APR depends on lender finance charges.
  • Cash plus borrowing cost adds the down payment, cumulative interest and PMI. Cash paid counts the down payment and all modeled payments, including principal. Borrowing cost adds interest and PMI alone. The remaining balance shows what you would still owe at each horizon.
  • Monthly payments include principal, interest and any modeled PMI. The estimate excludes closing fees, points, escrow, property taxes, homeowners insurance, tax savings and investment returns. The model assumes immediate amortization and a constant second-lien rate.
  • PMI uses original principal and the entered annual rate. For conventional PMI the estimate assumes current payments and scheduled termination at 78% of original value or term midpoint. Jumbo PMI terms can differ. CFPB PMI guidance

Frequently asked questions

What makes a mortgage jumbo in 2026?

A loan above the applicable one-unit conforming limit is jumbo by size. The 2026 baseline is $832,750, and the standard high-cost ceiling is $1,249,125. Use your county’s published limit. Loans within the limit still need to meet lender and agency requirements.

Why can the piggyback split differ from 80-10-10?

A classic 80-10-10 uses an 80% first mortgage, a 10% second lien and 10% down. When the county limit is below 80% of the price, the second lien must cover more of the purchase. The calculator assumes zero PMI on this split. Ask your lender about combined loan-to-value limits and available second-lien terms.

How does the calculator model PMI and HELOC payments?

For a single loan above 80% loan-to-value, monthly PMI uses the original principal and the entered annual PMI rate. The estimate ends PMI after scheduled balance reaches 78% of price or at term midpoint, whichever comes first. A zero jumbo PMI rate disables that charge. The second lien starts repaying immediately at a constant rate. A HELOC can have rate resets or an interest-only draw period.

What does the deductible interest ratio estimate?

The ratio is the smaller of 100% or $750,000 divided by total starting debt, assuming both liens are qualifying acquisition debt and the borrower files single or jointly. The IRS worksheet uses qualifying average balances and itemizing rules. The ratio does not estimate tax savings.

How is the return on extra cash down calculated?

At each horizon, subtract the extra-down option’s interest and PMI from the same-down single loan’s interest and PMI, then divide the savings by the additional down payment. This is a cumulative percentage. The model excludes alternative investment returns, closing fees and tax effects.
