Key takeaways

You can usually take up to 25% tax-free, subject to both remaining allowances. The standard Lump Sum Allowance (LSA) is £268,275 and the standard Lump Sum and Death Benefit Allowance (LSDBA) is £1,073,100. The same tax-free cash reduces both.

PCLS alone leaves the rest invested and avoids a new MPAA trigger. UFPLS pays the full amount in cash, with the taxable portion added to your other income and a £10,000 MPAA trigger.

A large withdrawal can cost you tax relief

Taxable pension cash can reduce your Personal Allowance once adjusted net income passes £100,000. Each £1 within the taper band can cost 60p in combined tax. A qualifying flexible payment can also reduce the amount you can contribute to defined contribution pensions without an annual allowance charge. If you or your employer still pay into a pension, check the £10,000 MPAA before taking taxable cash. HMRC MPAA guidance

How the estimate works

Income tax due on the payment is the difference between annual tax with and without the taxable pension cash. The calculator treats the combined ordinary income as adjusted net income. If pension relief, Gift Aid, savings, dividends or Scottish income tax rates affect your return, your actual liability can differ.

Enter protected limits and prior usage from your pension records. For benefits taken before 6 April 2024, use your provider’s confirmed figures, including any transitional tax-free amount certificate. Scheme-specific protection can change the 25% calculation. The estimate assumes standard PCLS and UFPLS eligibility and excludes separate pension commencement excess lump sums. HMRC allowance guidance · HMRC excess lump sum rules

For example, crystallising £40,000 with both allowances intact and taking PCLS only pays £10,000 in cash. An UFPLS of £40,000 pays £10,000 tax-free and adds £30,000 to taxable income. At £85,000 of other income, the UFPLS adds £15,000 of annual income tax and leaves £25,000 in net cash.

Frequently asked questions

How much pension cash is tax-free in 2026/27?

Most people can take up to 25% of the amount being crystallised or withdrawn tax-free. The payment is capped by both remaining allowances. The standard limits are £268,275 for the Lump Sum Allowance and £1,073,100 for the Lump Sum and Death Benefit Allowance. Qualifying tax-free cash reduces both balances.

How do PCLS and UFPLS handle cash above the tax-free cap?

With PCLS, the amount left after the tax-free payment enters drawdown. Tax applies to drawdown income when paid. With UFPLS, the entire requested amount is paid out and the amount above the tax-free slice is taxed as pension income. Some schemes may also offer a separate taxable pension commencement excess lump sum. The former 55% lifetime allowance charge was abolished.

How does the £100,000 Personal Allowance taper affect pension tax?

The Personal Allowance falls by £1 for every £2 of adjusted net income over £100,000 and reaches zero at £125,140. For ordinary income at the rates used here, a taxable pension payment within that range can face 40% income tax plus 20% from the lost allowance. Only the part within the taper band faces that combined rate.

Which pension withdrawals trigger the £10,000 MPAA?

Taking PCLS alone does not trigger the Money Purchase Annual Allowance. A first qualifying payment from flexi-access drawdown or an UFPLS normally triggers it. The result applies to this transaction, so a previously triggered MPAA can still apply even if you take PCLS only now.

Why might the provider withhold more tax than this calculator shows?

A provider may use an emergency tax code on a Month 1 basis for the first flexible taxable payment. That gives the payment one month’s allowances and tax bands, which can lead to excess withholding on a large one-off withdrawal. Compare your provider’s PAYE figures with the full-year tax estimate and check HMRC’s refund routes if you overpay.
