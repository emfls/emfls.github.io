How the Calculation Works & Mathematical Formula

The Grade Calculator allows you to compute a weighted grade from two assessment groups based on your scores, maximum possible scores, and their respective weights.

Input Variables

  • Score 1: Your actual score for the first assessment.
  • Maximum 1: The maximum possible score for the first assessment.
  • Weight 1: The percentage weight of the first assessment towards the final grade.
  • Score 2: Your actual score for the second assessment.
  • Maximum 2: The maximum possible score for the second assessment.
  • Weight 2: The percentage weight of the second assessment towards the final grade.

Mathematical Formula

The formula to calculate the weighted grade is:

Weighted Grade = ((Score 1 / Maximum 1) * Weight 1 + (Score 2 / Maximum 2) * Weight 2) / (Weight 1 + Weight 2)

Example Calculation

Assume you scored 80 out of 100 on your first assessment (Weight 1 = 60%), and 90 out of 100 on your second assessment (Weight 2 = 40%).

Step 1: Calculate the weighted scores:

- Score 1 contribution: (80 / 100) * 60 = 48

- Score 2 contribution: (90 / 100) * 40 = 36

Step 2: Total contribution:

48 + 36 = 84

Step 3: Total weight = 60 + 40 = 100

Step 4: Weighted Grade = 84 / 100 = 0.84 or 84%

Practical Tips

Always ensure that the weight percentages add up to 100% for accurate results.

A common misconception is that simply averaging scores gives you the weighted grade. Always take weights into account.

Keep in mind that institutions may apply different grading policies that could affect calculated grades.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

How do I use the Grade Calculator?

To use the Grade Calculator, input your scores, maximum scores, and weights for two assessments. Click 'Calculate' to get your weighted grade.

What if my assessments have different weights?

The Grade Calculator is designed to handle assessments with differing weights. Simply enter the appropriate weights to reflect their significance in your overall grade.

Can I use this calculator for more than two scores?

Currently, the Grade Calculator is optimized for two assessments. For more complex scenarios, consider breaking down your calculations into pairs.
