Key takeaways

  • £4,000 saved earns up to £1,000 a year per person. Two eligible buyers can save £8,000 and earn £2,000 in bonuses between them.
  • £450,000 or less: a qualifying first-home withdrawal is charge-free. Anyone aged 60 or older can also withdraw charge-free.

A 25% charge can cost you your own savings

With no growth, £4,000 plus a £1,000 bonus gives you £5,000. The 25% charge takes £1,250, leaving £3,750. That is £250, or 6.25%, less than you paid in. At 20%, the same pot gives you £4,000.

Both charges apply to the amount withdrawn, including any growth. At matched returns, the 20% charge cancels the bonus and its share of returns. A fall in investment value can still leave you below your original contributions.

Lifetime ISA questions

Can I use a Lifetime ISA for a first home over £450,000?

From 29 October 2026, an otherwise eligible first-home withdrawal for a property above £450,000 has a 20% charge. Before that date the charge was 25%. A purchase at exactly £450,000 can qualify for a charge-free withdrawal.

How much of my own money can the 25% charge cost?

With £4,000 paid in, a £1,000 bonus and no growth, the charge is £1,250. You receive £3,750 and lose £250, or 6.25% of your original contribution. The actual shortfall against what you paid in depends on returns.

Can two buyers use their Lifetime ISAs for the same home?

Yes, if each buyer meets the Lifetime ISA withdrawal conditions. Two people saving £4,000 each can receive £2,000 in combined annual bonuses. The £450,000 cap applies to the whole property.

When can I open, pay into or withdraw from a Lifetime ISA?

You can generally open one from age 18 to 39 and make your first payment before 40. Contributions and bonuses stop at 50. First-home withdrawals require at least 12 months from your first payment. From age 60, withdrawals are charge-free.

How does a Lifetime ISA compare with a standard ISA or a SIPP?

A standard Cash or Stocks and Shares ISA has no Lifetime ISA bonus or government withdrawal charge. A SIPP can receive pension tax relief, but its funds are subject to pension access rules and withdrawals may be taxable. The calculator shows the SIPP balance before withdrawal tax.
