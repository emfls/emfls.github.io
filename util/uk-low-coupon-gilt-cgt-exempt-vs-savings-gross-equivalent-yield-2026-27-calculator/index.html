Key takeaways

A discounted conventional gilt pays £100 at maturity for each £100 of face value. The pull-to-par gain is CGT-exempt. Coupon income can still be taxable.

On an illustrative £50,000 purchase at £94.50 with a 0.25% coupon, 1.5 years to maturity and £15 of fees, the compounded savings break-even is about 6.6% AER at 40% tax or 7.2% at 45% when the Personal Savings Allowance (PSA) is exhausted in both cases. Prices and rates here are examples.

Could you afford to sell at a loss?

A gilt’s market value can fall before maturity, especially when yields rise. Selling early can lock in a loss and incur a bid–ask spread and dealing charges. A capital loss on an exempt gilt gives no CGT relief. The comparison assumes £100 redemption at maturity and a savings account that keeps paying the benchmark rate for the same term. Check withdrawal restrictions before comparing either option with money you may need sooner.

How the comparison works

Face value equals your investment divided by the clean price as a fraction of £100. Gross coupon is face value × annual coupon rate × years. We subtract coupon tax and total platform fees from the combined coupon and redemption gain. Fees are deducted from the final proceeds for this comparison. Gilt coupons are held as cash without reinvestment. The annualised net return is the equivalent compound rate from purchase to maturity.

We apply the entered PSA independently to each alternative in every full holding year and again in a final partial period. Savings interest is compounded after tax at each anniversary, with a fractional-period accrual at maturity. The break-even AER is the rate that equates the two after-tax pound profits under those assumptions. The zero-PSA shortcut divides the gilt’s net annualised return by one minus the tax rate. It can differ slightly from the compounded break-even over a fractional year.

For a trade quote or tax calculation, use the actual settlement amount, coupon payment dates, interest credit dates and UK tax years. This estimate excludes accrued interest at settlement, the Accrued Income Scheme, bid–ask spreads beyond the price you enter, ISA treatment, starting-rate relief and tax-band changes. It assumes a conventional unstripped gilt and a UK individual investing outside an ISA. See the DMO gilt conventions and HMRC Accrued Income Scheme guidance.

Frequently asked questions

Is the gain on a discounted UK gilt tax-free?

For a conventional qualifying UK gilt held by an individual, the gain on sale or redemption is exempt from CGT under section 115. It uses none of your CGT annual exempt amount. A capital loss on the gilt gives no CGT relief. Coupons can still be taxable.

Can I use my Personal Savings Allowance for gilt coupons?

Yes. Gilt coupons and taxable bank interest can use the same annual allowance. In 2026/27 the maximum is £1,000 at basic rate, £500 at higher rate and £0 at additional rate. Enter the amount still available after other savings income. The calculator treats the gilt and savings account as alternative uses of the same money.

What does a 6% to 8% gross-equivalent savings yield mean?

It means a taxable savings account would need that AER to match a specific gilt scenario after tax and fees. For example, with exhausted PSA a £50,000 gilt purchase at £94.50 with a 0.25% coupon, 1.5 years to maturity and £15 in fees needs about 6.6% gross savings AER at 40% tax or 7.2% at 45%. The gilt’s own after-tax annualised return is around 4%.

Why might my broker show a different yield?

A broker may use the dirty price, which includes accrued interest, and discount the actual semiannual coupon cash flows. This calculator uses the clean price and values coupons as unreinvested cash held to maturity. It models tax over holding periods instead of using actual UK tax-year boundaries.

What if I pay more than £100 or sell before maturity?

Paying above par creates a capital loss if the gilt redeems at £100. Coupon income might offset that loss economically. A sale before maturity depends on the market price and transaction costs and can lock in a loss. You cannot deduct an exempt gilt’s capital loss from your chargeable gains.
