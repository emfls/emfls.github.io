Australia Family Tax Benefit Calculator 2026–27

Estimate FTB Parts A and B, including fortnightly payments, year-end supplements and the effect of earning more.

Key takeaways

  • Part A is per child. The family income test begins at $69,131. The 30% taper begins above $123,078.
  • Part B is per family. A single parent or a couple’s higher earner can have up to $124,327 in adjusted taxable income (ATI).
  • Supplements come after balancing. Part A pays up to $970.90 per eligible child at family ATI of $80,000 or less. Part B pays up to $478.15 per eligible family.

Annual estimates start with the published DSS annual rates. To estimate fortnightly instalments, divide the regular annual rate by 365, round the daily rate to cents (minimum one cent for a positive rate), then multiply by 14. The year is 365 ÷ 14 fortnights before daily rounding. Annual figures and benefit losses use annual rates before daily rounding.

This estimate assumes full care, stable circumstances for the full financial year and that the children meet eligibility rules. It covers standard rates for parents. Shared care, income support exemptions, maintenance income and child support rules, daily health-related reductions, additional payments, Parental Leave Pay and return-to-work rules can change the actual result.

Frequently asked questions

Which income goes into the calculator?

Enter each parent's annual adjusted taxable income (ATI). ATI can include reportable super contributions, fringe benefits and net investment losses. Part A uses combined ATI for couples. Part B applies different limits to the higher and lower earners.

How do the two Part A income tests work?

Part A reduces the maximum by 20 cents per extra dollar from $69,131 to $123,078, with the base rate as a floor in that band. Above $123,078 it reduces both the Method 1 comparison rate and the Method 2 base rate by 30 cents per extra dollar and uses the higher result, floored at zero.

When are the FTB supplements paid?

Services Australia pays eligible supplements after balancing the financial year. For 2026–27, Part A is up to $970.90 per eligible child if family ATI is $80,000 or less. Part B is up to $478.15 per family. The secondary earner test can reduce the reconciled Part B amount.

What happens at the $124,327 Part B limit?

At exactly $124,327 ATI the higher earner or single parent still passes the Part B income limit. Any amount above it ends Part B, including any supplement. Couples also need a youngest eligible child under 13 and must pass the secondary income test.

Why can the benefit withdrawal rate exceed 30%?

The calculator divides the total benefit lost by the $1,000 rise in annual ATI. Both parts can taper on the same increase, or the increase can cross a limit that removes a supplement or all of Part B. The displayed percentage covers FTB only.
