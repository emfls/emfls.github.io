Canada First-Time Home Buyers’ GST Rebate Calculator 2026

Estimate your Bill C-4 rebate, provincial relief and 25-year mortgage payments on a new home. All amounts are in Canadian dollars.

Key takeaways

Eligible first-time buyers can recover the full federal 5% GST on homes up to $1 million, for up to $50,000 in federal relief. The rebate falls to $25,000 at $1.25 million and reaches $0 at $1.5 million. A $1 million Ontario purchase with the standard provincial rebate has $74,000 in combined relief. Ontario’s newer programs can increase that amount.

Enter your purchase details

You must be 18 or older and a Canadian citizen or permanent resident. You must be among the first to occupy the home after construction or renovation and intend to use it as your primary residence. Frequently asked questions

How much does Bill C-4 rebate at $1.25 million or $1.4 million?

The federal rebate is $25,000 at $1.25 million and $10,000 at $1.4 million. The $50,000 maximum falls by $0.10 for each dollar of price above $1 million until it reaches zero at $1.5 million.

Can I add the standard federal rebate to the full Bill C-4 amount?

Bill C-4 tops up the standard federal rebate where both apply. On a qualifying $350,000 home, the standard rebate is $6,300 and the Bill C-4 top-up is $11,200, for $17,500 in total federal relief.

Can I claim the first-time buyer rebate on a resale or a co-op?

A typical resale does not qualify. Eligible new or substantially renovated homes, condos, co-op shares and owner-built homes may qualify. Co-ops, owner-built homes and leased land generally require different calculations and claim forms.

Is $74,000 the most I can get back on a $1 million Ontario home?

The $74,000 example combines $50,000 in federal relief with Ontario’s $24,000 standard rebate. Ontario has a separate first-time buyer top-up and temporary enhanced relief. The provincial portion can reach $80,000 under those rules, including the standard rebate.

When can I use the rebate to lower my mortgage?

The estimate assumes the rebate lowers the amount borrowed at closing. If the builder credits it then, check that your contract reflects that amount. If you receive it later, check your lender’s prepayment terms and budget for the timing gap.
