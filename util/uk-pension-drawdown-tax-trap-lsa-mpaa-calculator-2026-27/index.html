Before you give up pension contribution headroom

Even a small first flexi-access income payment or UFPLS can trigger the MPAA. If your standard annual allowance would otherwise apply, the difference between £60,000 and the £10,000 money purchase allowance is £50,000. Unused allowances from earlier years cannot increase the MPAA. Employer and personal defined contribution payments can both count towards it.

Eligible people can use unused annual allowance from the previous three tax years under the carry-forward rules. Going over an applicable allowance can create a tax charge. HMRC annual allowance rules

Which HMRC refund form applies?

A positive overpayment is a guide to what you may be due. Use the PAYE deduction on your pension statement and your expected full-year income when you claim. HMRC or your provider may also reconcile the deduction through PAYE.

  • P55 is generally for a flexible payment that leaves funds in the pot when you expect no further flexible payments from it this tax year and the provider cannot refund you.
  • P53Z is generally for a pot you emptied when you have other PAYE income. Check HMRC’s guidance if you received a different type of lump sum.
  • P50Z is generally for a pot you emptied after stopping work with no other PAYE income except possible State Pension. HMRC asks about benefits and expected new employment.

Frequently asked questions

How do flexi-access drawdown and UFPLS differ?

With flexi-access drawdown, crystallising funds can release a tax-free pension commencement lump sum. The remaining funds move to drawdown and cash taken as income is taxable. With UFPLS, a payment comes directly from uncrystallised funds and normally includes up to 25% tax-free cash with the rest taxed as pension income.

What happens if I have used most of my Lump Sum Allowance?

The model limits tax-free cash to the unused part of the standard £268,275 allowance. Any UFPLS cash above the allowed tax-free amount is taxable. For flexi-access drawdown the model takes cash above PCLS as taxable drawdown income. Check both your LSA and your lump sum and death benefit allowance with your provider, especially if you have protection or pre-April 2024 benefits.

Does the MPAA apply if the withdrawal costs no Income Tax?

Yes, a first UFPLS or a first flexi-access income payment ordinarily triggers the MPAA even if allowances leave you with no tax to pay. Taking only a tax-free pension commencement lump sum leaves it untriggered. Once triggered, the MPAA applies in the trigger year and subsequent years, with special timing rules in the first year.

Why can Month 1 PAYE be higher than the annual tax?

A provider that uses 1257L M1 treats that payment in isolation with one month of code-based free pay and rate bands. It uses the allowance in the code. The annual Personal Allowance taper depends on adjusted net income for the whole tax year. A current P45 or an existing code can change the actual deduction.

Can I reclaim emergency pension tax during the year?

You may be able to claim if too much was deducted. P55 generally covers a part-pot withdrawal with no further payments expected from that pot in the year. P53Z and P50Z generally cover full-pot withdrawals, depending on other income and employment. Use HMRC’s refund checker to confirm the form and use the tax actually deducted when applying.

How the estimate works

Annual withdrawal tax is the difference between projected annual Income Tax with and without the taxable pension payment. This model treats all entered income as non-savings, non-dividend income and uses it as adjusted net income. It excludes savings, dividends, Gift Aid, pension relief adjustments, Scottish rates, protected allowances, LSDBA restrictions and special lump sum rules.

PAYE uses the HMRC Table A adjustment then the 2026/27 manual PAYE Tables B–D. Payroll software can differ slightly because of rounding. The overpayment or shortfall is the difference between this withholding and the modelled annual withdrawal tax. HMRC calculates any actual refund.
