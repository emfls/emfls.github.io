Canada Workers Benefit Calculator 2026

Estimate your refundable Canada Workers Benefit (CWB), including the disability supplement and the effect of the secondary earner exemption. All amounts are in Canadian dollars.

Key takeaways

  • The 2026 basic credit is up to $1,665 for a single worker or $2,869 for a family. The disability supplement adds up to $860 per qualifying worker.
  • Eligible couples can exclude up to $16,714 of the lower earner’s income from the phase-out calculation. The actual increase in the credit depends on both incomes.
  • The July, October and January split below is a planning scenario. CRA bases actual advances on the prior year’s assessed CWB.

Frequently asked questions

How much is the Canada Workers Benefit in 2026?

The standard federal basic credit pays up to $1,665 for a single worker or $2,869 for a family. An eligible worker can also receive up to $860 as a disability supplement. Alberta, Quebec and Nunavut use separate calculations.

How does the secondary earner exemption work?

For eligible couples, Schedule 6 subtracts the lesser of the lower earner’s working income, that earner’s adjusted net income and $16,714. This reduces the income used for the phase-out. The boost shown here is the difference in credit with and without the exemption.

Can both spouses get the CWB disability supplement?

Yes. Each DTC-eligible worker calculates their own supplement from their individual working income above $1,150, up to $860 each. If both spouses qualify for the DTC, each supplement phases out at 7.5% above the family threshold. One spouse claims the basic credit.

When are Advanced Canada Workers Benefit payments sent?

CRA generally issues advances in July, October and January. It uses the prior year’s assessed CWB. The 50% split here illustrates what the payments and filing balance would look like if advances totalled half your estimated 2026 credit. Use your RC210 slip for the actual amounts for your return.

Can a full-time student claim the Canada Workers Benefit?

A student enrolled full-time at a designated institution for more than 13 weeks in the year generally needs an eligible dependant on December 31 to qualify. Other conditions include Canadian residency throughout the year and being at least 19 at year-end or living with an eligible spouse, partner or child.
