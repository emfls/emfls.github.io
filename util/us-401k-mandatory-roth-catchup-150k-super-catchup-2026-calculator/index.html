How the estimate works

The calculator combines your regular deferral and planned catch-up, fills the $24,500 regular limit first, then applies your age-based catch-up cap. A 401(k) plan can apply lower deferral limits or have different catch-up availability. The base deferral has the same tax treatment in both scenarios, so the displayed tax and wealth differences come from the catch-up alone.

The income tax difference equals the modeled catch-up times your current combined marginal rate, spread across your selected pay frequency for the take-home estimate. The estimate holds payroll taxes constant and assumes the same marginal rate on every catch-up dollar. Actual payroll withholding can differ.

The wealth comparison grows one 2026 catch-up for the selected years at a constant annual return. It assumes a qualified Roth withdrawal, taxes the entire pre-tax account at your retirement rate, and assumes you invest all of the pre-tax scenario’s initial tax savings in a brokerage account. The model taxes positive brokerage gains at the end and excludes annual tax drag, tax-loss benefits, fees, inflation, and later contributions.

Frequently asked questions

Does exactly $150,000 in 2025 wages trigger mandatory Roth catch-up?

The threshold is strict. Under the Box 3 wage test, exactly $150,000 with the sponsoring employer leaves the choice open if the plan offers both options. $150,000.01 triggers Roth treatment for eligible catch-up dollars. This test uses employer wages, independent of household income or your 2026 salary.

What happens if I changed jobs or earned only self-employment income?

A new employee with zero applicable 2025 wages from the new employer falls outside the usual wage trigger. K-1 income subject only to self-employment tax also falls outside the FICA wage test. If the employers are related, use a common paymaster, or were involved in an acquisition, ask your plan which wages apply.

When can I contribute the $11,250 super catch-up?

Use the age you reach by December 31, 2026. Ages 60 through 63 have an $11,250 catch-up and a $35,750 combined employee ceiling. At age 64 the standard $8,000 catch-up applies again, for a $32,500 employee ceiling. Your plan must offer catch-up contributions.

Does the rule make my entire 401(k) deferral Roth?

The Roth requirement applies to eligible catch-up deferrals. Your regular deferral can still be pre-tax if your plan allows it. If your plan offers no Roth feature, ask the administrator whether you can make any catch-up contributions under its terms.

When are Roth earnings tax-free, and do Roth 401(k)s have RMDs?

A qualified distribution generally requires five taxable years of Roth participation and age 59½, death, or disability. Designated Roth accounts have no required minimum distributions during the owner’s lifetime. Beneficiary distribution rules can still apply.
