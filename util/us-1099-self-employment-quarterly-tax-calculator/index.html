How the estimate works

Planning estimate for one US sole proprietor, standard deduction and wage income. It excludes credits, state taxes, regular employee payroll tax, other income, itemized and special deductions, business loss limitations and special Schedule SE methods. It applies the published rate schedules and rounds calculation lines to cents. Use the IRS worksheets when filing or paying estimated tax.

Schedule SE generally starts at $400 of net self-employment earnings, calculated as 92.35% of positive business profit. Social Security is 12.4% up to $184,500 after subtracting the business owner’s Social Security wages. Medicare is 2.9% without a cap. Additional Medicare is 0.9% above combined Medicare wages and net SE earnings of $200,000 (Single or Head of household), $250,000 (joint) or $125,000 (separate). The half-SE deduction applies to the regular Social Security and Medicare amounts.

The 2026 standard deduction is $16,100 (Single or separate), $32,200 (joint) or $24,150 (Head of household). The seven federal rates run from 10% to 37%. Section 199A generally allows up to 20% of qualified profit after the half-SE deduction and applies a taxable-income limit. For one business, the model phases in the wage and property limits above $201,750 (Single or Head of household), $403,500 (joint) or $201,775 (separate), over $75,000 ($150,000 joint). The SSTB share also phases out across that range. A $400 minimum applies to eligible taxpayers with at least $1,000 of active QBI under this model. Multiple businesses, QBI carryforwards, other QBI adjustments and REIT or partnership items are outside this estimate.

Frequently asked questions

Does a spouse’s Social Security pay reduce my own wage base?

Only the sole proprietor’s own Box 3 wages reduce that proprietor’s cap. For a joint return, include both spouses’ Box 1 and Box 5 wages as household amounts and enter the proprietor’s Box 3 amount separately.

What does the quarterly schedule estimate?

It splits 90% of estimated 2026 federal tax less annual withholding into four planned installments, subject to the general $1,000 balance test. It excludes prior-year safe harbors, annualized income, payments already made and catch-up amounts. Use Form 1040-ES for your required payments.

What if I receive a 1099-NEC but also have expenses?

Use gross receipts from the same business and deduct qualifying business expenses once to find net profit. A 1099-NEC can be part of gross receipts and the Schedule SE percentages apply to net earnings.
