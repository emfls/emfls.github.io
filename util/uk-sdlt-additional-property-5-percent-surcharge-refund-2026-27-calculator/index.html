2026/27 England & Northern Ireland SDLT Rates: Permanent £125k Reset + 5% Surcharge

Following the Autumn Budget hike of the Higher Rates for Additional Dwellings (HRAD) from 3% to 5% and the April 2025 permanent reduction of the nil-rate band back to £125,000, UK buy-to-let landlords, second-home buyers, SPV limited-company purchasers, and home-movers buying before selling face substantially steeper upfront Stamp Duty Land Tax (SDLT) bills in 2026/27. Unlike slab taxes of the past, SDLT is charged progressively on each slice of the purchase price—but the 5% additional-property surcharge applies from the very first pound (£0 to £125,000 is taxed at 5%, not 0%) whenever the purchase price is £40,000 or more.

On a £450,000 residential investment property in England or Northern Ireland, standard residential SDLT equals £12,500 (£0 on the first £125,000 + £2,500 at 2% on £125k–£250k + £10,000 at 5% on £250k–£450k). Triggering the 5% additional property surcharge adds exactly £22,500 (5% × £450,000), raising the upfront cheque due to HMRC within 14 days of completion to £35,000. If the buyer is also classified as a Non-UK Resident for SDLT purposes (fewer than 183 days in the UK in the 12 months before completion), an extra 2% overseas surcharge stacks across every band—pushing the top marginal rate above £1.5 million to an eye-watering 19%.

How the 36-Month HMRC 5% Surcharge Refund Works When You Buy Before Selling

Home-movers who purchase their new main residence before completing the sale of their existing home momentarily own two residential properties on completion day—forcing their conveyancer to pay the full 5% additional property surcharge upfront within 14 days. Fortunately, Finance Act 2003 Schedule 4ZA grants an automatic statutory refund mechanism provided you satisfy three strict conditions:

  1. Previous Main Residence Test: The property you subsequently sell must have been your genuine only or main residence (not a buy-to-let rental you never lived in) at some point during the 3 years leading up to buying the new home.
  2. 36-Month Disposal Window: You must complete the sale of your old main residence within 36 months (3 years) of the completion date of the new home. (HMRC only grants extensions beyond 36 months in truly exceptional circumstances outside your control.)
  3. Statutory Claim Deadline (12 Months): You must submit your online SDLT repayment claim to HMRC within 12 months of selling the old main residence, or within 12 months of the filing date of the original SDLT return—whichever is later. HMRC typically issues bank transfers within 15 working days of verifying the claim.

Three Common SDLT Traps That Catch UK Buyers Off Guard

  • Married Couples & Civil Partners Are Treated as One Unit: Even if only Spouse B buys a new flat in their sole name, if Spouse A owns a buy-to-let flat worth £40,000+ anywhere in the world, the purchase automatically triggers the 5% additional property surcharge.
  • Overseas Properties Count Toward the 5% Surcharge: Owning a holiday apartment or inherited flat outside the UK worth £40,000 or more counts as an existing residential dwelling under HMRC rules and triggers the 5% surcharge on a UK purchase unless replacing your main residence.
  • 2% Non-Resident Refund Window: Non-UK resident buyers who pay the +2% overseas surcharge can also reclaim that 2% slice from HMRC if they subsequently spend at least 183 days in the UK within any continuous 365-day period falling in the two-year window starting 12 months before and ending 12 months after completion.

When budgeting liquidity for completion day, buyers should never confuse mortgage equity with cash required for Stamp Duty. Lenders do not allow residential home-movers to add the 5% additional-property surcharge directly onto a high-LTV mortgage above borrowing limits—meaning a £650,000 bridge buyer must hold £55,000 in liquid cleared funds for HMRC within 14 days of completion even though £32,500 will later be reclaimed once the previous main home sells. Keep your conveyancer's SDLT5 certificate, original UTRN reference number, and both completion statements archived cleanly so your online HMRC refund application clears smoothly without compliance delays. Note also that genuine mixed-use purchases (such as a commercial retail shop with a residential flat above) are assessed under non-residential SDLT rates (maximum 5% top slice) rather than residential 5% additional-property bands, though HMRC closely scrutinizes residential annex or paddock claims.

Conveyancing Timelines and Evidential Requirements for HMRC Stamp Duty Audits

When completing the purchase of an additional residential dwelling in England or Northern Ireland, your solicitor must submit the electronic Stamp Duty Land Tax return (SDLT1) to HMRC within 14 calendar days of completion. Upon payment, HMRC issues a formal SDLT5 certificate verifying that stamp duty liabilities have been discharged. Retain the SDLT5 certificate, the 14-digit Unique Transaction Reference Number (UTRN), and the signed completion statement indefinitely. If you subsequently claim the 36-month refund after disposing of your previous main home, HMRC will require copies of both completion statements, proof of residency (such as council tax bills or electoral register confirmation covering your previous tenure), and confirmation of cleared bank transfer funds. In joint ownership purchases involving married couples or civil partners where one spouse retains a pre-marital rental property, HMRC treats both individuals as a single economic unit, meaning the 5% Higher Rates for Additional Dwellings apply unless an official deed of separation or court order is in place prior to contracts being exchanged.

Regional Scope Note: This calculator applies strictly to England and Northern Ireland (HMRC SDLT). Properties in Scotland pay Land and Buildings Transaction Tax (LBTT, with an 8% Additional Dwelling Supplement), and properties in Wales pay Land Transaction Tax (LTT).

Frequently Asked Questions (2026/27)

What are the 2026/27 UK SDLT additional property surcharge bands?

In England and Northern Ireland for 2026/27, additional property SDLT rates are 5% (£0–£125k), 7% (£125k–£250k), 10% (£250k–£925k), 15% (£925k–£1.5M), and 17% (over £1.5M). Non-UK residents add a further 2% to every band, reaching 19% above £1.5M.

How much SDLT can I reclaim if I sell my old home within 36 months?

You can reclaim the entire 5% additional property surcharge—for example, £22,500 on a £450,000 purchase or £32,500 on a £650,000 purchase—provided the sold property was your previous main residence and you file the HMRC refund claim within 12 months of selling.

Do limited company SPV buy-to-let purchases pay the 5% SDLT surcharge?

Yes. Every residential property bought by a limited company (even its very first property) automatically incurs the +5% additional property surcharge. Purchases above £500,000 by companies can face a 17% flat rate unless qualifying for the genuine property rental business exemption.

Does owning a property outside the UK trigger the 5% SDLT surcharge?

Yes. HMRC looks at worldwide residential property ownership worth £40,000 or more. If you own an overseas flat and buy a UK property without replacing your main residence, the 5% surcharge applies.

How long do I have to pay Stamp Duty after completion?

Your solicitor must file the SDLT return and remit payment to HMRC within 14 calendar days of the effective date of the transaction (usually completion day) to avoid automatic late-filing penalties and daily interest.
