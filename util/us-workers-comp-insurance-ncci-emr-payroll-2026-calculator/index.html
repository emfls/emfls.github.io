Understanding 2026 US Workers' Compensation Insurance Formulas

Workers' compensation insurance is mandatory for employers in nearly every US state (with limited exceptions such as non-subscribing employers in Texas). Insurance premiums are strictly governed by statutory formulas promulgated by the National Council on Compensation Insurance (NCCI) and independent state rating bureaus (e.g., WCIRB in California, NYSIF in New York).

1. The Three Fundamental Building Blocks

  • Payroll Multiplier: Premiums are computed per $100 of gross payroll. Overtime pay is typically calculated at straight-time rates in most jurisdictions.
  • Classification Code (NCCI): Every industry is assigned a 4-digit classification code representing historical occupational hazard levels. Low-risk clerical roles (Code 8810) carry minimal rates (~$0.22/$100), whereas high-hazard construction trades (Roofing 5551) can exceed $19.50/$100.
  • Experience Modification Rate (EMR / MOD): Calculated by NCCI based on 3 consecutive policy years of claims history (excluding the most recent year). A business with zero severe claims frequently earns a credit MOD of 0.80 or lower, directly shaving 20%+ off insurance overhead.

2. Surcharges, Assessments, and Audits

In addition to modified premiums, insurance policies include statutory state assessment charges (funding guarantee and uninsured employer funds), the federal Terrorism Risk Insurance Act (TRIA) fee, and standard expense constants ($200 to $300) to cover policy issuance costs. Final premiums are subject to an annual year-end payroll audit.
