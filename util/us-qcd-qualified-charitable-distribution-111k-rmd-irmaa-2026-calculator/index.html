2026 QCD vs. Cash Giving Calculator

Compare federal income tax, required minimum distributions (RMDs) and Medicare surcharges when you make a qualified charitable distribution (QCD) from your IRA or donate cash.

Key takeaways

  • A qualifying direct IRA gift at age 70½ or older can exclude up to $111,000 per owner from 2026 income and count toward that owner’s RMD. Two eligible spouses can exclude up to $222,000 from their own IRAs.
  • A $20,000 eligible cash gift can yield at most a $1,000 deduction for a single filer using the standard deduction, or $2,000 on a joint return. The other $19,000 or $18,000 produces no current federal deduction under that rule.
  • Moving below the first IRMAA cutoff saves $1,148.40 annually per person enrolled in both parts at 2026 rates. A 2026 gift generally affects 2028 premiums.

Taking the RMD first can cost you the exclusion on those dollars

An IRA distribution paid to you remains taxable even if you give the proceeds away. Arrange a qualifying direct transfer before taking taxable withdrawals if you want the gift to cover your outstanding RMD. A later qualifying transfer can still be excluded, subject to the limits, but cannot undo your earlier taxable distribution.

Deductible IRA contributions for years you were 70½ or older can reduce the exclusion until the unused offset is used up. Use the IRS worksheet to calculate your unused amount and enter it under the IRA owner’s options.

What the estimate includes

The cash scenario takes at least the entered IRA RMDs and donates the full planned amount from cash. The QCD scenario transfers eligible IRA funds, pays any outstanding RMD and funds the rest of the gift with cash. Each spouse has a separate IRA limit and RMD. The 0.5% haircut avoided is the difference in gifts disallowed by the floor between the two scenarios.

The calculation assumes fully pre-tax eligible IRAs, sufficient IRA assets and no other QCDs, gifts or carryovers for 2026. The cash estimate assumes a qualifying public charity. Donor-advised funds and supporting organizations are excluded from the non-itemizer deduction. It holds other AGI and the selected deduction method constant, uses ordinary federal rates, and assumes no foreign-income addbacks for the senior deduction. Taxable direct IRA gifts from the contribution offset enter the itemized estimate only. State taxes, credits, AMT, net investment income tax, changes in taxable Social Security and preferential capital-gain rates are outside the model.

Frequently asked questions

Who can make a QCD in 2026?

You must be at least 70½ on the distribution date and direct the IRA trustee to pay an eligible charity. Donor-advised funds, supporting organizations and most private foundations do not qualify. An ongoing SEP or SIMPLE IRA is ineligible. The $111,000 limit applies separately to each eligible IRA owner.

Does a QCD satisfy my RMD if I already took a withdrawal?

A qualifying transfer reduces only that IRA owner’s remaining RMD, dollar for dollar up to the amount still due. Earlier taxable withdrawals remain taxable. An eligible transfer can still be excluded after the RMD is met, subject to the annual limit and the deductible contribution offset.

Can I use the standard deduction and still benefit from a QCD?

Yes. The eligible amount is excluded from income and the standard deduction and enhanced senior deduction may still apply. For an eligible cash gift, the non-itemizer deduction is limited to $1,000 single or $2,000 joint. Itemizers are subject to the 0.5% AGI floor, and the portion of deductions subject to the 37% bracket limitation is generally worth 35%.

Can I use a QCD for a charitable trust or gift annuity?

An eligible IRA owner can make a one-time election to fund a qualifying charitable remainder annuity trust (CRAT), charitable remainder unitrust (CRUT) or charitable gift annuity (CGA). The 2026 election limit is $55,000 and uses part of the $111,000 annual limit. Special funding and payout rules apply.

When would a 2026 QCD affect Medicare IRMAA?

SSA generally uses tax information from two years before the Medicare premium year, so a 2026 QCD generally affects 2028 premiums. This calculator applies published 2026 rates to illustrate the income thresholds. Cash charitable deductions reduce taxable income and leave Medicare MAGI unchanged.
