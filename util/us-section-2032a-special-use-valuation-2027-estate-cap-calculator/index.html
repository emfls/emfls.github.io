10-year recapture tax warning

Under IRC §2032A(c), a disposition outside the qualified heir’s family or cessation of qualified use can trigger additional estate tax if it occurs within 10 years after the decedent’s death and before the qualified heir dies. A lapse in material participation can count as cessation. Special timing rules can extend the period when qualified use begins after death. See the IRS instructions for Form 706-A.

Eligibility and filing rules

  • Qualified use and heir. IRC §2032A(a), (b) and (e) cover qualifying U.S. real property used for farming or in a trade or business. The decedent must have been a U.S. citizen or resident at death. A qualified heir is a family member as defined in §2032A(e) who acquires the interest from the decedent.
  • Pre-death history. The general rule under §2032A(b)(1)(C) requires at least five years in total during the eight years ending at death in which the decedent or family owned and used the property in a qualified use and materially participated in operating the farm or business. See §2032A(b)(4)–(5) for special rules.
  • 50% threshold. Use the adjusted value of real and personal property used in a qualified use by the decedent or family at death and passing from the decedent to qualified heirs.
  • 25% threshold. Use the adjusted value of real property passing to qualified heirs and meeting the pre-death history rule. The election must include enough of that real property to meet this threshold.
  • Election and valuation. File Form 706 with Schedule T, formerly Schedule A-1. A regular election requires the notice, a signed agreement from the parties with an interest in the specially valued property, and supporting appraisals and statements. Use the applicable valuation method in IRC §2032A(e)(7)–(8).

Worked examples

  • 2027, cap applies. $3,600,000 FMV minus $1,700,000 special-use value yields a $1,900,000 uncapped reduction. Apply $1,500,000 and include $2,100,000 in the gross estate.
  • 2026, same parcel. Apply the $1,460,000 cap to the same $1,900,000 difference and include $2,140,000 in the gross estate.
  • 2027, below the cap. $2,400,000 FMV minus $1,350,000 special-use value yields a $1,050,000 reduction. The parcel enters the gross estate at $1,350,000.

The presets assume both factual answers are yes. In the first two examples, $4,800,000 of $8,000,000 meets the 50% test at 60%, and $2,400,000 meets the 25% test at 30%. In the third example, $3,000,000 and $1,500,000 of a $6,000,000 adjusted gross estate meet the thresholds exactly. These adjusted values assume applicable debt deductions.

Frequently asked questions

What is the Section 2032A cap for 2027 and 2026?

The estate-wide reduction is capped at $1,500,000 for deaths in 2027 and $1,460,000 for deaths in 2026. The cap limits the reduction in gross estate value. It is not a tax credit or an amount of tax owed.

How do the 50% and 25% tests differ?

Both tests use the adjusted gross estate as the denominator. The 50% test counts qualifying real and personal property in qualified use at death that passes to qualified heirs. The 25% test counts qualifying real property passing to qualified heirs that meets the pre-death ownership, use and material participation rules. The election must cover sufficient real property to satisfy the 25% test.

Where does the executor make the Section 2032A election?

Use Form 706 and Schedule T, formerly Schedule A-1. For a regular election, include the notice of election, the signed special-use valuation agreement, and the required appraisals and supporting statements.

What can trigger the 10-year recapture tax?

A disposition outside the qualified heir’s family or cessation of qualified use generally triggers additional estate tax if it occurs within 10 years after the decedent’s death and before the qualified heir dies. Special timing and participation rules may apply. Refer to IRC §2032A(c) and Form 706-A.
