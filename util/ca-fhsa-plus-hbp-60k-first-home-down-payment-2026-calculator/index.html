Stacking Canada's FHSA ($40,000) and Expanded HBP ($60,000) for the Same First Home in 2026

Canada's first-time homebuyer tax architecture allows qualifying buyers to combine both the First Home Savings Account (FHSA) and the expanded RRSP Home Buyers' Plan (HBP) toward the exact same qualifying home purchase. For a single buyer, that unlocks C$40,000 in lifetime FHSA contributions (plus unlimited tax-free investment growth) alongside C$60,000 in tax-free RRSP HBP withdrawals—C$100,000+ in tax-advantaged principal before investment gains and CRA tax refunds. For a qualifying couple purchasing together, the combined ceiling doubles to C$80,000 in FHSA principal + C$120,000 in HBP withdrawals = C$200,000+, easily clearing the 20% conventional down payment hurdle on a C$750,000 to C$1,000,000 home and saving tens of thousands in CMHC mortgage default insurance premiums.

Why You Should ALWAYS Max the FHSA Before Adding Extra RRSP Dollars

Although both accounts give you an upfront income tax deduction at your combined federal and provincial marginal rate (typically 36%–43% on incomes between C$90,000 and C$150,000 in Ontario, BC, Alberta, or Quebec), the FHSA is mathematically superior to the RRSP HBP on every single dimension:

  • Zero Repayment Forever: Every dollar withdrawn from an FHSA for a qualifying first home—including 100% of investment growth—is permanently tax-free and NEVER has to be repaid. By contrast, an HBP withdrawal is effectively a 0%-interest loan from your own RRSP that must be repaid over 15 years (1/15th per year), or else each missed annual installment is added straight to your taxable income.
  • No 90-Day Holding Rule on FHSA: Unlike RRSP contributions (which MUST sit inside the RRSP for at least 90 full days before being withdrawn under the HBP to remain tax-deductible), you can contribute C$8,000 (or C$16,000 with 1-year carryforward) into your FHSA and withdraw it for a home closing the very next week while keeping the full income tax deduction!
  • Carryforward Room Requires Opening the Account Early: FHSA contribution room (C$8,000/year, max C$8,000 unused carryforward = C$16,000 max contribution in any single year, C$40,000 lifetime) only begins accumulating once you formally open an FHSA account—even with a C$1 initial balance.

CRA's Extended 5-Year HBP Grace Period (2026 Withdrawal → First Repayment in 2031)

Historically, Canadians withdrawing from their RRSP under the Home Buyers' Plan had to begin making 1/15th annual repayments in the second calendar year after withdrawal. Under the federal temporary repayment relief rules (extended for qualifying withdrawals made through December 31, 2028), buyers who make a first HBP withdrawal in 2026 receive an extended 5-year grace period—meaning your very first required HBP repayment is pushed all the way out to the 2031 tax year (repaid across 15 equal annual installments from 2031 through 2045). On a couple's maximum C$120,000 HBP withdrawal, that five-year breathing window frees up C$8,000 per year (C$40,000 total) during your cash-tightest initial five years of mortgage ownership!

The "Tax Refund Multiplier" Strategy & 90-Day RRSP Trap Warning

Smart first-time buyers do not treat CRA tax refunds as spending money—they fold them directly into the closing down payment. On our default Ontario couple scenario (37.91% marginal bracket, C$48,000 in new FHSA contributions over 3 years plus C$30,000 in new RRSP contributions), CRA tax deductions generate C$29,570 in cash tax refunds (C$18,197 from FHSA + C$11,373 from RRSP). Combined with C$71,484 in projected FHSA funds (including C$7,484 tax-free investment growth), C$120,000 in HBP withdrawals, and C$20,000 in TFSA cash, the couple amasses C$241,054 (32.1% down on a C$750,000 home)—eliminating CMHC insurance (~C$16,800+ saved!) and shrinking monthly mortgage payments significantly. Just mark your calendar for two strict compliance deadlines: (1) deposit any new RRSP funds intended for the HBP at least 90 days before filing Form T1036 withdrawal, and (2) sign your written Agreement of Purchase and Sale and close before October 1 of the year following your FHSA/HBP withdrawal.

Provincial Land Transfer Tax Rebates & FHSA Deduction Carryforward Optimization

To squeeze maximum after-tax value from Canada's first-home incentives in 2026, pair your FHSA and HBP plan with two often-missed provincial and federal rules. First, remember that FHSA tax deductions do not have to be claimed in the year you contribute: if you expect a promotion or higher tax bracket next year (jumping from 29.65% to 43.41% in Ontario), you can contribute C$8,000 today to start tax-free compounding immediately and carry the deduction forward to claim at your peak marginal rate. Second, budget for closing costs alongside provincial First-Time Home Buyer Land Transfer Tax (LTT) rebates—worth up to C$4,000 provincially in Ontario (plus an extra C$4,475 municipal rebate in Toronto), full Property Transfer Tax exemption in British Columbia up to C$500,000 (partial up to C$835,000), and the federal First-Time Home Buyers' Tax Credit worth C$1,500 in non-refundable tax relief. Combining FHSA, HBP, CRA tax refunds, and LTT rebates regularly adds C$40,000 to C$65,000 in net purchasing power for Canadian couples buying their first home.

What If You Never Buy a Home? The Tax-Free FHSA-to-RRSP Fallback

Many Canadian renters hesitate to open an FHSA because they are unsure whether they will purchase real estate within the 15-year account participation window (or before turning age 71). Under CRA rules, there is virtually zero downside risk: if you ultimately decide not to buy a qualifying home, you can transfer your entire accumulated FHSA balance—including all C$40,000 in contributions plus every dollar of tax-free investment growth—directly into your RRSP or RRIF on a tax-deferred basis without consuming a single dollar of your existing RRSP contribution room. Note also that CRA's "first-time homebuyer" test looks only at the current calendar year plus the preceding four calendar years; if neither you nor your spouse/common-law partner lived in a home owned by either of you during that four-year lookback window, you reset eligibility for both programs.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use both the FHSA and the $60,000 RRSP Home Buyers' Plan for the same house in 2026?

Yes. CRA rules explicitly allow qualifying first-time homebuyers to combine tax-free withdrawals from both an FHSA ($40,000 contribution cap plus growth) and the RRSP HBP ($60,000 per buyer / $120,000 per couple) for the exact same qualifying home purchase.

When do I have to start repaying my 2026 HBP withdrawal?

Under CRA's extended 5-year grace period, an HBP withdrawal made in 2026 does not require its first annual repayment until the 2031 tax year, with repayments spread evenly over 15 years (2031 through 2045). FHSA withdrawals never need to be repaid.

How long must money stay in my RRSP or FHSA before withdrawing for a down payment?

RRSP contributions must remain in the account for at least 90 days before an HBP withdrawal to remain tax-deductible. By contrast, FHSA contributions have NO 90-day waiting period—you can deduct an FHSA contribution even if you withdraw it shortly afterward for a qualifying closing.

What happens if I miss an annual HBP repayment starting in 2031?

Any shortfall below your required 1/15th annual HBP repayment is automatically added to your taxable income on that year's T1 return and taxed at your marginal federal and provincial rate—and you permanently lose that RRSP contribution room.
