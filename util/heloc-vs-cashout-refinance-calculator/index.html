What this estimate includes

  
Both options use fixed monthly principal and interest (P&I). Net borrowing cost equals payments made plus upfront fees and the remaining principal at exit, less the starting debt. The comparison excludes variable HELOC rate resets, interest-only payments during a HELOC draw period, private mortgage insurance (PMI), property taxes, and escrow. This is an estimate and is not financial advice.

  
Simple break-even divides the extra upfront fees by the initial monthly P&I savings. Savings can change when one loan pays off. The net cost at your exit month accounts for those payment changes.

FAQ

  

    When does keeping an existing low-rate mortgage with a HELOC beat a Cash-Out Refinance?
    
When your existing first mortgage rate is much lower than current refinance rates and the extra cash needed is modest relative to closing costs, a blended first-mortgage-plus-HELOC structure often produces a lower blended rate and lower total net cost over short to medium holding periods.

  

  

    How is break-even handled if closing costs exceed monthly savings within the loan term?
    
Break-even is strictly bounded by the loan term; if arithmetic fee recovery exceeds the remaining maturity, no break-even occurs within the term and net borrowing cost through your horizon identifies the cheaper option.

How the Calculation Works & Mathematical Formula
This calculator allows you to compare two options: keeping your existing mortgage and adding a Home Equity Line of Credit (HELOC) versus opting for a cash-out refinance.
Input Variables
  • Existing first mortgage balance ($): The total amount of your current mortgage.
  • Existing first mortgage note rate (%): The interest rate on your current mortgage.
  • Existing mortgage remaining term (years): The duration left until your current mortgage is repaid.
  • Extra cash needed ($): The amount of additional cash you require.
  • HELOC interest rate (%): The interest rate on the HELOC.
  • HELOC repayment term (years): The duration you have to repay the HELOC.
  • HELOC upfront fees ($): Any fees incurred to set up the HELOC.
  • New refinance rate (%): The interest rate on the new refinance loan.
Mathematical Formula
The blended rate for both options can be calculated as:
Blended Rate = (Current Mortgage Payment + HELOC Payment) / Total Loan Amount

Decision-Making Tips
1. Analyze how long you plan to stay in your home; if short-term, a HELOC might be better.
2. Avoid common misconception: HELOC rates can vary and may increase in the future.
3. Be aware of the limitations, including potential PMI for new loans and the variability of HELOC rates.

  

    
Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

    

      

        What are the main differences between HELOC and cash-out refinance?
        
A HELOC allows you to borrow against your home's equity with a variable interest rate, while cash-out refinance replaces your existing mortgage with a larger one, providing fixed cash upfront. Your decision should consider interest rates, fees, and your long-term plans.

      

      

        How does the break-even point affect my decision?
        
The break-even point indicates when the cost of refinancing equals the savings. If you plan to exit your home before this point, a HELOC may offer more immediate savings compared to a cash-out refinance.

      

      

        Are there any hidden costs I should be aware of?
        
Yes, both financing options may include hidden costs like appraisal fees, closing costs, and potential PMI. Always review your loan estimates thoroughly to avoid unexpected expenses.
