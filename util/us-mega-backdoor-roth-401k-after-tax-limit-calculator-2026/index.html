Frequently asked questions

How much can I put into a mega backdoor Roth in 2026?

Start with the lesser of $72,000 and 100% of eligible compensation. Subtract regular elective deferrals and employer additions in this employer group. With a $24,500 regular deferral and $9,000 in employer additions, that leaves $38,500 before plan or payroll restrictions.

Does the $11,250 super catch-up increase after-tax room?

The $11,250 catch-up for employees ages 60–63 at year-end is an additional elective deferral. It sits above the §415(c) ceiling and can bring total additions to $83,250. Voluntary after-tax room still comes from the base $72,000 ceiling.

When must my 2026 catch-up go to Roth?

For an eligible age-50-plus participant, 2026 catch-up dollars generally must go to Roth if 2025 FICA wages from the employer sponsoring the plan exceeded $150,000. Wages of exactly $150,000 fall at the threshold. Ask payroll which wages count if your employer changed through a merger or acquisition.

What happens to earnings before I convert?

After-tax contribution basis can move to Roth without additional income tax. Pre-conversion earnings generally become taxable ordinary income if moved to Roth. An eligible direct rollover may send the pretax portion to a traditional IRA or another eligible plan under IRS allocation rules.

Can every 401(k) participant use this strategy?

Your plan has to allow voluntary after-tax contributions and either in-plan Roth conversion or an eligible in-service rollover. Plan contribution caps, conversion schedules, and nondiscrimination testing can restrict how much you can use.
