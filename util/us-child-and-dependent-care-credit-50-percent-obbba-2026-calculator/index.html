How the comparison works

Form 2441 subtracts excluded benefits from both qualified costs and the statutory expense cap, then applies the earned-income limit. The calculator applies the 2026 rates and any entered tax liability limit to the remaining amount. Extra credit compares the 2026 and 2025 rate schedules using the same AGI, remaining expenses, and tax limit. The 2025 dependent care exclusion cap was $5,000.

The optimizer adjusts AGI for the change in the estimated exclusion and holds earned income, the marginal tax rate and any entered credit liability limit constant. It assumes current-year qualifying care is incurred, paid and eligible for reimbursement. Carryovers, employer-paid benefits, forfeiture losses and effects on other credits or state taxes are outside the estimate.

Frequently asked questions

How much is the 2026 child and dependent care credit?

The credit is 20% to 50% of eligible expenses. Before other limits, the maximum is $1,500 for one qualifying person or $3,000 for two or more. The credit is nonrefundable and is limited by the amount available on the Form 2441 Credit Limit Worksheet.

When does the 2026 credit rate fall below 35%?

The rate drops below 35% once AGI exceeds $75,000 for single and head-of-household filers or $150,000 for joint filers. The 20% floor starts above $103,000 or $206,000, respectively.

Can I use a dependent care FSA and claim Form 2441?

Yes, if you still have eligible expenses and room under the credit cap. Excluded dependent care benefits reduce both your eligible expenses and the $3,000 or $6,000 expense cap. A full $7,500 exclusion leaves no credit for current-year expenses.

Who and what count for the credit?

Qualifying people generally include a dependent child under 13 or a spouse or other qualifying person who cannot care for themselves. Qualifying care lets you work or look for work. Day care and day camp may qualify. Overnight camp and school tuition from kindergarten onward are excluded. Use Form 2441 to report provider information and check the earned-income rules.

Can I claim the credit if I file separately from my spouse?

Married filing separately generally gives a $0 credit. A lived-apart exception can apply if you lived apart during the last six months, your qualifying person lived in your home for more than half the year, and you paid more than half the cost of keeping up that home. Check Form 2441 line A. The calculator supports Single, Head of household, and Married filing jointly.
