Key takeaways

  • TFSA withdrawals generally leave GIS unchanged and carry no income tax. Ordinary taxable RRSP and RRIF withdrawals count toward GIS.
  • Where the 50% base GIS recovery applies, adding 20% income tax can leave about $30 of extra cash for each $100 withdrawn. Your result depends on the income bands and GIS remaining.
  • Eligible work earnings have a separate exemption per person. The first $5,000 is fully exempt and half of the next $10,000 is exempt.

How the estimate works

We subtract the eligible work exemption for each person and use the published income band to find monthly GIS. The RRSP/RRIF scenario adds the gross taxable withdrawal to assessable income. The effective loss rate is estimated tax plus the drop in annualized GIS, divided by the gross withdrawal. Because payments use income bands, a small withdrawal that crosses a band can have an unusually high effective loss rate.

Q4 2026 normally uses 2025 income. Use a consistent assessment year for all inputs when comparing scenarios. For later withdrawals, the calculation illustrates what the same income would do at Q4 2026 rates. Service Canada may use estimated current-year income in certain cases, such as a drop in pension income or retirement.

The model assumes the GIS recipient receives full OAS and counts only GIS in the benefit totals, including for a household whose partner receives the Allowance. The tax estimate uses a single marginal rate. It excludes credits, provincial benefits, OAS recovery tax and RRIF minimum withdrawal requirements.

Frequently asked questions

Is the GIS clawback always 50%?

A common base reduction is about 50 cents per dollar of countable income for a single recipient, or 25 cents each when both partners receive GIS. The top-up, household category and income bands can change the actual reduction.

Do RRSP, RRIF and CPP payments count toward GIS?

Ordinary taxable RRSP and RRIF withdrawals, CPP and other countable pension or investment income generally enter the GIS income test. The employment earnings exemption applies to eligible work income.

How does the GIS employment exemption work?

For each person, the first $5,000 of eligible employment or self-employment earnings and half of the next $10,000 are exempt. The maximum exemption is $10,000 per person.

Does a TFSA withdrawal reduce GIS?

An ordinary TFSA withdrawal is tax-free and does not count as income for federal GIS. With the same gross withdrawal, the cash difference equals the estimated tax and GIS loss on the RRSP or RRIF withdrawal.

Can withdrawing from an RRSP before age 65 avoid a GIS reduction?

An early withdrawal can avoid GIS recovery if that income falls outside the income period used for GIS and does not affect a partner’s benefits. RRSP income is still generally taxable, and prior-year income can affect your first GIS payment period.
