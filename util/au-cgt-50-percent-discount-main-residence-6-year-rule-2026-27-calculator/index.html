Key takeaways

If you lived in the home before renting it out, an eligible election can exempt up to six calendar years of a continuous rental absence. For a partial exemption, multiply the gain by non-exempt days divided by ownership days. Apply available capital losses before the 50% discount, then add the remaining gain to your other taxable income.

Frequently asked questions

Does the six-year limit always equal 2,191 days?

The limit is six calendar years. A six-year span can contain 2,191 elapsed days, and leap days can make it longer. For a continuous rental, the calculator follows the ATO example and protects rental days through the sixth anniversary, counting the start day. The election requires a previous main residence and generally precludes nominating another home for the same period.

What if I rented the property before moving in?

Initial rental days remain taxable. Once you establish the property as your main residence, the absence election can cover an eligible later rental. The first-income-use market value reset does not apply to a home rented from acquisition.

When must I use the market value at first rental?

Section 118-192 generally requires the reset when the sale is partly exempt, the first income-producing use was after 20 August 1996 and a sale immediately before that use would have been fully exempt. Use the market value on that date plus eligible later costs, and measure ownership days and discount eligibility from that deemed acquisition.

When do losses and the 50% discount apply?

Multiply the gain by non-exempt days divided by ownership days. Use current and carried-forward capital losses before the discount. An eligible Australian resident generally qualifies when the sale contract is signed after the 12-month anniversary of the purchase contract or deemed acquisition. Unused losses can offset other capital gains or carry forward.

How is the extra income tax worked out?

Add the net capital gain to other taxable income and compare the tax on that total with the tax on other income alone. For 2026–27 the resident rate between $18,200 and $45,000 is 15%. The optional 2% Medicare estimate uses the full rate and excludes reductions and surcharge.
