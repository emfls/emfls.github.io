Key takeaways

  • The 2026 §461(l) threshold is $256,000 for most noncorporate filers and $512,000 for joint returns.
  • W-2 employee wages stay outside the business loss calculation. They can provide taxable income for the allowed loss to offset.
  • The disallowed excess becomes a 2027 net operating loss (NOL) carryforward. In this estimate, you can use it against up to 80% of taxable income each year, and the unused balance carries forward indefinitely.

Form 461 and NOL questions

What are the 2026 Form 461 thresholds?

The 2026 threshold is $512,000 for a joint return and $256,000 for other filers, including single filers, heads of household, married people filing separately, estates and trusts. The One Big Beautiful Bill Act made the excess business loss limitation permanent.

Can W-2 wages offset my business loss?

Exclude W-2 wages and other employee compensation when calculating the Form 461 business loss. After applying the limitation, the allowable loss can reduce taxable nonbusiness income, including wages, interest, dividends and taxable capital gains.

Can bonus depreciation or cost segregation increase my deferred loss?

Yes. Additional eligible depreciation can raise the aggregate loss above the Form 461 threshold. Apply basis, at-risk and passive activity limits before including deductions in the calculation. The amount deferred is measured after netting eligible business income against eligible deductions.

What happens to a disallowed 2026 excess business loss?

The disallowed amount is treated as a net operating loss carryover to 2027. Any unused balance from this loss can be carried forward indefinitely. Form 172 provides the rules for combining it with any separately computed NOL.

How does the 80% NOL cap work in 2027?

If the Form 461 carryforward is your only NOL and 2027 taxable income before NOL, section 199A and section 250 deductions is $150,000, the maximum deduction is $120,000. A $214,000 carryforward would leave $94,000 available for later years.
