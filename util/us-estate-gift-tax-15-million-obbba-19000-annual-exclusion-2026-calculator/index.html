Key takeaways

  • OBBBA sets the 2026 federal estate and gift tax basic exclusion at $15 million per person, with inflation adjustments after 2026 and no scheduled sunset. A $0 federal estimate can still leave a tax bill

Skipping the Form 706 portability election can cost a surviving spouse access to unused federal exemption. A federal estate tax estimate of $0 can also leave a separate state liability. Twelve states and Washington, D.C. impose estate taxes. For 2026, examples include Oregon at $1 million, Massachusetts at $2 million, Washington at $3.076 million through June and $3 million from July 1, and New York at $7.35 million. Domicile, property location, and the date of death can affect state filing and tax.

How the estimate works

Subtract debts and charitable bequests from the gross estate. Subtract prior taxable gifts and the taxable portion of planned gifts from the lifetime exemption. The model floors both amounts at zero, then applies 40% to the net estate above the remaining exemption.

The sunset comparison substitutes an illustrative $7 million per person ($14 million with full portability), holding the other assumptions constant. The maximum modeled savings are $3.2 million for an individual or $6.4 million in the married scenario. This is a planning estimate for U.S. citizens or residents. It excludes separate gift tax, state taxes, and other Form 706 deductions and credits.

Frequently asked questions

How does a married couple reach the $30 million exemption?

The first spouse’s estate generally must file Form 706 to elect portability of any unused exclusion. The model assumes the survivor has $15 million of their own basic exclusion in 2026 plus the full $15 million of unused exclusion from the first spouse. Actual DSUE depends on the first spouse’s applicable exclusion and how much they used.

When does a 2026 gift trigger Form 709?

For the present-interest gifts modeled here, the calculator flags gifts above $19,000 per recipient from one donor. Gift splitting can reduce the taxable amount to zero, but the spouses still need to make the election on Form 709. The IRS generally requires separate returns from both spouses, with limited exceptions. Future-interest gifts and certain other transfers can have additional filing rules.

Which tuition and medical payments qualify for unlimited exclusion?

Pay qualifying tuition directly to an eligible school or qualifying medical expenses directly to the provider or insurer. Eligible amounts leave the annual exclusion and lifetime exemption available for other gifts. Room and board, books, reimbursements to the recipient, and medical expenses reimbursed by insurance fall outside this exclusion.

How do lifetime gifts change the estate tax estimate?

Taxable gifts use lifetime exemption after any applicable annual exclusion. For example, an individual’s $30,000 present-interest gift to one recipient uses $11,000 of lifetime exemption in 2026. In this estimate, that leaves $14,989,000 of exemption before any other taxable gifts.

What does the pre-OBBBA sunset savings figure measure?

It compares the estate tax estimate under the 2026 OBBBA exemption with the same calculation using an illustrative $7 million exemption per person. The maximum difference is $3.2 million per person at the 40% rate. Actual modeled savings depend on net estate value and taxable gifts.
