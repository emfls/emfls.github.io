Key takeaways

Your regular safe harbor is the smaller of 90% of projected 2026 tax or 100% or 110% of eligible 2025 tax. Subtract expected federal withholding and split the remainder over four due dates. Frequently asked questions

How much estimated tax should I pay each quarter in 2026?

For the regular installment method, take the smaller of 90% of 2026 tax or 100% or 110% of eligible 2025 tax. Subtract expected 2026 federal withholding, floor the result at zero, and divide by four. The small-balance exception can reduce the installment to zero.

Who uses the 110% prior-year safe harbor?

Generally, use 110% if 2025 AGI was above $150,000, or above $75,000 if filing separately in 2026. At exactly the applicable threshold, the prior-year percentage is 100%. The 90% current-year amount may still be lower.

Does the $1,000 exception apply at exactly $1,000?

The exception applies only if projected 2026 tax after withholding is less than $1,000. At exactly $1,000, compare the safe-harbor targets and subtract withholding to see if an installment remains.

Can increasing year-end withholding help with earlier shortfalls?

Federal income tax withholding is generally allocated one-fourth to each installment deadline. Extra withholding through a 2026 paycheck can therefore reduce earlier shortfalls. A later estimated payment can stop further penalty accrual on the amount it covers.

Can I owe an underpayment penalty after catching up in January?

Yes. An earlier shortage can accrue a penalty until the payment that covers it. If income arrived unevenly, Form 2210 Schedule AI may reduce earlier required installments. This calculator uses the regular installment method and one assumed annual rate.
