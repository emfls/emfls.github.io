Key takeaways

  • FIG can cut UK tax on qualifying foreign income and gains to 0%. Claim in your first four-year window after at least 10 consecutive non-UK tax years. Amounts covered by relief can be brought to the UK without a further UK tax charge.
  • Designating £100,000 at 12% instead of 15% costs £3,000 less. The 12% tax year ends on 5 April 2027. The final 15% year ends on 5 April 2028.
  • Worldwide inheritance tax exposure starts with 10 of the previous 20 UK tax years. A 3–10 year tail can apply after departure.

Compare a FIG claim with standard UK tax

A FIG claim can increase your UK tax. A claim gives up the Personal Allowance of up to £12,570 and the £3,000 CGT annual exemption for the whole tax year. With small foreign amounts, the extra tax on other income and gains can outweigh the relief.

Frequently asked questions

Who qualifies for four-year FIG relief?

You must be UK tax resident and within four consecutive tax years of first becoming UK resident after at least 10 consecutive non-UK tax years. An intervening non-resident year uses up one of the four years. Only qualifying foreign income and gains arising in a claim year can receive the relief.

Can a FIG claim increase my tax?

Yes. Even a claim on a single source removes your Personal Allowance and Capital Gains Tax annual exemption for the whole year. The standard arising basis can cost less when the foreign tax saved is smaller than the extra UK tax on your other income and gains.

Can I choose which foreign amounts to claim or remit?

You may claim on selected qualifying foreign sources through Self Assessment using SA109 and the relevant foreign income or gains schedules. Amounts covered by the claim can be remitted to the UK without a further UK charge. Keep legacy pre-6 April 2025 amounts separate because they follow the earlier remittance rules.

Do I have to transfer funds before the TRF window ends?

You must make a valid designation for 2025/26, 2026/27 or 2027/28. The first two years charge 12% and the last year charges 15%. A former remittance-basis user must be UK resident in the designation year. Once designated, the funds can be remitted later without a further UK charge.

When can UK inheritance tax reach my overseas assets?

The general adult test looks for 10 UK-resident tax years in the 20 years before the chargeable event year. After leaving the UK, the general tail is three years for 10 to 13 years of residence and increases by one year for each additional year, up to 10. Transitional rules and other exceptions can apply.
